## single brief(s) per idea

**subject `soundness`**

> You are given a research plan: the problem, the method and the experiment design that a team intends to carry out. It has not been executed. Apply your method to the technical soundness of the plan: whether its theory, method and experiment design are valid and would support its claims. Check each part of the theory, method and experiment design thoroughly, one claim, assumption, step, baseline, control or comparison at a time. Ask whether it makes sense, whether it follows naturally from the part before it, whether it is backed by enough credible support (evidence, derivation, citation or justification), and whether that support is enough to actually prove it. Use only the plan and your own general knowledge; do not search the web. Do not report writing, style or formatting issues. In Evidence, quote verbatim the passages of the plan that your finding rests on.

**subject `experiments`**

> You are given a research plan: the problem, the method and the experiment design that a team intends to carry out. It has not been executed. Apply your method to the technical soundness of the plan's experiment design: whether its baselines, controls, ablations, metrics and sample sizes would actually support its claims. Check each part of the experiment design thoroughly, one baseline, control, metric or comparison at a time. Ask whether it makes sense, whether it follows naturally from the claim it tests and from the part before it, whether it is backed by enough credible support (evidence or justification), and whether the evidence it would produce is enough to actually prove the claim. Use only the plan and your own general knowledge; do not search the web. Do not report writing, style or formatting issues. In Evidence, quote verbatim the passages of the plan that your finding rests on.
