## To assess recognition memory over time and investigate conditions under which a memory can be preserved, retrieved, or forgotten, we will utilize a mouse model of object memory based on an object recognition task that exploits the natural tendency of mice to explore novel stimuli.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed object-recognition method comes from [(Nomoto et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d5530db8f36fd580af3f270df37d058aab2bab0), which used a novelty-based novel object recognition (NOR) paradigm in mice to compare short-term and long-term object memory after weak training, behavioural tag training, and control conditions. This directly supports the plan's core use of a mouse object-recognition task to assess recognition memory over time, because it demonstrates that NOR can be used to distinguish memory strength across retention intervals and that a weak training episode can be converted into long-term memory by a tagging procedure. It also supports the plan's engram manipulation strategy, especially Experiment 2, because the paper assessed neuronal overlap between initial weak training and tag training and used optogenetic manipulation of the initial context-ensemble during retrieval to show that NOR-LTM retrieval depended on that ensemble. This is methodologically analogous to the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE labeling and ChR2/ArchT manipulation of dentate gyrus engrams, although Nomoto et al. did not specify in the excerpt that the ensemble was dentate gyrus-specific. A weaker, more indirect support comes from [(Fitz et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2b08c9edd5196bf3826c35928145e7b17f6538), which used behavioral testing in APP/E3 and APP/E4 mice with Trem2 deletion and found worsened cognitive performance. This supports the general feasibility of using mouse behavioral paradigms to detect memory-related performance changes in genetically manipulated animals, but it does not provide direct support for object recognition, engram labeling, optogenetics, or natural forgetting. As a newer study, it also extends the older NOR/engram work by showing that cognitive deficits can be influenced by microglial and amyloid-related pathology, which is relevant to the plan's need to separate adaptive engram suppression from non-specific cognitive decline.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution comes from [(Fitz et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2b08c9edd5196bf3826c35928145e7b17f6538), because it shows that cognitive performance in APP/E3 and APP/E4 mice can be worsened by Trem2 deficiency, with related readouts including plaque compaction and microglial recruitment. This contradicts a purely adaptive engram-accessibility account if the proposed object-memory declines are not dissociated from pathology, inflammation, aging, or non-specific cognitive impairment; in other words, a drop in recognition performance could reflect neurodegenerative or microglial dysfunction rather than reversible suppression of a dentate gyrus engram. The second caution comes from [(Nomoto et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d5530db8f36fd580af3f270df37d058aab2bab0), which supports NOR and optogenetic ensemble manipulation but does not directly contradict the plan; rather, it limits the strength of the analogy because the paper used behavioural tagging and dopaminergic modulation, assessed an initial context-ensemble, and did not test natural forgetting, dentate gyrus-specific engrams, Rac1 signaling, environmental enrichment, reminder exposure, or context-only exposure. Therefore, the plan's claim that forgetting reflects adaptive suppression of dentate gyrus engrams is not directly supported by this older work and could be contradicted if the observed forgetting is instead due to failure of tagging, consolidation, or ensemble specificity. A weaker limitation is that both provided excerpts lack quantitative details: Nomoto et al. did not report recognition indices, overlap percentages, group sizes, p-values, retention intervals, viral constructs, light parameters, drug doses, or statistical thresholds in the supplied text, and Fitz et al. did not report quantitative behavioral scores, group sizes, baselines, statistical significance, task parameters, housing conditions, or statistical tests. This absence of quantitative evidence makes it difficult to confirm effect sizes, reproducibility, or whether the proposed method's expected forgetting timeline and engram reactivation metrics are well calibrated.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the object-recognition evaluation quantitative, pre-registered, and statistically powered, because both related works highlight the need for precise behavioral readouts but the excerpts lack metrics. The plan should specify discrimination indices based on novel versus familiar object exploration times, total exploration, nose contacts, latency, and habituation controls; define retention intervals at 24 hours, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks; report group sizes, randomization, blinding, and statistical tests such as mixed-effects models or repeated-measures analyses with correction for multiple comparisons. The second most important action is to validate the c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE and ChR2/ArchT engram approach in the dentate gyrus, because [(Nomoto et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d5530db8f36fd580af3f270df37d058aab2bab0) supports optogenetic manipulation of an initial ensemble but does not establish dentate gyrus specificity. The plan should include doxycycline timing, viral titer, expression quantification, light parameters, non-engram control cells, AAV-only and no-light controls, and histological colocalization of eYFP with c-Fos to confirm that the manipulated cells are the original engram. The third action is to add controls that separate adaptive forgetting from pathology or non-specific decline, inspired by [(Fitz et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a2b08c9edd5196bf3826c35928145e7b17f6538); this could include wild-type or non-transgenic controls, AAV-only controls, health and microglial/plaque checks if using disease-prone models, and baseline cognitive tests before viral or optogenetic manipulation. The fourth action is to align Experiment 2 with the strongest related evidence by testing both necessity and sufficiency of the dentate gyrus engram: inhibit engrams during recall to test necessity, activate engrams after natural forgetting to test sufficiency, and include a behavioural-tagging or dopaminergic control if weak training is used, because Nomoto et al. showed that tagging and dopamine can convert short-term NOR into long-term NOR. The fifth action is to refine the computational model and pharmacological experiments: fit maximum-likelihood models to discrimination indices and engram reactivation, estimate learning-rate and prediction-error parameters, compare against null forgetting models, and specify Rac1 inhibitor/activator doses, timing, specificity, and control conditions. Finally, for enrichment, reminder, and context-only experiments, include object-novelty and context-habituation controls to ensure that changes in forgetting are not driven by altered exploration, stress, or context familiarity.

## To label and manipulate engram cells within the dentate gyrus, we will employ c-fos-tTA transgenic mice combined with AAV9-TRE viral vectors, using the c-fos promoter to selectively label neurons active during learning and doxycycline control to restrict labeling to specific time windows.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Murthy et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da036951b18fbe5c0a7c18f2957d107e2f0413d4) directly tested the same cFos/tTA/TRE logic used in the proposed method, using cFostTA transgenic mice and doxycycline-regulated tTA activity to gate TRE-dependent expression to periods of c-fos activation after enriched environment or trace fear conditioning. This validates the central genetic premise that doxycycline-controlled c-fos-driven expression can label experience-active hippocampal neurons, which is the foundation of the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE dentate gyrus engram-labeling plan. A weaker but still relevant support is that the study used dorsal hippocampal CA1 and CA3, histological fluorescent quantification, and comparison to ArcCreER T2, providing a useful template for validating engram-labeling specificity, kinetics, and experience dependence in a hippocampal circuit relevant to the proposed object-recognition forgetting experiments. However, this support is limited because the study used a fluorescent reporter rather than ChR2 or ArchT, targeted CA1 rather than the dentate gyrus, and did not address optogenetic manipulation, eYFP/c-Fos colocalization, Rac1 modulation, or computational modeling.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that cFostTA-dependent mGRASP expression after enriched environment or trace fear conditioning labeled a subset of dorsal CA1 cells but did not produce apparent GFP reconstitution, whereas ArcCreER T2-dependent expression did produce GFP reconstitution, including transsynaptic reconstitution. This suggests that the cFos/TRE system may be less efficient or less robust than alternative immediate-early-gene systems, which is a concern for the proposed method if AAV9-TRE-driven eYFP, ChR2, or ArchT expression must be strong enough for reliable engram identification and optogenetic manipulation. A second contradiction is that the ArcCreER T2 system labeled more dorsal CA1 cells than the cFostTA system and the two systems showed different expression kinetics, indicating that c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE labeling may underrepresent the active engram population or have a timing mismatch relative to the learning window. A weaker contradiction is the lack of quantitative and implementation detail: the supplied sections do not report exact percentages, cell counts, p-values, effect sizes, animal numbers, viral titers, doxycycline dosing, injection coordinates, or statistical thresholds, so the evidence cannot be used to benchmark the proposed dentate gyrus protocol or to confirm reproducibility. The study also did not test the dentate gyrus, object recognition, ChR2/ArchT optogenetics, or the specific eYFP/c-Fos colocalization readout planned in the research proposal.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a dentate gyrus-specific pilot validation before the main forgetting experiments, using AAV9-TRE-eYFP or AAV9-TRE-ChR2/ArchT in c-fos-tTA mice with doxycycline administration timed to object-recognition acquisition, and to quantify labeling efficiency, cell-type composition, and kinetics with explicit metrics such as number of labeled DG granule cells, percentage of DG cells labeled, colocalization with Arc or c-Fos, and comparison to an ArcCreER T2 or other baseline. The next most important action is to verify that the labeled population is functionally optogenetically controllable, by measuring ChR2 or ArchT expression levels, light-evoked responses, and behavioral effects during recall, while including no-dox, no-virus, and non-targeted viral controls to rule out off-target or nonspecific activation. A further action is to optimize doxycycline timing and dose to match c-fos induction during learning, because the related work shows different expression kinetics and the proposed plan depends on restricting labeling to specific time windows. Finally, the method should add pre-registered statistical power, blinded histological scoring, effect sizes, and quantitative colocalization and spine-density analyses, and should consider a complementary labeling system if cFos/TRE labeling is too sparse or temporally mismatched in the dentate gyrus.

## To quantify engram reactivation and memory performance throughout the experiments, we will measure colocalization of original engram markers (eYFP) with c-Fos expression during memory tests and discrimination indices based on novel versus familiar object exploration times.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed quantification method comes from [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d), which used a spatial object recognition paradigm in male C57BL/6JRj mice, either 8-10 weeks or 18-20 months old, individually housed on a 12-hour light/dark cycle with ad libitum food and water. Mice received doxycycline-containing diet at 40 mg/kg after stereotaxic surgery, behavioral testing began 3 weeks after surgery and after 5 days of handling, and experiments were performed during the light phase. The task used four 6-min training sessions separated by 3-min home-cage intervals in a 50 cm x 50 cm x 50 cm open field with a central zone comprising 32% of the arena; the first session had one visual cue and the next three used a glass bottle and a metal tower placed diagonally, followed by a 6-min 24-h test with one object displaced. Exploration was defined as sniffing or nose orientation within 1 cm, leaning was excluded, and the discrimination index was displaced-object time divided by total object time times 100. Engram reactivation was quantified with Nikon A1R or Leica SP8 confocal microscopes, NIS-Elements or LAS X software, z-stacks of 3 frames at 2 µm intervals at 20x, maximum projections, background subtraction, thresholding, and manual marking of HA-GFP-positive and Fos-positive cells in Fiji; reactivation rate was HA-GFP+/Fos+ divided by total HA-GFP+ times 100, and overlap was HA-GFP+/Fos+ divided by the union times 100. The study also used blinding, Shapiro-Wilk normality testing with alpha = 0.05, one-way ANOVA with Tukey or Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn post hoc tests, paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, Pearson or Spearman correlations, and GraphPad Prism 8. This is the closest methodological template for the proposed eYFP/c-Fos colocalization and novel-versus-familiar object exploration discrimination index, and it aligns with Experiment 1's 24-h recall assessment. The next strongest support is [(Guskjolen, and Cembrowski, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1520337fe58e3073719959a7335ccc3cfeaae5cc), which used activity-dependent hippocampal engram labeling and optogenetic activation during post-learning rest to enhance remote memory and promote systems consolidation, while inhibition impaired remote memory; this supports the plan's central assumption that engram reactivation is linked to memory access and justifies Experiment 2's optogenetic necessity/sufficiency tests, although it did not use object recognition, eYFP/c-Fos colocalization, or discrimination indices. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16), which included c-Fos immunohistochemistry and an AAV-DIO-ChR2-eYFP engram construct, showing that c-Fos/eYFP colocalization logic is feasible in engram circuit studies, but the supplied excerpt did not provide immunostaining protocol, colocalization scoring, quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, antibody details, imaging parameters, or behavioral metrics, and the behavioral context was contextual fear conditioning with mPFC rather than dentate gyrus object memory. Taken iteratively, the 2020 work provides the closest behavioral and imaging template, the 2022 work extends the marker logic to c-Fos/eYFP, and the 2023 work strengthens the causal link between engram reactivation and memory access, but none supplies quantitative colocalization-behavior correlations in the proposed dentate gyrus object-memory forgetting paradigm.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the proposed method, but the strongest limitation is that the closest object-recognition colocalization study did not use eYFP/c-Fos and did not provide quantitative outcomes. [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d) used HA-GFP and Fos, not eYFP and c-Fos, tested only a single 24-h recall time point, and reported no discrimination indices, reactivation rates, overlap indices, group comparisons, sample sizes, or statistical significance; therefore it cannot establish that eYFP/c-Fos colocalization will track natural forgetting across the proposed 24-h, 1-week, 2-week, and 3-week timeline or that colocalization will correlate with object discrimination. The next strongest limitation is that the c-Fos/eYFP evidence is task- and region-mismatched. [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16) listed c-Fos IHC and AAV-DIO-ChR2-eYFP, but in the context of contextual fear conditioning and mPFC engram circuits, with no quantitative colocalization percentages, freezing scores, object exploration times, statistical comparisons, population sizes, antibody details, or imaging parameters; this leaves open whether c-Fos/eYFP colocalization in dentate gyrus object memory will have the same specificity, sensitivity, or behavioral correlation. The weakest limitation is that the optogenetic reactivation literature may complicate interpretation of test-time c-Fos as a passive readout of natural recall. [(Guskjolen, and Cembrowski, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1520337fe58e3073719959a7335ccc3cfeaae5cc) showed that optogenetic activation of hippocampal engrams during post-learning rest can enhance remote memory and promote systems consolidation, while inhibition impairs remote memory, but it used a c-Fos-driven reporter and did not provide stimulation parameters, viral constructs, sample sizes, numerical memory scores, or statistical significance; thus, in the proposed plan, c-Fos/eYFP colocalization after optogenetic, reminder, enrichment, context-only, or Rac1 manipulations may reflect induced plasticity rather than baseline engram accessibility, requiring careful controls. Overall, the newer work extends the older colocalization logic but does not fully validate its application to eYFP/c-Fos object-memory forgetting in the dentate gyrus.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to validate the eYFP/c-Fos colocalization readout in the dentate gyrus before using it as the primary forgetting metric. Adapt the c-Fos/eYFP logic from [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16) and the object-recognition colocalization workflow from [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d), using the c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE system with doxycycline-controlled labeling, doxycycline-free and doxycycline-treated controls, and confocal z-stacks comparable to the 3-frame, 2 µm, 20x protocol. Process images with background subtraction, thresholding, and blind manual or automated cell marking in Fiji, and report at least three metrics: engram reactivation rate = eYFP+/c-Fos+ cells divided by total eYFP+ cells times 100; overlap/Jaccard index = eYFP+/c-Fos+ cells divided by the union of eYFP+ and c-Fos+ cells times 100; and specificity = eYFP-/c-Fos+ cells divided by total c-Fos+ cells. Correlate these metrics with discrimination index at 24 h and at intermediate time points, and include negative controls for c-Fos antibody specificity, eYFP expression stability, and non-engram c-Fos expression. The second most important action is to standardize the object recognition behavioral readout. Adopt a protocol similar to [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d): 50 cm x 50 cm x 50 cm arena, 6-min training and test sessions, 24-h test, counterbalanced object positions, Smart Video Tracking or equivalent, exploration defined as sniffing or nose orientation within 1 cm with leaning excluded, and discrimination index = novel/displaced object time divided by total object exploration time times 100. Add habituation, object preference, and activity controls, blind scoring, an a priori power analysis to determine sample sizes, and pre-registered statistical analyses such as Shapiro-Wilk normality testing, one-way ANOVA with Tukey or Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn post hoc tests for group comparisons, paired t-test or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests for within-subject comparisons, Pearson or Spearman correlations between colocalization and behavior, and GraphPad Prism 8 or equivalent. The third most important action is to extend the forgetting timeline beyond the single 24-h time point used by [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d). Test at 24 h, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks, perfuse mice at the c-Fos peak after the memory test, and verify that eYFP labeling remains stable and c-Fos signal is detectable at remote time points. The fourth most important action is to add optogenetic and manipulation controls informed by [(Guskjolen, and Cembrowski, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1520337fe58e3073719959a7335ccc3cfeaae5cc). Specify ChR2 and ArchT expression levels, fiber placement, light wavelength, intensity, pulse duration, and stimulation timing; include eYFP-only, non-engram, and non-targeted controls; and distinguish natural c-Fos reactivation from c-Fos induced by optogenetic, reminder, enrichment, context-only, or Rac1 manipulations. The least important but still necessary action is to fit the computational model to the behavioral and colocalization data using maximum likelihood, report learning-rate parameters, compare the model to null or baseline models, and use cross-validation or held-out data to test predictions across experimental conditions.

## To establish the temporal dynamics of object memory forgetting, we will test mice at 24 hours, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks after acquisition training and measure both behavioral performance and engram reactivation patterns.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Experiment 1 is that [(Nomoto et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d5530db8f36fd580af3f270df37d058aab2bab0) used a mouse novelty object recognition paradigm to assess short-term and long-term object memory and inferred ensemble reactivation from overlapping neuronal activity during training and retrieval, which is directly analogous to the proposed plan’s use of NOR discrimination indices and eYFP/c-Fos colocalization to track dentate gyrus engram reactivation at 24 hours, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks. A secondary support is that the paper found retrieval of NOR long-term memory depended on the initial context ensemble, supporting the proposed focus on measuring whether the original training ensemble remains reactive over the forgetting timeline rather than relying only on behavioral performance. A weaker but relevant support is that the study used stereotactic viral infection and optogenetic manipulation to test whether specific ensembles support retrieval, which validates the broader experimental framework in which the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE, ChR2, and ArchT manipulations will later be used to test necessity and sufficiency, although this is less directly tied to the natural-forgetting timeline itself.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that the supplied related work does not provide quantitative NOR outcomes, sample sizes, effect sizes, statistical significance, or temporal performance curves, so it cannot directly support the proposed 24-hour, 1-week, 2-week, and 3-week forgetting timeline or the expected magnitude of engram reactivation decline. A secondary issue is that the related work does not specify exact NOR test delays, group sizes, viral constructs, optogenetic wavelengths, engram labeling strategy, or discrimination-index thresholds, which limits reproducibility and makes it difficult to benchmark the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE eYFP/c-Fos colocalization metric against an established quantitative baseline. A weaker concern is that the related work emphasizes behavioral tagging converting short-term memory into long-term memory and dopaminergic modulation of this process, which suggests that memory stability may be strongly influenced by tagging-like reinforcement; this does not directly contradict the proposed natural-forgetting hypothesis, but it implies that the proposed timeline must control for incidental tagging, context re-exposure, and dopaminergic state to avoid conflating adaptive suppression with tagging-dependent stabilization.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a quantitative, statistically powered NOR timeline that reports discrimination indices for novel versus familiar object exploration at 24 hours, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks, with explicit sample sizes, randomization, blinding, habituation, object-novelty controls, effect sizes, and statistical tests, and to pair each behavioral time point with a defined eYFP/c-Fos colocalization metric for dentate gyrus engram reactivation. Next, specify the labeling and quantification pipeline in detail: doxycycline windows for c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE, eYFP expression levels, c-Fos thresholding, colocalization coefficient, region-of-interest boundaries, and how the original context ensemble is defined, because [(Nomoto et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d5530db8f36fd580af3f270df37d058aab2bab0) only inferred ensemble reactivation from overlapping activity and did not provide the exact NOR delays, group sizes, viral constructs, optogenetic wavelengths, or discrimination-index thresholds. Finally, include control conditions that separate natural forgetting from tagging or reinforcement effects, such as a no-tagging baseline, a behavioral-tagging or dopaminergic-modulation condition, and context-only exposure controls, so that any decline in NOR performance or engram reactivation can be interpreted as adaptive engram suppression rather than loss of a tagged long-term memory trace.

## To test whether dentate gyrus engrams are necessary and sufficient for memory retrieval, we will use optogenetic approaches with ChR2 for activation and ArchT for inhibition of identified engram cells, inhibiting them during recall tests and activating them after natural forgetting has occurred.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Roy et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc963736961f46f3aaea6a92db2c582c1ad24448), which used the same core optogenetic logic as the proposed dentate gyrus engram experiment: c-Fos-dependent labeling of engram cells during learning, expression of ChR2 or ArchT in those labeled cells, and light delivery during recall to test whether the labeled ensemble is necessary and sufficient for memory expression. In that study, FosTRAP mice were labeled during contextual fear conditioning with AAV9-CAG-DIO-ChR2 or AAV9-CAG-DIO-ArchT, and blue or red light was delivered during a 24-h context recall test; the reported headings indicate that inhibition of labeled engram cells impaired recall while activation enhanced recall, directly supporting the proposed use of ArchT inhibition during recall to test necessity and ChR2 activation to test sufficiency. Because only one related work is provided, the iterative contrast is limited, but as a recent study it extends the broader engram-manipulation paradigm by combining c-Fos mapping with optogenetic and chemogenetic validation. A weaker but still relevant support is the study's use of brain-wide c-Fos mapping after learning and recall, plus overlap between epochs, to identify candidate engram regions; this supports the research plan's strategy of identifying dentate gyrus engrams by c-Fos induction during object-recognition acquisition and later quantifying engram reactivation through c-Fos/eYFP colocalization during memory tests. The weakest support is conceptual: the paper treats engrams as behaviorally specific cellular ensembles whose activity can be modulated to change memory expression, which is consistent with the research plan's hypothesis that natural forgetting reflects altered engram accessibility rather than irreversible trace loss. However, the supplied excerpt does not provide freezing scores, effect sizes, p-values, sample sizes, light parameters, or statistical tests, so the support is methodological and conceptual rather than quantitative.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that the related work does not test activation after natural forgetting; it manipulated engrams during a 24-h recall test, whereas the proposed method requires ChR2 activation after forgetting has occurred at 1, 2, or 3 weeks. Therefore, the related work supports the necessity/sufficiency paradigm but does not directly validate the central post-forgetting sufficiency claim. The next major limitation is task and anatomical specificity: the study used contextual fear conditioning and targeted hippocampal and prefrontal engram populations, not dentate gyrus-specific object-recognition engrams, so its findings cannot be assumed to transfer to the dentate gyrus or to object memory. A further limitation is that the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes, comparisons to baselines, statistical significance, population size, hardware/system specifications, hyperparameters, or implementation details such as AAV titer, fiber placement, light intensity, pulse parameters, and histological validation, which weakens the evidential strength and reproducibility. Finally, the related work used FosTRAP with AAV9-CAG-DIO opsins and chemogenetic hM4Di/CNO as a complement, whereas the proposed plan uses c-fos-tTA with AAV9-TRE; this is not a direct contradiction, but it means the labeling precision, temporal window, and cell-type specificity of the proposed system must be independently validated rather than inferred from the related work.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to explicitly test post-forgetting sufficiency by activating dentate gyrus engrams with ChR2 at 1, 2, and 3 weeks after object-recognition acquisition, with matched controls for non-engram cells, non-viral mice, light-only mice, and opsin-expressing mice tested before forgetting, while measuring discrimination index and c-Fos/eYFP colocalization to confirm that activation restores both behavior and engram reactivation. The next priority is to restrict and validate dentate gyrus-specific engram labeling: use c-Fos IHC or eYFP/c-Fos colocalization to confirm that AAV9-TRE-ChR2/ArchT expression is confined to dentate gyrus neurons active during object acquisition, report injection coordinates, viral titer, fiber placement, and histological coverage, and consider FosTRAP or tighter doxycycline timing if c-fos-tTA labeling is too broad. The method should also include a quantitative evaluation plan: pre-registered sample sizes and power analysis, effect sizes, mixed-effects or repeated-measures ANOVA with appropriate corrections, and reporting of p-values, confidence intervals, and baseline object exploration times. Additional controls should include ArchT inhibition during recall at 24 h to establish necessity before testing post-forgetting activation, non-engram ArchT/ChR2 controls, and, if optogenetic effects are ambiguous, a chemogenetic hM4Di/CNO backup analogous to [(Roy et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc963736961f46f3aaea6a92db2c582c1ad24448). Finally, the computational model should be tied to these behavioral and engram-reactivation measures by fitting maximum-likelihood learning-rate parameters to discrimination indices and c-Fos colocalization across the 24-h, 1-week, 2-week, and 3-week time points, with model comparisons testing whether post-forgetting activation improves fit under an adaptive engram-relevancy account.

## To investigate experience-dependent modulation of forgetting, we will house mice in enriched environments containing additional objects and stimuli, then assess forgetting rates and engram reactivation patterns compared to standard housing conditions.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed enrichment experiment comes from [(Leger et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcd24585a098ff44e325ffaddf2f35ae418aa23), which directly compared enriched housing with standard housing in mice and tested object recognition memory at 2 h and 24 h inter-stimulus intervals, using novel versus familiar object exploration as the behavioral readout and regional neuronal activation after presentation and test sessions. This work supports the central premise of Experiment 3 that experience can modulate memory persistence, because enriched mice showed prolonged object recognition memory at the 24 h ISI, while no enrichment effect was reported at 2 h, and because enriched mice showed hippocampal and infralimbic cortex activation associated with novel object discrimination and inter-regional correlations. The next strongest support is methodological rather than conceptual: [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d) provides a detailed spatial object recognition protocol in male C57BL/6JRj mice that is compatible with the proposed object-memory task and dentate gyrus engram reactivation measurement, including doxycycline-controlled labeling, four 6-minute training sessions separated by 3-minute home-cage intervals, a 50-cm square open field with a 32 percent central zone, a 24 h test, a displacement-based memory index, and dentate gyrus HA-GFP/Fos colocalization quantified by 20x confocal z-stacks at 2-micrometer intervals. This supports the feasibility of the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE and eYFP/c-Fos colocalization approach, although it did not test enrichment or longer forgetting intervals. The third supporting line is from [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16), which used c-Fos immunohistochemistry and AAV-DIO-ChR2-eYFP labeling to identify engram/recall neurons in medial prefrontal cortex after contextual fear conditioning and reported experience-dependent strengthening during systems consolidation and weakening after extinction of remote memories. This supports the broader hypothesis that engram circuits can shift between accessible and less accessible states, and it validates c-Fos/eYFP colocalization as an engram-reactivity metric, but it is less directly relevant because it used fear memory and mPFC rather than object memory, dentate gyrus, or enriched housing. The weakest support is from [(Madroñal et al., 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db40b1da93ff49d17ade08432860e6b3cfdd9da0), which used a 30-day environmental-condition paradigm in four mouse groups and assessed rotarod performance, eyeblink conditioning, CA3-CA1 field EPSPs, operant conditioning, and hippocampal neurogenesis. This older work supports the general idea that environmental conditions alter experience-dependent learning and hippocampal plasticity, but it does not explicitly confirm an enriched environment with additional objects and stimuli, does not measure forgetting rates, and does not quantify engram reactivation, so its support for the proposed method is indirect. Viewed iteratively, the older Madroñal work is the weakest direct support, while the newer Leger, Karaca, and Lee works progressively strengthen the methodological and conceptual basis for linking environmental experience, object-memory persistence, and engram reactivation, although the supplied excerpts generally lack numerical values, sample sizes, effect sizes, and p-values, so this support is qualitative and protocol-level.

### Contradictions

The strongest caution or partial contradiction comes from [(Leger et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcd24585a098ff44e325ffaddf2f35ae418aa23), because the enrichment effect was reported only at the 24 h ISI and not at the 2 h ISI, which contradicts a simple assumption that enriched housing uniformly slows forgetting across all retention intervals. It also used regional activation rather than direct engram-cell reactivation, so it cannot by itself support the proposed claim that enrichment changes dentate gyrus engram reactivation patterns. The next strongest limitation is that [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d) did not include enriched versus standard housing, did not test retention beyond 24 h, and did not report quantitative behavioral or imaging outcomes in the supplied excerpt; therefore it cannot validate the proposed enrichment-induced changes in forgetting rates or engram reactivation, and its object-memory/engram protocol must be treated as a methodological template rather than evidence for the enrichment hypothesis. The third caution is that [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16) examined contextual fear conditioning and medial prefrontal cortex engram circuits, not object recognition, dentate gyrus engrams, or environmental enrichment, and its excerpt provided only qualitative reports of strengthening and weakening without numerical values, effect sizes, or p-values; thus it should not be overgeneralized to predict that enriched housing will produce analogous dentate gyrus engram accessibility changes in object memory. The weakest contradiction is from [(Madroñal et al., 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db40b1da93ff49d17ade08432860e6b3cfdd9da0), whose environmental-condition paradigm did not explicitly confirm that the enriched condition contained additional objects and stimuli, did not assess forgetting or engram reactivation, and did not provide quantitative outcomes, so it offers little direct evidence against the proposed method but also cannot support the specific enrichment-forgetting-engram link.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed Experiment 3 should adopt the iterative design logic of the related work by combining the enrichment/memory-persistence design of [(Leger et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcd24585a098ff44e325ffaddf2f35ae418aa23) with the object-memory and dentate gyrus engram-reactivation protocol of [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d). Specifically, use standard versus enriched housing with a clearly defined enrichment protocol containing additional objects and stimuli, test object recognition at multiple retention intervals including 2 h, 24 h, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks, and calculate a discrimination index from novel versus familiar or displaced versus non-displaced object exploration. The enrichment condition should be controlled for duration, object rotation, stimulus novelty, cage size, and standard housing baseline, and the behavioral arena should follow the Karaca parameters where feasible: 50-cm square open field, visual cue, 32 percent central zone, 6-minute sessions, 3-minute home-cage intervals, and explicit exploration criteria such as sniffing or nose direction within 1 cm. Second, implement engram reactivation quantification using doxycycline-controlled c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE labeling and eYFP/c-Fos colocalization in the dentate gyrus, with confocal z-stacks at approximately 2-micrometer intervals, background subtraction, thresholding, and blinded cell counting; report reactivation rate as the proportion of eYFP-positive engram cells that are c-Fos-positive and a similarity index for overlap, mirroring the HA-GFP/Fos metrics in [(Karaca et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f836d570f65f2c10bab20475a9346728eaafc9d) and the c-Fos/eYFP approach in [(Lee et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f25b51973ad794190f6db027c45a79f3cddad16). Third, add control measures to rule out non-memory confounds, including open-field locomotion, center entries, total distance traveled, and possibly rotarod or other motor/coordination assessments if enrichment might alter movement, as in [(Madroñal et al., 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db40b1da93ff49d17ade08432860e6b3cfdd9da0); also consider hippocampal or infralimbic c-Fos mapping because [(Leger et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfcd24585a098ff44e325ffaddf2f35ae418aa23) linked enrichment-related memory persistence to hippocampal and infralimbic activation. Fourth, because the related excerpts lack sample sizes, effect sizes, and p-values, the proposed method should pre-specify sample sizes, power analysis, blinding, normality testing, and appropriate statistical tests such as one-way ANOVA with Tukey post hoc tests for normally distributed data, Kruskal-Wallis with Dunn tests for non-normal data, paired tests for dependent comparisons, and correlation tests for engram reactivation versus behavior, using software such as GraphPad Prism or equivalent. Finally, connect the behavioral and engram data to the computational model by fitting learning-rate and engram-relevancy parameters to the discrimination indices and reactivation rates across retention intervals, so that enrichment is tested as a modulator of adaptive error-driven forgetting rather than as a general memory-enhancing manipulation.

## To test whether forgotten memories can be recovered, we will expose mice to brief reminder sessions consisting of the original training environment and objects, then test whether this can restore access to forgotten memories and increase engram reactivation.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed reminder-recovery method is that [(Jiang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2d52fb80d6c9b8e1d55b189868d97a9a4e6f8d0) used a mouse object-recognition battery and showed that dentate gyrus (DG) lesions with ibotenic acid impaired object recognition memory, while adult-born neurons contributed to improved object recognition in DG-lesioned mice. This directly supports the research plan's premise that DG-dependent object memory can be modulated by cellular plasticity, which is compatible with the proposed idea that forgetting reflects reversible engram accessibility rather than permanent trace loss. A second, weaker support is that the study used a standard object-recognition readout with experimenters blinded to group and drug treatment, fixed test order, and GraphPad Prism analyses using mean ± SEM, unpaired t-tests, and two-way ANOVA with Bonferroni post-hoc testing for cell counts, which supports the proposed use of discrimination indices and blinded behavioral scoring in the reminder-recovery experiment. The weakest support is methodological: the 7-day post-surgical recovery, fixed behavioral order, and quantitative cell-count framework provide a template for controlling confounds, but they do not address reminder exposure, engram labeling, or reactivation.

### Contradictions

The strongest counter-evidence is not a direct refutation but a critical absence: [(Jiang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2d52fb80d6c9b8e1d55b189868d97a9a4e6f8d0) did not test brief reminder sessions, original training environment/objects, forgotten-memory recovery, engram labeling, or optogenetic reactivation, so it provides no direct evidence that reminder exposure can restore access to forgotten object memories or increase DG engram reactivation. The next strongest limitation is that the excerpt reports only qualitative outcomes and lacks quantitative discrimination indices, group sizes, p-values, lesion coordinates, object exposure durations, and reminder protocols, making it impossible to benchmark the proposed reminder-recovery effect size or to reproduce the behavioral paradigm. A weaker contradiction is that the study's manipulations were ibotenic acid DG lesions and antisense oligonucleotide treatment, which model lesion-induced or molecularly altered object-memory deficits rather than natural forgetting over 24 hours to 3 weeks, so its findings cannot be extrapolated to the proposed natural-forgetting timeline or to experience-dependent reminder recovery.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a direct reminder-recovery test that mirrors the proposed method: after acquisition, allow natural forgetting to occur, then deliver a brief reminder session with the original training environment and objects, followed by a discrimination-index test comparing novel versus familiar object exploration, with control groups receiving no reminder, a novel-context reminder, or a context-only reminder. The second most important improvement is to integrate the proposed engram readout by using c-fos-tTA mice, AAV9-TRE labeling, and c-Fos/eYFP colocalization during the post-reminder test to determine whether reminder exposure increases reactivation of the original DG engram, while including optogenetic ChR2/ArchT controls to test necessity and sufficiency. The third improvement is to adopt the related work's behavioral rigor by pre-registering sample sizes/power, lesion or viral coordinates, reminder duration and intensity, fixed test order, blinded scoring, and using discrimination indices with appropriate repeated-measures or mixed-effects ANOVA rather than only unpaired t-tests, because the related work's lack of quantitative indices and sample sizes is a major reproducibility gap.

## To test whether context-only exposure accelerates forgetting by updating the original memory engram, we will repeatedly expose mice to the training context without objects and test whether optogenetic inhibition of the engram during these exposures prevents the accelerated forgetting.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed context-only exposure and engram-inhibition test is that [(Poll et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7146d4db450005e9be70c21b1410ae52f349c080) showed that reducing novelty-like activity ameliorated memory recall in APP/PS1 mice, which is consistent with the research plan claim that forgetting may reflect reversible suppression or altered accessibility of engram ensembles rather than irreversible trace loss. As an earlier step in the iterative literature, this finding implies that activity patterns associated with context or novelty can modulate whether an engram is accessible, even if the original engram label remains present, and therefore motivates Experiment 5. A second, weaker but methodologically useful support is that the same study used a fosGFP reporter in APP/PS1 mice, hippocampal window surgery, AAV preparation and injection, confocal microscopy, Ca2+ event frequency analysis, fosGFP expression pattern and network similarity analysis, and identification of up and down cells to quantify an activity-dependent engram-like CA1 pyramidal population; these metrics can be adapted to the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE dentate gyrus engram design to measure whether context-only exposure changes engram expression before and after optogenetic inhibition. The weakest support is that learning-induced fosGFP expression remained intact in CA1 of fosGFP-APP/PS1 mice, suggesting that a labeled engram can persist while recall is impaired, which aligns with the plan's focus on engram accessibility rather than trace loss, but this observation was not made in an object-recognition task, was not restricted to dentate gyrus, and did not include repeated context-only exposure or ArchT inhibition.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction or limitation for the next iterative step is that [(Poll et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7146d4db450005e9be70c21b1410ae52f349c080) did not test the proposed causal manipulation: it did not use repeated context-only exposure without objects, did not use optogenetic inhibition of an object-memory engram during such exposures, and did not report whether preventing engram activity prevented accelerated forgetting. The study also used APP/PS1 mice, CA1 pyramidal neurons, and a fosGFP reporter rather than the proposed c-fos-tTA transgenic mice, AAV9-TRE vectors, dentate gyrus engram cells, ChR2/ArchT optogenetics, and object-recognition discrimination indices, so its findings cannot be directly transferred to Experiment 5. The excerpt also did not report sample sizes, effect sizes, statistical significance, quantitative comparisons to baselines, hardware/system specifications, or computational model parameters, so reproducibility and quantitative comparison to the proposed method are limited. A second, more conceptual contradiction is that the paper reported a sparse CA1 engram occurred independent of memory, which undermines the assumption that a c-fos-labeled dentate engram is necessarily the memory-relevant ensemble whose inhibition during context-only exposure will prevent forgetting; if engram-like labeling is not tightly coupled to memory performance, ArchT inhibition may not produce the predicted preservation of object recognition. The weakest contradiction is that the beneficial manipulation in the related work was reducing novelty-like activity, not inhibiting the engram itself; this could imply that context-only exposure accelerates forgetting through competing or novelty-driven activity that should be suppressed, whereas the proposed method suppresses the engram during context-only exposure, which might instead reduce the activity needed to maintain engram accessibility and therefore fail to prevent, or even worsen, forgetting.

### Suggested Action

Most important, to move the iterative program forward, the proposed method should first establish in the exact c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE dentate gyrus object-recognition model that repeated context-only exposure without objects accelerates forgetting relative to no-exposure or object-present reminder controls, using discrimination indices for novel versus familiar object exploration and eYFP/c-Fos colocalization to quantify engram reactivation at 24 hours, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 3 weeks; this addresses the main gap in [(Poll et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7146d4db450005e9be70c21b1410ae52f349c080), which did not test context-only exposure or object memory. Second, add rigorous optogenetic controls: ArchT in non-engram dentate cells, ArchT in engram cells during object-present reminders, ArchT during context-only exposure with objects absent versus present, sham light, and ChR2/ArchT expression verification, to distinguish whether preventing accelerated forgetting requires silencing the original engram rather than reducing competing novelty-like activity. Third, adopt the quantitative engram-expression metrics from [(Poll et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7146d4db450005e9be70c21b1410ae52f349c080), including up and down cell classification, Ca2+ event frequency, and network similarity, but apply them to dentate gyrus engram cells and report sample sizes, effect sizes, and statistical significance because the related work excerpt lacked numerical outcomes. Finally, use the computational error-driven model to predict how context-only prediction errors update engram relevancy, and fit maximum likelihood learning-rate parameters to the behavioral data to test whether engram inhibition during context-only exposure specifically blocks the predicted relevancy update.

## To assess the role of Rac1 signaling in active forgetting, we will pharmacologically inhibit or activate Rac1 signaling and assess effects on forgetting rates and engram reactivation patterns.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7dd2feca6a9015add0169a875eb667b6b075d8ca), which directly examined Rac1 in dentate gyrus engrams during context fear conditioning and reported that Rac1 activity impaired fear memory while promoting autophagy, ATG7 expression, microglial activation, TLR2/4 signaling, and dendritic spine remodeling. This is the closest conceptual match to the proposed Experiment 6 because it places Rac1 inside DG engrams and links Rac1-dependent signaling to forgetting-like memory impairment and structural engram changes, which also motivates the plan’s Experiment 7 spine analysis. The next strongest support is [(Lv et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98d1379b79445285e251c1b57012e3a111e86728), which manipulated Rac1 activity and α2-chimaerin in engram cells during contextual fear conditioning and reported that Rac1 inhibition enhanced memory and that Rac1 activity in engram cells affects memory maintenance; this supports the feasibility of targeting Rac1 in engram-relevant circuits, although it used fear conditioning rather than object recognition and did not quantify c-Fos/eYFP engram reactivation. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Wu et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba9174d81d903f1f69fc067d70808dd8150af29), which used a pharmacological Rac1-inhibition strategy, including Drosophila behavioral screening, Western blot Rac1 activity assays, blood-brain barrier permeability, pharmacokinetics, liver microsome stability, Morris water maze, probe test, and hippocampal slice LTP, and reported that Rac1 inhibition rescued LTP deficits in AD-related models; this supports the practical feasibility of pharmacologically modulating Rac1 in vivo and measuring synaptic plasticity, but it is less directly relevant because it focused on AD pathology, not natural object-memory forgetting or engram reactivation.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that none of the related studies directly validates the proposed two-sided pharmacological manipulation in the proposed object-recognition forgetting paradigm with c-Fos/eYFP engram reactivation. [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7dd2feca6a9015add0169a875eb667b6b075d8ca) used context fear conditioning and did not report pharmacological Rac1 inhibition or activation or direct engram reactivation indices; [(Lv et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98d1379b79445285e251c1b57012e3a111e86728) used contextual fear conditioning and did not describe pharmacological Rac1 activation or c-Fos/eYFP colocalization; and [(Wu et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba9174d81d903f1f69fc067d70808dd8150af29) used AD models, Drosophila screening, Morris water maze, and LTP rather than object recognition or engram labeling. Therefore, the assumption that both Rac1 inhibition and activation will produce interpretable changes in forgetting rates and engram reactivation in healthy mice is not directly supported. A second contradiction is mechanistic: [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7dd2feca6a9015add0169a875eb667b6b075d8ca) linked Rac1-dependent forgetting to ATG7-mediated autophagy, microglial TLR2/4 activation, and spine remodeling, implying that forgetting may depend on slower immune and structural processes rather than only acute Rac1-GTP changes; the proposed method’s primary readouts of forgetting rate and engram reactivation may miss these necessary downstream effects. A third contradiction is that [(Lv et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98d1379b79445285e251c1b57012e3a111e86728) found α2-chimaerin knockdown impaired multiple memories, indicating that Rac1 GTPase regulation is context-dependent and that reducing Rac1 activity may not uniformly enhance memory; this complicates the interpretation of a Rac1 inhibitor as a simple forgetting suppressor. The weakest contradiction is reproducibility and translational distance: the supplied excerpts lack quantitative effect sizes, sample sizes, p-values, drug doses, viral titers, slice parameters, and statistical thresholds, and [(Wu et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba9174d81d903f1f69fc067d70808dd8150af29) relied on AD and Drosophila systems, so direct translation to the proposed mouse object-recognition timeline is limited.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to reframe Experiment 6 as a dose-timed pharmacological test in the object-recognition model with explicit engram readouts: use c-fos-tTA mice and AAV9-TRE eYFP labeling during acquisition, then administer vehicle, a validated Rac1 inhibitor, and a Rac1 activator or Rac1-GTPase modulator at acquisition, consolidation, and pre-test or during the 24 h to 3 week forgetting window, and measure discrimination indices plus c-Fos/eYFP colocalization in DG engrams. Because the related work is strongest for DG engrams and fear memory, the protocol should include a pilot contextual fear conditioning arm or secondary readout to benchmark Rac1 manipulation, while keeping object recognition as the primary test. Second, the method should specify target engagement and safety: identify compounds with BBB permeability, pharmacokinetics, and microsome stability as in [(Wu et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba9174d81d903f1f69fc067d70808dd8150af29), measure Rac1-GTP by Western blot or a Rac1 biosensor, include vehicle and off-target controls, and avoid relying on non-specific GTPase effects. Third, add the downstream mechanisms reported by [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7dd2feca6a9015add0169a875eb667b6b075d8ca), including ATG7/autophagy markers, microglial activation, TLR2/4 signaling, and engram-cell spine density/volume, so that Experiment 6 can distinguish whether Rac1 changes alter forgetting through engram reactivation, structural plasticity, or immune-mediated remodeling. Fourth, incorporate the caution from [(Lv et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98d1379b79445285e251c1b57012e3a111e86728) by testing α2-chimaerin or Rac1 GTPase-activating protein modulation as a genetic or pharmacological control, and by analyzing whether Rac1 inhibition enhances memory only when engram cells are selectively targeted. Finally, pre-register sample sizes, effect sizes, mixed-effects models for repeated time points, multiple-comparison correction, randomization, and blinding, because the related work excerpts do not provide sufficient quantitative details to justify the proposed effect sizes.

## To examine structural plasticity associated with memory access, we will perform detailed analysis of dendritic spine density and volume in engram cells following successful recall versus forgetting conditions.

### Support

The strongest support for Experiment 7 is that [(Medina et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed971fba573ae431d649aacd7dcec442ef2a0bb8) used the same core quantitative spine-morphometry logic proposed here, classifying dendritic spines into mushroom, stubby, and thin categories and measuring spine volume, head diameter, and neck diameter with Neuronstudio to relate structural plasticity to behavioral memory states. This directly supports the feasibility of using spine density and volume as interpretable structural readouts in a memory-related behavioral contrast, although it was performed in CA1 pyramidal neurons rather than dentate gyrus engram cells. The newer [(Kastellakis et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a13b17ad7e0a69bbdef38d97ae3994ee0b8913dd) extends this static morphometric logic by showing that high-resolution time-lapse imaging of defined dendritic domains can link localized synaptic structural dynamics to learning and memory performance, including spine elimination after fear conditioning, new spine formation after extinction, and cue-segregated spine clustering in cortical pyramidal neurons. This iterative progression supports the broader premise that memory access changes can be accompanied by measurable dendritic structural changes, and it motivates analyzing spines in defined dendritic segments rather than as a single global density value. A weaker but still relevant support is the Medina et al. rationale that within-category morphometric variability can reveal group differences even when total spine density is unchanged, which supports adding head and neck measurements to the proposed density and volume analysis.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related study provides direct evidence for the proposed engram-cell-specific comparison in dentate gyrus neurons during object-recognition recall versus forgetting. The older [(Medina et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed971fba573ae431d649aacd7dcec442ef2a0bb8) analyzed CA1 pyramidal neurons across behavioral groups without c-fos-tTA, AAV9-TRE, eYFP, or c-Fos colocalization, so it does not establish that spine density or volume changes are specific to engram cells or to the successful recall versus forgetting contrast planned in Experiment 7. The newer [(Kastellakis et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a13b17ad7e0a69bbdef38d97ae3994ee0b8913dd) is similarly indirect because it used fear conditioning and extinction in cortical pyramidal neurons, not object recognition, dentate gyrus engrams, or the c-fos-tTA labeling strategy, and it did not compare successful recall with forgetting. A second contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes in both excerpts: Medina et al. did not report numerical spine density, volume, head diameter, neck diameter, effect sizes, sample sizes, imaging hardware, classification thresholds, or statistical tests, and Kastellakis et al. reported qualitative correlations rather than spine density or volume values, effect sizes, sample sizes, time points, hardware specifications, or statistical significance. This weakens the ability to use these studies to justify expected effect sizes, statistical power, or reproducibility for the proposed morphological analysis. A weaker contradiction is that both studies suggest the proposed method may be too coarse if it relies only on density and volume: Medina et al. emphasized within-category variability of head and neck parameters, and Kastellakis et al. emphasized localized spine turnover, clustering, and spatial segregation, all of which may be missed by a simple density and volume comparison.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make Experiment 7 explicitly engram-cell-specific and behaviorally anchored: restrict spine quantification to dentate gyrus cells labeled by the c-fos-tTA and AAV9-TRE system, ideally eYFP-positive cells with c-Fos colocalization during the memory test, and define successful recall versus forgetting using the planned discrimination indices from novel versus familiar object exploration at the 24-hour, 1-week, 2-week, and 3-week time points. Include non-engram DG cells, non-labeled controls, and optogenetic activation or inhibition controls to distinguish engram-specific structural plasticity from general hippocampal or dentate gyrus plasticity. Next, adopt the morphometric workflow from [(Medina et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed971fba573ae431d649aacd7dcec442ef2a0bb8) by classifying spines into mushroom, stubby, and thin categories and reporting spine volume, head diameter, and neck diameter in addition to density, using Neuronstudio or an equivalent validated pipeline with pre-registered classification thresholds, blinded analysis, and explicit dendritic segment selection criteria. Also incorporate the high-resolution, domain-specific logic from [(Kastellakis et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a13b17ad7e0a69bbdef38d97ae3994ee0b8913dd) by imaging defined dendritic domains before and after recall or forgetting, and by quantifying spine turnover, clustering, and spatial segregation rather than only static density and volume. Finally, specify the missing implementation and statistical details needed for reproducibility: imaging hardware and resolution, animal numbers, dendritic segment selection, spine classification thresholds, time points, statistical tests such as mixed-effects models or permutation tests with multiple-comparison correction, effect sizes, and power analysis, so that the morphological findings can be tied to the behavioral recall-versus-forgetting contrast and to the computational model of engram relevancy.

## To formalize the hypothesis that forgetting represents adaptive learning, we will develop a computational model based on error-driven learning principles that incorporates engram relevancy dynamically updated based on prediction errors, where engrams subjectively less relevant for adaptive behavior are more likely to be forgotten.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Tomé et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6bf46daf6f943ef16e0678f4c0fceacb1b9c11d) used a computational engram network with explicit neurons, synapses, short-term plasticity, long-term excitatory plasticity, and inhibitory plasticity, and reported that consolidation reshapes engrams and that inhibition is crucial for memory selectivity. This directly supports the research plan's central claim that forgetting can be a reversible, plasticity-driven change in engram accessibility rather than simple trace loss, because their model treats engram recall as a dynamic network property rather than a static stored trace. A second, still strong, point is that their use of recall and engram-selectivity metrics provides a modeling template for the proposed object-memory plan, where discrimination indices and c-Fos/eYFP colocalization can be mapped onto analogous recall and selectivity outputs. A weaker but relevant point is that their fear-conditioning mouse context used activity-dependent engram labeling, optogenetic/chemogenetic inhibition, calcium imaging, and slice recordings, which supports the feasibility of combining engram labeling, optogenetic manipulation, and computational modeling in the proposed c-fos-tTA/AAV9-TRE, ChR2/ArchT, and Rac1 experiments.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Tomé et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6bf46daf6f943ef16e0678f4c0fceacb1b9c11d) does not implement or test an error-driven learning rule, prediction-error signal, or engram-relevancy variable, so it cannot support the specific proposed mechanism that less relevant engrams are forgotten because prediction errors update relevancy. The next strongest limitation is that the evidence is qualitative, with no numerical effect sizes, sample sizes, p-values, network sizes, synaptic weights, plasticity rates, simulation durations, or statistical comparisons, which undermines the ability to validate the proposed maximum-likelihood fitting of learning-rate parameters to behavioral data. A further contradiction is domain mismatch: the related work concerns fear-memory engrams and contextual fear conditioning, whereas the plan targets object recognition memory in the dentate gyrus, so its findings on inhibition and engram selectivity may not transfer to novelty-driven object memory, where exploration time, object novelty, and dentate gyrus pattern separation may impose different constraints. Finally, the related work's emphasis that consolidation reshapes engrams could be read as evidence for structural remodeling or competition rather than purely reversible suppression, which would challenge the plan's assumption that forgotten memories remain accessible through optogenetic reactivation after natural forgetting.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the computational model explicitly error-driven by adding a prediction-error term that updates an engram-relevancy variable, and to derive testable predictions for Experiments 1 through 7, such as how prediction-error magnitude during context-only exposure should change c-Fos/eYFP colocalization and discrimination index. The next priority is to adopt the related work's recall and engram-selectivity metrics as formal model outputs and compare them to the plan's behavioral and molecular measures, including baseline models without error-driven forgetting, fixed engram weights, Hebbian plasticity, and inhibition-only plasticity. The model should also incorporate inhibitory plasticity and competing engrams, because [(Tomé et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6bf46daf6f943ef16e0678f4c0fceacb1b9c11d) found inhibition crucial for selectivity, and this would help explain whether context-only exposure accelerates forgetting through engram competition or relevancy decay. To improve reproducibility and statistical rigor, the plan should specify network size, synaptic weight initialization, plasticity rates, simulation duration, MLE likelihood function, confidence intervals, and significance tests, and it should pre-register quantitative thresholds for recovery after ArchT inhibition or ChR2 activation. Finally, the model should be adapted from fear to object memory by including dentate gyrus-specific constraints, object-novelty exploration dynamics, and Rac1 as a modulatory gate on plasticity, so that the computational predictions are directly testable in the proposed mouse experiments.
