## To test the central hypothesis about complementary temporal reference frames: We will use functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) to investigate neural correlates of temporal sequence and duration processing from different perspectives.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed fMRI method comes from [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf), which used fMRI, GLM analyses, and searchlight MVPA to show that frontal and parietal ROIs carried duration information during a duration task, while an occipital ROI carried orientation information. This directly supports the feasibility of using fMRI to detect neural representations of duration and supports the proposed emphasis on parietal cortex in temporal processing. It also supports the broader methodological choice of combining GLM-style activation analyses with multivariate decoding, although the proposed plan currently relies more heavily on parametric modulation of sequential distance and duration than on MVPA. A weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Hayashi et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfea4efde550cf5ae458eae2efe0fc303f713623), which used fMRI adaptation with short visual durations of 400 and 600 ms and 300 and 450 ms and found duration adaptation in the right IPL during a time task. This supports the idea that parietal regions, especially IPL, are sensitive to temporal duration and that fMRI can reveal duration-specific neural effects. However, this support is weaker because the study tested low-level visual duration adaptation rather than high-level event sequences, internal versus external temporal perspectives, or hippocampal sequence storage, and the provided excerpt lacks quantitative effect sizes, statistical thresholds, participant numbers, scanner parameters, and behavioral metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related study provides direct evidence for the central hypothesis that the hippocampus stores temporal sequences allocentrically while parietal cortex retrieves them egocentrically. [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf) is the closest methodological precedent because it used fMRI, GLM, and searchlight MVPA to examine duration processing, but it did not manipulate egocentric versus allocentric temporal perspectives, did not test sequential distance, did not include a hippocampal ROI, and did not report numerical decoding accuracies, behavioral accuracy or reaction-time values, effect sizes, statistical significance, participant numbers, scanner field strength, sequence parameters, trial structure, duration values, or correction thresholds. This limits the extent to which it can support the proposed interaction between task perspective and temporal variables, especially the predicted parietal perspective-dependent and hippocampal perspective-independent patterns. A further contradiction is that [(Hayashi et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfea4efde550cf5ae458eae2efe0fc303f713623) found duration adaptation in the right IPL regardless of whether duration was task-relevant, which suggests that parietal duration signals may partly reflect low-level sensory adaptation or task-irrelevant duration encoding rather than high-level egocentric temporal sequence retrieval. This is relevant to the proposed internal-perspective task because parietal activation correlated with sequential distance or duration could be driven by basic duration processing rather than by adopting an internal temporal viewpoint. A weaker but important limitation is that both studies lack the quantitative and reproducibility details needed to benchmark the proposed permutation-based cluster FWE analysis, including sample size, scanner specifications, preprocessing, statistical thresholds, effect sizes, and behavioral validation metrics.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to strengthen the hippocampal allocentric test by adding high-resolution fMRI and representational analyses, such as MVPA or representational similarity analysis, to directly test whether hippocampal activity encodes the learned 15-event ritual sequence in a perspective-agnostic way. This is necessary because the current plan predicts hippocampal main effects from parametric modulation of sequential distance and duration, but the related work shows that duration and sequence-related signals may also appear in frontal, parietal, and occipital regions, as in [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf), so a dedicated hippocampal sequence-decoding analysis would make the allocentric hypothesis more testable. The next most important action is to control for low-level visual duration confounds, because [(Hayashi et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfea4efde550cf5ae458eae2efe0fc303f713623) showed duration adaptation in right IPL even when duration was task-irrelevant. The proposed design should therefore include duration-matched non-temporal controls, separate event duration from visual stimulus duration, and possibly use adaptation or MVPA to distinguish low-level duration encoding from high-level temporal sequence processing. The third priority is to report quantitative and reproducibility details that are missing from the related work, including participant numbers, scanner field strength, acquisition parameters, preprocessing, cluster-forming threshold, number of permutations, effect sizes, decoding accuracies, behavioral accuracy, reaction times, and statistical significance. The fourth priority is to add searchlight MVPA or RSA in addition to parametric modulation, following the methodological approach in [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf), to test whether frontal, parietal, occipital, and hippocampal regions carry distinct temporal sequence or duration information. Finally, the design should ensure that sequential distance and duration are orthogonally manipulated, that RT, syllable length, and head motion are included as regressors, and that the expected symbolic distance effect in the internal-perspective task and reverse effect in the external-perspective task are validated with pre-registered behavioral criteria.

## To map neural sensitivity to temporal sequence and duration: We will employ parametric modulation analysis to identify brain regions where activation correlates with temporal variables (sequential distance and event duration).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Baker et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beae3f2b36080c965b214dd5fa1fcaec650dc548), which directly linked parietal connectivity to a continuous temporal duration variable. In a resting-state sample of 141 participants, seeds in the right anterior and posterior superior parietal lobule were used to generate whole-brain connectivity maps, and those maps were regressed against geometric mean dominance durations for binocular rivalry and Necker cube percepts. The finding that anterior superior parietal lobule connectivity with striatal regions was associated with faster alternations, and posterior superior parietal lobule connectivity with left inferior temporal gyrus was associated with slower alternations, supports the proposed plan's prediction that parietal cortex is sensitive to temporal duration and may be a key region for perspective-dependent temporal processing. A second, weaker form of support is the independent sample of 26 participants in which geometric mean dominance durations for binocular rivalry and the Necker cube correlated at r = 0.46 with p < 0.05, indicating that duration-related temporal variables can be stable and meaningful across related bistable stimuli. A third support is the use of group-level mixed-effects modeling with motion-related covariates and cluster-level inference at Z > 2.3 and corrected cluster p < 0.05, which supports the proposed plan's emphasis on controlling motion and using cluster-based correction, although the proposed plan specifically calls for permutation testing rather than the FLAME mixed-effects approach used by [(Baker et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beae3f2b36080c965b214dd5fa1fcaec650dc548). The weakest support is the broader resting-state connectivity pattern involving occipital, parietal, dorsal temporal, temporo-parietal, and dorsolateral prefrontal regions, which is consistent with a distributed network for temporal-perceptual processing but does not directly validate task-based parametric modulation, sequential distance, or egocentric versus allocentric perspective effects.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is a methodological mismatch: [(Baker et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beae3f2b36080c965b214dd5fa1fcaec650dc548) used seed-based resting-state fMRI connectivity rather than task-evoked parametric modulation analysis. The study placed two 2 mm spherical seeds in the right anterior and posterior superior parietal lobule, extracted resting-state time series, and regressed whole-brain connectivity maps against a single continuous duration variable per stimulus. This design cannot support the proposed method's central test of trial-level parametric modulation of sequential distance and event duration, nor can it test interaction effects between task perspective and temporal variables. A second major contradiction is the absence of any perspective manipulation or hippocampal analysis. The related work did not distinguish internal versus external temporal perspectives, did not test egocentric versus allocentric temporal reference frames, and did not examine the medial temporal lobe or hippocampus, so it cannot support the proposed complementary hippocampal-parietal framework. A third contradiction concerns statistical and preprocessing choices: the related work used FSL preprocessing with 6 mm Gaussian smoothing, highpass temporal filtering with sigma = 100 s, lowpass temporal filtering with sigma = 2.8 s, and FLAME stage 1 mixed-effects inference with Z > 2.3 and corrected cluster p < 0.05, whereas the proposed plan specifies permutation testing with cluster-level family-wise error correction and likely requires finer spatial resolution for hippocampal effects. The 6 mm smoothing in particular may be too coarse for hippocampal or fine-grained parietal analyses. A weaker contradiction is the population and task mismatch: the related work involved 26 participants performing bistable perception with oblique gratings and a Necker cube, reporting percepts with mouse buttons, and using geometric mean dominance durations, whereas the proposed plan involves native Italian speakers learning a 15-event fictional ritual and making reference-target judgments with reaction times, accuracy, syllable length, and parts-of-day controls.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to retain the proposed task-based parametric modulation design and not substitute resting-state connectivity, because [(Baker et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beae3f2b36080c965b214dd5fa1fcaec650dc548) only provides indirect evidence for parietal-duration sensitivity and cannot test the proposed perspective-by-temporal-variable interactions. The fMRI model should include trial-level regressors for reference-target event pairs, parametric modulators for sequential distance and target duration, main effects of perspective, and explicit interaction terms between perspective and sequential distance and between perspective and duration. Reaction time, syllable length, head motion, trial type, part-of-day, and learning-test performance should be included as covariates to reduce confounds. Second, because [(Baker et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beae3f2b36080c965b214dd5fa1fcaec650dc548) used geometric mean dominance durations because dominance durations followed a positively skewed gamma distribution, the proposed duration variable should be checked for skewness and, if necessary, log-transformed or modeled as a geometric mean before parametric modulation, while preserving trial-level values rather than collapsing to a single aggregate duration per participant or stimulus. Third, the acquisition and analysis should be adjusted for hippocampal and parietal resolution: avoid 6 mm smoothing if hippocampal effects are central, use 3 mm or submillimeter acquisition where feasible, and use permutation-based cluster family-wise error correction as planned, while also reporting effect sizes and possibly running a sensitivity analysis with mixed-effects or standard general linear model inference. Fourth, the behavioral validation should be strengthened by preregistering the expected symbolic distance effect in the internal-perspective task and the reverse effect in the external-perspective task, and by ensuring that the event-sequence judgment, self-paced duration reproduction, and parts-of-day categorization tests confirm adequate learning before scanning. Fifth, if resting-state connectivity is included, it should be treated as auxiliary rather than primary, using parietal and hippocampal seeds or task-based connectivity, and should not be used as the main evidence for the proposed parametric modulation hypotheses.

## To test whether parietal and hippocampal regions show complementary temporal representations: We will examine interaction effects between task type and temporal variables to identify regions showing perspective-dependent representations (predicted in parietal cortex), and main effects to identify regions showing perspective-independent representations (predicted in hippocampus).

### Support

The strongest support from the related work is that fMRI can reveal duration-specific activity in parietal cortex, which is directly relevant to the proposed prediction that parietal regions encode temporal variables in a perspective-dependent manner. [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf) used functional localizer and main duration-task scans, general linear model analyses, and searchlight multivoxel pattern analysis to report that frontal and parietal ROIs carried duration information while an occipital ROI carried orientation information. This supports the proposed use of fMRI, ROI-based and searchlight analyses, and the broader assumption that parietal cortex represents temporal duration rather than only spatial or visual features. A weaker but still relevant support is the paper's separation of duration from orientation, which parallels the proposed need to distinguish temporal variables such as sequential distance and event duration from non-temporal linguistic or visual confounds such as syllable length and phrase identity. The weakest support is general: the study demonstrates that temporal duration can be neurally decoded in parietal regions, but it does not test the proposed complementary parietal-hippocampal account, internal versus external temporal perspectives, or interaction effects between task perspective and temporal variables, so it cannot directly validate the planned parametric modulation of sequential distance and duration in the Italian ritual paradigm.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is a scope mismatch rather than a direct negative result: the related work provides no evidence for the proposed complementary parietal-hippocampal architecture because it did not analyze hippocampal ROIs, medial temporal lobe regions, or perspective-independent main effects. [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf) focused on frontal and parietal duration ROIs and an occipital orientation ROI, so it cannot support the prediction that hippocampus stores temporal sequences allocentrically while parietal cortex retrieves them egocentrically. A second contradiction is that the study did not manipulate internal versus external temporal perspectives, did not include a fictional 15-event ritual or native Italian linguistic materials, and did not test symbolic distance effects, so its parietal duration encoding cannot be interpreted as evidence for perspective-dependent temporal reference frames. A third contradiction is methodological: the paper used searchlight MVPA decoding of duration versus orientation rather than the proposed parametric modulation of sequential distance and duration, and the excerpt reports no decoding accuracies, effect sizes, statistical thresholds, participant numbers, scanner parameters, GLM regressors, or permutation cluster-correction details, making it insufficient to justify the planned interaction-effect and main-effect inference with cluster-level family-wise error correction. The weakest contradiction is that the related work did not control for the specific confounds in the proposed plan, such as reaction time, syllable length, head motion, or parts-of-day categorization, so its findings cannot be taken as evidence that the proposed behavioral and fMRI controls will isolate temporal perspective effects.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to explicitly implement the complementary parietal-hippocampal test by defining a priori hippocampal and posterior parietal ROIs, or using searchlight MVPA across both regions, and by modeling the interaction between task perspective and temporal variables in parietal cortex while testing perspective-independent main effects in hippocampus, as proposed in the research plan. [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf) supports adding a searchlight MVPA complement to the planned parametric modulation because it showed that duration information can be decoded in parietal ROIs, but the proposed method should not rely only on parametric modulation; it should also decode sequential distance and duration patterns in parietal and hippocampal regions and compare pattern similarity or decoding accuracy across internal and external tasks. The next most important action is to specify the GLM and statistical pipeline in enough detail to avoid the gaps in the related work: include all regressors for reference and target events, sequential distance, duration, parts-of-day, reaction time, syllable length, head motion, and perspective condition; report permutation testing with cluster-level family-wise error correction, cluster-forming and inference thresholds, and whether ROIs are derived from localizer scans or independent data. A further improvement is to report quantitative outcomes, including decoding accuracies, effect sizes, and comparisons to chance or non-temporal controls, because the related work only indicates that duration and orientation were decoded without numerical values. The least urgent but still useful action is to include a non-temporal control analogous to the orientation control in [(Hayashi et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d9101426abdb68a07d4f49760f77c5b1d795cf), such as linguistic or visual phrase features, to demonstrate that parietal and hippocampal effects are specific to temporal sequence and duration rather than general phrase processing.

## To induce an external, allocentric temporal perspective: External-perspective task (Mental Time Watching): Participants will judge whether target events occurred in the same or different part of the day as reference events.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed external-perspective task comes from [(Bellmund et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05d25cae2f8b92be391f3c1b0cee9e60348b7194), which used a day-learning paradigm in which participants learned a sequence of events and their association with times of day and then made event-time judgments. This is methodologically close to the plan because the plan also trains participants on a fictional ritual with 15 events, durations, and parts of day, and then asks same/different part-of-day judgments to induce an allocentric, panoramic temporal view. The related work shows that part-of-day and event-time knowledge can be acquired and used to reconstruct temporal order, which supports the feasibility of the plan's learning phase and the assumption that part-of-day judgments can access a stable temporal representation. A second, weaker but still relevant support is that the related work used fMRI and representational similarity analysis in hippocampal and entorhinal ROIs to show that sequence relations and event times are encoded in medial temporal regions. This supports the plan's prediction that the hippocampus may contain perspective-independent temporal sequence information, although the related work did not directly test an egocentric versus allocentric interaction. The weakest support is that the related work reported anatomical overlap between within-sequence relations and across-sequence generalization, suggesting that temporal sequence representations can generalize beyond the exact trained sequence, which is compatible with the plan's goal of using a fictional ritual to probe flexible temporal construals, but this evidence is indirect for the specific same/different part-of-day manipulation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work's judgment task was event-time reconstruction, not the plan's binary same/different part-of-day judgment. Because the related work did not test whether participants adopted an external, allocentric perspective or whether part-of-day judgments produce the predicted reverse symbolic distance effect, it cannot validate the plan's central manipulation. A second contradiction is that the related work focused on hippocampal and entorhinal RSA and did not report parietal cortex, egocentric task comparisons, parametric modulation by sequential distance or duration, or interaction effects between perspective and temporal variables; therefore it does not support the plan's specific claim that parietal cortex will show perspective-dependent representations while hippocampus shows perspective-independent representations. A third contradiction is that the related work reported sequence representations differed between hippocampus and entorhinal cortex and showed overlap between within-sequence and across-sequence representations, which complicates the plan's simpler prediction that the hippocampus alone will provide a stable allocentric template; the plan should not overgeneralize from hippocampal main effects without including entorhinal and other medial temporal regions. The weakest contradiction is that the supplied excerpt lacks sample size, scanner field strength, exact trial structure, event durations, statistical thresholds, effect sizes, p-values, and baseline comparisons, so the evidence cannot be mapped one-to-one onto the plan's fMRI analysis or used to justify the plan's permutation-based cluster FWE inference.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the external-perspective task more directly comparable to the related work by including explicit part-of-day and event-time validation measures: retain the same/different part-of-day judgment, but also add a part-of-day categorization test and, if feasible, an event-time reconstruction or ordering test to confirm that participants have learned the temporal structure and are using an allocentric representation rather than simple memory retrieval. The next most important action is to extend the fMRI analysis beyond parametric modulation by adding representational similarity analysis in hippocampal, entorhinal, and parietal ROIs, testing whether sequence and event-time representations are perspective-independent in medial temporal regions and perspective-dependent in parietal regions, with explicit interaction contrasts between external and internal tasks, as motivated by the medial temporal sequence and event-time findings in [(Bellmund et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05d25cae2f8b92be391f3c1b0cee9e60348b7194). The plan should also pre-register quantitative details that the related work left unclear, including sample size, scanner field strength, trial timing, event durations, motion correction, and cluster-level FWE thresholds, and should report effect sizes, accuracy, reaction times, and statistical significance for the symbolic and reverse symbolic distance effects. Finally, the plan should include control conditions that separate part-of-day knowledge from general sequence memory, such as trials with matched sequential distance but different part-of-day status, to ensure that the external-perspective effect is not confounded by event familiarity, phrase length, or motor response differences.

## To provide a learned temporal sequence for the fMRI tasks: Participants will learn the temporal structure through reading and imagination phases.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from the peer-reviewed 2022 Nature Communications paper, which reports a Day learning task and the result “Successful construction of event times” in an fMRI study that also used MRI preprocessing and representational similarity analysis to examine sequence representations in hippocampus and entorhinal cortex [(Bellmund et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05d25cae2f8b92be391f3c1b0cee9e60348b7194). This directly parallels the proposed plan’s day-before learning phase, because both studies use a preparatory learning stage to create a learned temporal sequence that can later be probed during fMRI. The earlier 2021 bioRxiv preprint provides weaker but consistent support because it contains the same Day learning task heading and the same “Successful construction of event times” result, suggesting that the 2022 paper is an iterative refinement or publication of the same methodological approach rather than a contradictory one [(Bellmund et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f5de4cdce235c3d59c530bad7f43733092c1ab). However, because both supplied excerpts are limited to headings and a result title, the support is only at the level of a day-before learning task for event-time construction, not at the level of the proposed reading and imagination phases, 15-event ritual, duration-proportional imagination, or perspective-specific fMRI tasks.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not an explicit negative result but a critical absence: neither the 2022 Nature Communications paper [(Bellmund et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05d25cae2f8b92be391f3c1b0cee9e60348b7194) nor the 2021 preprint [(Bellmund et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f5de4cdce235c3d59c530bad7f43733092c1ab) provides evidence that the learning task used reading and imagination phases, so the proposed method’s specific learning manipulation is not supported by the related work. The next most important limitation is that both papers report only “Successful construction of event times” without accuracy, reaction time, effect size, statistical test, or comparison to a baseline, so the proposed plan’s reliance on behavioral validation through event-sequence judgment, self-paced duration reproduction, and parts-of-day categorization cannot be benchmarked against these studies. A further limitation is the lack of methodological detail needed for reproducibility: no participant sample size, scanner field strength, sequence parameters, event count, event durations, learning-test conditions, ROI definitions, or hyperparameters are available in the supplied excerpts, and a numeric fragment “2.70]” appears without enough context to be interpreted as a quantitative outcome. Finally, the related work does not address internal versus external temporal perspectives, symbolic distance effects, or parietal-hippocampal complementarity, so it cannot be used to support the proposed perspective manipulation or the predicted interaction effects between task perspective and temporal variables.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the learning phase explicit and behaviorally validated: specify the narrative, the 15 events, their durations, parts-of-day labels, number of reading repetitions, imagination instructions, and the exact event-sequence, duration-reproduction, and parts-of-day tests, and report accuracy, reaction time, and statistical evidence that participants met a pre-registered learning criterion before scanning. The next most important action is to align the fMRI analysis with the related work’s demonstrated use of event-time construction and representational similarity analysis in hippocampus and entorhinal cortex, by adding RSA or pattern-based analyses alongside parametric modulation, defining hippocampal, entorhinal, and parietal ROIs, and documenting preprocessing, motion correction, and cluster-level FWE permutation testing. A further improvement is to report the learning phase as an iterative methodological detail, noting how the proposed reading and imagination phases extend or differ from the generic Day learning task in [(Bellmund et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f5de4cdce235c3d59c530bad7f43733092c1ab) and [(Bellmund et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05d25cae2f8b92be391f3c1b0cee9e60348b7194), including any pilot data or behavioral evidence that the learned sequence is stable enough for the internal and external perspective tasks. Finally, include controls for event phrase length, syllable count, reaction time, and head motion, and pre-specify how failed learners will be excluded or modeled, because the related work does not provide these details.

## To reduce confounds in the fMRI analysis: We will control for potential confounds including reaction time, syllable length of event phrases, and head motion.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed confound-control step comes from the direct use of head-motion correction and regression-based fMRI modeling in an analogous memory-related fMRI paradigm [(Gomez et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69775409ccb5bc4106a250986e2e68a2216c6b85). That study combined an out-of-scanner spatial learning phase with an in-scanner word-recognition task and used whole-brain analysis, hippocampal ROI analysis, and multiple regression/correlation to relate retrieval activity to memory performance, operationalized as hits. This is methodologically aligned with the proposed plan's fMRI analysis of temporal sequence and duration processing, because both require nuisance-variable control in a memory task and both use behavioral measures to modulate or contextualize retrieval-related activation. A weaker but still relevant support is the use of regression/correlation with a behavioral memory-performance measure, which parallels the proposed plan's intention to use reaction time and accuracy to validate perspective manipulation and to control for trial-level behavioral variability. However, the related work used hits rather than reaction time or syllable length, so its support for those specific regressors is indirect. The weakest support is the general fMRI experimental structure, learning a sequence out-of-scanner and testing recognition in-scanner, which resembles the proposed fictional 15-event ritual learning phase and scanning tasks, but it does not address temporal perspective, parametric modulation of sequential distance or duration, or permutation-based cluster family-wise error correction.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct empirical refutation but a clear absence of evidence that the proposed specific confounds, reaction time and syllable length of event phrases, were modeled or validated in the related work [(Gomez et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69775409ccb5bc4106a250986e2e68a2216c6b85). The supplied excerpt reports head-motion correction and regression-based modeling, but it does not report reaction time, syllable length, phonological complexity, or any linguistic stimulus-level covariates; therefore it cannot support the claim that these variables are necessary or effective confound controls for the proposed temporal-perspective fMRI analysis. A second, weaker contradiction is that the related work's behavioral modulation used hits as a memory-performance measure, whereas the proposed plan uses reaction time and accuracy to validate perspective manipulation and expects a symbolic distance effect in the internal-perspective task and a reverse effect in the external-perspective task. This mismatch means the related work does not demonstrate that reaction-time covariates will capture the intended perspective manipulation rather than merely trial difficulty or motor speed. A third, weaker contradiction is that the related work provides no quantitative statistical thresholds, effect sizes, cluster statistics, permutation details, or cluster-level family-wise error correction, so it cannot confirm that the proposed permutation testing and cluster-level family-wise error correction will be adequately calibrated for the proposed parametric modulation and interaction contrasts. Finally, the related work is spatially rather than temporally focused, involving spatial processes during word learning and hippocampal retrieval activity, so it offers limited direct evidence for the proposed temporal sequence and duration parametric modulators or for the predicted hippocampal-parietal complementary representation.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed method should specify exactly how reaction time, syllable length, and head motion will be entered into the fMRI model, because the related work only supports head-motion correction and regression-based nuisance control [(Gomez et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69775409ccb5bc4106a250986e2e68a2216c6b85). For the proposed Italian 15-event ritual, the analysis should define per-trial reaction-time regressors, such as mean-centered reaction time and possibly squared reaction time or outlier-trial indicators, syllable length as a phonological or linguistic covariate, such as the number of syllables in the target event phrase, and standard head-motion parameters, such as six rigid-body parameters and optionally 24-parameter motion or spike regressors or framewise displacement. Second, the method should add a behavioral-performance covariate analogous to the related work's hits, such as accuracy or correct versus incorrect trials, while keeping reaction time separate, so that the model can distinguish perspective-specific temporal processing from general memory success or trial difficulty. Third, because syllable length may be task-relevant rather than purely nuisance, especially if event phrases vary in linguistic complexity, the plan should balance or orthogonalize syllable length against the parametric modulators of sequential distance and duration, and report sensitivity analyses showing whether perspective-by-temporal-variable interactions survive with and without these covariates. Fourth, the method should report the statistical details needed to evaluate confound control, including the permutation scheme, cluster-forming threshold, cluster-level family-wise error correction, effect sizes, and whether reaction-time and syllable-length regressors were included in main effects, interactions, or both. Finally, the plan should explicitly justify why reaction time and syllable length are expected to confound the specific contrasts of interest, rather than relying on general fMRI confound-control precedent, and should consider additional linguistic controls, such as lexical frequency, word length, or phonotactic complexity, if the event phrases are not fully matched.

## To make statistical inferences for the fMRI results: Statistical inference will use permutation testing with cluster-level family-wise error correction.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(unknown_author, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a56f02d4ebfef73d76b0dec0f614f9ec9dd9f17), which explicitly describes a nonparametric permutation/randomization inference framework for neuroimaging activation data and connects it to multiple-comparisons correction through single-threshold voxel tests and suprathreshold cluster tests, i.e., cluster-level family-wise error correction. This directly matches the proposed method for fMRI parametric modulation of sequential distance and duration, because the plan requires voxel-level test statistics, a summary statistic for clusters, and permutation-derived thresholds to control FWE for internal- and external-perspective contrasts and interactions. The framework also clarifies the inferential prerequisites that the proposed method must satisfy: exchangeability of observations under the null, a defined voxel-level statistic, a defined cluster summary statistic, and an enumeration of permissible relabelings, with worked examples for single-subject PET, multi-subject PET, and multi-subject fMRI activation. The weaker, indirect support comes from [(Poldrack et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04776d1dd4252e7588186684a6c961443da34e2c), which used permutation testing on fMRI-derived statistical maps and ICA features, with leave-one-out cross-validation and 1000 label permutations, to establish empirical null distributions for classification accuracy; its quantitative results, including whole-brain Z-map linear SVM accuracy of 48.8 percent versus a 95th percentile null value of 7.7 percent and subject identification accuracy of 66.9 percent versus random relabeling mean 0.3 percent and 95th percentile 0.9 percent, show that permutation nulls can demonstrate non-chance fMRI-derived effects, with all reported classification values substantially greater than chance, although the analysis was map-level classification rather than voxelwise cluster inference.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the use of permutation testing with cluster-level FWE, but there are important limitations that contradict an overgeneralized reading of the evidence. The most consequential limitation is that [(Poldrack et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04776d1dd4252e7588186684a6c961443da34e2c) applied permutation testing to classification accuracy on fMRI maps and ICA features, not to voxel-level activation or cluster-level FWE; its 1000 label permutations, 26-way task classification, ICA dimensionality from 2 to 200 components, linear/RBF SVM and L2 logistic regression classifiers, leave-one-out and eight-fold cross-validation, 337-subject OpenFMRI data, 174,264-voxel whole-brain Z-map mask, 152,704-voxel subject-identification mask, FSL/FreeSurfer high-performance cluster pipeline, and non-independent subject contributions therefore do not validate the proposed cluster-level inference for parametric modulation. This is a weaker but still relevant tension because the proposed method needs a permutation distribution for cluster sizes or cluster mass under a spatial multiple-comparisons framework, not merely a null for a single map-level accuracy metric. The second limitation is that [(unknown_author, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a56f02d4ebfef73d76b0dec0f614f9ec9dd9f17) provides the theoretical basis but no quantitative outcomes, activation coordinates, p-values, cluster sizes, effect sizes, participant numbers, scanner specifications, or exact permutation counts; consequently, it supports the method conceptually but does not demonstrate that the specific permutation scheme will be valid for the proposed fMRI design, which includes within-subject internal and external tasks, parametric modulations, reaction-time and syllable-length covariates, and head-motion control. If exchangeability is violated by temporal autocorrelation, block structure, repeated trials, or non-independence between internal and external conditions, the permutation test and cluster-level FWE would not be valid.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify a complete cluster-level permutation inference pipeline for the fMRI contrasts: define the voxel-level statistic for each parametric modulation and interaction, such as a t-statistic for sequential distance or duration modulation and an F-statistic for perspective-by-temporal-variable interactions, specify the cluster-forming threshold, such as uncorrected p less than 0.01 or z greater than 2.3, specify the cluster summary statistic, such as cluster size or cluster mass, and use a sufficient number of permutations, ideally 5,000 to 10,000 or more, to estimate the FWE-corrected cluster threshold at p less than 0.05. This should be implemented with a standard tool such as FSL randomise, AFNI 3dClustSim, or an equivalent permutation cluster inference package, and should account for spatial smoothness, temporal autocorrelation, and nuisance regressors. The next most important action is to ensure exchangeability and avoid leakage by permuting trial labels or parametric values within each subject and task condition after regressing out reaction time, syllable length, and head motion, while preserving the temporal structure of the fMRI time series; do not permute across subjects unless the analysis is explicitly group-level and the null model supports it. A further action is to predefine whole-brain and region-of-interest analyses for hippocampus and posterior parietal cortex, report peak coordinates, cluster sizes, permutation p-values, effect sizes, and the exact permutation count and thresholds, and include sensitivity analyses using voxel-level FWE and cluster mass. Finally, because [(Poldrack et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04776d1dd4252e7588186684a6c961443da34e2c) used only 1000 permutations for a map-level classification null and included non-independent subjects, the proposed study should avoid relying on a single small permutation null and should document that internal- and external-perspective trials are treated as exchangeable units under the null, with any non-independence from repeated events, learning phase, or task order explicitly modeled or tested.
