## Aggression-direction metric: We will employ Elo-rating methods to infer individual female dominance hierarchies using decided avoidance and displacement behaviors, which are considered more reliable proxies of power relationships than aggression itself. We will assign interaction scores to each aggressive encounter by calculating the difference between the standardized Elo-score of the recipient and the aggressor, creating a continuous measure ranging from -1 to 1, where positive scores represent aggression up the hierarchy and negative scores represent aggression down the hierarchy.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed aggression-direction metric comes from [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407), whose section headings describe an Elo-rating procedure for social hierarchies that is algorithmically aligned with the proposed gorilla method: assign initial ratings, update ratings after decided interactions using the standard Elo expected-outcome formula and a K-factor, and compare the resulting dynamic dominance rankings with matrix-based methods such as I&SI and David's score. This directly supports the plan's core decision to use decided avoidance and displacement events as binary outcomes for inferring female dominance rather than treating aggression itself as the primary dominance signal, because the related work frames Elo as a dynamic ranking method for repeated, decided interactions across individuals. A weaker but still relevant support is that the related work's context emphasizes reliability and robustness of Elo ratings in long-term social datasets, which aligns with the proposed use of long-term focal and ad libitum observations of adult females across one western gorilla group and four mountain gorilla groups over multiple years; the repeated-interaction structure is exactly the condition under which Elo updates can accumulate information about power differentials.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the supplied related work [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407) contains no quantitative outcomes, effect sizes, or statistical tests, so it cannot demonstrate that Elo ratings are more reliable than matrix-based methods or that the proposed standardized recipient-minus-aggressor Elo difference is a valid aggression-direction metric. This is especially important because the proposed method depends on a bounded continuous score from -1 to 1, but the related work only indicates that Elo ratings were evaluated for reliability and robustness without reporting the numerical performance that would justify the specific scoring transformation. A second contradiction is that the related work highlights sensitivity to undecided interactions, interaction quality, and choice of K-factor, whereas the proposed method does not specify the K-factor, initial ratings, treatment of undecided or ambiguous avoidance/displacement events, interaction-quality weighting, temporal decay, or how the Elo scores are standardized to produce a -1 to 1 difference. Without these implementation details, the proposed linear mixed-effects model may be sensitive to arbitrary Elo parameter choices, and the residual distribution and multicollinearity checks planned in the research plan would not address this source of uncertainty. A third, weaker contradiction is that the related work compares Elo with matrix-based dominance measures such as I&SI and David's score, but the proposed plan does not include such baseline comparisons, leaving open whether the aggression-direction score derived from Elo is superior to, or at least consistent with, established static or matrix-based hierarchy metrics for the same gorilla data.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to fully specify and validate the Elo implementation before using it as the response variable: state the initial rating, K-factor, update formula, handling of undecided or low-quality interactions, any temporal decay, and the exact standardization procedure that converts recipient-minus-aggressor Elo differences into a bounded -1 to 1 score, including whether standardization is global, by group, by year, or rolling. The next priority is to run sensitivity analyses across plausible K-factors and standardization schemes, and to compare the resulting aggression-direction scores against matrix-based dominance metrics such as I&SI and David's score, as suggested by [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407), to show that the metric is robust and not an artifact of parameter choice. Finally, the model should be strengthened by adding diagnostics for bounded or transformed outcomes, checking distributional assumptions beyond residual plots, and including random effects or autocorrelation structures that account for repeated dyadic interactions over time, while retaining the planned random factors for interacting females, dyad, and group.

## Model diagnostics: We will validate our models using residual distribution tests and check for multicollinearity among explanatory variables to ensure robust statistical inference.

### Support

As the only related work provided, the strongest support for the proposed model diagnostics step is that [(Bates et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b536c7b0f45d4b512f337d0acda09ca3e4cd953) describes the lme4 output module for fitted linear mixed-effects models, which can return fitted values, residuals, variance components, covariance matrices for fixed-effect coefficients, and diagnostic plots for merMod or lmerMod objects. This directly supports the gorilla plan because the planned model is a linear mixed-effects model with aggression direction score as the response and fixed effects for species, reproductive state, number of females, number of adult males, and aggression intensity, with random effects for interacting females, dyads, and groups; lme4 can therefore supply the empirical residuals and fitted values needed for residual distribution checks. A second, slightly weaker but still relevant support is that the module exposes getME and post-estimation quantities such as conditional variances of random effects, profiling, parametric bootstrapping, p-values, and confidence intervals, which can be used to inspect the stability of the fixed-effect estimates and to support robust inference after residual and collinearity checks. The weakest support is that the paper is primarily a software and algorithmic description built on penalized least squares, REML, sparse matrix structures, Cholesky-based computations, CHOLMOD, compressed sparse column storage, and Eigen, illustrated with the sleepstudy example rather than gorilla behavioral data; it therefore supports the computational feasibility of fitting and diagnosing the proposed mixed model, but it does not provide gorilla-specific validation evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that [(Bates et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b536c7b0f45d4b512f337d0acda09ca3e4cd953) does not explicitly provide residual distribution tests or multicollinearity diagnostics, so the proposed method cannot be considered fully specified or validated by this related work alone. The plan states that models will be validated using residual distribution tests and checked for multicollinearity, but the cited lme4 output module only supplies fitted values, residuals, variance components, and diagnostic plots; it does not report Shapiro-Wilk or other residual normality tests, VIFs, condition numbers, or collinearity metrics. A second limitation is that the related work reports no quantitative diagnostic outcomes, baseline comparisons, statistical significance, or residual test statistics, so there is no empirical evidence that the proposed diagnostics will behave well for the gorilla dataset. The weakest but still important contradiction is that the lme4 framework assumes a Gaussian linear mixed model with normally distributed residuals and homoscedastic variance, whereas the planned response is a bounded aggression direction score from -1 to 1 and includes categorical predictors such as reproductive state and aggression intensity; if residual tests reveal boundedness, skewness, or heteroscedasticity, the default lme4 Gaussian specification may be inadequate, and the related work does not address alternative model forms or multicollinearity among group-level predictors and random group effects.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify an explicit diagnostic pipeline beyond lme4: fit the REML linear mixed model in lme4, then extract residuals, fitted values, and variance components using residuals, fitted, and getME; compute residual distribution diagnostics including normal Q-Q plots, scale-location plots, Shapiro-Wilk or Anderson-Darling tests on Pearson or deviance residuals, and, because of the random effects for female, dyad, and group, use simulation-based residuals or parametric bootstrap diagnostics to account for non-independence. The next most important action is to define multicollinearity checks for the fixed effects species, reproductive state, number of females, number of adult males, and aggression intensity by computing VIFs, a fixed-effect correlation matrix, and condition numbers, centering continuous group-size predictors, and pre-registering thresholds such as VIF greater than 5 or 10 for follow-up. Because species is nested in the five observed groups, one western group and four mountain groups, and group is a random effect, the plan should also check whether the species fixed effect is confounded with group-level variation, inspect correlations between fixed effects and random-effect BLUPs, and consider simplifying or reparameterizing the model if convergence or variance-component estimates are unstable. A further improvement is to run sensitivity analyses for the bounded -1 to 1 response, such as a logit-transformed Gaussian model, beta regression, or an ordinal model if aggression direction is treated categorically, and to report convergence diagnostics, condition numbers, residual test statistics, p-values, variance components, and sample sizes so that the diagnostic claims are reproducible.
