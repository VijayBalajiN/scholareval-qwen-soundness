## Strengths 
The strongest support is for using Elo ratings to infer female dominance from decided avoidance and displacement events, because [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407) describes an Elo procedure for repeated decided social interactions and dynamic dominance rankings, which aligns with the proposal's long-term behavioral data and use of power differentials rather than aggression alone. The second strongest support is for the planned linear mixed-effects modeling and post-estimation checks, because [(Bates et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b536c7b0f45d4b512f337d0acda09ca3e4cd953) shows that lme4 can provide fitted values, residuals, variance components, covariance matrices, and diagnostic plots for the proposed mixed model.

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradiction is that the proposed Elo-based aggression-direction score is not fully validated or specified: [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407) does not provide quantitative evidence that Elo is superior to matrix-based dominance metrics, and it highlights sensitivity to undecided interactions, interaction quality, K-factor, and rating updates, while the proposal omits initial ratings, K-factor, handling of ambiguous events, temporal decay, and the exact standardization to a -1 to 1 score. The second strongest contradiction is that the planned diagnostics are not fully supported by [(Bates et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b536c7b0f45d4b512f337d0acda09ca3e4cd953), which supplies lme4 outputs but not explicit residual distribution tests, multicollinearity metrics, or validation for a bounded -1 to 1 response that may violate Gaussian linear mixed-model assumptions.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Specify the full Elo pipeline before modeling: state initial ratings, K-factor, update formula, treatment of undecided or low-quality avoidance/displacement events, temporal decay, and the exact standardization of recipient-minus-aggressor Elo differences into a -1 to 1 score, then run sensitivity analyses across plausible K-factors and standardization schemes and compare results with matrix-based metrics such as I&SI and David's score, as suggested by [(Neumann et al., 2011-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6b0905f684169ab24c99a730554ad1b244c407).

Add an explicit diagnostic pipeline for the linear mixed-effects model: use lme4 to extract residuals, fitted values, and variance components, then compute Q-Q and scale-location plots, normality tests on Pearson or deviance residuals, simulation-based or parametric bootstrap diagnostics for non-independence, VIFs, fixed-effect correlations, condition numbers, and convergence checks, with pre-registered thresholds for follow-up, building on the lme4 outputs described in [(Bates et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b536c7b0f45d4b512f337d0acda09ca3e4cd953).

Strengthen model specification for the bounded aggression-direction score and group structure: test alternative models such as logit-transformed Gaussian, beta regression, or ordinal models; center continuous group-size predictors; check whether species is confounded with group random effects given one western and four mountain groups; and include dyad or temporal autocorrelation structures if repeated interactions over time are non-independent, while retaining female, dyad, and group random effects.
