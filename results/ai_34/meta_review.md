## Context: To address the challenge of high-dimensional black-box adversarial attacks. Method: We propose TETRADAT (TEnsor TRain ADversarial ATtacks), a novel black-box attack method that combines TT-based optimization with attribution-guided pixel selection.

### Support

The strongest support for TETRADAT comes from [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d), which on 1,000 ImageNet validation images, one per class, shows that a low-frequency sensing matrix plus probability-driven weighted random walk improves black-box attack efficiency: PPBA achieved 84.8% ASR with 430/668 queries and AUC 1331.3 on ResNet50, 90.3% with 392/548 and AUC 1451.5 on VGG-16, and 65.3% with 546/1051 and AUC 948.9 on Inception V3 under L-infinity 0.05, while Bandits T_D achieved 74.7%, 621/970, 1030.4; 78.6%, 565/871, 1129.3; and 55.9%, 701/1274, 726.6, respectively; this directly supports TETRADAT's core idea of reducing the perturbation space before probabilistic optimization and its 1,000-image ImageNet protocol. Next, [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1) supports the 10,000-query ImageNet setting and structured priors: on 10,000 ImageNet images with a 10,000-query limit, Bandits T D with time and data priors was six times less failure-prone than NES in L-infinity and three times less failure-prone in L2, used about half as many queries, and was 2.5 times more query-efficient in L-infinity and 5 times in L2 when restricted to NES-successful images, indicating that spatially structured low-dimensional representations can make high-dimensional black-box attacks feasible. [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) supports attribution-guided pixel selection because ZOO uses zeroth-order coordinate descent with attack-space dimension reduction and optimizes important pixels first, although the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes. [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23a177) supports TETRADAT's latent tensor representation by showing that a learned encoder/decoder embedding space with NES can attack black-box models, though it reports no numerical outcomes and uses a 50,000-query budget. [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) supports the feasibility of TT decomposition on ImageNet-scale tensors, reporting that Tensor-Train was faster than Parafac and Tucker and that 4-mode TT achieved the highest accuracy among tensor representations, although it is a defense rather than an attack. [(Dong et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44d43bfbd23d55b1e7c4c4fd91fe101c0eaf1a06) supports using an auxiliary model to guide perturbations, because translation-invariant surrogate-based attacks improve black-box transfer, with Gaussian kernels of size 15 by 15 chosen after plateauing, although it is not query-based. [(Papernot et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53b047e503f4c24602f376a774d653f7ed56c024) supports black-box label-only attacks and the importance of perturbation magnitude, reporting 84.24% misclassification against a MetaMind MNIST oracle and more than 64.24% against a local GTSRB oracle, with Papernot-style distortion from 7.14% to 28.57% raising transferability from 14.70% to 55.53%. [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) supports the premise that small imperceptible perturbations can transfer across models and training sets, although cross-training-set transfer decreased and exact transfer percentages are absent. [(Xie et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7867244de690a1ae11a7a6d5a021e868fa75a3c) supports structured spatial perturbations, showing large mAP drops for Faster R-CNN and FCN models and cross-task transfer, though it is not black-box or query-budgeted. [(Wan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/645e2401c838f690a7a5e14367ac54fc1afe950a) supports TETRADAT's multi-restart search over a mixed perturbation space, but provides no attack-specific quantitative results. [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) supports evaluating adversarially trained targets and black-box transfer attacks, but the excerpt lacks numerical outcomes. Finally, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) aligns with the ImageNet evaluation goal but provides no quantitative evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction concerns the TT-rank and robustness assumption: [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) uses Tensor-Train decomposition as a defense on ImageNet ResNet-v2 50, with 4-mode TT ranks searched from 40 to 150 and batch sizes 1 to 50, and reports that TT can remove adversarial perturbations and outperform Parafac and Tucker; this undermines the plan's fixed TT-rank r=5 and suggests that low-rank TT perturbations may be too constrained for effective attacks and potentially easier to denoise. A second major contradiction is the reliance on a single VGG-19 auxiliary model for attribution across diverse targets: [(Papernot et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53b047e503f4c24602f376a774d653f7ed56c024) found that substitute-model transferability depended on architecture, with a copy of the oracle best and other architectures reaching only 72.24% to 80.21% asymptotic transferability, and [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) found that cross-training-set transfer on disjoint 30,000-image MNIST splits decreased considerably, so VGG-19 Integrated Gradients may not identify the same important pixels for AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, or adversarial models. Third, the evidence for dimensionality reduction favors spatial and frequency structure over semantic attribution: [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1) shows that a simple data prior from average-pooling/tiling improves bandit gradient estimation, and [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d) shows that a low-frequency sensing matrix improved success rate by up to 27.2% and reduced queries by nearly 26%, while random Gaussian projection had no positive influence; TETRADAT's selection of only about 10% of pixels and HSV saturation/value modifications may discard low-frequency or spatially distributed perturbations that are empirically effective. Fourth, adversarial training and defended models may limit success: [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) evaluates adversarially trained classifiers against black-box transfer attacks, and [(Dong et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44d43bfbd23d55b1e7c4c4fd91fe101c0eaf1a06) shows that translation-invariant smoothing and kernel transformations improve transfer to defenses, whereas TETRADAT does not include input transformations, smoothing, or adversarial-specific search. Fifth, the spatial-structure evidence is mixed: [(Xie et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7867244de690a1ae11a7a6d5a021e868fa75a3c) reports that random row and column permutation of dense perturbations caused negligible accuracy drop, which weakens the assumption that TT's spatial correlation modeling is essential, although the same paper also reports large accuracy drops and cross-task transfer. Sixth, the query-budget evidence is not uniformly favorable: [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23a177) uses a 50,000-query budget and reports that this upper limit already gave very high success rates for nearly all methods, suggesting that 10,000 queries may be tight for some latent-space attacks, although [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1) shows 10,000 queries can be sufficient with strong priors. Finally, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) provides no quantitative outcomes, so it cannot validate the proposed TT/PROTES protocol.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add controlled ablations that isolate whether TT/PROTES and attribution selection actually improve black-box attack efficiency: compare TETRADAT against full-pixel PROTES, random 10% pixel selection, attribution 10%, spatial tiling prior from [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1), low-frequency sensing matrix from [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d), learned latent NES from [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23a177), and the planned baselines OnePixel, Square, and Pixle, while also varying TT-rank from 5 to 10, 20, 40, 80, and 150 based on [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) and tuning PROTES K=100, k=10, lambda=0.01; report ASR, average and median queries, AUC, step-effective rate, L1/L2 norms, and visual quality. Second, validate attribution transfer before committing to VGG-19: test target-specific or ensemble auxiliary models, top-k pixel fractions of 5%, 10%, 20%, 50%, and 100%, Integrated Gradients steps and discretization nodes, and correlation with target gradients where available, because [(Papernot et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53b047e503f4c24602f376a774d653f7ed56c024) and [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) show architecture and training-set mismatch can reduce transfer. Third, incorporate low-frequency, spatial, or translation-invariant constraints into the TT factors or restart schedule, motivated by [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d), [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1), [(Dong et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44d43bfbd23d55b1e7c4c4fd91fe101c0eaf1a06), and [(Xie et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7867244de690a1ae11a7a6d5a021e868fa75a3c), to improve transfer to adversarial Inception and Adversarial Inception-ResNet. Fourth, broaden perturbation application beyond HSV saturation/value: test RGB additive perturbations, L-infinity and L2 constrained perturbations, and report norms on a 0 to 1 scale, because [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d) achieves strong results with L-infinity 0.05 and [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) emphasizes minimum distortion. Fifth, strengthen statistical evaluation: use the planned 1,000 ImageNet images, multiple random seeds, confidence intervals or significance tests, per-class and per-architecture breakdowns, query curves up to at least 10,000 and optionally 50,000 queries as in [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23a177), and report the fraction of images correctly classified by both target and attribution models. Finally, document reproducibility details including hardware, query counting, epsilon halving schedule, initialization from previous probability distributions, and TT factorization constraints, consistent with the detailed experimental conditions reported in [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) and [(Li et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d446f22f86596d7bee3a177e5cc846fc8bc02d).

## Context: To represent high-dimensional perturbations compactly. Method: We will formulate the adversarial perturbation problem as discrete optimization over a multidimensional tensor, where each dimension corresponds to a selected pixel and tensor elements represent perturbation values.

### Support

The strongest support is direct: [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) introduces PROTES, a gradient-free black-box optimizer for multidimensional discrete problems in tensor-train format, using probabilistic tensor sampling, log-likelihood automatic differentiation, and Adam gradient ascent under a fixed 10,000-query budget; it outperformed TTOpt, Optima-TT, and nevergrad baselines on 19 of 20 benchmarks, e.g., P-07 6.2E+06 vs NoisyBandit 2.2E+10 and Portfolio 3.1E+08, P-10 -8.7E+02 vs Portfolio 2.6E+03, P-14 -3.1E+03 vs TTOpt -2.8E+03, P-15 6.7E-03 vs Optima-TT 2.3E-02, and solved constrained control P-19 at 6.4E-02 where baselines failed, with P-17 runtime 641 s vs TTOpt 607 s, Optima-TT 4245 s, and Portfolio 780 s on a laptop. Its default K=100, k=10, and R=5 closely match the proposed K=100, k=10, and TT-rank 5, supporting the core discrete TT formulation. The next strongest support is that TETRADAT itself has been reported on ImageNet in [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177), although the excerpt gives no tensor formulation, pixel selection, PROTES parameters, query budget, metrics, or outcomes, so it only weakly supports feasibility. Further support comes from [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca), which shows Tensor-Train decomposition of ImageNet image tensors with TensorLy on one NVIDIA Titan Xp 12 GB can be faster and more accurate than Parafac/Tucker, with 4-mode TT best and rank searched from 40 to 150, demonstrating that TT representations are computationally viable for high-dimensional ImageNet tensors. [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) supports the broader premise that low-rank tensor factorizations interact with adversarial perturbations, reporting about 1% clean-accuracy loss and improved robustness against FGSM, BIM, DeepFool, and C&W on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and 1,000 ImageNet images, with CP rank 8 for 32x32x3 CIFAR-10 and Tucker preserving the channel dimension. [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) supports the attribution-selected-pixel component by providing Integrated Gradients, an axiomatic path-integral method satisfying completeness, sensitivity, and implementation invariance, requiring only forward and gradient calls, which can be used with VGG-19 and 15 steps/nodes to select approximately 5,000 pixels. Finally, [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6adf016e7531c91100d3cf4a74f5d4c87b26b528) supports the adversarial problem setting by showing full-image gradient-sign perturbations with L-infinity budgets 0.3 on MNIST and 0.1 on CIFAR-10 reduce standard network accuracy to 12.0% and 0.0%, while distilled networks retain 87.1% and 42.0%, confirming that small perturbations matter and that robust or adversarially trained targets require stronger attacks.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is the absence of direct quantitative validation for the exact proposed pipeline: [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) reports TETRADAT on ImageNet but provides no discrete tensor formulation, pixel selection, TT-rank, PROTES hyperparameters, query budget, attack success rates, perturbation norms, or statistical significance, so it cannot support the proposed 10,000-query, 5,000-pixel, rank-5 design. A major weakening contradiction is that the only detailed PROTES evidence is synthetic: [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) evaluates analytic functions, QUBO, and optimal control, not DNN image queries, and uses lambda=0.05 rather than the proposed lambda=0.01, with robustness varied only over K, k, and R, leaving the proposed learning rate and 5,000-dimensional pixel-tensor setting unvalidated. [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) also contradicts the choice of TT-rank 5 by using 4-mode ImageNet TT ranks searched from 40 to 150, with Figure 4 covering 50 to 120, for full-image reconstruction, suggesting that rank 5 may be too low to capture meaningful image structure, although its defense task differs from discrete perturbation optimization. [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) suggests that low-rank tensor preprocessing can act as a defense, improving robustness against FGSM, BIM, DeepFool, and C&W while reducing clean accuracy by about 1%, which implies that a low-rank TT perturbation may be especially vulnerable to low-rank denoising or that selected-pixel perturbations must be tested against such defenses. [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6adf016e7531c91100d3cf4a74f5d4c87b26b528) contradicts the assumption that simple perturbations will easily defeat robust models: distilled networks raised adversarial accuracy from 12.0% to 87.1% on MNIST and from 0.0% to 42.0% on CIFAR-10 under gradient-sign attacks, so the proposed attacks on adversarially trained Inception models may face substantially higher robustness than standard models. Finally, [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) does not provide empirical evidence that top-10% Integrated Gradients pixels are sufficient for adversarial search; its completeness axiom attributes the full prediction difference across all inputs, so restricting to 5,000 pixels may discard distributed low-salience perturbations, and baseline choice, such as a black image, can affect attribution quality.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a controlled pilot before the full ImageNet protocol: use a small subset of ImageNet or CIFAR-10/MNIST with the same 10,000-query budget, compare TETRADAT against OnePixel, Square, Pixle, and at least one gradient-free baseline, and tune PROTES lambda around the validated 0.05 from [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) and the proposed 0.01, while testing TT-rank values above 5 in light of the 40-150 rank range used for ImageNet TT in [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca); report attack success rate, L1/L2, clean accuracy drop, runtime, and multiple random seeds with significance tests. Second, validate the attribution-selected-pixel assumption using [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) by comparing top-10% VGG-19 Integrated Gradients pixels against random pixels, target-model saliency, and lower pixel fractions, and by testing multiple baselines and 15-step/15-node settings to ensure the 5,000-pixel tensor is not missing distributed perturbations. Third, because low-rank tensor preprocessing can suppress adversarial perturbations, as shown by [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) and [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca), include robustness tests against low-rank or TT denoising defenses and adversarially trained models, measuring whether the HSV saturation/value perturbations remain effective and imperceptible. Fourth, align the evaluation with the adversarial-robustness literature by reporting clean and adversarial accuracy, perturbation budgets, and comparisons to white-box gradient attacks where appropriate, using the L-infinity/L2 budget framing from [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6adf016e7531c91100d3cf4a74f5d4c87b26b528), and by documenting the missing protocol details from [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) such as discretization, restart schedule, and query accounting. Finally, ensure computational reproducibility by specifying TensorLy or an equivalent TT implementation, GPU/CPU resources, memory for 5,000 dimensions at rank 5, batch or patch strategies if needed, and exact PROTES sampling and Adam settings, since the related work shows TT on ImageNet can be resource-sensitive and rank or batch size affect runtime and accuracy.

## Context: To search the multidimensional perturbation tensor space. Method: The PROTES optimizer will be employed to efficiently search this space using probabilistic tensor sampling in TT format.

### Support

The strongest support is direct evidence for the exact optimizer: [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) evaluates PROTES with probabilistic TT sampling, tt_sample, tt_log, automatic differentiation, and Adam ascent under fixed K=100, k=10, k_gd=1, lambda=0.05, R=5, and M=10^4 objective calls, achieving best or tied-best objective value on 19 of 20 discrete benchmarks and a 641 s runtime on P-17 versus 4245 s for Optima-TT; this directly supports the plan's 10,000-query PROTES/TT search, but the benchmarks are analytic, QUBO, and control problems rather than ImageNet adversarial perturbations, and the excerpt reports no L1/L2 norms, visual quality, multiple seeds, or significance. The next strongest support is quantitative evidence that structured perturbation subspaces can make black-box attacks query-efficient: [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) shows SimBA-DCT, using a 1/8 DCT low-frequency subspace, step size 0.2, and 10,000 untargeted iterations, reaches median 582 queries on ResNet-50 and 4,854 targeted queries, outperforming QL and other baselines, which supports the plan's premise that reducing the perturbation tensor space can improve search, although it uses DCT rather than TT. Theoretical support that a compact subspace can be effective is provided by [(Dohmatob et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10a31b65fe2e2b82ff79e1242d653dfc0c0ebbf8), which proves fooling-rate lower bounds for adversarially viable subspaces under gradient strength beta, Lipschitz L, and oscillation theta, supporting TT-based low-dimensional search if the selected pixels are aligned with the target classifier. Probabilistic query selection in a structured subspace is supported by [(Shukla et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11bd044e8b8067dd43daf4125eaa40244196e78f), which uses a Gaussian process surrogate and expected improvement over FFT or low-resolution subspaces on 1,000 correctly classified images, and by [(Guo et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e6c979fb345f673ffc997e377ae724a8c6cd022), which evaluates low-frequency DCT boundary and NES attacks on ResNet-50, though the excerpt reports no numerical success rates. Earlier black-box work supports the broader query-budgeted gradient-free setting: [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) uses zeroth-order coordinate descent with important-pixel dimension reduction on MNIST/CIFAR-10/ImageNet, batch size 128, and K80/GTX 1080 Ti hardware, and [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) uses NES with l-infinity projection on 1,000 ImageNet images, epsilon=0.05, and query timeout L=10^6. The direct TETRADAT report, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177), provides only that evaluation was conducted on ImageNet, so it supports the plan's dataset choice but not the PROTES mechanism. Finally, weaker support comes from [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), whose PGD/CW/transfer evaluation on MNIST/CIFAR-10 motivates strong adversaries and adversarially trained targets, and [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee), whose CP/Tucker preprocessing on 10,000 MNIST, 50,000 CIFAR-10, and 1,000 ImageNet images shows tensor decomposition can alter adversarial inputs, though as a defense rather than an attack optimizer.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is theoretical: [(Dohmatob et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10a31b65fe2e2b82ff79e1242d653dfc0c0ebbf8) shows that low-dimensional perturbations are effective only when the subspace is adversarially viable, i.e., has non-negligible inner product with the classifier gradient, with bounds depending on beta, L, theta, and R; the plan selects pixels using VGG-19 Integrated Gradients with 15 steps/nodes rather than target-model gradients, so the 10% pixel subspace may be non-viable for AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, or adversarial models, undermining the TT search. The next strongest contradiction is that the direct PROTES evidence does not validate the adversarial transfer: [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) uses M=10^4 on generic discrete benchmarks with lambda=0.05, while the plan uses lambda=0.01, r=5, and a 5,000-pixel image tensor, and reports no image ASR, L1/L2, visual quality, multiple seeds, or significance; moreover PROTES was not uniformly best, with TTOpt better on P-18 at 1.1E-02, so the assumption that PROTES will outperform black-box baselines on ImageNet is not established. Quantitative evidence that overly restrictive structured subspaces can fail also contradicts the fixed 10% pixel selection: [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) reports SimBA-DCT targeted failures on about 2.5% of images after 60,000 queries and harder Inception v3 cases requiring more than 10,000 queries, suggesting that a fixed attribution subspace and 10,000-query budget may be insufficient without adaptive expansion. Norm and visual-quality assumptions are contradicted by [(Shukla et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11bd044e8b8067dd43daf4125eaa40244196e78f), which finds that mapping from a structured subspace to full resolution matters for L2 versus L-infinity attacks, with FFT better for L2 and nearest-neighbor better for L-infinity at epsilon=0.05 on ResNet-50; the plan's HSV saturation/value modifications may not preserve L1/L2 imperceptibility or query efficiency. The plan's budget and amplitude choices are also at odds with prior black-box settings: [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) uses epsilon=0.05 and query timeout L=10^6 for targeted InceptionV3 attacks, whereas the plan uses initial epsilon=1 and a hard 10,000-query budget, creating risk of visible perturbations or premature failure. Stronger adversary and defense evidence contradicts optimistic success on robust targets: [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) shows PGD adversarial training and strong CW/transfer adversaries on MNIST/CIFAR-10, implying adversarial Inception and Inception-ResNet may be much harder than OnePixel, Square, or Pixle baselines can reveal. Tensor-decomposition defenses also contradict the assumption that TT perturbations will remain effective after preprocessing: [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) reports CP/Tucker reconstruction with CP rank 8 on CIFAR-10 and about 10x slower CP than Tucker on ImageNet, showing low-rank tensor operations can alter adversarial examples, though it does not test TT attack perturbations. Weaker still, the omission of established structured black-box baselines contradicts the plan's comparative claim: [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) and [(Guo et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e6c979fb345f673ffc997e377ae724a8c6cd022) provide effective zeroth-order and low-frequency DCT black-box attacks, so comparing only to OnePixel, Square, and Pixle may overstate TETRADAT's advantage.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the attribution-selected TT subspace adversarially viable for each target: compute target-specific gradient or finite-difference saliency when possible, measure correlation between VGG-19 Integrated Gradients and target gradients, and include random, target-gradient, VGG-IG, DCT/FFT, and low-frequency baselines; if viability is low, reselect pixels per target or use a hybrid pixel-plus-frequency subspace. The second most important action is to validate PROTES on the actual adversarial task with statistical rigor: run multiple seeds, report confidence intervals and significance, compare PROTES against TTOpt, Optima-TT, nevergrad baselines, SimBA, LFBA, ZOO, NES, OnePixel, Square, and Pixle under identical 10,000-query budgets, and report ASR versus query count, L1/L2, visual quality, and runtime; tune lambda around 0.01 and 0.05, K, k, r, and k_gd, and allocate the 10,000 queries across restarts. The third action is to add adaptive subspace expansion: if the 10% pixel TT search fails at a given amplitude, expand to more pixels or add a DCT/FFT low-frequency component, analogous to SimBA-DCT's optional 1/32 frequency expansion, and use the previous TT distribution only when it improves success. The fourth action is to control perturbation norms and visual quality: map discrete TT perturbations to RGB/HSV with explicit L1/L2 constraints, test HSV saturation/value versus direct RGB perturbations, and use norm-aware mappings or projections inspired by [(Shukla et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11bd044e8b8067dd43daf4125eaa40244196e78f) and [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21). The fifth action is to strengthen the experimental protocol: use 1,000 ImageNet images correctly classified by both target and attribution models, evaluate all five standard and two adversarial architectures, report per-architecture and per-class results, include targeted and untargeted variants if feasible, and specify hardware/runtime given PROTES' 641 s benchmark runtime. The least important but still useful action is to test robustness against tensor-decomposition preprocessing and adversarial training, using CP/Tucker reconstruction or denoising autoencoders from [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) and PGD-style adversarial training insights from [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), to show whether TT perturbations survive practical defenses.

## Context: To reduce the optimization dimensionality while focusing on pixels most likely to affect model predictions. Method: We will use an auxiliary white-box model to compute attribution maps via Integrated Gradients, identifying the most semantically important pixels for perturbation.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162), which uses an auxiliary white-box ResNet-152 surrogate to compute a gradient map M, uses |M| as sampling probabilities for coordinates, applies Gaussian smoothing, and evaluates ImageNet 299x299 attacks under a 10,000-query budget, directly supporting the TETRADAT idea of using an auxiliary model to bias black-box perturbation search toward influential pixels, although it uses raw gradients rather than Integrated Gradients and probabilistic sampling rather than a fixed top-10% pixel mask. Next, [(Kumar et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e8eb3d8b0c67f593a1a64d28624aeeaa7c736f8) provides direct quantitative evidence that gradient-based interpretability can constrain a differential-evolution search and speed convergence by about 6x on 100 CIFAR-10 images with SqueezeNet, with constrained average runtimes around 0.52-0.58 seconds versus 2.08-2.16 seconds and average iterations around 2.26-2.52 versus 35.61-37.40, supporting attribution-guided dimensionality reduction, but the scale is far smaller than ImageNet 299x299 and the method targets a single pixel rather than 5000 pixels. [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) shows that Integrated Gradients maps identify salient pixels that can be shifted by small perturbations, with top-1000 overlap below 30% at L-infinity 8 on 512 ImageNet SqueezeNet images, supporting the premise that salient pixels are actionable, but the setting is white-box saliency manipulation rather than black-box query efficiency. [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) supports auxiliary white-box guidance for black-box transfer attacks, with MI-FGSM ensemble logits achieving 37.6%, 40.3%, and 23.3% black-box transfer success on hold-out Inception-v3, Inception-v3 ens4, and InceptionRes-v2 ens, compared with I-FGSM at 18.6%, 18.7%, and 9.9%, though it uses full-image gradients rather than IG pixel selection. Dimensionality-reduction evidence is also strong: [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1) reports that a data-dependent spatial prior made bandit attacks six times less failure-prone than NES in l-infinity and used about half as many queries on 10,000 ImageNet images with 10,000 queries; [(Moon et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d26aa7cb2cba8f172b4299c61835509b1090ecd) shows block-level lazy local search achieved about 10% higher success than Bandits with 33% fewer queries on CIFAR-10 and 91% fewer queries than NES on NES-successful samples; [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) shows SimBA/SimBA-DCT basis restriction reached untargeted ResNet-50 success with median 944 and 582 queries and targeted SimBA median 7,038 queries within 10,000-query budgets; [(Sahu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d4433228f30e6ec565e751c39f8948078802f186) shows FFT/gradient-covariance dimensionality reduction outperformed PARSIMONIOUS below about 450 queries and dominated NES/BANDITS-TD/NES-FFT on ImageNet; and [(Xu et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bc67a8a47c796053d5ed77aaecd3cbbd4c5d4c1) shows group-sparse perturbations can preserve success while reducing l0 support. Finally, [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) provides the theoretical basis for IG, including Completeness, Implementation Invariance, and Sensitivity, making it a defensible attribution choice, although it does not validate attack efficiency.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is [(Adebayo et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dc8f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8), which shows Integrated Gradients can retain input-like or edge-like structure even when labels or weights are randomized, including Inception v3 ImageNet and MNIST/Fashion MNIST sanity checks, directly challenging the assumption that VGG-19 IG maps identify target-relevant semantically important pixels rather than generic convolutional or input artifacts. Next, [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) contradicts the stability of a fixed IG-selected pixel set: on 512 ImageNet images with SqueezeNet and M=100 IG steps, small L-infinity 8 perturbations reduced top-1000 overlap below 30% and changed rank correlation, meaning the pixels selected before the attack may no longer be the most salient pixels during the attack. [(Singh et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05ba68950ac4ca970cdd77f02bbeff7ff9f776d3) similarly shows attribution maps can be manipulated and requires attribution-stability training, implying that attribution-guided selection can be unreliable unless robustness is enforced. [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) also cautions that auxiliary-model guidance is not automatically strong: black-box transfer success was only 37.6%, 40.3%, and 23.3% for MI-FGSM ensembles, and performance depended on ensemble fusion and architecture, so a single VGG-19 IG map may transfer poorly to AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, and adversarially trained targets. [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162) reports that even auxiliary-gradient-guided SimBA+ had unsatisfying query efficiency and failures due to cold start and local minima, suggesting that merely selecting coordinates from an auxiliary map is insufficient without warm starts, momentum, or iterative refinement. [(Uesato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4b434c3ab979ecdd71bbed894b34de77590c6dd) shows full-space SPSA can reduce many defenses to 0.0 accuracy, but adversarial training remains high at about 85.5 CIFAR-10 and 67.5 ImageNet, implying that attribution-guided pixel selection may not overcome adversarially trained models and that strong full-space baselines must be included. Weaker contextual contradictions include [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) and [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791), which establish query-limited black-box optimization without attribution, so the added value of IG pixel selection must be demonstrated against such baselines; and [(Alvarez-Melis, and Jaakkola, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b7fcbb19c75ad65be522b64d5f4b23dbcb3b883b) highlights explanation robustness concerns, though it does not provide IG-specific attack metrics.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add sanity-check controls for the IG pixel-selection component: compute VGG-19 IG maps on ImageNet with the planned 15 gradient steps and 15 discretization nodes, but compare top-10% pixel masks against random-label VGG-19, random-weight VGG-19, random pixel selection, edge detectors, and, where possible, target-model gradients or occlusion sensitivity, reporting Spearman/Kendall correlation, top-k overlap, and attack success deltas, because [(Adebayo et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dc8f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8) shows IG can reflect input structure. Second, make the attribution signal more robust and target-aware: use an ensemble of auxiliary models or multiple attribution maps (IG, gradient*input, SmoothGrad, occlusion), smooth the IG map with a Gaussian or translation-invariant kernel as in [(Dong et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44d43bfbd23d55b1e7c4c4fd91fe101c0eaf1a06) and [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162), and consider block-level or group-level selection as in [(Moon et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d26aa7cb2cba8f172b4299c61835509b1090ecd) and [(Xu et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bc67a8a47c796053d5ed77aaecd3cbbd4c5d4c1) to avoid brittle single-pixel masks. Third, test whether the selected 5000-pixel subset should be static or dynamically updated during the 10,000-query PROTES search, because [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) and [(Singh et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05ba68950ac4ca970cdd77f02bbeff7ff9f776d3) show attribution maps change under perturbation; a cheap option is to recompute or refresh the top-10% mask every fixed number of queries or after each amplitude restart. Fourth, strengthen the experimental baseline and reporting: compare TETRADAT against SimBA/SimBA+, Bandits, NES, PARSIMONIOUS, GMRF, Square, OnePixel, and Pixle under identical 10,000-query budgets, 1000 ImageNet images, untargeted objectives, and multiple seeds, reporting success rate, median/mean queries to success, L0/L1/L2/L-infinity norms, hardware, runtime, exact query accounting, and statistical significance, because [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21), [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f1), and [(Sahu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d4433228f30e6ec565e751c39f8948078802f186) show that dimensionality-reduced black-box attacks can already be highly query-efficient. Fifth, ablate the IG hyperparameters and selection fraction: 15 discretization nodes may be low relative to [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) which used M=100, so test 15, 50, and 100 steps, black versus mean-image baselines, and 5%, 10%, and 20% pixel fractions, while also tuning PROTES K=100, k=10, lambda=0.01, TT-rank r=5, and the multi-restart amplitude schedule. Sixth, evaluate adversarially trained targets separately and consider adding a warm-start or momentum component, such as TIMI in [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162), because auxiliary-guided coordinate selection alone may suffer from cold starts and local minima, and [(Uesato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4b434c3ab979ecdd71bbed894b34de77590c6dd) indicates adversarial training remains a hard case.

## Context: To balance exploration and exploitation under a query budget. Method: We will implement a multi-restart approach, beginning with large perturbation amplitudes and progressively reducing them by half until successful attacks are found or the query budget is exhausted. Each restart will initialize using the probability distribution learned in the previous iteration.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed multi-restart, decreasing-amplitude strategy comes from Square Attack, which uses a 10,000-query black-box budget on 1,000 ImageNet validation images and halves the patch-size percentage p at iterations 10, 50, 200, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, and 8,000 to move from coarse to fine search, achieving below 1 percent failure for untargeted l-inf and l2 attacks on Inception v3, ResNet-50, and VGG-16-BN and sometimes outperforming white-box PGD [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). This directly supports the research plan's 10,000-query ImageNet protocol and the idea that a decreasing search scale can be effective, although Square halves spatial patch size rather than perturbation amplitude and does not reuse a learned distribution. The next strongest support is for the learned probabilistic initialization component: NP-Attack uses a neural process to define a distribution over adversarial pixel values and, on ImageNet with l-inf bound 0.05, reaches 100 percent untargeted success with 867 queries and over 98 percent targeted success with 8,001 or 11,383 queries, showing that a learned distribution can be highly query efficient [(Bai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac9a851d7a257869dbfc0fcb03f04daa55bea92). This supports TETRADAT's PROTES/TT distribution and the intuition that a distribution learned from previous queries can guide later search, although NP-Attack optimizes the distribution continuously rather than restarting with halved amplitudes. Natural evolutionary strategies also support distribution-guided black-box search, maintaining a probabilistic distribution over perturbations under query-limited, partial-information, and label-only settings on 1,000 ImageNet images with epsilon 0.05 and a query cap such as 1,000,000 [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0). This is weaker for the proposed method because it is targeted and does not test amplitude halving, but it validates the core probabilistic-search premise. Sequential coarse-to-fine restarts are further supported by a block-wise FGSM-emulation method that restarts with finer partitions and reports 90.6 percent untargeted success on 990 MNIST images at 784 cumulative queries, close to 91.0 percent white-box FGSM, with mean 219.32 queries, and 68.6 percent success on 784 CIFAR-10 images at 3,072 queries with mean 185.47 queries [(Senzaki et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1ac93e7e81b7c7bb599bfbdfed9361ca14edf56). This supports restarts under a cumulative query budget, but it increases spatial resolution rather than reducing perturbation amplitude and does not carry over a learned distribution. TREMBA adds support for query-budgeted stochastic search and for reporting success rate as a function of query count, using a 50,000-query cap and comparing NES, Trans-NES, AutoZOOM, P-RGF, and Trans-P-RGF [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9). Weaker support comes from ZOO, which establishes a query-budgeted black-box coordinate-descent baseline with 128 coordinate evaluations per iteration and hardware details, and from Liu et al., whose white-box optimizer uses Adam with learning rate 4 for 100 iterations and produces RMSD 22.51 to 23.81 with cross-model transfer on 100 ILSVRC images [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) [(Liu et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e5a8c10cf92749d4a7c2949691c3a6046e499a). Older work provides only indirect support: Szegedy et al. show minimum-distortion perturbations and transfer across models but without query budgets or restarts [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad); Madry et al. use PGD restarts in white-box training and evaluation but do not address black-box query allocation [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386); Athalye et al. use bounded iterative optimization for 100 to 10,000 gradient iterations, which is comparable in scale but white-box [(Athalye et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651adaa058f821a890f2c5d1053d69eb481a8352); CorrAttack, Maho et al., Willmott et al., and Zheng et al. support query-budgeted probabilistic or benchmarked search but provide less direct evidence for the proposed halving schedule [(Huang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ba563894894267b1386b2d293e6dbe4b03807) [(Maho et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e574db0923c1d6e364c9b655ba7da831a9afd1d) [(Willmott et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d29b3c2163659fd34e548fdb80fd8001dec7db19) [(Zheng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d145dc61c9967f4529f18a4946fdf1eb6621ad08).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the provided related work directly validates the proposed combination of halving perturbation amplitude and reinitializing each restart from the previous learned distribution. Square Attack halves the patch-size percentage p, not the perturbation amplitude, and uses a single run with a fixed sampling schedule rather than multi-restart warm-starting [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). NP-Attack uses a learned distribution but keeps a fixed l-inf bound and continuously optimizes the neural-process parameters for each image, so it does not test whether a previous restart's distribution should be reused after amplitude reduction [(Bai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac9a851d7a257869dbfc0fcb03f04daa55bea92). NES similarly uses a fixed epsilon and a single probabilistic search rather than amplitude-halving restarts [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0). The block-wise restart method changes partition resolution, not perturbation magnitude, and therefore supports restarts only in a different sense [(Senzaki et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1ac93e7e81b7c7bb599bfbdfed9361ca14edf56). A second major contradiction concerns the proposed initial perturbation amplitude epsilon=1 in HSV. Most ImageNet black-box evaluations use much smaller l-inf bounds, such as 0.05 in Ilyas et al., NP-Attack, and CorrAttack, or 8/255 in the Square Attack line of work, while MNIST and CIFAR-10 work often uses 0.2 or 0.05 [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) [(Bai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac9a851d7a257869dbfc0fcb03f04daa55bea92) [(Huang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ba563894894267b1386b2d293e6dbe4b03807) [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). Starting at epsilon=1 may be far larger than the imperceptibility regime and may waste the 10,000-query budget on visibly large perturbations before reaching useful amplitudes such as 0.0625 or 0.03125. A third contradiction is query-budget allocation. The proposed method says it will halve until success or budget exhaustion, but it does not specify how many queries are spent per restart. Square Attack's schedule is embedded in one 10,000-query run, and Senzaki et al. accumulate queries across partition levels, so a naive implementation that gives each restart a large share of the budget could fail to reach small amplitudes [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) [(Senzaki et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1ac93e7e81b7c7bb599bfbdfed9361ca14edf56). A fourth concern is dimensionality and optimizer capacity. TETRADAT plans to optimize about 5,000 attribution-selected pixels with PROTES K=100 candidates, k=10 selections, learning rate 0.01, and TT rank 5 under 10,000 queries. Related black-box methods either operate on lower-dimensional patches, use coordinate budgets such as ZOO's 128 coordinates per iteration, or use random square patches whose size is explicitly controlled; none of the provided evidence shows that a TT/PROTES search over 5,000 pixels can be adequately explored with only 10,000 queries [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). Finally, because Square Attack already achieves below 1 percent failure at the same 10,000-query budget on ImageNet, the proposed method must demonstrate a clear advantage in query efficiency, distortion, or robustness; otherwise the related work contradicts the need for a new coarse-to-fine black-box attack on these settings [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). Weaker contradictions include the fact that older white-box restart evidence from Madry et al. does not transfer to black-box query budgets, and that several related works provide no quantitative outcomes, limiting their ability to support the proposed design [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) [(Zheng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d145dc61c9967f4529f18a4946fdf1eb6621ad08).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define an explicit per-restart query allocation and stopping rule for the 10,000-query budget, for example allocating fixed query blocks to amplitudes 1, 0.5, 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625, and 0.03125, or adopting a Square-style schedule that halves the search scale at predefined iterations within a single run [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). This should be ablated against no restarts, fixed amplitude, and amplitude halving with fresh PROTES initialization, so the paper can isolate whether warm-starting from the previous distribution actually helps. The second most important action is to justify or reduce the initial HSV epsilon=1 by reporting L1, L2, and visual quality metrics and by testing standard imperceptibility bounds such as 0.05 or 8/255, because related ImageNet black-box attacks succeed at much smaller bounds and NP-Attack reaches 100 percent untargeted success with 867 queries at l-inf 0.05 [(Bai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac9a851d7a257869dbfc0fcb03f04daa55bea92) [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0). The third action is to add stronger probabilistic black-box baselines and query-efficiency metrics, including Square, NES, NP-Attack, ZOO, TREMBA, and CorrAttack, and to report success rate versus query count, median or mean queries, failure rate, and confidence intervals rather than only final success at 10,000 queries [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9) [(Huang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ba563894894267b1386b2d293e6dbe4b03807) [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791). The fourth action is to validate the attribution-selected 5,000-pixel TT/PROTES design with ablations over pixel count, TT rank, K, k, and learning rate, and to compare against random pixel selection and Square's patch-size schedule, because 5,000 pixels is high-dimensional for a 10,000-query budget and K=100 candidates may allow only about 100 query iterations if each candidate is queried separately [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791). The fifth action is to follow benchmarking best practices from Zheng et al. and TREMBA by fixing correctly classified images, reporting query counting rules, using multiple seeds, and including defended models, so that the proposed method is evaluated under the same controlled conditions as prior black-box attacks [(Zheng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d145dc61c9967f4529f18a4946fdf1eb6621ad08) [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9). A less urgent but useful action is to include transferability or cross-model checks inspired by Szegedy et al. and Liu et al., because the research plan targets multiple architectures and such evidence would strengthen the claim that TETRADAT finds robust perturbations rather than model-specific artifacts [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) [(Liu et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e5a8c10cf92749d4a7c2949691c3a6046e499a).

## Context: To apply perturbations in a perceptually natural way. Method: For perturbation application, we will convert RGB pixel values to HSV color space and modify saturation (for decreases) or value (for increases) channels, which should produce more natural-looking adversarial examples.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Hosseini, and Poovendran, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48d88124dd89d988386ae452516dbba6c4a96e85), which directly uses HSV hue and saturation shifts in a query-based attack on CIFAR-10 VGG16 with at most 1000 trials per image, showing that structured color-space edits can be highly effective: pretrained VGG16 accuracy fell from 93.6% to 5.7% on color-shifted images, a worst-case robust VGG16 retained only 8.4% accuracy, targeted success was 35.4% on initially correct images, and more than 14% of images were misclassified after a single hue-only shift. This is the most direct evidence that HSV channel manipulation can produce adversarial examples under limited query access, although it does not test value-channel increases, ImageNet, or tensor-train optimization. The next strongest support is [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e5afc4eb4d9ad5281b94542f38ab716dc796854), which perturbs Lab a/b channels rather than HSV but demonstrates that non-RGB color-space perturbations can achieve high attack success and robustness, for example StarGAN ASR 100.0 with PSNR 5.292 and SSIM 0.251 at epsilon 0.05, AttGAN ASR 85.30 with PSNR 15.975 and SSIM 0.634, and Fader Network ASR 93.10 with PSNR 11.047 and SSIM 0.320, while also outperforming PGD and C&W under JPEG, blur, and MagDR in several settings. This supports the general idea that color-space edits can be more natural or robust than raw RGB perturbations, but it targets DeepFake disruption rather than black-box ImageNet classification. [(Zhao et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54c15364b5873e39257c221112fdf22299ed9e8a) provides methodological support for perceptual color constraints by adding a per-pixel DeltaE00 color-distance penalty to C&W and alternating loss on ImageNet-compatible 299x299 images with Inception V3, although the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes, so its support is weaker. [(Bhattad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e01fd005f5f1c2cf1b27c1ac8b014dfd3983da3) supports color-based adversarial manipulation on ImageNet ResNet-50 with transfer to DenseNet-121 and VGG-19 and robustness to JPEG quality 75, feature squeezing, and adversarial training, but the excerpt is qualitative and the method uses cluster-based colorization rather than HSV saturation/value edits. [(Xu et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9fec45e1ff97ffb0e0cf9f039e39b46043430301) supports the relevance of color-channel transformations by showing that color-depth reduction and smoothing alter classifier predictions and can be used for adversarial detection on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet, but it provides no quantitative attack success rates and is a defense rather than an attack. [(Wang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a17b0280066ae8b632194f7ff44d98028ff60b7d) supports the importance of perceptual quality by showing that spatial and frequency JND-aware perturbations improve multiSim3, NIQE, FSIMc, and MOS while keeping ASR comparable, for example FSA-DIM reached 50.5% ASR and FSIMc 0.921 versus DIM 49.5% ASR and FSIMc 0.816, and when visual quality was held comparable frequency variants improved ASR by 10.1% to 13.1%; this is relevant to the plan's visual-quality evaluation but does not test HSV edits. [(Modas et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf18d546ae7aea144663136c5704049953ce4e2) supports bounded perturbations by showing SparseFool with delta around 25 retained 100% fooling on MNIST and CIFAR-10, perturbed 0.21% of ImageNet pixels on average, and ran in 7 seconds per sample, indicating that small bounded changes can preserve effectiveness, although it uses RGB bounds rather than HSV. [(Evtimov et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d295a620fc10a7a656dc693e1b1bf668d1508a8e) supports natural-looking localized perturbations in physical settings, with 100% targeted success for a poster-printed Stop sign on LISA-CNN and 84.8% drive-by success for a camouflage art sticker, but it does not use HSV or black-box query optimization. The weakest support is [(Mirjalili et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1c30907f2a675c1e19c1cd0c6c2eee98ef9b4ed), which uses perceptual constraints in face autoencoder perturbations but provides no HSV, black-box, ImageNet, or quantitative evidence relevant to the proposed method.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Bhattad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e01fd005f5f1c2cf1b27c1ac8b014dfd3983da3), which reports a trade-off in which more realistic colorization, achieved with more color clusters or hints, decreases transferability and defense robustness; this directly challenges the assumption that making HSV saturation/value perturbations more natural will automatically preserve or improve attack effectiveness. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e5afc4eb4d9ad5281b94542f38ab716dc796854) uses Lab a/b channels, a perceptually uniform color space, and obtains strong quantitative results against DeepFake models, suggesting that HSV saturation/value edits may be less perceptually well-founded than Lab-based edits, although the task is not black-box ImageNet classification. [(Zhao et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54c15364b5873e39257c221112fdf22299ed9e8a) also contradicts the simplicity of HSV channel editing by using a per-pixel DeltaE00 perceptual color-distance objective, implying that perceived color change should be constrained explicitly rather than assumed to be natural from saturation decreases or value increases; however, the excerpt lacks numerical results, so this is a methodological rather than empirical contradiction. [(Wang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a17b0280066ae8b632194f7ff44d98028ff60b7d) suggests that spatial and frequency JND-aware masking may be more effective for perceptual naturalness than global HSV channel edits, because structure-aware variants improved FSIMc, NIQE, multiSim3, and MOS while maintaining or improving ASR, and because ImageNet transfer ASR remained modest at 27.8% to 50.5%, cautioning that perceptual naturalness does not guarantee high black-box success. [(Dong et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44d43bfbd23d55b1e7c4c4fd91fe101c0eaf1a06) indicates that spatial smoothing and translation-invariant gradient shaping can improve transferability against defense models, which may be more important than color-space naturalness for the plan's adversarial-model evaluation, although it does not test HSV. [(Modas et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf18d546ae7aea144663136c5704049953ce4e2) provides a weaker contradiction by showing that simple bounded RGB sparse perturbations can achieve 100% fooling with very small delta and high sparsity, suggesting that an HSV restriction may add complexity without clear benefit unless it improves visual quality or query efficiency. [(Hosseini, and Poovendran, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48d88124dd89d988386ae452516dbba6c4a96e85) also contains a limitation that contradicts the proposed narrow channel rule: hue-only shifts were already effective, with more than 14% of images misclassified after one hue shift, so restricting TETRADAT to saturation decreases and value increases may omit a highly effective HSV dimension. [(Xu et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9fec45e1ff97ffb0e0cf9f039e39b46043430301) weakly contradicts the method by showing that color-depth transformations can expose adversarial examples through feature squeezing, so HSV saturation/value edits may be detectable if they alter color statistics, although the excerpt lacks quantitative detection accuracy. The remaining works, [(Evtimov et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d295a620fc10a7a656dc693e1b1bf668d1508a8e) and [(Mirjalili et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b1c30907f2a675c1e19c1cd0c6c2eee98ef9b4ed), do not provide strong contradictions because they do not test HSV saturation/value edits in the proposed black-box ImageNet setting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to broaden and ablate the HSV perturbation space rather than fixing it to saturation decreases and value increases, because [(Hosseini, and Poovendran, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48d88124dd89d988386ae452516dbba6c4a96e85) shows hue and saturation shifts are effective under limited queries, and the proposed asymmetric rule may underperform; the plan should test hue shifts, saturation increases and decreases, value increases and decreases, and RGB baselines under the same 10,000-query budget on the 1000 ImageNet images. Second, the plan should add explicit perceptual color-quality metrics, such as DeltaE00, PSNR, SSIM, NIQE, FSIMc, multiSim3, and MOS, because [(Zhao et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54c15364b5873e39257c221112fdf22299ed9e8a), [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e5afc4eb4d9ad5281b94542f38ab716dc796854), and [(Wang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a17b0280066ae8b632194f7ff44d98028ff60b7d) show that perceptual color difference and structure-aware quality metrics are needed to validate naturalness; the current plan's visual quality assessment is too vague. Third, TETRADAT should constrain HSV edits with a perceptual color-distance or Lab-based baseline, for example by comparing HSV saturation/value edits against Lab a/b edits and by reporting whether HSV edits reduce DeltaE00 or improve FSIMc/NIQE at equal ASR, because [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e5afc4eb4d9ad5281b94542f38ab716dc796854) and [(Zhao et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54c15364b5873e39257c221112fdf22299ed9e8a) suggest HSV may not be the best perceptual color representation. Fourth, the method should combine HSV edits with the existing attribution-guided pixel selection and, if possible, spatial or frequency JND masking, because [(Wang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a17b0280066ae8b632194f7ff44d98028ff60b7d) and [(Evtimov et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d295a620fc10a7a656dc693e1b1bf668d1508a8e) show that localized or structure-aware perturbations improve perceptual quality and can maintain success; this would make the HSV edits less global and more natural. Fifth, the evaluation should include defenses and robustness checks relevant to color perturbations, including feature squeezing with color-depth reduction and spatial smoothing, JPEG compression at quality 75, and the planned adversarial Inception and Inception-ResNet models, because [(Xu et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9fec45e1ff97ffb0e0cf9f039e39b46043430301) and [(Bhattad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e01fd005f5f1c2cf1b27c1ac8b014dfd3983da3) show that color transformations and compression can affect attack success and that naturalness may trade off with robustness. Sixth, the plan should report perturbation bounds in HSV and RGB, including maximum saturation decrease, maximum value increase, L1 and L2 norms, and query efficiency, and should ablate bounds analogous to [(Modas et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf18d546ae7aea144663136c5704049953ce4e2) delta values to determine whether small HSV changes preserve ASR. Seventh, the plan should include color-specific baselines, such as a query-based HSV random search similar to [(Hosseini, and Poovendran, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48d88124dd89d988386ae452516dbba6c4a96e85) and, if feasible, a Lab or colorization baseline, in addition to OnePixel, Square, and Pixle, to isolate the benefit of HSV editing from the benefit of TT optimization. Eighth, the plan should add statistical rigor by using multiple random seeds, reporting confidence intervals or significance tests, and analyzing results per architecture, because many related works report single-run results without significance tests. Finally, the implementation should document HSV conversion, clipping, channel constraints, and avoid value increases that cause overexposure or unrealistic brightness, with visual examples and quantitative quality metrics for each target model.

## Context: To provide a standardized benchmark. Method: We will use the ImageNet dataset with one image per class (1000 total).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 1000-image, one-image-per-class ImageNet benchmark is [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87), which explicitly uses the same ImageNet-compatible dataset with 1000 images and 1000 unique class labels, evaluates Inc-v3 as source with ResNet-152 and VGG-16 targets, reports linear CKA values of 0.3769/0.4407 for clean features, 0.2066/0.2182 without SVD, and 0.2273/0.2447 with SVD, and completes 1000 images in 1029 seconds on a 2080Ti with 25-30% added cost; this directly supports the plan's standardized benchmark, target-model family, and computational feasibility. The next strongest support is [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), which evaluates Square Attack on 1000 ImageNet validation images with a 10,000-query budget, untargeted L-infinity and L2 settings, and reports failure rates below 1% for Inception v3, ResNet-50, and VGG-16-BN; this validates the plan's 1000-image scale, 10,000-query budget, untargeted objective, and Square baseline. Iteratively, [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) established a 1000-image ImageNet black-box targeted protocol with InceptionV3, L-infinity 0.05, and a 1,000,000-query cap, while the newer [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f) reduced the protocol to 10,000 oracle queries for untargeted attacks on 10,000 random ImageNet images and showed bandit methods were 6x less failure-prone than NES in L-infinity and 3x in L2, using about half as many queries; this supports the plan's 10,000-query budget and untargeted framing, though it uses a larger random set rather than one per class. [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) further supports a 1000-image ImageNet validation benchmark by filtering for initially correct classification, using 10,000 untargeted queries, reporting median query counts of 944 for SimBA and 582 for SimBA-DCT, and showing Inception v3 can require more than 10,000 queries for some images, which is directly relevant to the plan's target models and query budget. [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51) supports the same 1000-image/10,000-query scale with 1000 correctly classified ImageNet test images, L-infinity epsilon=0.05, ResNet-50 and Inception V3, and NVIDIA 2080 Ti hardware, making the proposed benchmark reproducible. [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9) supports the 10,000-query budget on ImageNet with Inception v3 and ResNet-50, comparing NES, ZS, Bandit, and Square, and reports that increasing attacker noise from 0.01 to 0.05 raises L-infinity failure rates for NES and Bandit from 5.1%/5.0% to 22.8%/25.7%, which is useful for interpreting robustness. [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) supports the 1000-image ImageNet scale and the plan's Inception/Inception-ResNet/ResNet-152 model family by using 1000 validation images correctly classified by seven models, including Inception v3, Inception v4, Inception ResNet v2, and ResNet v2-152, with L-infinity epsilon=16, 10 iterations, and MI-FGSM black-box transfer success of 37.6%, 40.3%, and 23.3% for hold-out adversarial ensembles. Older architecture work by [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) and [(Szegedy et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c26ab8767d046cb6e32d959fdf726aee89bb62) supports the target models' ImageNet training context, reporting top-1 accuracies of 75.2/76.4/76.6 for receptive-field variants and residual Inception improvements, with 50 and 20 NVIDIA Kepler GPUs, RMSProp, learning rate 0.045, and decay 0.94 every two epochs. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f) for a 1000-instance attack evaluation protocol with success probability and mean distortion over successes, [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) for black-box transfer evaluation, [(He et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3636d3f0562f3ab5f5df68c1c9a23530c0fbce64) for adversarial robustness and black-box simulation on CIFAR-10, [(Moon et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d26aa7cb2cba8f172b4299c61835509b1090ecd) for query-limited hierarchical search on Cifar-10 and ImageNet with 20,000 queries and block size 32, [(Cheng et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edabab811aaf44b7b626efe5278a32bddd3bb77f) for 1000 random ImageNet images and prior-guided gradient estimation, [(Croce, and Hein, 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91a05cb84f1c7dbb0354da2ff11ae92549152435) for ImageNet-scale attack benchmarking with runtime details, [(Suya et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03d0978de148862e3bf7027110940212d1aa93da) for 100-seed ImageNet query-cost evaluation, [(Croce, and Hein, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18939eadc9c4460c8385e0591cde214a1ead067b) for 1000 ImageNet validation points and Square Attack as a strong black-box component, [(Maho et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffeffcd2dac0525319dcff8136188bc10a11f50e) and [(Long et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8680c075abfb7832ff1321b5f409f2a5e57570f2) for 1000-image ImageNet evaluation sets, and [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b) for query-budgeted ImageNet black-box attacks with 2K/5K/10K/20K budgets.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the one-image-per-class construction is not the dominant ImageNet black-box protocol; most comparable 1000-image studies use random or correctly classified subsets, which may better reflect the validation distribution. [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f) uses 10,000 randomly selected ImageNet images, [(Cheng et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edabab811aaf44b7b626efe5278a32bddd3bb77f) uses 1000 randomly selected validation images, [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) uses 1000 initially correctly classified validation images without class balancing, [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51) uses 1000 correctly classified test images, [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b) uses 250 correctly classified images, and [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9) and [(Long et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8680c075abfb7832ff1321b5f409f2a5e57570f2) do not confirm one-per-class sampling. Although [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87) validates the one-per-class set, the broader literature suggests the proposed benchmark may overrepresent classes and reduce comparability with prior 1000-image results. The next major contradiction is the dual correctness filter: the plan attacks only images correctly classified by both the target model and the VGG-19 attribution model, whereas related work typically filters only for target correctness, e.g., [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21), [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51), and [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b). No provided work shows that requiring attribution-model correctness improves benchmark validity; it may shrink the effective sample, especially for weaker targets such as AlexNet or MobileNet V3, and introduce class imbalance. A third important contradiction is that 10,000 queries may be insufficient for the plan's adversarially trained models. [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) shows black-box transfer success to adversarial ensembles is far below white-box success, e.g., MI-FGSM 37.6% versus 99.6% for Inception v3 ens3 and 23.3% versus 99.7% for Inception ResNet v2; [(Suya et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03d0978de148862e3bf7027110940212d1aa93da) finds robust-model improvements from candidate initialization are not significant; [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b) shows defenses reduce average attack success from 87.9% at 20K queries to 10.0% with Gaussian sigma=0.01 and 29.5% with sigma=0.001; and [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9) shows noise increases failure rates. Thus the plan's fixed 10,000-query budget may understate difficulty for Adversarial Inception and Adversarial Inception-ResNet. Fourth, attribution-guided pixel selection is not directly supported for black-box ImageNet attacks. [(Cheng et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edabab811aaf44b7b626efe5278a32bddd3bb77f) shows transfer gradients are useful early but become less useful over iterations, and [(Suya et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03d0978de148862e3bf7027110940212d1aa93da) reports that local candidates can be worse than original seeds for robust models, with 269 of 1000 local candidates worse for robust CIFAR10 AutoZOOM. Meanwhile, [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) shows Square Attack, a simple random square search over the full image, achieves below 1% failure on 1000 ImageNet images with 10,000 queries, suggesting attribution may not be necessary and may fail if VGG-19 saliency misaligns with target or adversarial models. Fifth, the HSV perturbation strategy and visual-quality metric lack support; none of the cited black-box ImageNet studies evaluates HSV saturation/value modifications or reports a standardized visual-quality instrument, while they report L1/L2/L-infinity norms, query counts, and success rates, e.g., [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) reports average L2-norm progression and [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51) uses L-infinity epsilon=0.05. Sixth, model coverage is uneven: the strongest black-box 1000/10k evidence centers on Inception-v3, ResNet-50, VGG-16, DenseNet, and ResNet-152, e.g., [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f), [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21), and [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51), but there is little direct evidence for AlexNet, GoogleNet, or MobileNet V3 under the same query budget. Seventh, statistical rigor is often missing: [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) does not report significance tests, [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9) reports no confidence intervals, and [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) excerpt lacks exact query counts, so the proposed 1000-image benchmark should not be treated as sufficient without confidence intervals and paired comparisons. Finally, computational scale is a concern: the plan compares TETRADAT against OnePixel, Square, and Pixle on seven target models, implying up to 28 attack runs over 1000 images and 10,000 queries, whereas related work typically evaluates fewer models or attacks, e.g., [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) evaluates three models and [(Croce, and Hein, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18939eadc9c4460c8385e0591cde214a1ead067b) uses 5000 Square queries within AutoAttack.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define and release the exact 1000-image one-per-class set, including class IDs, image IDs, resolution, normalization, and preprocessing, and justify it by comparing results on a random 1000-image subset and, if feasible, a 10,000-image subset following [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87) and [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f); this will address the strongest contradiction about class balance and comparability. Second, report the number of images retained after the dual target/attribution correctness filter for each model, and make target-correct-only the primary benchmark with attribution-correct-only as a sensitivity analysis, because related work standardly uses target correctness only, as in [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21), [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), and [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51). Third, specify the threat model and perturbation constraint, e.g., L2 radius 5.0 as in [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b) or L-infinity epsilon=0.05 as in [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0) and [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51), and report L1/L2/L-infinity norms, median/mean queries, failure rates, and ASR-versus-query curves at 1K, 5K, 10K, and 20K, because 10K may be insufficient for adversarial models per [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), [(Byun et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3e6ba0d027c92937b12d688a81c1b888102e14b), and [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9). Fourth, add statistical reporting: Wilson confidence intervals for ASR, paired tests across methods on the same images, and multiple-comparison correction across seven models and four attacks, since prior work often lacks significance, e.g., [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21) and [(Qin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07b65c99f615ad2333eb427a5c3da204725939d9). Fifth, ablate attribution-guided pixel selection against random 10% pixels, target-model saliency if available, Square Attack's adaptive square sizes from [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), Moon's hierarchical blocks from [(Moon et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d26aa7cb2cba8f172b4299c61835509b1090ecd), and transfer-gradient priors from [(Cheng et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edabab811aaf44b7b626efe5278a32bddd3bb77f), with sensitivity to 5%, 10%, and 20% pixels and IG steps of 10/15/20, to test whether VGG-19 Integrated Gradients actually improves query efficiency on standard and adversarial models. Sixth, evaluate the HSV perturbation strategy by comparing HSV saturation/value edits to RGB edits under identical L1/L2 constraints and objective visual-quality metrics such as PSNR/SSIM plus optional human evaluation, because no cited work supports HSV or visual-quality claims. Seventh, align baseline implementations and query counting with prior work: use Square Attack with 10,000 queries as in [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) and also report 5,000-query Square as in [(Croce, and Hein, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18939eadc9c4460c8385e0591cde214a1ead067b) for comparability; document OnePixel and Pixle hyperparameters, stopping criteria, and whether queries are score-based or hard-label, following the query definitions in [(Ilyas et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10ab21b120e305b6d3cbf81c5a906d36521152f) and [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51). Eighth, report clean accuracy, target model versions, framework versions, and hardware, using 2080Ti-class runtimes as in [(Chen, and Gu, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123877109e4a203b0db35984aa4808583573ab51) and [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87), and estimate total query/runtime for 28 attack runs to ensure reproducibility. Ninth, if compute allows, add ResNet-50 or DenseNet-121 because they are well-supported in prior 1000-image black-box studies, e.g., [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) and [(Guo et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65fd9ded2c411d90bcf6d38132463797754d2d21), and include adversarial models with reported robustness training details to contextualize the lower success rates expected from [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) and [(Croce, and Hein, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18939eadc9c4460c8385e0591cde214a1ead067b).

## Context: To evaluate across diverse vision models. Method: Target models will include five standard architectures (AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152) and two adversarially trained models (Adversarial Inception, Adversarial Inception-ResNet).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed target-model method is that the exact seven-model suite has already been used as a coherent ImageNet attack benchmark: [(Lin et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb0568dcb546bbb4d7d1ed71ce395f4b66691003) evaluates SI-NI-FGSM on 1000 ILSVRC 2012 validation images, one per class, selected as almost correctly classified by all testing models, and explicitly uses AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, Adversarial Inception, and Adversarial Inception-ResNet, reporting 99.9% attack success rate on both the normally trained and adversarially trained model sets; this directly validates the proposed five standard plus two adversarial target set and the one-image-per-class ImageNet protocol, although it is white-box and does not impose the proposed 10,000-query black-box budget. A slightly weaker but still direct support is that the same one-per-class ImageNet protocol and overlapping Inception/ResNet/adversarial targets have been used to show that adversarial Inception-family models are not impregnable: [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) uses 1000 correctly classified ILSVRC 2012 validation images, one per class, L-infinity perturbation 16, 10 MI-FGSM iterations, momentum decay 1.0, and logit-fused ensembles, achieving 87.9% transfer to held-out Inception v3 from an ensemble of Inception v4, Inception ResNet v2, and ResNet-152, and 37.6%, 40.3%, and 23.3% transfer to held-out adversarial Inception v3 ens3, adversarial Inception v3 ens4, and adversarial Inception ResNet v2 ens, respectively, compared with 18.6%, 18.7%, and 9.9% for I-FGSM; this supports the plan's inclusion of Adversarial Inception and Adversarial Inception-ResNet as meaningful but harder targets. Newer transfer-attack work reinforces the same evaluation design: [(Wang, and He, 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7301d58eb8a750e44b7da5ab0266b134d1651237) uses 1000 clean ILSVRC 2012 validation images, one per class, and VMI/VNI-FGSM with N=20 and beta=3/2 to attack Inception V3, Inception V4, Inception-ResNet-v2, ResNet-101, and adversarial/ensemble models; [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96ea0658b9813706834966433107f9343e8389db) uses 1000 randomly sampled ILSVRC 2012 validation images, an RTX 2080Ti, learning rate 0.0025, maximum perturbation 16 from 15 iterations and step size 1.07, and reports DANAA improvements of 9.0% over NAA and 7.1% average on adversarial models across InceptionV3, InceptionV4, InceptionResnetV2, and ResNet-v2-152; and [(Zhu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70f8ac80b56dea5a3335879d42ddb2d38aa84754) uses 1000 randomly selected ILSVRC 2012 validation images with Inception-v3, Inception-v4, Inception-ResNet-v2, and ResNet-152 sources and three adversarial models, reporting 12.2% average ASR improvement over NAA and 26.9% on adversarial models. These newer studies support the proposed 1000-image, diverse-architecture, standard-plus-adversarial evaluation, but they remain gradient-based transfer attacks rather than query-budget black-box TT attacks. Attribution-guided perturbation is also supported, though less directly: [(Zhang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/411b07870690a9492aec0331e07ede019f3d6814) uses neuron attribution as a path integral of output gradients, achieves nearly 100% white-box accuracy, improves black-box transfer by 10.5% against adversarially trained models and 10.7% with PIM/DIM, and reaches 57.9% average success against advanced defenses using Inception V3 as source; [(Zhu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4a9fc9153fe4a43f07d0d3c80cf01474c97e0df) uses Integrated Gradients to choose integerization directions and reports success rates from 0.9534 to 0.9591 across learning rates 0.001 to 0.02 and 0.8280 to 0.9944 across thresholds 0.5 to 5 on CIFAR-100/ImageNet with VGG16-BN and C&W, supporting the plan's use of Integrated Gradients for pixel-level guidance, although it does not test the proposed target suite or 10,000-query budget. Weaker but relevant contextual support comes from the original target-model papers and benchmark definitions: [(Szegedy et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e15cf50aa89fee8535703b9f9512fca5bfc43327) establishes GoogLeNet on ILSVRC 2014 with 1.2M training images, 50,000 validation images, 100,000 test images, top-5 error 6.67%, and 144-crop/7-model averaging; [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) establishes Inception-v3 with 299x299 top-1 76.6% on 48,238 non-blacklisted validation images, RMSProp learning rate 0.045, decay 0.94 every two epochs, and multi-crop/ensemble evaluation; [(Szegedy et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c26ab8767d046cb6e32d959fdf726aee89bb62) provides standard Inception-ResNet training context with 20 TensorFlow replicas, RMSProp learning rate 0.045, and blacklisted-image sensitivity of about 0.3 top-1 points, but it is not the adversarial target; [(Howard et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e19eba1e6644f7c83f607383d256deea71f87ae) establishes MobileNetV3 with 4x4 TPU Pod training, RMSProp momentum 0.9, batch size 4096, and Pixel latency measurements; [(Russakovsky et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e74f9b7f8eec6ba4704c206b93bc8079af3da4bd) defines the 1000-class ImageNet benchmark with approximately 1.28M training images, 50,000 validation images, 100,000 test images, and GoogLeNet error analysis; and [(Tan, and Le, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f2eda8077dc7a69bb2b4e0a1a086cf054adb3f9) provides standard ImageNet benchmark context for ResNet-152 comparisons, including EfficientNet-B1 running 5.7x faster than ResNet-152. The weakest support is from studies that use only part of the protocol or do not report quantitative attack outcomes: [(Guo et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e225dd59ef4954db21479cdcbee497624b2d6d0f) uses ImageNet 1.2M training and 50,000 test images with ResNet-50, FGSM, I-FGSM M=10, DeepFool M=5, CW-L2 kappa=0 and lambda_f=10, and black-box transformed-input evaluation, but reports no quantitative outcomes; [(Long et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8680c075abfb7832ff1321b5f409f2a5e57570f2) uses 1000 images at 299x299x3 and Inception V3/V4/Inception-ResNet v2/ResNet-152 but provides no quantitative outcomes in the excerpt; [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87) uses a 1000-image ImageNet-Compatible set, Inception V3 source, ResNet-152/VGG-16 CKA analysis, and a 2080Ti GPU with 1029 seconds for 1000 images, but does not cover the full proposed target set; [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea) supports feature-importance-guided transfer attacks with 9.5% average improvement and 12.8% against defenses, but omits AlexNet, GoogleNet, and MobileNet V3; [(Wong et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d4a87759917132913319960389f17fa1fe8b630) supports FGSM adversarial training on ImageNet ResNet50 with four RTX 2080Ti GPUs and PGD evaluation with 10 restarts and 50 iterations, but does not evaluate the proposed target suite; [(Salman et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17293cd36ee5e7ec37dcec1d5ab85f9b77ad65d5) supports robust ImageNet model behavior with epsilon=3 across six architectures, but does not report attack success, query counts, or perturbation norms; [(Cohen et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7f73185e3975bb62a3c42b2ba6bd4db57fee8ed) supports measuring perturbation distance with a ResNet-50 smoothed classifier, sigma=0.25, alpha=0.01, and PGD success of 17% at 1.5R and 53% at 2R, but is white-box and single-target; and [(Liao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca9c1224636b0a7dd37340a4691c34a9914b5af8) supports ImageNet adversarial evaluation with ensV3, 30K clean images, 210K adversarial images, and black-box holdout Inception V4 attacks at perturbation levels 4 and 16, but focuses on defenses rather than the proposed attack.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed Adversarial Inception-ResNet target may be substantially harder than a standard adversarially trained checkpoint if it includes the competition-style randomization inference protocol: [(Kurakin et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca5642f522cd2cd44948c7e9f337c91e5f26fdcf) evaluates an adversarial Inception-ResNet-v2 ensemble with resizing range [310,331), padding to 331x331x3, 30 randomization patterns, flip probability 0.5, and batch limit 100 images in 500 seconds, achieving a normalized defense score of 0.924 against 156 attacks and ranking 2nd among 107 defenses, while ensemble adversarial training alone scored 0.773 and the highest worst-case defense score was only 53.6%; this directly contradicts the assumption that a 10,000-query black-box attack will reliably succeed on Adversarial Inception-ResNet, especially if the target uses randomization or test-time augmentation. A closely related contradiction is that adversarial Inception-family models can exhibit gradient masking, which undermines any method that assumes attribution or gradient-like signals are well aligned with the target loss: [(Tramèr et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/136dee73f203df2f4831994bf4f0c0a4ad2e764e) trains Inception v3 and Inception ResNet v2 with single-step least-likely-class perturbations bounded by 16/256, then evaluates 1000 random test points against an iterative lower bound, finding that the single-step attack reaches only 7% of the iterative loss for adversarial Inception v3 and 8% for adversarial Inception ResNet v2, versus 19% and 17% for standard models, and that cosine similarity between single-step and multi-step perturbations drops from 0.13 to 0.02 for Inception v3 while average gradient norm rises from 0.10 to 0.17; although TETRADAT uses VGG-19 attribution rather than target gradients, this evidence contradicts the broader premise that attribution-guided pixel selection will transfer cleanly to adversarial Inception targets. This newer finding builds on and partially contradicts the earlier adversarial-training recipe in [(Kurakin et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a85a6766b982ff7c8980e57ca6342d22493827), which trained Inception v3 with one-step least-likely-class examples after a 10,000-iteration clean delay, reduced clean top-1 accuracy from 78.4% to 77.6%, raised adversarial top-1 accuracy from 30.8% to 73.5% at epsilon=2, and reported that one-step adversarial training did not confer robustness to iterative examples; the contradiction is not that adversarial targets are useless, but that their robustness is highly dependent on the attack class, and a black-box TT attack must be validated against iterative or adaptive attacks rather than assumed to behave like a one-step attack. Additional contradictions concern the evaluation protocol and target inference settings. [(Szegedy et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e15cf50aa89fee8535703b9f9512fca5bfc43327) shows that GoogLeNet's reported ILSVRC 2014 top-5 error of 6.67% relied on 144 crops per image and averaging over seven models, so attacking a single 224x224 or 299x299 input may not represent the deployed model's robustness; [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) similarly evaluates Inception-v3 with multi-crop and ensemble settings and reports 76.6% top-1 for the 299x299 configuration, implying that input resolution, cropping, and ensembling can materially change attack difficulty. [(Liao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca9c1224636b0a7dd37340a4691c34a9914b5af8) further contradicts the assumption that adversarial Inception targets are easy by using ensV3 as a strong ImageNet defense and generating black-box attacks from a holdout Inception V4 model at perturbation levels 4 and 16, with 40K white-box and black-box test images derived from 10K clean validation images. Newer benchmark evidence also cautions against overinterpreting robust-model performance: [(Croce et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2aab97e35c43d961d645e650808d5b052ec180ab) finds that many published CIFAR-10 l-infinity defenses have overestimated robust accuracy under AutoAttack, that robust models still suffer clean-accuracy degradation, and that uncalibrated expected calibration error can reach 70% for one model and average 21% versus 4% for standard models; although this is CIFAR-10 rather than ImageNet, it supports the need to report adaptive, norm-controlled attack success rather than nominal robustness. Finally, the related work provides little direct evidence that a 10,000-query black-box budget is sufficient for ImageNet-scale 1000-class attacks: the strongest attack-success evidence on the exact target set comes from white-box SI-NI-FGSM in [(Lin et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb0568dcb546bbb4d7d1ed71ce395f4b66691003), and the strongest adversarial-target transfer evidence comes from gradient-based MI-FGSM in [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), neither of which uses query budgets, tensor-train optimization, HSV perturbations, or the proposed OnePixel/Square/Pixle comparison; this is a gap rather than a direct contradiction, but it weakens confidence that the proposed 10,000-query protocol will produce comparable success rates.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify and freeze the exact target checkpoints and inference pipelines for all seven models, especially whether Adversarial Inception and Adversarial Inception-ResNet use single-input inference, multi-crop, ensembling, or the randomization protocol from [(Kurakin et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca5642f522cd2cd44948c7e9f337c91e5f26fdcf), because that protocol achieved a 0.924 normalized defense score and can change attack success dramatically; the plan should report input size, preprocessing, number of crops, ensemble size, and whether predictions are averaged, mirroring the 144-crop/7-model GoogLeNet protocol in [(Szegedy et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e15cf50aa89fee8535703b9f9512fca5bfc43327) and the Inception-v3 multi-crop/ensemble protocol in [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58). Second, the query budget should be treated as a variable rather than a fixed 10,000-query claim: report attack success rate versus query count for TETRADAT, OnePixel, Square, and Pixle, and include a higher-budget ablation, because the related work that reports high success on the same target set uses white-box gradients or transfer attacks without query constraints, such as [(Lin et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb0568dcb546bbb4d7d1ed71ce395f4b66691003) and [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), while the strongest black-box-style defense evidence shows adversarial Inception-ResNet can remain robust under many attacks. Third, validate the VGG-19 Integrated Gradients attribution against the actual targets, especially adversarial Inception and Inception-ResNet, by comparing VGG-19-selected pixels with target-specific Integrated Gradients, neuron attribution, or feature-importance maps, because [(Tramèr et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/136dee73f203df2f4831994bf4f0c0a4ad2e764e) shows gradient masking in adversarial Inception-family models, while [(Zhang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/411b07870690a9492aec0331e07ede019f3d6814) and [(Zhu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4a9fc9153fe4a43f07d0d3c80cf01474c97e0df) show that attribution can improve attacks when it is aligned with the model being attacked. Fourth, add white-box or transferable upper-bound baselines such as MI-FGSM, SI-NI-FGSM, DANAA, or GE-AdvGAN, using the same 1000-image one-per-class ImageNet protocol, so that TETRADAT's black-box performance can be interpreted against methods that already achieve high success on overlapping target sets, including [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), [(Lin et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb0568dcb546bbb4d7d1ed71ce395f4b66691003), [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96ea0658b9813706834966433107f9343e8389db), and [(Zhu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70f8ac80b56dea5a3335879d42ddb2d38aa84754). Fifth, make the image-selection protocol explicit and report the number of images that satisfy the requirement of being correctly classified by both target and VGG-19, because [(Lin et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb0568dcb546bbb4d7d1ed71ce395f4b66691003) selected images almost correctly classified by all testing models and [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) used 1000 correctly classified images, one per class; if fewer than 1000 images remain, the plan should state whether it substitutes images or reports per-class coverage. Sixth, align perturbation metrics with the literature and with the HSV modification strategy: report L-infinity as well as L1 and L2, because most related ImageNet transfer attacks use L-infinity budgets such as 16/255 in [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b), [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96ea0658b9813706834966433107f9343e8389db), and [(Zhu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70f8ac80b56dea5a3335879d42ddb2d38aa84754), and include perceptual metrics such as PSNR or SSIM alongside visual quality. Seventh, include statistical reporting for stochastic black-box attacks, such as multiple random seeds, confidence intervals, or paired significance tests, because several related works report average improvements without significance tests, for example [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96ea0658b9813706834966433107f9343e8389db) and [(Zhu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70f8ac80b56dea5a3335879d42ddb2d38aa84754), and the proposed PROTES sampling with K=100, k=10, lambda=0.01, and TT-rank r=5 is stochastic. Finally, if the goal is to compare against established black-box attacks, include query-normalized comparisons and compute cost, because related work such as [(Zhu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70f8ac80b56dea5a3335879d42ddb2d38aa84754) reports frames per second and perturbation rate, and [(Weng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ed5ffcfc588a879a00476e8ac4599b92232ce87) reports a 25-30% computational overhead and 1029 seconds for 1000 images on a 2080Ti GPU, showing that computational efficiency should be reported alongside attack success.

## Context: To supply attribution maps for pixel selection. Method: VGG-19 will serve as the auxiliary model for attribution computation.

### Support

The strongest support for the specific choice of VGG-19 is that VGG-19 is a strong ImageNet auxiliary classifier and can produce spatially resolved class score maps. [(Simonyan, and Zisserman, 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb42cf88027de515750f230b23b1a057dc782108) reports the 19-layer network E with 16 convolutional layers and 3 fully connected layers, channel widths increasing from 64 to 512, achieving 25.5% top-1 and 8.0% top-5 validation error at Q=384, 24.8% top-1 and 7.5% top-5 with multi-scale Q={256,384,512}, and 7.3% test top-5 error, and it uses dense fully convolutional inference before sum pooling, which is compatible with extracting spatial maps. This directly supports using VGG-19 as a white-box auxiliary model, but the paper does not evaluate Integrated Gradients or adversarial pixel selection. The next strongest support is that Integrated Gradients is a standard differentiable attribution method suitable for VGG-19. [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) defines attributions as path integrals of gradients from a baseline to the input, approximated by finite gradient evaluations, and applies it to object recognition, but the excerpt is qualitative and does not report step-count sensitivity, attribution accuracy, or attack outcomes, so it supports the mechanism rather than the plan's 15-step, 15-node, 10%-pixel configuration. The next support is that auxiliary spatial maps can improve black-box pixel attacks on ImageNet. [(Zhou et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1ad26fb31beaf63961b3fb20df26ed9c5ad7103) uses YOLOv4 and HVPNet masks to restrict perturbations, achieving 99.70%, 99.70%, 97.50%, and 94.70% success on ResNet50, ResNet18, VGG16, and Inception V3 with average queries 1465, 1118, 1559, and 3573 and average L2 norms 4.04, 3.80, 3.63, and 6.39, and it reduces SimBA average queries by 8.62% and 18.97% for VGG16 on ImageNet and COCO-Reduced-ImageNet; this supports the TETRADAT premise that an auxiliary map can reduce the perturbation search space, but it does not validate VGG-19 or Integrated Gradients. Weaker support comes from earlier auxiliary-model and dimensionality-reduction attacks: [(Papernot et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53b047e503f4c24602f376a774d653f7ed56c024) shows a substitute model can guide black-box perturbations on MNIST and GTSRB, with transferability rising from 14.70% to 55.53% as distortion increased from 7.14% to 28.57%, and [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) proposes ZOO with hierarchical pixel prioritization on MNIST, CIFAR, and ImageNet, but neither uses VGG-19, Integrated Gradients, or reports the 10,000-query ImageNet outcomes needed to validate the proposed method.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the newest and closest quantitative evidence for auxiliary-map pixel selection does not use VGG-19 or Integrated Gradients. [(Zhou et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1ad26fb31beaf63961b3fb20df26ed9c5ad7103) obtains its query and distortion gains from YOLOv4 object regions and HVPNet salient masks, with P_t=0.3 and perturbation parameter 3, L2 perturbations, and target models ResNet50, ResNet18, VGG16, and Inception V3; it does not test VGG-19 attribution scores, top-10% pixel selection, HSV saturation/value edits, PROTES, or the plan's 10,000-query budget, so the specific VGG-19 attribution pipeline remains unvalidated and may be less aligned with target decision boundaries than object/salient maps. The next contradiction is that VGG-19's older classification accuracy may limit the experimental population and the quality of its attributions. [(Simonyan, and Zisserman, 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb42cf88027de515750f230b23b1a057dc782108) reports 25.5% top-1 validation error for VGG-19, and the plan attacks only images correctly classified by both the target and VGG-19; this can remove a substantial fraction of the 1000 ImageNet images and bias the evaluation toward classes where VGG-19 and the target agree, while the paper's dense class score maps are not the same as Integrated Gradients attributions. A further contradiction is that adversarial targets are much harder to fool via transfer-style auxiliary models. [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) reports MI-FGSM black-box success of only 37.6%, 40.3%, and 23.3% against adversarially trained Inc-v3 ens3, Inc-v3 ens4, and IncRes-v2 ens, compared with near-saturated success on non-adversarial ensembles, suggesting that restricting perturbations to 10% of pixels selected by VGG-19 may be especially weak on the plan's Adversarial Inception and Adversarial Inception-ResNet targets. Weaker contradictions include the limited scope of auxiliary-model attacks: [(Papernot et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53b047e503f4c24602f376a774d653f7ed56c024) validates substitute-model black-box attacks only on MNIST and GTSRB, with architecture A best and transferability sensitive to epsilon and distortion, and [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) provides only setup details for ZOO without quantitative outcomes, so neither contradicts the method directly but fails to support the specific VGG-19 attribution pipeline on ImageNet under a 10,000-query budget.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to decouple attribution quality from PROTES and add a direct ablation of the VGG-19 attribution map against random pixels, target-model white-box gradients where available, and the object/salient maps used by [(Zhou et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1ad26fb31beaf63961b3fb20df26ed9c5ad7103), using the same 1000 ImageNet images, 10% pixel budget, 10,000-query budget, and untargeted objective, reporting success rate, average and median queries, L1 and L2 norms, PSNR, and SSIM. This is necessary because no related work validates that VGG-19 Integrated Gradients maps are the best pixel-selection source for TETRADAT. The second most important action is to report how many images remain after requiring correct classification by both target and VGG-19, and to test whether VGG-19 multi-scale inference, as in [(Simonyan, and Zisserman, 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb42cf88027de515750f230b23b1a057dc782108) with Q={256,384,512}, or an alternative auxiliary model, changes the selected pixels and attack outcomes; if VGG-19 filters too many images, the plan should either expand the initial pool or justify the subset. The third action is to validate the Integrated Gradients hyperparameters, especially 15 gradient steps and 15 discretization nodes, by testing 10, 20, and 50 steps and different baselines, and by measuring stability of the top 5000 pixels across runs, because [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) does not provide quantitative step-count evidence for this setting. The fourth action is to align evaluation with the strongest related black-box pixel-selection work by reporting query distributions, not only final success, and by comparing against SimBA, ZOO, or the object-attentional method of [(Zhou et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1ad26fb31beaf63961b3fb20df26ed9c5ad7103), since those works show query count and visual quality are central to pixel-selection attacks. The least important but still useful action is to specify hardware, software, and VGG-19 preprocessing, and to include adversarial-target sensitivity analyses, such as increasing the pixel fraction or perturbation amplitude, because [(Dong et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e37a3b227b68953f8067215828dc8b8714cb21b) shows adversarial models resist transfer attacks and the plan's initial epsilon=1 in HSV may need calibration.

## Context: To benchmark TETRADAT under comparable query constraints. Method: We will compare against three established black-box attacks: OnePixel (using differential evolution), Square (random square perturbations), and Pixle (pixel rearrangement). All methods will use identical query budgets of 10,000 requests.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 10,000-query baseline comparison comes from [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), which evaluates Square Attack on 1,000 ImageNet validation images with Inception v3, ResNet-50, and VGG-16-BN under exactly 10,000 queries per image, untargeted l-infinity and l2 objectives, and a square-size schedule that halves p at iterations 10, 50, 200, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, and 8,000; it reports failure rates below 1% and cases where Square outperforms white-box PGD, directly validating Square as a strong, query-matched baseline for the plan. The next strongest support is [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/819167ace2f0caae7745d2f25a803979be5fbfae), which uses differential evolution to modify one pixel on 1,000 ImageNet images, one per class, against AlexNet, VGG-16, GoogLeNet, and ResNet-152, with about population size 50 and 100 generations per intensity direction, yielding roughly 10,000 total forward-pass evaluations and 99.8% to 99.9% success on standard models, while success drops sharply under adversarial training; this validates OnePixel as a query-matched baseline and supports including adversarially trained targets. The third strongest support is [(Pomponi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/19216336ba37b64d066cf89d4418f5b5e625f35c), which introduces Pixle with 3-pixel patches, Restart-Iterative random search, and an ImageNet protocol of 1,000 correctly classified images, one per class, using ResNet-50 and VGG-16, and reports success rate, query/iteration counts, and L0 norm, directly supporting Pixle as a relevant black-box baseline even though the excerpt lacks numeric outcomes and its default configuration corresponds to about 5,000 query iterations. Further support comes from [(Qiu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7260778fabbf5870c41488c1186538d2107bfb), which uses 224x224 ImageNet images, VGG-16, ResNet-50, and Inception-v3, untargeted L-infinity epsilon=0.05, and a 10,000-query budget, finding that CMA-ES generally had higher success rates and lower mean queries, although (1+1)-ES sometimes had smaller median queries, and that Inception-v3 was more robust, thereby supporting the feasibility of 10,000-query untargeted ImageNet benchmarks and the need to report model-specific robustness. [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9) supports the evaluation framework by emphasizing success-rate-versus-query curves and noting that 50,000-query caps can produce high success rates, although its higher cap and latent-space NES method are not identical to the proposed baselines. Earlier and more conceptual support includes [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791), whose ZOO attack uses attack-space dimension reduction and hierarchical optimization of important pixels, aligning with TETRADAT's attribution-guided pixel selection, and [(Croce et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b5a677bf371a4f357bb739bc954d376b41b0c9a), whose l0-RS framework formalizes sparse derivative-free search under query limits, although neither provides the exact OnePixel, Square, or Pixle comparison. [(Croce, and Hein, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c46dade1aaedce96ecd03178379b5921a90306) supports sparse pixel-modification attacks on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and Restricted ImageNet with fixed changed-pixel budgets, but it does not use the proposed 10,000-query protocol. More peripheral support comes from [(Lapid et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98f2d0681e0b5acc0292bdac8574e17e90e5a514) and [(Alter et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c80805893b30d2d779ecfbe236c2a1c32290dd8), which both use Square Attack as an established black-box baseline, albeit in object detection with 110,000 queries and KAN classification without detailed query settings, respectively. Finally, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) supports the relevance of evaluating TETRADAT on ImageNet, but the excerpt does not confirm the 10,000-query baseline protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed Pixle baseline is not natively specified at 10,000 queries: [(Pomponi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/19216336ba37b64d066cf89d4418f5b5e625f35c) describes Restart-Iterative Pixle with at most 100 restarts and 50 iterations per restart, 3-pixel patches, and a random mapping function, which corresponds to roughly 5,000 query iterations per image, and the supplied excerpt contains no numeric success rates, query counts, or L0 outcomes; forcing Pixle to 10,000 queries therefore requires changing restarts, iterations, or query accounting, which may alter the published method and weaken the claim of an identical-budget comparison. The next strongest contradiction is that a fixed 10,000-query cutoff may be too restrictive or misleading for some black-box attacks: [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9) uses a 50,000-query upper limit and argues that average query counts can be misleading because of heavy-tailed query distributions, recommending success-rate-versus-query curves; this suggests that reporting only ASR at 10,000 queries could understate methods that need more queries, especially on adversarially trained models, although newer work such as [(Qiu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7260778fabbf5870c41488c1186538d2107bfb) partially mitigates this by showing 10,000-query untargeted ImageNet experiments, albeit without exact table values. An older but relevant contradiction is [(Ilyas et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3f83e8416010e9c3a705a0b6390d268e5ddf5c0), which uses a 1,000,000-query cap for targeted NES attacks on InceptionV3 with epsilon=0.05, indicating that query budgets can be much larger for harder objectives; however, this is less directly contradictory because the proposed plan is untargeted and [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) later shows 10,000 queries can be sufficient for untargeted ImageNet Square Attack. [(Lapid et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98f2d0681e0b5acc0292bdac8574e17e90e5a514) also suggests that 10,000 queries may be task-dependent, because it uses 110,000 queries for Square Attack and other baselines in a detection-patch setting, with Square achieving 43.9% to 86.3% average precision depending on detector; this does not directly contradict ImageNet classification but warns against assuming 10,000 queries is universally adequate. [(Croce, and Hein, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c46dade1aaedce96ecd03178379b5921a90306) contradicts a query-only view by showing that sparse pixel attacks can have very different runtimes and success rates under fixed changed-pixel budgets, for example SparseFool runtime about 55 times smaller than CornerSearch on Restricted ImageNet and 100-pixel-budget successes of 49.4% for SparseFool, 64.0% for CornerSearch, and 39.3% for white-box PGD0; this implies that the proposed benchmark should not ignore runtime or perturbation support. Weaker contradictions include the fact that [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/819167ace2f0caae7745d2f25a803979be5fbfae) found adversarial training sharply reduces OnePixel success, so OnePixel may be a weak baseline on the plan's adversarial Inception and Inception-ResNet models, and the plan lacks direct evidence for MobileNet V3 or those exact adversarial architectures. Additional weaker concerns are missing quantitative or implementation details in [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791), [(Croce et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b5a677bf371a4f357bb739bc954d376b41b0c9a), [(Pomponi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/19216336ba37b64d066cf89d4418f5b5e625f35c), [(Alter et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c80805893b30d2d779ecfbe236c2a1c32290dd8), and [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177), which limits the ability to verify exact query budgets, hyperparameters, statistical significance, or TETRADAT-specific outcomes.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define and enforce an identical 10,000-query protocol with explicit query accounting, and to implement each baseline as close to its published form as possible: for Square, use the [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) 10,000-query untargeted setting with square-size p halved at iterations 10, 50, 200, 1,000, 2,000, 4,000, 6,000, and 8,000, projection to [0,1], and l-infinity or l2 constraints; for OnePixel, use the [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/819167ace2f0caae7745d2f25a803979be5fbfae) differential-evolution configuration with population size 50, 100 generations, and both increasing and decreasing intensity directions to reach about 10,000 queries, while explicitly stating whether the variant modifies one pixel or the five-pixel OnePixel variant used in [(Pomponi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/19216336ba37b64d066cf89d4418f5b5e625f35c); for Pixle, adapt the Restart-Iterative 3-pixel-patch method to a hard 10,000-query stop by increasing restarts/iterations or using a query-aware loop, and report both the adapted 10,000-query result and the original approximately 5,000-query configuration as a sensitivity analysis. The second most important action is to report success-rate-versus-query curves, median and mean queries, failure rates, and runtime, because [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9) warns that fixed-budget ASR and average queries can be misleading due to heavy-tailed query distributions, and [(Croce, and Hein, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c46dade1aaedce96ecd03178379b5921a90306) shows that runtime and changed-pixel budgets materially affect sparse attack comparisons. Third, use the same 1,000 ImageNet one-image-per-class set for all methods and report the subset that is correctly classified by the target model and by the VGG-19 attribution model; if TETRADAT requires both correct classifications, run baselines on the same intersection and also report target-correct-only results to avoid selection bias. Fourth, include multiple random seeds, confidence intervals, and statistical significance tests per model, especially for the adversarial Inception and Inception-ResNet models, because [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/819167ace2f0caae7745d2f25a803979be5fbfae) shows adversarial training can sharply reduce OnePixel success and [(Qiu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7260778fabbf5870c41488c1186538d2107bfb) shows model-specific robustness differences such as Inception-v3 being more robust than VGG-16 and ResNet-50. Fifth, report L1, L2, L0, number of modified pixels, and visual quality metrics, and consider a human perception or naturalness assessment, because the plan includes visual quality and [(Papernot et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/819167ace2f0caae7745d2f25a803979be5fbfae) demonstrates that one-pixel perturbations can be near chance-level detectable, while [(Lapid et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98f2d0681e0b5acc0292bdac8574e17e90e5a514) shows naturalness ratings can differ substantially across patch methods. Sixth, document TETRADAT-specific implementation details, including the approximately 5,000 attribution-selected pixels, Integrated Gradients with 15 gradient steps and 15 discretization nodes, PROTES K=100, k=10, lambda=0.01, TT-rank r=5, HSV saturation/value modifications, and the multi-restart epsilon-halving schedule, and log queries per restart to ensure the 10,000 budget is comparable to the baselines. Seventh, include hardware, runtime, and computational cost, because [(Croce, and Hein, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c46dade1aaedce96ecd03178379b5921a90306) and [(Lapid et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98f2d0681e0b5acc0292bdac8574e17e90e5a514) show that query count alone does not capture practical cost. Eighth, if feasible, add a larger-budget sensitivity analysis, such as 50,000 queries following [(Huang, and Zhang, 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f2095bbbf3e47fbac26da70e95a219c23e3bac9) or 110,000 queries following [(Lapid et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98f2d0681e0b5acc0292bdac8574e17e90e5a514), to test whether the 10,000-query conclusion is budget-dependent. Finally, clarify and document any deviations from the original baselines, especially Pixle's query budget and OnePixel's pixel count, and release code, seeds, and hyperparameters to address reproducibility gaps noted in [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791), [(Croce et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b5a677bf371a4f357bb739bc954d376b41b0c9a), [(Pomponi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/19216336ba37b64d066cf89d4418f5b5e625f35c), [(Alter et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c80805893b30d2d779ecfbe236c2a1c32290dd8), and [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177).

## Context: To quantify attack effectiveness and imperceptibility. Method: We will measure attack success rates, L1 and L2 norms of perturbations, and conduct visual quality assessments of generated adversarial examples.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Zhou et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6daacb018509f78f91085efdc20e01d068fe86fb) because it is the most directly comparable newer black-box protocol: it evaluates attacks under explicit query budgets on 100 correctly classified validation examples from MNIST, CIFAR10, and ImageNet with target CNNs and ResNet-50, and reports success rate, median L2 loss, L0 loss as percentage of unattacked pixels, and L-inf distortion; with a 4000-query budget and P=500 for ImageNet it reports 71.41% attacked pixels and reductions in attacked pixels versus Sign-OPT of 46.9%, 39.0%, and 30.3% at 4000, 8000, and 14000 queries on MNIST, directly supporting the proposed plan's black-box query-budgeted ASR and perturbation-magnitude evaluation, although it does not report L1 or visual quality. The next strongest support is [(Liu et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d47b0392fce617d44fa2b681892241a69d534c9c) because it is newer empirical evidence that black-box pixelwise attacks require visual/perceptual evaluation, using a human-eye study with 150 adversarial images, 60 participants, 0-10 visibility scores within 5 seconds, and perceptual-loss analysis on CIFAR-10 against DenseNet, supporting the proposed visual quality assessment. [(Wei, and Liu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d05dc9780044ee6ba2a186c8d3b55115b56c22) supports the ASR component by defining ASR as the percentage of attack inputs achieving misclassification or a target label in black-box transfer settings, but it provides no empirical ASR, norm, or visual-quality results. [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395d9d3b1e2044bb2ce77abb6d6ca121dcc86842) supports the ImageNet multi-model and perturbation-budget framing by using 5,000 correctly classified validation images, l_inf 8/255 and l2 5 budgets, 100 iterations, step sizes 1/255 and 1, an NVIDIA V100, and multiple victim models, with quantitative outcomes such as UN-DP-DI2-TI-PI-FGSM average-of-average accuracy 42.42% and SE best-average accuracy 18.61% on DeiT-B, but it reports accuracy rather than ASR and does not report measured L1/L2 or visual quality. [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) provides older foundational support for measuring attack success and imperceptibility through misclassification rates and minimum average pixel-level distortion, with perturbations generally below 0.1 standard deviation and magnifying from 0.06 to 0.1 increasing error, and it supports cross-model evaluation, but it is white-box and does not use query budgets or L1/L2 norms. Finally, [(Akhtom et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff35d2c6da1cd91e6fba8b9d345cb3dd4491f031) supports the metric family by specifying attack-success, distortion-rate, and query-count measures for black-box attacks, but it is a review and does not empirically validate L1/L2 or visual quality.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is newer [(Liu et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d47b0392fce617d44fa2b681892241a69d534c9c) because it directly challenges older norm-based imperceptibility assumptions by showing that Lp norms did not always reliably quantify imperceptibility, whereas a multi-factor perceptual loss was a more reliable indicator of perceptual similarity; this contradicts the proposed method's reliance on L1 and L2 norms as the main imperceptibility measures, especially because TETRADAT modifies HSV saturation or value channels and may produce perturbations that are small in L1/L2 but visually noticeable. The next strongest contradiction is [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395d9d3b1e2044bb2ce77abb6d6ca121dcc86842) because it shows that reintroducing official victim-model preprocessing increased average victim accuracy for I-FGSM examples from 86.16% to 87.79%, meaning that ignoring preprocessing can overestimate vulnerability; the proposed plan does not explicitly specify ImageNet preprocessing, normalization, or whether L1/L2 are computed before or after preprocessing, which could confound ASR and norm comparisons across AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, and adversarially trained models. [(Zhou et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6daacb018509f78f91085efdc20e01d068fe86fb) also contradicts the sufficiency of the proposed metric set by reporting only success rate, median L2, L0, and L-inf, with no L1, no visual quality, no confidence intervals, and only 100 examples per dataset, suggesting that the proposed 1,000-image ImageNet evaluation must add L1, visual quality, and statistical significance to be comparable. [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) contradicts direct transferability of the evaluation protocol because it is a white-box, full-model-access protocol using minimum average pixel-level distortion and no query budget, L1/L2 norm, or visual quality, so its imperceptibility evidence cannot be taken as validation of TETRADAT's black-box query-based setting. [(Wei, and Liu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d05dc9780044ee6ba2a186c8d3b55115b56c22) and [(Akhtom et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff35d2c6da1cd91e6fba8b9d345cb3dd4491f031) provide weaker contradictions because they define ASR or distortion/query metrics but do not empirically report L1/L2 norms or visual quality, leaving the proposed method's specific metric choices under-supported.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define a concrete visual-quality/perceptual protocol in addition to L1/L2, because [(Liu et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d47b0392fce617d44fa2b681892241a69d534c9c) shows Lp norms can be unreliable; use a human study or validated perceptual metric on a fixed subset of the 1,000 ImageNet examples, report mean/median scores and confidence intervals, and compare TETRADAT against OnePixel, Square, and Pixle under the same 10,000-query budget. Second, specify exactly how L1 and L2 norms are computed: on normalized [0,1] RGB or HSV, before/after official preprocessing, per image and aggregated as mean/median with distributions, and also report L0 percentage and L-inf because [(Zhou et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6daacb018509f78f91085efdc20e01d068fe86fb) and [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395d9d3b1e2044bb2ce77abb6d6ca121dcc86842) show L0/L-inf and fixed budgets are informative. Third, make the black-box query accounting explicit: count only target-model queries toward the 10,000 budget, report query-to-success curves, state whether attribution, PROTES candidate evaluations, or restarts consume target queries, and report selected-pixel counts, comparing the planned approx 5000 attributed pixels with [(Zhou et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6daacb018509f78f91085efdc20e01d068fe86fb) P values of 10, 20, 30, and 40 for MNIST/CIFAR10 and 100, 200, 500, and 700 for ImageNet, following the query-budget framing in [(Zhou et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6daacb018509f78f91085efdc20e01d068fe86fb) and [(Akhtom et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff35d2c6da1cd91e6fba8b9d345cb3dd4491f031). Fourth, adopt an ImageNet evaluation protocol with official preprocessing and correct-classification filtering, as in [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395d9d3b1e2044bb2ce77abb6d6ca121dcc86842), and report ASR using the definition in [(Wei, and Liu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16d05dc9780044ee6ba2a186c8d3b55115b56c22) for untargeted attacks. Fifth, add statistical significance tests or bootstrap confidence intervals across the 1,000 images and seven target models, and include cross-model transferability analysis inspired by [(Szegedy et al., 2013-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d891dc72cbd40ffaeefdc79f2e7afe1e530a23ad) to show whether perturbations generated against one model transfer to the other standard and adversarially trained models.

## Context: To ensure a fair and robust evaluation. Method: We will only attack images correctly classified by both the target model and attribution model. Each attack will use untargeted objectives, aiming to change the predicted class to any incorrect label. We will analyze results across different model types, including standard and adversarially trained networks, to assess method robustness.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed evaluation protocol comes from Square Attack, which used 1,000 ImageNet validation images, untargeted l_inf and l_2 attacks, a 10,000-query budget, and computed query statistics only for successful attacks on images originally correctly classified by the target model, reporting sub-1% failure rates and even outperforming white-box PGD on several models [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). This newer black-box protocol contrasts with older white-box work by Carlini and Wagner by showing that query-based attacks can be evaluated under a fixed budget and can outperform white-box PGD, directly supporting the plan's target-correct filter, untargeted objective, 10,000-query budget, and inclusion of Square as a baseline, although it does not require an attribution model to be correct or include adversarially trained networks. The next strongest support is SimBA, newer than Square, which evaluated 1,000 images correctly classified by Inception-V3 under an L2 budget of about 16.37 and 10,000 queries, reported attack success rate and average query count over three runs, and included defensive-model analysis, supporting fair fixed-budget untargeted evaluation across standard and defensive models [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162). It does not require the surrogate to be correct, so it supports but does not validate the stricter both-model filter. Wang et al. provide support for attribution-guided perturbations by showing that an aggregate gradient from an auxiliary source model improves transferable untargeted-style attacks by 9.5% on average against normally trained targets and about 12.8% against defense models, with Vgg-16 as source exceeding 90% success on normally trained targets [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea). This supports using an auxiliary model to guide pixels and analyzing adversarial networks, but the protocol is white-box transfer and does not require target-attribution agreement. Admix supports the untargeted and adversarial-model comparison by reporting 100% white-box success on Inception-v3, 82.6% black-box success on Inception-v4 versus 69.4% for SIM, and average defense margins of 3.4% over SI-TI-DIM on 1,000 ILSVRC validation images [(Wang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/558f7fa8c8e909e7aa67b20d41ba5896b68dcf54). It is less direct because it is gradient-based and does not use attribution filtering. Carlini and Wagner provide older foundational support for untargeted misclassification on correctly classified inputs, using 1,000 random instances, constrained optimization, average L2 distortion, and success probability [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f). Liu et al. support iterative attacks and adversarial-model inclusion by attacking an ensemble of six classifiers, including adversarially trained Inception ResNet V2 and Inception V3, with a Jensen upper-bound loss, although the attack is targeted and lacks quantitative outcomes in the excerpt [(Liu et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5358a6e8f654e0f189217be0558eb1fd53c40ab6). EOT supports robust evaluation under transformations with InceptionV3 at 78.0% top-1 and adversariality percentages of 91%, 96%, 83%, and 97% for four examples, but it is targeted and does not use attribution filtering [(Athalye et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dce99e33c6fceb3e79023f5894fdbe733c91e92). ZOO supports black-box query-based search and dimensionality reduction, with ImageNet experiments on a single GTX 1080 Ti and batch size 128, but provides no quantitative outcomes in the excerpt [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791). Huang et al. support multi-model robustness and defense evaluation in physical detection, where AP p0.5 dropped from 0.98 to 0.07 for FR-VGG16 and transfer patterns reduced precision across eight detectors, but the task is detection AP rather than untargeted ImageNet classification [(Huang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b0a779731c4dbca1d23eca00de8c8d43426bd5b). Zhang et al. support attention-guided black-box transferability, showing about 20% ASR improvement and more than 10% AP reduction after averaging attention maps over three compound transformations on 3,600 CARLA images, but the setting is physical object detection [(Zhang et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f8841840339c5eabf5faddf104008eb187ffe6c). Yang et al. support black-box multi-model evaluation with a conditional generative attack on standard and adversarial ImageNet classifiers, though the main results are targeted and the untargeted appendix is not quantified in the excerpt [(Yang et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad3e83c5e5471e9fd388e36adb7950e7586b9b5f). Szegedy et al. support controlled evaluation of Inception-family targets, noting that omitting about 1,700 blacklisted entities biased top-1 error by about 0.3 percentage points, which reinforces the need for a clearly defined correctly classified subset [(Szegedy et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c26ab8767d046cb6e32d959fdf726aee89bb62). Russakovsky et al. support ImageNet single-label evaluation and qualitative error analysis, with GoogLeNet evaluated on 1,500 ILSVRC images and 0.3% annotation errors, but the study is natural-image error analysis rather than adversarial attacks [(Russakovsky et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e74f9b7f8eec6ba4704c206b93bc8079af3da4bd). The weakest support is Chertkov and Oseledets, which directly names TETRADAT and uses ImageNet but provides no attack details, metrics, or quantitative outcomes [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177), and Zheng et al., which supports systematic black-box benchmarking under budget and defense effects but provides no quantitative protocol in the excerpt [(Zheng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d145dc61c9967f4529f18a4946fdf1eb6621ad08).

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the stricter requirement to attack only images correctly classified by both target and attribution model is not supported by the strongest black-box protocols and may reduce the evaluation set. Square Attack achieved sub-1% failure on 1,000 target-correct ImageNet images with 10,000 queries and no attribution filter [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), and SimBA evaluated 1,000 Inception-V3-correct images with 10,000 queries and L2 budget 16.37 without requiring the surrogate to be correct [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162). This suggests that adding VGG-19 correctness may be unnecessary and could bias the subset toward images where a different architecture happens to agree. A second contradiction concerns using a single VGG-19 attribution model for all targets. Wang et al. show that source-model choice and ensemble aggregation matter, with middle-layer aggregate gradients and dropout ensembles improving transferable attacks, and note that early layers are data-specific while later layers are model-specific [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea). Zhang et al. similarly show that attention maps are model-specific and must be averaged over compound transformations to improve black-box transferability, with about 20% ASR improvement after averaging [(Zhang et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f8841840339c5eabf5faddf104008eb187ffe6c). Therefore a single VGG-19 Integrated Gradients map may select pixels that are not optimal for Inception, ResNet, MobileNet, or adversarial variants. A third contradiction is that attribution-guided pixel selection may not be required for strong black-box success. Square Attack, a random square search without attribution, outperformed white-box PGD on several models and had sub-1% failure [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2), while SimBA++ improved query efficiency but still suffered failures, indicating that attribution alone does not guarantee query efficiency [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162). ZOO also motivates dimensionality reduction but provides no quantitative evidence that attribution-based selection is superior [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791). A fourth, weaker contradiction is that untargeted-only evaluation may miss targeted vulnerabilities. Liu et al. use targeted ensemble attacks against adversarially trained Inception models [(Liu et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5358a6e8f654e0f189217be0558eb1fd53c40ab6), Yang et al. show targeted multi-target and face-impersonation success [(Yang et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad3e83c5e5471e9fd388e36adb7950e7586b9b5f), and EOT produces targeted transformation-tolerant examples with high adversariality [(Athalye et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dce99e33c6fceb3e79023f5894fdbe733c91e92). These do not invalidate untargeted evaluation, but they suggest that untargeted ASR alone may understate model vulnerability. A fifth contradiction is that correct-classification filtering can be confounded by label noise and ambiguous images. Russakovsky et al. found 0.3% incorrect annotations in a 1,500-image ILSVRC sample and error categories such as multiple objects and fine-grained recognition [(Russakovsky et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e74f9b7f8eec6ba4704c206b93bc8079af3da4bd), while Szegedy et al. showed that omitting about 1,700 blacklisted entities biased top-1 error by about 0.3 percentage points [(Szegedy et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5c26ab8767d046cb6e32d959fdf726aee89bb62). Thus a simple both-model correctness filter may include ambiguous images or exclude valid hard images unless the subset is audited. The weakest contradiction is the absence of quantitative validation for TETRADAT's specific protocol; Chertkov and Oseledets mention ImageNet experiments but no metrics [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177), and Zheng et al. provide a benchmark framework without quantitative details in the excerpt [(Zheng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d145dc61c9967f4529f18a4946fdf1eb6621ad08).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to pre-register and report the intersection of target-correct and VGG-19-correct images for each target model, including counts, class distribution, overlap with the 1,000 one-image-per-class set, and comparison to target-correct-only, because Square Attack and SimBA show that target-correct filtering is sufficient for strong black-box evaluation and the both-model filter may shrink or bias the sample [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2) [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162). If the intersection is small or class-biased, use target-correct as the primary evaluation set and treat attribution-correct as a sensitivity analysis, or replace hard VGG-19 correctness with an attribution confidence threshold. The second most important action is to reduce reliance on a single VGG-19 attribution map by using multiple auxiliary models or transformation-averaged Integrated Gradients, because Wang et al. show ensemble source gradients improve transferability and layer/model specificity matters [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea), and Zhang et al. show attention averaging over transformations improves black-box transferability [(Zhang et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f8841840339c5eabf5faddf104008eb187ffe6c). A practical option is to average IG maps from VGG-19, ResNet-152, and InceptionV3, or to use target-specific attribution when a white-box proxy is available. The third action is to align metrics and reporting with the strongest black-box protocols: use 10,000 queries, report attack success rate, average and median query counts with failures counted as 10,000, L1 and L2 norms, success probability, and at least three independent runs with confidence intervals and paired significance tests such as McNemar, as in SimBA and Square Attack [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162) [(Andriushchenko et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8733fe2371b615609b04e2e910b1ecfa8e77cbc2). The plan should also specify the perturbation budget in standard image space, for example an L2 budget comparable to 16.37 for 299 by 299 images, or clearly define how epsilon=1 in HSV maps to L1 and L2. The fourth action is to report standard and adversarial models separately, with base accuracy and the number of correctly classified images for each, because adversarial models are harder and the both-model filter may be especially restrictive for them, as suggested by Liu et al. and Wang et al. [(Liu et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5358a6e8f654e0f189217be0558eb1fd53c40ab6) [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea). The fifth action is to add stronger baselines and a white-box reference: Square is already appropriate, but SimBA++ or LeBA and Carlini-Wagner would better bound TETRADAT, because SimBA++ improves query efficiency over prior methods and Carlini-Wagner established untargeted constrained attack metrics [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162) [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f). The sixth action is to audit label noise and visual quality using a human error taxonomy, because Russakovsky et al. show 0.3% annotation errors and error types such as multiple objects and fine-grained recognition can confound correctness-based filtering [(Russakovsky et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e74f9b7f8eec6ba4704c206b93bc8079af3da4bd). Finally, document implementation details for reproducibility, including image resizing, pixel range, HSV conversion, PROTES hyperparameters, random seeds, hardware, and multi-restart schedule, following the level of detail provided by ZOO, SimBA, and Wang et al. [(Chen et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ab7319dbe80549ba80e3320d0546d741a7a5791) [(Yang et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a556a1ca23e4b2aaa7dacf928a2803d4faeaa162) [(Wang et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b18580c6746a47603955074931d4687e268781ea).

## Context: To configure TETRADAT for the experiments. Method: For TETRADAT, we will use approximately 10% of image pixels (≈5000 pixels) selected via attribution, initial perturbation amplitude ε=1, and PROTES parameters: K=100 candidates per iteration, k=10 best selections, learning rate λ=0.01, and TT-rank r=5. Integrated Gradients will use 15 gradient steps and 15 discretization nodes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed TETRADAT configuration comes from [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675), which evaluates PROTES on 20 benchmarks with M=10^4 objective queries and fixed K=100, k=10, k_gd=1, lambda=0.05, and R=5, matching the proposed K=100, k=10, TT-rank r=5, and 10,000-query budget except for lambda. PROTES achieved the best objective on 19 of 20 benchmarks, including P-07 6.2E+06 versus 6.3E+06, 1.7E+07, 2.2E+10, 3.1E+08, and 6.2E+06 for baselines, P-19 6.4E-02 versus Optima-TT 6.7E-02 and TTOpt 5.7E-01, and P-17 runtime 641 s versus Optima-TT 4245 s, while TTOpt was slightly better only on P-18 with 1.1E-02 versus PROTES 1.4E-02; this newer optimizer result directly supports using PROTES for a discrete 5,000-pixel perturbation tensor and complements the older IG attribution idea by providing a TT-based search mechanism, although it does not test adversarial images. The next strongest support is newer sparse-coordinate work that builds on older black-box query-efficiency methods by showing that updating only a small number of selected coordinates improves query efficiency: [(Dong et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/486c94d035d9966cdd4078f697307faff03683df) reaches 70% attack success rate at 14,000 queries on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet distortion below 5.0 in about 30,000 queries versus more than 150,000 for the Opt-attack baseline, supporting the plan's 10 percent pixel subset and query-budgeted black-box attack, although it does not use attribution or TT. TT representation is supported by newer image-domain tensor work, [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca), which uses TT decomposition on the 50,000-image ImageNet validation set with ResNet-v2 50 and finds 4-mode TT outperforming Tucker and Parafac, with rank and batch size affecting accuracy and runtime, demonstrating that TT can compactly represent image tensors. Black-box ImageNet feasibility is supported by older but relevant work, [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e8c897f6087cf08a733884f2bb7e6c5e58ac177), whose Frank-Wolfe attack on Inception V3 with epsilon=0.05 and 250 validation images reports the best attack success rate and query efficiency among NES-PGD and Bandit, although it is targeted and does not use attribution or TT. Low-rank image structure is supported by [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee), which uses CP and Tucker reconstruction on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and 1,000 ImageNet images with ResNet-101 and reports only about 1 percent clean accuracy drop and improved robustness against FGSM, BIM, DeepFool, and C&W, indicating that low-rank constraints are viable for image perturbations. Integrated Gradients is supported theoretically by the foundational work [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be), which defines the straight-line path integral, proves Completeness and Implementation Invariance, and motivates using an auxiliary white-box model to select important pixels. Finally, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) provides only weak support by naming TETRADAT and using ImageNet, but no quantitative evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that newer sparse-coordinate evidence contradicts the proposed fixed 10,000-query assumption for ImageNet: [(Dong et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/486c94d035d9966cdd4078f697307faff03683df) required about 30,000 queries to reach distortion below 5.0 on ImageNet, while the plan fixes 10,000 queries for all methods and reports only final attack success rate, so TETRADAT may be underpowered relative to prior sparse-coordinate attacks. A second major contradiction is that newer TT defense work suggests the proposed TT-rank r=5 may be too low: [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) swept ranks 50 to 120 in steps of 5 for 4-mode ImageNet tensors and found that rank and batch size affect accuracy and runtime, and [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee) used CP rank 8 for 32 by 32 by 3 CIFAR-10 images and Tucker cores retaining full spatial dimensions, suggesting that a rank-5 TT over 5,000 selected pixels may be overly restrictive for capturing effective perturbations. Third, the proposed PROTES learning rate lambda=0.01 is not validated in the direct PROTES evidence: [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) uses lambda=0.05 in its main 10,000-query benchmarks and reports stability over K, k, and R, but does not test lambda=0.01, so the lower learning rate could underfit the TT distribution within the same query budget. Fourth, the Integrated Gradients discretization of 15 gradient steps and 15 nodes is not supported by the foundational IG source: [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) provides the path-integral formulation but does not specify or validate 15 steps, baseline near-zero assumptions, or adversarial pixel-selection accuracy, leaving the 10 percent pixel selection potentially noisy. Fifth, the older black-box ImageNet evidence is limited and not directly comparable: [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e8c897f6087cf08a733884f2bb7e6c5e58ac177) evaluates targeted attacks on only 250 Inception V3 validation images with epsilon=0.05 and does not use attribution, TT, or untargeted objectives, so its query-efficiency results do not guarantee TETRADAT's untargeted 10,000-query performance. Finally, [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) provides no quantitative contradiction but also no evidence for the proposed hyperparameters, creating a reproducibility gap.

### Suggested Action

Most important, expand the query-budget evaluation: report attack success rate versus query count up to at least 30,000 or 50,000 queries for ImageNet, and if 10,000 is fixed, include query-normalized curves and compare to [(Dong et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/486c94d035d9966cdd4078f697307faff03683df) and [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e8c897f6087cf08a733884f2bb7e6c5e58ac177) to show whether TETRADAT's 10 percent pixel selection compensates for the smaller budget. Second, run a TT-rank and tensor-mode sensitivity study for the 5,000-pixel tensor, testing r=5, 10, 20, and 50 and comparing 2D spatial x 3 RGB tensor modes, flattened modes, and patch modes, motivated by [(Entezari, and Papalexakis, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d17508f5038aad3cdc20fff8378027633fa243ca) and [(Cho et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a27d774cfe6d427e80898f3993497962c62020ee), and include CP and Tucker baselines to verify that rank 5 is not the bottleneck. Third, tune PROTES lambda and report sensitivity for lambda in {0.005, 0.01, 0.05, 0.1} with K=100, k=10, and r=5, because [(Batsheva et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c399f4724bde401589e206064e93e9663d93c675) validates lambda=0.05 but not 0.01; also test K, k, and R sensitivity and report runtimes on the planned hardware. Fourth, validate the attribution stage: compare 15-step and 15-node Integrated Gradients to 50 and 100 steps, random 10 percent pixels, Grad-CAM or saliency baselines, and different baselines, using VGG-19 on the 1,000 correctly classified ImageNet images, and measure downstream attack success rate and L1/L2 norms to ensure the 5,000-pixel selection is causal rather than merely plausible, as required by the theoretical but non-empirical IG evidence in [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be). Fifth, strengthen the experimental protocol: increase the ImageNet sample beyond 1,000 where feasible, report per-architecture attack success rate, L1/L2 norms, visual quality, statistical significance or confidence intervals, and include stronger black-box baselines such as Frank-Wolfe, NES-PGD, Bandit, Square, OnePixel, and Pixle under identical 10,000-query budgets, while documenting hardware, model versions, HSV conversion details, and the multi-restart epsilon=1 halving schedule across AlexNet, GoogleNet, Inception V3, MobileNet V3, ResNet-152, Adversarial Inception, and Adversarial Inception-ResNet. Finally, provide full TETRADAT implementation details and results, since [(Chertkov, and Oseledets, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bab54ca1f24554caba0e12736770873b9c23a177) currently offers only an ImageNet mention and no reproducible evidence.
