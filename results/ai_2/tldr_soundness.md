## Strengths 
The proposal is best supported where it uses molecular subsets with known completeness and controlled complexity, because [(Brown et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bfeb6901db481c08874cfe0ae807d8564513765) shows similarity and synthetic-accessibility filtering can create controlled molecular evaluation sets, and [(Liu et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b83e7e9a7bf59347eb9bc53ecaf139e301a8b72d) supports complexity stratification across QM9, ZINC, and CEPDB. The pretrain-then-fine-tune design is supported by [(Irwin et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f7f690e003176316d0ee56fbcbfed4b6b0948), which found pretrained molecular language models outperform randomly initialized models and provides training details. The sampling and probability-diagnostics axis is supported by [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1), which compares temperature, top-k, nucleus, and beam search and uses validation probabilities, while [(Krenn et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8338a903d8078481ff8af777475f7394d00e9d57) and [(Campos, and Ji, 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c449207a8a94412f34f18c05c8a02cec43635776) support SELFIES validity and random-sampling baselines.

## Weaknesses 
The strongest cautions are that equivalent molecular representations can distort coverage, as [(Irwin et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f7f690e003176316d0ee56fbcbfed4b6b0948) shows augmented SMILES forms can reduce top-k diversity, so raw string generation must be canonicalized before recall. The recall-prediction framework is not directly validated by [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1), which studies open-ended text generation and shows temperature or truncation can distort probability-based quality signals. Benchmark saturation is also a risk because [(Brown et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bfeb6901db481c08874cfe0ae807d8564513765) found some molecular objectives are too easy, and [(Liu et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b83e7e9a7bf59347eb9bc53ecaf139e301a8b72d) shows validity, novelty, and uniqueness can be high without measuring complete-set recall. Finally, the aggregation-loss idea lacks direct support in [(Yüksel et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8489b55d992e6a3ac54aec7094a42ec8e333012f) and [(Krenn et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8338a903d8078481ff8af777475f7394d00e9d57), which do not test mean, minimum, or maximum aggregation for recall.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Define the four GDB-13 subsets and scoring protocol explicitly using RDKit canonicalization, ECFP4 or Morgan similarity, synthetic-accessibility scores, documented thresholds, and disjoint train-test splits, following [(Brown et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bfeb6901db481c08874cfe0ae807d8564513765) and [(Liu et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b83e7e9a7bf59347eb9bc53ecaf139e301a8b72d); report precision and recall over canonical unique molecules, duplicate rate, coverage curves, and baselines including random, unigram, best-in-dataset, and theoretical upper bounds with bootstrap confidence intervals.

Before scaling to 1-10 million generations, pilot the recall-prediction framework on one subset by deriving expected precision and recall for random, temperature, top-k, nucleus, and duplicate-avoiding beam search, including zero-probability smoothing analogous to [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1), then compare predicted and actual recall with calibration curves and significance tests.

Add a duplicate-avoiding beam search that merges SELFIES equivalence classes and compare it against random and temperature sampling, while first establishing a single-SELFIES cross-entropy fine-tuning baseline before testing mean, minimum, and maximum aggregation, using [(Irwin et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f9f7f690e003176316d0ee56fbcbfed4b6b0948), [(Krenn et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8338a903d8078481ff8af777475f7394d00e9d57), and [(Yüksel et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8489b55d992e6a3ac54aec7094a42ec8e333012f) as controls and piloting on 800K and 125M models before 1.3B.
