## Strengths 
The strongest support is for ChunkKV's core components: consecutive-token grouping is validated by segment-level compression [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c966838f3361911c434574bef0dd5c3bf4e149d) and chunk-level long-context compression [(Zhang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f69f494ab691481ec353da4be480b334fada6607); summed attention scores as importance are supported by H2O [(Barrett et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e586a4591ba0303b769f2c07cbddaf1899cb72e4); top-k span or chunk selection is supported by ReAttention [(Liu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f8a928167dbac891c01d3250409959c61ace23b); recent observation windows are supported by StreamingLLM [(Xiao et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdc53c2c10742464087c0525f77e32604827a21d); order-preserving selection is supported by token pruning [(Kim et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c156b1b30e3dd9284615e5304f2826c09d0ff) and narrative-loss motivation [(Yang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89e0fde2dc60f8520c176a57396c6cad3af5dc40); layer-wise index reuse is motivated by adjacent-layer KV similarity [(Liu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d372fb69c485472385f152bc832bf1d35e223324); drop-in deployment is supported by H2O [(Barrett et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e586a4591ba0303b769f2c07cbddaf1899cb72e4), Keyformer [(Adnan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c69d79c0ee7ac964284a75135b317d1ce7fb2d6), StreamingLLM [(Xiao et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdc53c2c10742464087c0525f77e32604827a21d), and PagedAttention [(Kwon et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83b90f4a0ae4cc214eb3cc140ccfef9cd99fac05); and the LongBench and NIAH evaluation protocol is supported by PyramidKV [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/812356c723c082f88fb722531beaf45e344ffa1e) and Quest [(Tang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c7db9fb18246787fbe3de6e0eaa370ae749e795).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions are that token-level and query-aware methods already achieve strong long-context performance, reducing the need for semantic chunks: H2O [(Barrett et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e586a4591ba0303b769f2c07cbddaf1899cb72e4) matches full-model performance within 1 percent, Quest [(Tang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c7db9fb18246787fbe3de6e0eaa370ae749e795) outperforms static baselines with query-aware top-K selection, PyramidKV [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/812356c723c082f88fb722531beaf45e344ffa1e) shows layer-wise budgets outperform fixed token baselines, and norm-based eviction [(Devoto et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99161a4b8fd2c7e4b39c3782b41e86fcc45c0c59) preserves NIAH and passkey retrieval at aggressive compression. Static observation-window selection may be brittle when questions are not at the end [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/022f386eb66fc5532dd6f439e7a356fd33ebb9a2), and span-level top-k can fail on multi-key RULER or NIAH probes [(Liu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f8a928167dbac891c01d3250409959c61ace23b). Layer-wise index reuse is challenged by layer-specific importance [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/812356c723c082f88fb722531beaf45e344ffa1e), non-uniform layer importance [(Wang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d4d920dad3e7ab80363d31d554a366d0ac076371), and per-layer score functions [(Adnan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c69d79c0ee7ac964284a75135b317d1ce7fb2d6). Fixed chunk sizes may hurt short sequences [(Kwon et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83b90f4a0ae4cc214eb3cc140ccfef9cd99fac05), quantization also provides an alternative efficiency path without semantic chunking [(Sheng et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42a14d824caa3348046eb34c37e2ab7985faa7a3), and three-run means may be insufficient for small or noisy subsets [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Add a query-aware, normalized chunk-scoring variant: compute chunk criticality from the current query or recent generated tokens and use per-head or per-layer normalized attention, then ablate static historical scores, query-aware scores, and hybrid scores against Quest, H2O, SnapKV, and PyramidKV on LongBench, NIAH, and GSM8K chain-of-thought, because query-aware selection outperforms static historical attention [(Tang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c7db9fb18246787fbe3de6e0eaa370ae749e795) and raw accumulated attention can underperform normalized saliency [(He et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca1952a54cf85f0f449cb337f70b083100069c9c).

Make layer-wise index reuse conditional and benchmark it against per-layer selection: compute Jaccard similarity of preserved chunk indices across adjacent layers and heads, reuse only when similarity exceeds a threshold, protect first and last layers, ablate reuse intervals N=1, 2, 4, and 8, and compare accuracy, selection overhead, latency, and memory against PyramidKV per-layer budgets and MiniCache-style merging, because layer-specific budgets improve small-cache performance [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/812356c723c082f88fb722531beaf45e344ffa1e) and adjacent layers are not uniformly redundant [(Liu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d372fb69c485472385f152bc832bf1d35e223324), [(Wang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d4d920dad3e7ab80363d31d554a366d0ac076371).

Tighten the evaluation protocol to matched absolute budgets and robust statistics: lock the 17 LongBench datasets, report per-dataset F1, Rouge-L, accuracy, and edit similarity, match average per-layer KV budgets to baselines and report both percentage and absolute budgets, add RULER or multi-key NIAH probes and Chinese per-subtask Qwen2 scores, and report mean, standard deviation, confidence intervals, paired significance tests, compression time, peak memory, prefill and decode latency, and hardware, because related work shows absolute budgets and retrieval probes are discriminative [(Cai et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/812356c723c082f88fb722531beaf45e344ffa1e), [(Tang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c7db9fb18246787fbe3de6e0eaa370ae749e795), [(Liu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f8a928167dbac891c01d3250409959c61ace23b), and small subsets can have high variance [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b).
