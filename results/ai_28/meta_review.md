## Systematic Investigation: We will conduct a systematic investigation of evaluation metrics for protein generative models by examining their behavior under various controlled conditions. This frames the plan as a controlled benchmark of metric reliability rather than a single model evaluation.

### Support

The strongest support is methodological: [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) directly benchmarks GAN-train, GAN-test, Inception score, FID, FID-5K, and sliced Wasserstein distance under subsampling, salt-and-pepper noise from 1% to 20%, Gaussian noise with sigma=5, convnet versus random forests, and 1500 pairwise human comparisons on MNIST, CIFAR10, CIFAR100, and ImageNet; it showed GAN-test drops from 82% to 15% under noise while GAN-train barely changes, and FID was 27.1 between full CIFAR10 and Gaussian noise but 29.6 between full data and a random 5k subset with the same noise, supporting the plan's need to separate quality, diversity, and distributional similarity. This later work qualified the earlier Inception-score optimism in [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) by showing that FID can conflate quality and diversity, while still supporting the plan's controlled benchmark design. It builds on [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369), which earlier used CIFAR-10 with 50,000 training images, 28 by 28 crops shifted 1 to 4 pixels, Euclidean nearest neighbors, Parzen-window likelihood with 10,000 samples, and a k-means baseline to show that tiny perturbations and degenerate baselines can destabilize sample-based metrics; that evidence strongly supports the plan's controlled synthetic perturbations, sample-size sensitivity, and baseline controls. [(Kynkaanniemi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d383dd8ced85d7898d8b1546c514a34fb626ea16) later reinforced and qualified this line by showing FID can vary by up to plus or minus 14% between consecutive StyleGAN iterations and can favor high-recall configurations, while kNN precision-recall frontiers reveal tradeoffs such as no minibatch standard deviation shifting to higher precision and lower recall, with FID values of 4.43 for baseline StyleGAN, 8.58 for no minibatch standard deviation, 10.34 for low gamma, 6.14 for no progressive growing, 4.27 for random translation, and 4.16 for no instance normalization; this supports reporting precision-recall curves, multiple snapshots, and not relying on FD or EMD alone. In protein-specific work, [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) supports the plan's metric suite by evaluating DiMA with ESM-2 encoders from 8M to 3B and ESM-C 300M using pLDDT, FD-seq, CD at 50% identity, TM-score, scPerplexity, MMD, 1-Wasserstein, and perplexity on 2,048 sequences; pLDDT rose from 74.3 to 83.4, FD-seq fell from 0.560 to 0.314, and CD at 50% identity fell from 0.981 to 0.969, showing model-size effects and quality-diversity tradeoffs. [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) supports pLDDT as a quality signal by folding 617 million MGnify90 sequences on about 2,000 GPUs, finding about 59% good and 36% high confidence, and reporting pLDDT-LDDT Pearson r=0.79 on about 4,000 sequences, though it is structure-prediction calibration rather than generative metric benchmarking. Earlier, [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) supports sample-size and human-protocol sensitivity by using 2000 MTurk votes, 50k Inception samples, and noting that annotator feedback changes results, while [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) provides the foundational controlled metric design with a 500-sentence corpus, 20 blocks of 25 sentences, paired t-statistics, reference variation, and BLEU-human correlation of 0.99 for monolingual and 0.96 for bilingual judges. More recent domain-adjacent work adds support: [(Tadesse et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/343ff89766b35ccc680774b89751f478bd8cd6cf) shows feature-level independence and subgroup scanning can reveal local over- or under-generation in molecules, and [(Frey et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d82835c86f49ec507bf95495d2a958137b26ac6e) uses a distributional conformity score for antibody generation, while [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) illustrates ad hoc protein evaluation with 95-residue CM2 sequences, HMM scores, amino acid statistics, and GB-1 variants, motivating standardization. [(Grnarova et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bd0445e48f576036514fa0b179ac6ae8d3c3b4a) also supports treating metrics as objects of study under failure modes, though qualitatively.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is to the assumption that the planned metrics will be stable at the planned sample sizes. [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) showed that nearest-neighbor retrieval changed after only a two-pixel shift on CIFAR-10 28 by 28 crops and that Parzen-window likelihood with 10,000 samples did not approximate true log-likelihood even for 6 by 6 patches, with k-means scoring 313 nats above the true distribution's 243 despite having true log-likelihood negative infinity; this contradicts any expectation that distributional similarity metrics such as MMD, FD, or EMD will be reliable from 2^5 to 2^14 samples in high-dimensional protein spaces. [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) contradicts the idea that a single distributional scalar suffices: FID was 27.1 between full CIFAR10 and Gaussian noise sigma=5 but 29.6 between full data and a random 5k subset with the same noise, while GAN-test stayed 95% and GAN-train fell from 91% to 80%, and salt-and-pepper noise reduced GAN-test from 82% to 15% while barely changing GAN-train; this warns that FD or EMD-like metrics may conflate quality, diversity, and sample size. [(Kynkaanniemi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d383dd8ced85d7898d8b1546c514a34fb626ea16) contradicts single-snapshot evaluation by reporting FID variation of up to plus or minus 14% between consecutive StyleGAN iterations and showing FID favored high-recall configurations such as baseline StyleGAN 4.43 and no instance normalization 4.16 over high-precision configurations such as no minibatch standard deviation 8.58 and low gamma 10.34; this later evidence also qualifies the earlier reliance on Inception score and FID in [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) and [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), and it implies the plan must include training-stage variability and precision-recall tradeoffs rather than only scalar FD or EMD. [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) partially contradicts treating pLDDT as a universally calibrated generative quality metric: although pLDDT correlated with LDDT at Pearson r=0.79 on about 4,000 metagenomic sequences and supported thresholds on 617 million ESMFold predictions, the calibration is for structure prediction on MGnify90, not for generated sequences, and the correlation leaves substantial unexplained variance. [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) contradicts the assumption that larger representation models improve all evaluation dimensions: ESM-2 3B improved pLDDT from 74.3 to 83.4 and reduced FD-seq from 0.560 to 0.314, but CD at 50% identity decreased from 0.981 to 0.969, and ESM-C 300M had pLDDT 82.7 with weaker coverage than ESM-2 650M; this supports multi-metric reporting but warns against a single quality score. [(Tadesse et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/343ff89766b35ccc680774b89751f478bd8cd6cf) suggests that global aggregate metrics can miss local divergent subgroups, as GFA identified a MolGX subgroup with overrepresented fingerprint value 3217380708, so the plan's global IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD may be insufficient without subgroup diagnostics. [(Frey et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d82835c86f49ec507bf95495d2a958137b26ac6e) provides weaker contradiction by favoring distributional conformity over sequence recovery for antibodies but not reporting numerical reliability, sample size, or baseline comparisons, so it cannot by itself validate the planned distributional metrics. [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) and [(Grnarova et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bd0445e48f576036514fa0b179ac6ae8d3c3b4a) are weaker: Johnson uses ad hoc CM2 metrics without controlled perturbations or statistical significance, and Grnarova reports qualitative duality-gap behavior without quantitative baselines, both indicating that metric claims require the controlled evidence the plan proposes.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the controlled perturbations into a metric-reliability benchmark with explicit ground truth: for SwissProt noise from 0% to 30% in 5% increments and the five-family cluster experiments, report not only metric values but sensitivity curves, rank correlations to the known perturbation level, AUC or ROC for detecting cluster elimination, cluster imbalance, and intra-cluster duplication, bootstrap confidence intervals, and degenerate baselines such as random sequences, duplicate sequences, k-means-like mode collapse, and shuffled clusters, following [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) and [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115). Second, extend the sample-size analysis beyond 2^5 to 2^14 for distributional and diversity metrics, adding 2^15 to 2^18 where feasible, because [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) showed 10,000-sample likelihood estimates can be infeasible, [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) recommended 50k samples for diversity-sensitive Inception score, and [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) showed 5k subsets can distort FID; use permutation or bootstrap confidence intervals to define the minimum reliable sample size per metric. Third, report IPR and D&C as precision-recall curves and Pareto frontiers, and compare them against FD, MMD, and EMD, because [(Kynkaanniemi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d383dd8ced85d7898d8b1546c514a34fb626ea16) showed FID can obscure precision-recall tradeoffs and vary by up to plus or minus 14% across snapshots; include multiple generator seeds or training snapshots and report variance. Fourth, calibrate pLDDT, ppl, pppl, and scPerplexity against structural and sequence ground truth: compare AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold pLDDT to LDDT or TM-score on held-out PDB or CAMEO sequences, report calibration curves and threshold-specific precision and recall, and control for tokenizer, max length, padding, and sampling mode as in [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), and [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c). Fifth, add feature-level and subgroup diagnostics adapted from [(Tadesse et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/343ff89766b35ccc680774b89751f478bd8cd6cf), such as amino acid composition, domain or family, motif, structural class, and cluster membership, to detect local over- or under-generation that global CD and distributional metrics may miss. Sixth, optimize the MMD RBF kernel parameter sigma by grid search on controlled corruption and GPT2 progression, reporting the sigma sensitivity curve rather than a single value, and document all implementation details including MMseqs2 thresholds, embedding model sizes, random seeds, batch sizes, hardware, and runtime. Seventh, validate on real state-of-the-art generators with multiple seeds and, where feasible, expert structural review or functional assays, while acknowledging that human or functional validation is less scalable than automatic metrics.

## Quality Metrics Analysis: We will analyze four widely used quality metrics - predicted Local Distance Difference Test (pLDDT), perplexity (ppl), pseudoperplexity (pppl), and self-consistency perplexity (scPerplexity). We will examine their sensitivity to sample size, correlation with each other, and the impact of different underlying models on their performance. This tests whether standard quality scores are stable, comparable, and sensitive to model undertraining.

### Support

The strongest support comes from direct protein-generation evaluations that already use the same metric family and show that pLDDT and perplexity-family scores respond to model scale and generator choice. [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) reports pLDDT, scPerplexity, perplexity, FD-seq, CD0.5, MMD, and 1-Wasserstein on 2,048 generated sequences; pLDDT rises from 74.3 with ESM-2 8M to 83.4 with ESM-2 3B, while FD-seq decreases from 0.560 to 0.314 and CD0.5 declines from 0.981 to 0.969, directly supporting the proposed test of model-size impact and quality-diversity tradeoff. The next strongest support is [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which compares AlphaFold2 pLDDT and model-specific ppl for ProGen2 700M, EvoDiff, DPLM 650M, and ESM3 1.4B at lengths 100, 200, 300, and 500 using 50 sequences per condition; DPLM has pLDDT 85.38 to 93.54 and ppl 3.33 to 6.21, while EvoDiff has pLDDT 43.14 to 50.66 and ppl 16.51 to 17.28, supporting cross-model comparison but also showing high variance at n=50. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) supports pLDDT as a practical foldability threshold, using ESMFold pLDDT greater than 70 for DPLM motif scaffolding and reporting successful motif pLDDT values of 86.84, 93.85, and 87.79, while [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) provides the foundational validation that AlphaFold pLDDT tracks local accuracy on CASP14 with median GDT_TS 92.4, GDT_L 86.4, LDDT 94.6, and TM-score 0.94. Older image-metric work supplies the controlled-evaluation template: [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) shows FID, Inception score, sliced Wasserstein, GAN-train, and GAN-test behave differently under 5k and 10k sample sizes, subsampling, salt-and-pepper noise from 1% to 20%, and model size, with FID 27.1 for full CIFAR10 versus 29.6 for a 5k subset against Gaussian noise while GAN-test remains 95%; [(Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d383dd8ced85d7898d8b1546c514a34fb626ea16) shows FID can vary by up to +/-14% between consecutive StyleGAN iterations and motivates precision/recall and realism scores; [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) shows Inception score depends on an external classifier and should use 50k samples, with MTurk discrimination only 52.4% over 2000 votes; [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) and [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) provide early evidence that sample-based scores and automatic metrics require sample-size blocks, reference-count controls, and correlation with external judgments, with BLEU block analysis on 500 sentences, paired t-statistics, and human correlations of 0.99 and 0.96. Together, these works form an iterative line from automatic metric validation in translation and image generation to protein-specific pLDDT and ppl comparisons. Weaker but relevant protein-specific precedents include [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c), [(Alamdari et al., 2024-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840), and [(Grnarova et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bd0445e48f576036514fa0b179ac6ae8d3c3b4a), which use perplexity, HMM/profile scores, BLOSUM, secondary structure, structural plausibility, or training-state diagnostics, but provide limited quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpts.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is direct protein evidence that perplexity computed with each generator's own likelihood is not directly comparable across architectures: [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) reports ppl for ProGen2, EvoDiff, DPLM, and ESM3 using each model's own sequence likelihood, so DPLM's lower ppl may partly reflect model-specific calibration rather than universal sequence naturalness, and its n=50 design yields large standard deviations such as pLDDT 85.38 +/- 14.20 at length 100. A second strong contradiction is that sample-based likelihood or density estimates can be unstable and misleading, undermining the assumption that ppl, pppl, or scPerplexity are automatically stable or comparable: [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) shows Parzen-window estimates rank k-means above the true distribution and require infeasible sample counts on 6x6 CIFAR-10 patches, while nearest-neighbor overfitting checks detect only stark overfitting after two-pixel shifts. [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) contradicts the idea that a single scalar quality score is sufficient: FID fails to separate quality from diversity, GAN-test drops from 82% to 15% under 1% to 20% salt-and-pepper noise while GAN-train barely changes, and human preferences are mixed, with SNGAN preferred over WGAN-GP 10M in only 230 of 500 trials, p = 0.37. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) warns that model-based scores depend on the external evaluator and may need 50k samples, whereas the proposed sweep starts at 2^5 = 32 and ends at 2^14 = 16,384, so lower sample sizes may be unreliable for diversity-sensitive components. [(Kynkäänniemi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d383dd8ced85d7898d8b1546c514a34fb626ea16) shows FID can fluctuate by +/-14% between consecutive training iterations, implying pLDDT or perplexity may also be checkpoint-sensitive if undertraining is not controlled. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) and [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) limit pLDDT evidence to one structure predictor each, ESMFold and AlphaFold respectively, so pLDDT may be model-specific and not a portable quality score. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) shows reference-count dependence, with human translator BLEU 0.3468 against four references versus 0.2571 against two, analogous to pLDDT dependence on the folding model or reference structure. Weaker contradictions include the lack of quantitative results in [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c), and [(Alamdari et al., 2024-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840), and the absence of protein-specific evidence in [(Grnarova et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bd0445e48f576036514fa0b179ac6ae8d3c3b4a), which only qualitatively argues that training-state metrics and sample-quality metrics capture complementary properties.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make perplexity-family metrics comparable by fixing the reference model(s) rather than using each generator's own likelihood. Because [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) computes ppl with each model's own sequence likelihood, the proposed analysis should compute ppl, pppl, and scPerplexity against one or more fixed protein LMs, such as ESM-2 650M or 3B and ProtT5, and report both model-specific and reference-fixed values, with explicit definitions for pppl and scPerplexity. Second, expand the sample-size protocol beyond 2^5 to 2^14 by adding bootstrap or block resampling, confidence intervals, paired tests, and a larger upper bound such as 50k samples where feasible, because [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) recommends 50k for Inception score, [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) uses 25-sentence blocks and paired t-statistics, and [(Theis et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39e0c341351f8f4a39ac890b96217c7f4bde5369) shows sample-based estimates can require infeasible counts; where feasible, use cheaper ESMFold or OmegaFold for 50k pLDDT estimates and reserve AlphaFold for a smaller validation set. Third, add controlled perturbation and undertraining curves that mirror [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), including subsampling, amino-acid substitution noise at 0% to 30% in 5% increments, cluster elimination or imbalance, and checkpoint snapshots, to distinguish sensitivity to true quality from sensitivity to corruption or training state. Fourth, explicitly compare pLDDT across AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold, reporting inter-model correlations, rank stability, threshold behavior at pLDDT 70, and version-specific calibration, because [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) uses only ESMFold and [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) validates only AlphaFold. Fifth, supplement pLDDT and perplexity with diversity, novelty, and functional proxies, such as CD0.5 and CD95, FD-seq, MMD, EMD, TM-score, BLOSUM similarity, secondary-structure accuracy, and random-mutation or all-alanine baselines, following [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), and [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c). Finally, document reproducibility details missing from many related works, including random seeds, hardware, model checkpoints, MMD sigma values, clustering thresholds, and statistical tests, as emphasized by the gaps in [(Alamdari et al., 2024-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0f0b680ac74960922634e3da5cf1e6f6883c840) and [(Grnarova et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bd0445e48f576036514fa0b179ac6ae8d3c3b4a).

## Diversity Metrics Evaluation: We will utilize Cluster Density (CD) as our primary diversity metric, employing MMseqs2 for sequence clustering at 50% and 95% similarity thresholds to capture both broad diversity patterns and potential mode collapse scenarios. This provides a controlled way to measure whether generated sequences span distinct sequence families.

### Support

The strongest support for using Cluster Density (CD) comes from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), which directly evaluates CD at 50 percent sequence identity, denoted CD 0.5, as a diversity metric for DiMA-generated protein sequences against held-out natural test sets. This work operationalizes the same family of sequence-identity clustering that the proposed method plans to use, and it does so at a large sample size of 2,048 sequences per setting, making it the closest precedent for the proposed 50 percent CD component. It also situates CD within a broader evaluation suite including sequence Frechet distance, pLDDT, novelty, and other distributional or structural metrics, which supports the research plan's broader goal of comparing quality, diversity, and distributional similarity metrics, although it does not report the clustering software, exact dataset, confidence intervals, or statistical significance. A second, somewhat weaker but still relevant support is [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which evaluates protein sequence and structure generation with diversity measures including pairwise TM-score and Max Clust., and shows that high-quality models can have lower diversity, for example DPLM at length 100 has pLDDT 85.38 but pairwise TM 0.50 and Max Clust. 0.80, whereas EvoDiff has pLDDT 50.20, pairwise TM 0.43, and Max Clust. 0.98. Although this paper does not use MMseqs2 CD at 50 or 95 percent identity, it supports the proposed method's motivation that diversity must be measured explicitly and that diversity can trade off with quality. A third support is [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515), which uses a structural inner-TM metric, computed from ESMFold-predicted structures and averaged over sampled candidates, to assess unconditional protein sequence diversity; its finding that DPLM achieves low average inner-TM while maintaining pLDDT above 75 supports the general need for a diversity metric that can distinguish broad sampling from mode collapse, even though it does not validate MMseqs2-based CD and does not report exact inner-TM values, confidence intervals, or significance. The weakest support is [(Dip et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eecb9addcd1b9cabbb0027dd233f40cd25efb8f), which uses MMseqs2 clustering at 80, 60, and 40 percent similarity thresholds for train-test partitioning in pathogenicity classification of ESKAPEE bacterial and viral genomes, with an 8:2 cluster-level split, rather than generative-model diversity; it supports the broader premise that MMseqs2 similarity thresholds can materially change evaluation outcomes, but it does not directly support CD as a protein-generation diversity metric.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), because although it uses CD 0.5, the metric changes only from 0.981 with ESM-2 8M to 0.969 with ESM-2 3B, a difference of 0.012, while pLDDT improves from 74.3 to 83.4 and FD-seq improves from 0.560 to 0.314. This suggests that 50 percent CD may be too coarse to capture meaningful diversity differences or early mode collapse in some encoder-scaling settings, which challenges the proposed plan's reliance on CD as the primary diversity metric unless it is supplemented or paired with more sensitive metrics. A second contradiction is [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which uses structural/TM-score-based diversity and Max Clust. rather than MMseqs2 sequence-identity CD, and reports diversity estimates from only 50 sequences per model and length, with averages and standard deviations for sequence design and medians for backbone design. Its results show that DPLM can have the highest pLDDT but lower diversity than EvoDiff and ESM3, and that high-diversity backbone models often have lower quality, for example FoldFlow1 variants and Genie reach Max Clust. 1.00 but scTM 0.35 to 0.37 or 0.25, while Chroma has scTM 0.72 and Max Clust. 0.99. This contradicts a sequence-only CD approach by showing that structural diversity and quality can diverge, and it also warns that small sample sizes may make diversity estimates unstable relative to the proposed 2^5 to 2^14 sample-size analysis. A third contradiction is [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515), which evaluates diversity with average inner-TM in structure space rather than MMseqs2 clustering at 50 or 95 percent identity, and reports DPLM as highly diverse structurally while maintaining pLDDT above 75. Because it does not report CD, MMseqs2 thresholds, confidence intervals, or exact inner-TM values, it does not directly refute CD, but it contradicts the assumption that sequence-identity clustering alone is sufficient to characterize diversity, especially when structural mode collapse or structural novelty is of interest. The weakest contradiction is [(Dip et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eecb9addcd1b9cabbb0027dd233f40cd25efb8f), which uses MMseqs2 thresholds of 80, 60, and 40 percent for classification dataset construction and reports that performance improves as the threshold increases, but it does not evaluate protein generative models, CD, 50 or 95 percent thresholds, or diversity-specific outcomes; therefore it only weakly contradicts the proposed method by showing that threshold choice can alter results in a non-diversity task.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to treat CD as one component of a multi-metric diversity evaluation rather than the sole primary metric, adding structural diversity measures such as pairwise TM-score, average inner-TM, or Max Clust. computed from ESMFold, AlphaFold2, or OmegaFold predictions, because [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) and [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) show that structural diversity can reveal quality-diversity tradeoffs that sequence-only metrics may miss. The second most important action is to explicitly test the proposed 95 percent CD threshold against the 50 percent threshold in the planned synthetic cluster-elimination, cluster-imbalance, and intra-cluster-diversity-reduction experiments, reporting both raw cluster counts and normalized CD, because [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) only demonstrates 50 percent CD and shows limited sensitivity across encoder sizes, while the proposed plan's mode-collapse scenarios require evidence that 95 percent CD can detect finer-grained collapse. Third, the method should specify all MMseqs2 implementation details, including the exact clustering command, sequence coverage, e-value, minimum sequence length, and whether sequences are length-filtered or binned, and should report confidence intervals, bootstrap resampling, or paired significance tests for CD differences, since none of the related works provide full statistical uncertainty for diversity metrics. Fourth, the sample-size sensitivity analysis should use at least 2,048 sequences as a stable baseline, matching [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), while still evaluating 2^5 to 2^14 to quantify instability at smaller sizes such as the 50-sequence setting in [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef). Finally, the real-world validation should include natural reference sets such as UniRef50 or PDB-derived sequences, report hardware and runtime for MMseqs2 and structure prediction, and compare CD against distributional metrics such as FD-seq, MMD, IPR, D&C, and EMD so that the proposed metric is judged in the full context of the research plan.

## Distributional Similarity Metrics Assessment: We will evaluate five distributional similarity metrics including Improved Precision and Recall (IPR), Density and Coverage (D&C), Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD), Fréchet Distance (FD), and Earth Mover's Distance (EMD). These will be tested across different protein language models of varying sizes to assess the impact of model choice on metric behavior. This tests whether distributional metrics depend on the embedding model used to represent proteins.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), which directly tests whether distributional similarity measurements depend on the protein language model used to represent sequences. In DiMA protein sequence generation, the authors scale ESM-2 encoders from 8M to 3B parameters and compare ESM-C 300M with ESM-2 650M, sampling 2,048 generated sequences per condition against independent held-out natural protein test sets. They report that FD-seq decreases from 0.560 with ESM-2 8M to 0.314 with ESM-2 3B, while pLDDT increases from 74.3 to 83.4, showing that the embedding model materially changes measured distributional similarity and quality. The same study also evaluates MMD and 1-Wasserstein optimal transport, an Earth mover's distance analogue, on sequence and structure representations, which supports the proposed inclusion of FD, MMD, and EMD-family metrics. A second, weaker support is [(Khalaf et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/826a8b35c93eca0e1255b624ebd27b5473ea65d9), which uses EMD in a quantitative distribution-distance assessment of a ROD-WGAN protein structure generation model, comparing generated protein structural features against a reference distribution. This supports EMD as a plausible protein-domain distributional metric, but it does not test IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, or PLM-size effects, so it is less directly relevant than the Meshchaninov scaling analysis.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the most relevant recent work does not validate the full five-metric framework. [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) reports FD-seq, MMD, and 1-Wasserstein optimal transport, but does not benchmark Improved Precision and Recall or Density and Coverage, and it uses 1-Wasserstein rather than a separately named EMD metric. Its setting is natural DiMA sequence generation rather than the proposed controlled synthetic corruption, cluster elimination, cluster imbalance, or intra-cluster diversity reduction experiments, so it cannot confirm that IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD behave as expected under mode collapse or undertraining. A related contradiction to any assumption of metric invariance is that larger ESM-2 encoders improve distributional matching and quality, with FD-seq falling from 0.560 to 0.314 and pLDDT rising from 74.3 to 83.4, while CD 0.5 falls from 0.981 to 0.969, indicating that changes in distributional metrics may be confounded by generator quality and diversity rather than purely by the metric or embedding model. The weaker contradiction is that [(Khalaf et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/826a8b35c93eca0e1255b624ebd27b5473ea65d9) provides no numerical EMD values, baseline comparisons, statistical significance tests, dataset size, hardware, hyperparameters, reference distribution details, or exact structural features, so it cannot support or refute the proposed sensitivity analysis and is insufficient as standalone evidence. The newer Meshchaninov evidence therefore makes the older Khalaf EMD use less decisive for the proposed PLM-size sensitivity question.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the Meshchaninov scaling protocol as the backbone of the distributional similarity assessment: use ESM-2 encoders spanning 8M to 3B parameters, include ESM-C 300M as a non-ESM-2 architecture control, generate or embed 2,048 sequences per condition, and compare against independent held-out test sets while reporting FD-seq, MMD, 1-Wasserstein/EMD, pLDDT, CD 0.5, and novelty. Because the proposed method requires IPR and D&C, these should be added as explicit metrics and validated against the established FD-seq, MMD, and 1-Wasserstein results rather than assumed to be interchangeable. The EMD component should be implemented as a 1-Wasserstein optimal transport calculation with the same sample size, representation space, and solver settings as the FD and MMD comparisons, and the paper should state whether it is computed on sequence embeddings, structure embeddings, or both. To address the confounding shown by Meshchaninov, the synthetic experiments should hold generator quality and diversity as controlled variables: report pLDDT and CD 0.5 alongside all five distributional metrics, test correlations among metrics, and use the SwissProt 0%-30% amino-acid substitution and five-cluster mode-collapse designs to separate metric sensitivity from changes in sample quality. For reproducibility, include sample sizes from 2^5 to 2^14, confidence intervals or bootstrap tests, multiple-comparison corrections, and computational costs for embedding generation, especially for the 3B ESM-2 model, since the encoder is not needed at inference but embedding computation can dominate runtime. For MMD, document the RBF kernel sigma optimization across corruption, GPT2 progression, and real generator settings, and compare sigma choices to the Meshchaninov MMD baseline. Finally, use [(Khalaf et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/826a8b35c93eca0e1255b624ebd27b5473ea65d9) only as a secondary motivation for EMD on structural features, and if EMD is applied to structural features, specify the structural feature vector, reference distribution, distance solver, and significance testing, because that work lacks the quantitative detail needed to justify metric choices.

## Protein Quality Framework: Our approach will establish a framework for protein quality based on structural stability and self-consistency properties, leveraging the bidirectional mapping between sequence and structure spaces through folding and inverse folding functions. This grounds quality evaluation in structural self-consistency rather than relying on a single predictor score.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed bidirectional self-consistency framework comes from [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which directly evaluates protein generative models with sequence-structure self-consistency, using scTM and scRMSD for co-design, AlphaFold2 pLDDT for sequence design, ProteinMPNN inverse folding for backbone design, and physical rationality metrics such as clashes, CN-score, total energy, hydrophobic proportion, and AntiBERTy inverse perplexity. On 50 sequences per model and length, Multiflow achieved scTM 0.96±0.04 and scRMSD 1.10±0.71 at length 100, while native PDB references had scTM around 0.88-0.92, showing that self-consistency can be benchmarked against native baselines and length dependence. DPLM reached pLDDT 93.54±3.73 at length 200, supporting pLDDT as a quality signal but also showing model dependence. This newer benchmark directly extends the older pLDDT evidence from [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) and the inverse-folding/folding validation logic in [(Anand-Achim et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/752908b0c7ebd56d6075f616461992ca0fc8f342) by adding scTM, scRMSD, inverse folding, physical rationality, and native references, although it does not perform the plan's controlled 0-30% perturbation, 2^5 to 2^14 sample-size, or IPR/D&C/MMD/FD/EMD distributional-similarity analyses. Next, [(Melnyk et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c13a53332392f98cf621504cc4aaa426401a2bb) supports the inverse-folding side by regularizing a GVP inverse-folding model with an AFDistill structure-consistency score on CATH 4.2, using 18,204 training, 608 validation, and 1,120 test structures, with a fixed SC loss weight of 1. SC regularization improved recovery by 1 to 3 percentage points, increased diversity by up to 45%, and raised AlphaFold TM-score from 0.92 to 0.95 in one comparison, while pTM-based SC outperformed pLDDT-based SC, supporting the plan's emphasis on self-consistency but also warning that pLDDT alone may be suboptimal. Next, [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846) demonstrates a bidirectional sequence-structure pipeline using DARK, AlphaFold, and Rosetta, with pLDDT 91.4 for a 4-helix bundle, pLDDT 91.0 after AlphaFold refinement, pLDDT 93.1 for an immunoglobin-fold design, and Rosetta validation with 10,000 decoys costing 433 CPU hours and C-alpha RMSD 3.11 A, supporting the use of pLDDT plus structure-based agreement and physical relaxation, though the evidence is case-study level without formal significance tests. Next, [(Chu et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b794176493f02f655f7f0bb32c4584ff171363a2) supports the bidirectional design principle by jointly modeling all-atom structure with a U-ViT diffusion denoiser and sequence with miniMPNN, using the full ProteinMPNN at the final diffusion step to improve self-consistency, and reporting approximately 38% sequence recovery and mean scRMSD approximately 8 A in 1-5 sampling steps, but it does not report pLDDT, scPerplexity, stability, or statistical significance, so it is weaker evidence for a standardized metric framework. Next, [(Anand-Achim et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/752908b0c7ebd56d6075f616461992ca0fc8f342) provides an earlier inverse-folding/folding validation example using learned-potential sequence design, rotamer repacking, Rosetta AbInitio, and circular dichroism for a TIM-barrel design, supporting the conceptual loop but lacking numerical metric values, sample sizes, and statistical tests. Next, [(Anand, and Huang, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cc7cd57349e271f5970e423fca4b1dbf06d7006) supports structural self-consistency from an earlier GAN setting by generating alpha-carbon distance maps and requiring Rosetta or ADMM coordinate recovery, with generated-map reconstruction errors only slightly higher than real maps and recognizable secondary structure, but it is structure-only and does not use sequence inverse folding. Next, [(Ferruz et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcb31b98ec58f3fff9f94f148e2952595f017fd9) supports pLDDT as a large-scale folding-quality signal for 10,000 ProtGPT2 sequences of roughly 100-1000 residues, with about 73% pLDDT >50, about half pLDDT >70, roughly 80% ordered by disorder prediction, and multiple sequence clusters, but it is one-way and lacks inverse folding or scPerplexity. Next, [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) supports confidence-based stratification at scale using ESMFold on 617 million MGnify90 sequences in about 2 weeks on approximately 2,000 GPUs, with 59% good confidence at mean pLDDT and pTM >0.5, 36% high confidence at >0.7, and pLDDT-LDDT Pearson r=0.79 on a 4,000-sequence calibration subset. Next, [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) provides the foundational pLDDT evidence, with AlphaFold2 CASP14 median GDT_TS 92.4, LDDT 0.92, TM-score 0.94, and pLDDT strongly correlated with local accuracy. Weakest direct support is [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), which is not protein-specific but validates BLEU as a composite reference-based metric using 500 sentences, 20 blocks of 25 sentences, block-wise paired t-tests with 95% threshold 1.7, and correlations of 0.99 with monolingual and 0.96 with bilingual human scores, providing a template for ranking stability, reference variation, and sample-size sensitivity relevant to the plan's metric validation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction or caveat is that self-consistency and pLDDT are not universal quality certificates. [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) shows large model- and length-dependent variation: Multiflow scTM falls from 0.96±0.04 at length 100 to 0.83±0.15 at length 500, ProteinGenerator falls from 0.91±0.08 at length 100 to 0.41±0.19 at length 500, ESM3 is 0.72±0.19 at length 100 and 0.54±0.20 at length 500, and RFdiffusion backbone scTM falls from 0.98±0.12 at length 100 to 0.79±0.19 at length 500; pLDDT also varies widely, with DPLM 85.38±14.20 to 93.54±3.73 and ESM3 48.08±13.34 to 54.26±15.35. This newer benchmark refines the older pLDDT optimism in [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) and [(Ferruz et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcb31b98ec58f3fff9f94f148e2952595f017fd9) by showing that a single self-consistency threshold or pLDDT cutoff cannot define quality across lengths and models, and it supports the plan's need for controlled model comparison and length stratification. Next, [(Anand, and Huang, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cc7cd57349e271f5970e423fca4b1dbf06d7006) shows that foldable or self-consistent generated structures can still be non-native or implausible: the GAN produced low-energy non-native solutions, discriminator scores were artificially high because optimized during inpainting, and alignment error could penalize plausible non-native folds, so geometric recoverability alone does not guarantee biological quality. Next, [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846) reports that a simulated annealing AIG-score baseline produced structures described as an adversarial attack on AlphaFold, contradicting the idea that high pLDDT or AlphaFold-based self-consistency alone is sufficient without physical or energetic checks. Next, [(Melnyk et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c13a53332392f98cf621504cc4aaa426401a2bb) contradicts a pLDDT-centric design by finding pTM-based SC scores more effective than pLDDT-based scores, and it uses a fixed SC loss weight of 1 without systematic perturbation or sample-size analysis, so the proposed framework should not treat pLDDT as the dominant self-consistency signal without calibration. Next, [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) shows pLDDT is an imperfect proxy for accuracy, with Pearson r=0.79 against LDDT to AlphaFold2 on only 4,000 sequences, and it relies on pTM jointly with pLDDT for confidence thresholds, contradicting reliance on pLDDT alone. Next, [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) indicates pLDDT depends on MSA depth and is a predictor confidence score rather than a direct physical stability measure, with lower pLDDT and reduced accuracy for shallow MSAs, so the plan must control MSA depth and model version. Next, [(Ferruz et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcb31b98ec58f3fff9f94f148e2952595f017fd9) is one-way folding-based, using pLDDT thresholds >50 and >70 and disorder prediction, with MD and Rosetta only on a small subset, so it may overstate structural plausibility if inverse folding and energetic validation are omitted. Next, [(Chu et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b794176493f02f655f7f0bb32c4584ff171363a2) shows that bidirectional sequence-structure generation can be implemented without a standardized quality-metric suite, reporting only sequence recovery, scRMSD, and chemical validity, and without pLDDT, scPerplexity, stability scores, or significance tests, so it does not by itself support the proposed metric framework. Next, [(Anand-Achim et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/752908b0c7ebd56d6075f616461992ca0fc8f342) provides only qualitative outcomes without numerical metric values, effect sizes, sample sizes, or statistical significance, so it cannot by itself support robust metric claims. Weakest contradiction is [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), which shows composite reference-based metrics can be sensitive to reference count, sample blocks, and brevity penalties, with human scores 0.3468 against four references versus 0.2571 against two references and single-reference rank preservation, warning that protein metrics may also be unstable if reference or sample conditions are not controlled.

### Suggested Action

Most important action is to implement the proposed framework as a multi-signal bidirectional quality suite rather than a single score: combine folding confidence from AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold, including pLDDT and pTM, with inverse-folding self-consistency using ProteinMPNN or comparable tools, scPerplexity, scTM, scRMSD, and sequence recovery, and then add physical rationality checks such as clashes, CN-score, total energy, hydrophobic proportion, and Rosetta relax, following [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), [(Melnyk et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c13a53332392f98cf621504cc4aaa426401a2bb), [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846), [(Lee, and Kim, 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c4e0cef3aa5e26e16e58adcf4551d5047eeccf), and the co-design practice in [(Chu et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b794176493f02f655f7f0bb32c4584ff171363a2). Second, calibrate all quality metrics against native PDB references and controlled synthetic perturbations, reporting length-stratified baselines because [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) shows scTM and pLDDT change from length 100 to 500, and using block-wise or repeated-sample statistics similar to [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) to test the plan's 0-30% noise and 2^5 to 2^14 sample-size design. Third, explicitly compare pLDDT and pTM across structure predictors and MSA depths, because [(Melnyk et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c13a53332392f98cf621504cc4aaa426401a2bb) found pTM-based SC more effective than pLDDT-based SC, [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) used pLDDT and pTM jointly with r=0.79 calibration, and [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) showed MSA depth affects pLDDT; the plan should report pLDDT/pTM versus LDDT/TM, MSA depth, sequence length, and model version. Fourth, include a physical validation subset with Rosetta relax or equivalent, reporting decoy counts and compute cost, because [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846) used 10,000 Rosetta decoys and 433 CPU hours, [(Lee, and Kim, 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c4e0cef3aa5e26e16e58adcf4551d5047eeccf) used Rosetta relaxation on 282 structures with nine sequences per structure, and [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) used clashes, CN-score, total energy, and hydrophobicity; this will guard against adversarial high-pLDDT cases such as those reported by [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846) and non-native foldable solutions in [(Anand, and Huang, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cc7cd57349e271f5970e423fca4b1dbf06d7006). Fifth, retain the plan's diversity and distributional-similarity analyses but anchor them to existing protein-specific diversity signals, using Cluster Density at 50% and 95% MMseqs2 thresholds, maximum TM-score, pairwise TM-score, cluster counts, and sequence-similarity networks, as motivated by [(Ferruz et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcb31b98ec58f3fff9f94f148e2952595f017fd9) and [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), while noting that IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD are not directly validated in the supplied related work and therefore require the plan's synthetic cluster-elimination, cluster-imbalance, and intra-cluster-diversity-reduction tests. Sixth, report computational reproducibility details, including GPU/CPU hours, sampling counts, Rosetta decoys, MMD sigma, clustering thresholds, and random seeds, because related work ranges from 2,000 GPUs for [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), 433 CPU hours for [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846), and 10 V100 GPU-day estimates in [(Moffat et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc9e3d2128ca9c11cd401a884d72183beb6a846) to unspecified resources in [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef). Least important but still useful is to adopt BLEU-style validation practices from [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), such as block-wise paired tests, reference-count sensitivity, and correlation with external judgments, adapted to protein metrics by using native PDB, experimental structures, or physical validation as external anchors.

## Training Progress Simulation: Using the SwissProt dataset as reference, we will introduce controlled perturbations by randomly substituting amino acids while preserving overall amino acid distribution. Noise levels will range from 0% to 30% in 5% increments to simulate varying degrees of model undertraining. This creates a known degradation signal to test whether metrics can detect undertrained generation.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Training Progress Simulation comes from [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), because it directly uses random amino-acid substitution as a controlled degradation baseline for protein sequence generation. That work substitutes a 25-100% proportion of residues with one of the 20 standard amino acids, excluding the original residue, and evaluates the perturbed sequences with protein-relevant metrics including normalized BLOSUM62 global pairwise alignment with gap open penalty -0.5 and gap continue penalty -0.1, PSIPRED secondary-structure accuracy using PSI-BLAST multiple sequence alignments against UniRef90 with confidence greater than 0.5, and conformational energy analysis. This supports the central premise of the proposed method that random substitution can create a known degradation signal for evaluating protein generative models, and it makes the SwissProt-based 0-30% perturbation experiment plausible as a milder, undertraining-oriented analogue. The next strongest support is methodological rather than domain-specific: [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) demonstrates that controlled quality degradation can be used to test whether an automatic metric detects and ranks known quality differences reliably. Its five-system evaluation, 500-sentence test corpus, 20 blocks of 25 sentences, paired t-statistics between adjacent systems, single-reference robustness check, and strong correlation with human judgments, 0.99 for monolingual judges and 0.96 for bilingual judges, directly support the research plan need to test whether pLDDT, perplexity, pseudoperplexity, self-consistency perplexity, Cluster Density, IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD can detect graded degradation. The next strongest support is from [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which evaluates real protein generative models, ProGen2, EvoDiff, DPLM, and ESM3, at sequence lengths 100, 200, 300, and 500 using AlphaFold2 pLDDT, model perplexity, pairwise TM-score, maximum cluster value, and maximum TM-score to the PDB. This supports the proposed metric pipeline because it shows that pLDDT, perplexity, diversity, and novelty metrics are already used together in protein generation evaluation, and its use of 50 sequences per model and length with reported standard deviations supports the planned 2^5 to 2^14 sample-size sensitivity analysis. [(Hu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c757ffd5b8cc24ba12c9b0d80fecb7c035097b7d) supports the training-progress aspect of the proposed method by analyzing training and validation loss curves with moving-average smoothing and comparing hyperparameter choices such as average pooling versus attention pooling, learning rate, dropout rate, and sqrt noise schedule. Its evaluation of 50 held-out CATH 4.3 structure templates with 200 generated sequences per template, ESMFold structure prediction, and diversity, novelty, and consistency metrics also supports using multiple protein-specific metrics rather than relying on a single quality score. [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) supports the need for controlled generation conditions in protein sequence evaluation. It uses 95-residue CM2-family sequences, a default E. coli pheA seed, alternative 20-residue and empty seeds, sequential sampling, random sampling of 10% of positions per iteration for 20 iterations, and a burn-in phase using the highest-probability amino acid for the final 10 iterations. Its evaluation with similarity, HMM profile scores, first- and second-order amino-acid statistics, second-order correlations, and GB-1 variant prediction supports adding local compositional and family-specific controls to the SwissProt perturbation experiment. The weakest but still relevant support is from [(Li et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651e5bcc14f14605a879303e97572a27ea8c7956), which shows that sequence-generation metrics can be sensitive to decoding settings, reference count, and objective function. Its use of BLEU, distinct-1, distinct-2, beam-size variation, and multi-reference versus single-reference evaluation supports the broader research plan goal of testing metric sensitivity under controlled conditions, although it is not protein-specific and does not use amino-acid substitution or undertraining simulation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest protein-domain precedent, [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), does not validate the proposed 0-30% noise range or the proposed distribution-preserving substitution protocol. It uses random substitution at 25-100% mutation levels, excludes the original residue, and also uses an all-alanine baseline for conformational energy, but the supplied excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance for the random-mutation baselines. This leaves open whether 5% increments up to 30% will produce a detectable degradation signal in pLDDT, perplexity, pseudoperplexity, self-consistency perplexity, Cluster Density, or distributional similarity metrics. Its substitution protocol also does not explicitly preserve the overall amino-acid distribution, whereas the proposed method does, so preserving composition may weaken the degradation signal or create a different type of perturbation than the one tested in that work. The next strongest contradiction comes from [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which shows that pLDDT, perplexity, and diversity metrics can dissociate across protein generative models. DPLM had the highest pLDDT and lowest perplexity across lengths, for example pLDDT 93.54±3.73 and perplexity 4.61±2.63 at length 200, but lower diversity, with maximum cluster values of 0.70 at length 200 and pairwise TM-scores of 0.54±0.24 at length 200. EvoDiff and ESM3 had lower pLDDT, for example 50.20±10.27 and 54.26±15.35 at length 100, but higher or comparable diversity, with maximum cluster values of 0.98-1.00 and 0.90-1.00. Because the benchmark uses only 50 sequences per model and length and does not report formal significance tests, this contradicts the assumption that the proposed metrics will uniformly and reliably detect undertraining without large sample sizes, confidence intervals, and model-specific interpretation. [(Hu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c757ffd5b8cc24ba12c9b0d80fecb7c035097b7d) also contradicts the sufficiency of synthetic SwissProt noise as a direct proxy for undertraining. It reports that training and validation loss curves rapidly converge to a stable state and uses hyperparameter comparisons to justify model choices, but it does not show that controlled amino-acid substitution reproduces the metric trajectory of an actually undertrained generator. Its reported superiority on 9 of 10 metrics also lacks confidence intervals or statistical significance tests, which weakens the evidence that metric differences in protein generation are robust without explicit statistical validation. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) warns that metric scores can depend strongly on reference count and sample size. In that work, the human translator scored 0.3468 against four references but 0.2571 against two references, and the 25-sentence block analysis provided only an upper-bound variance estimate for larger test sets. This contradicts any assumption that the proposed SwissProt perturbation experiment can use small samples or a single reference model without testing reference dependence and statistical reliability. [(Li et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651e5bcc14f14605a879303e97572a27ea8c7956) contradicts overreliance on likelihood-based perplexity metrics. It explicitly did not use perplexity because the MMI objective was intended to move away from standard likelihood-based generation, and it showed that BLEU performance depended on reference count and decoding beam size. This cautions that ppl, pppl, and scPerplexity may not capture quality for protein generators whose objectives are not standard autoregressive likelihood, including masked, diffusion, or multimodal models. The weakest contradiction is from [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c), which provides no quantitative outcomes and does not test random substitution, SwissProt-wide perturbation, or undertraining simulation. Therefore it cannot confirm that its CM2-specific controlled sampling and evaluation metrics will generalize to the proposed 0-30% SwissProt degradation experiment.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to revise the perturbation protocol so that the degradation signal is both scientifically defensible and likely detectable. The proposed 0-30% range in 5% increments should be retained as a mild undertraining simulation, but the experiment should also include higher corruption levels, for example 40%, 50%, 75%, and 100%, as well as an all-alanine or random-uniform baseline, following the random-mutation baseline design in [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3). The distribution-preserving substitution should be implemented explicitly, for example by drawing replacement residues from the empirical SwissProt amino-acid frequencies or a position-aware SwissProt frequency table while excluding the original residue, and the paper should report whether preservation is global, family-specific, or position-specific. The protocol should also specify the random seed, number of SwissProt sequences, length distribution, family controls, and whether substitutions are applied independently or with constraints. The second most important action is to add the statistical reliability apparatus demonstrated by [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) and motivated by [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef). The experiment should compute paired differences or bootstrap confidence intervals across noise levels, test monotonicity and statistical significance for pLDDT, ppl, pppl, scPerplexity, Cluster Density at 50% and 95% similarity, IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD, and use the planned 2^5 to 2^14 sample sizes to determine the minimum reliable sample size. Sequence-level or family-level resampling should be used to account for autocorrelation among related SwissProt sequences. The third most important action is to broaden the metric suite with protein-specific controls from [(Madani et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f7074a264356c9a022a8dff24df79d1db8c3d3), [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c), and [(Hu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c757ffd5b8cc24ba12c9b0d80fecb7c035097b7d). In addition to pLDDT and perplexity-based metrics, the experiment should include BLOSUM62 alignment to SwissProt references, PSIPRED or ESMFold secondary-structure accuracy, HMM or Pfam profile scores, GB-1 or equivalent variant-effect prediction, first- and second-order amino-acid statistics, ESMFold, AlphaFold, or OmegaFold pLDDT, TM-score, RMSD, and consistency metrics, and diversity or novelty via TM-score or Foldseek. This prevents the experiment from relying solely on metrics that may be model-specific or insensitive to local sequence corruption. The fourth most important action is to control generation and model confounds as in [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c), [(Hu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c757ffd5b8cc24ba12c9b0d80fecb7c035097b7d), and [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef). The experiment should fix sequence length, seed, sampling or decoding mode, beam size or temperature, tokenizer, and model checkpoint, and should compare structure predictors AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold for pLDDT, protein language models such as ESM-2 and ProtT5 of varying sizes for distributional metrics, and autoregressive models such as ProtGPT2, ProGen2, and RITA for perplexity. Hardware, hyperparameters, random seeds, and software versions should be reported to support reproducibility. The fifth most important action is to validate the synthetic undertraining signal against real undertrained checkpoints or actual training-progress curves, using the loss-curve and hyperparameter-ablation approach of [(Hu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c757ffd5b8cc24ba12c9b0d80fecb7c035097b7d). The researchers should test whether 0-30% SwissProt noise predicts the metric trajectory observed during real training, rather than assuming that random substitution is equivalent to undertraining. The final action is to address the perplexity limitation highlighted by [(Li et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651e5bcc14f14605a879303e97572a27ea8c7956) by reporting correlations among ppl, pppl, scPerplexity, pLDDT, and non-likelihood metrics, and by interpreting perplexity cautiously for non-likelihood, masked, diffusion, or multimodal protein generators.

## Diversity Assessment Setup: We will construct a dataset from five distinct protein families that naturally form well-defined clusters, then introduce three systematic variations:
   - Cluster Elimination: Sequential removal of entire clusters to simulate mode collapse
   - Cluster Imbalance: Progressive reduction of four clusters while maintaining one at full size
   - Intra-cluster Diversity Reduction: Gradual replacement of unique sequences with duplicates within clusters
This creates controlled diversity failures to test whether diversity metrics detect mode collapse, imbalance, and reduced within-cluster variation.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Diversity Assessment Setup comes from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), which uses the same Cluster Density concept at 50 percent sequence identity, reports CD 0.5 across multiple thresholds, evaluates 2,048 generated sequences per encoder setting, and shows that CD 0.5 changes with encoder scale from 0.981 for ESM-2 8M to 0.969 for ESM-2 3B, with ESM-C 300M at 0.963 versus ESM-2 650M at 0.986, while pLDDT improves from 74.3 to 83.4 and FD-seq decreases from 0.560 to 0.314. This directly supports using CD with MMseqs2-style 50 percent clustering, testing multiple identity thresholds, and using sample sizes within the proposed 2^5 to 2^14 range, especially around 2^11. The next strongest support is [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), which benchmarks ProGen2 700M, EvoDiff, DPLM 650M, and ESM3 1.4B with 50 sequences per length, pairwise TM-score, Max Clust, pLDDT, perplexity, and Max TM, and reports standard deviations such as DPLM length 100 pLDDT 85.38±14.20 and pairwise TM 0.50±0.20 versus EvoDiff length 100 pLDDT 50.20±10.27 and Max Clust 0.98. This supports the plan's real-world validation and the need to compare cluster-based diversity with quality and novelty metrics, although its 50-sequence protocol is smaller than the proposed upper sample-size range. Next, [(Kong et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/706374d6db5eb6aa3fe8d857f43e5e62d5853337) supports cluster-based evaluation by generating 40 peptide candidates per receptor, using hierarchical C-alpha RMSD and sequence-similarity clustering, reporting sequence-structure consistency with Cramer's V, and showing that successful designs had diversity 0.632 versus 0.506 for all designs, with Rosetta delta-G success rates. This supports adding cluster consistency and quality controls to the proposed five-family diversity test. Next, [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) supports evaluating whether a diversity metric captures meaningful population differences by using DPLM unconditional sampling at lengths 100 to 1000 with 500 reverse diffusion steps, ESMFold structure prediction, and average pairwise TM-score as inner-TM, reporting low inner-TM and pLDDT above 75, which motivates a structural complement to sequence CD. Next, [(Luo et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37355fe82b7a9cf96ead194018b1775eec9af605) provides weaker but relevant support by filtering SAbDab to antibody-antigen complexes below 4 Å resolution, clustering CDR-H3 at 50 percent identity, manually selecting five clusters containing 19 complexes, generating 100 samples per CDR, and comparing amino acid recovery, CDR C-alpha RMSD, and IMP against RosettaAntibodyDesign, which aligns with the proposed five-cluster and 50 percent identity design but only indirectly because it does not use a formal diversity metric. The weakest support is [(Nathansen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da7cf88ff2eeecc69fc412d49fcf5c5d4c45c052), which uses clustered train/test splits at 100, 95, 65, and 35 percent identity for RITA-S/M/L prompt tuning, with 10-token prompts, Adam learning rate 0.001, batch size 2, up to 300 epochs, early stopping after 20 epochs, three seeds, and ProtCNN PF03272 family membership using a 120-residue window and stride 10, supporting multiple identity thresholds and family-specific validation but not controlled diversity failures.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension with the proposed method is that cluster-based diversity metrics can saturate or be underpowered in existing evaluations. [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) reports Max Clust reaching 1.00 for EvoDiff and ESM3 at longer lengths, while using only 50 generated sequences per model and length and no formal significance tests, suggesting that a cluster metric may fail to distinguish high-diversity outputs and that small sample sizes may be insufficient for reliable cluster-density estimates. This directly motivates the proposed sample-size sweep from 2^5 to 2^14 but also warns that the planned CD metric must be checked for saturation and variance. The next strongest concern is that CD may be insensitive to subtle diversity changes unless paired with quality and distributional metrics: [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) observes only a modest CD 0.5 decrease from 0.981 to 0.969 across ESM-2 8M to 3B while pLDDT improves from 74.3 to 83.4 and FD-seq improves from 0.560 to 0.314, and it does not test controlled mode collapse, imbalance, or intra-cluster duplication. Next, [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) contradicts the assumption that sequence-level cluster density alone is sufficient by using structural inner-TM from ESMFold-predicted structures rather than sequence clustering, and by not comparing against a controlled collapse baseline, indicating that sequence CD could miss structural mode collapse. Next, [(Kong et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/706374d6db5eb6aa3fe8d857f43e5e62d5853337) shows that cluster-based diversity on real design candidates can be confounded with task success, because successful peptide designs had diversity 0.632 versus 0.506 for all designs, and the study uses 40 candidates per receptor, Rosetta delta-G, and no error bars or controlled synthetic perturbations, so it does not validate metric sensitivity to the proposed failures. Next, [(Luo et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37355fe82b7a9cf96ead194018b1775eec9af605) provides limited contradictory evidence because it uses only five manually selected CDR-H3 clusters and 19 complexes, interprets higher CDR C-alpha RMSD as greater diversity rather than computing a formal diversity metric, and reports no numerical values, confidence intervals, or significance tests, making it weak evidence for a controlled CD benchmark. The weakest concern is [(Nathansen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da7cf88ff2eeecc69fc412d49fcf5c5d4c45c052), where base-model perplexity did not fluctuate much across 100, 95, 65, and 35 percent clustered test sets, suggesting that sequence-identity clustering alone may not capture all evaluation-relevant differences, although the paper focuses on prompt-tuning perplexity rather than diversity.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed Diversity Assessment Setup should be implemented as a ground-truth controlled benchmark with explicit five-family cluster labels, MMseqs2 clustering at 50 and 95 percent identity, and a full sample-size sweep from 2^5 to 2^14, reporting bootstrap confidence intervals, multiple random seeds, and significance tests for each perturbation level, because [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) shows cluster metrics can saturate with only 50 sequences and [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) uses 2,048 sequences to obtain stable CD estimates. Second, add secondary diversity metrics beyond CD, including pairwise TM-score or Max Clust for sequence/structure diversity and Cramer's V for sequence-structure consistency, because [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) uses ESMFold-based inner-TM and [(Kong et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/706374d6db5eb6aa3fe8d857f43e5e62d5853337) uses hierarchical C-alpha RMSD, sequence clustering, and consistency metrics. Third, pair diversity with quality and distributional metrics such as pLDDT, perplexity, FD, MMD, EMD, IPR, and D&C, and include family-specific validity checks such as ProtCNN or a family classifier, because [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) reports a quality-diversity trade-off, [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) compares pLDDT and perplexity with diversity, and [(Nathansen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da7cf88ff2eeecc69fc412d49fcf5c5d4c45c052) uses ProtCNN family membership with a 120-residue window and stride 10. Fourth, define detection performance for the three perturbations by plotting metric response against known cluster elimination, imbalance, and intra-cluster duplication levels, and report minimum detectable effect, AUC or sensitivity, and threshold behavior, so that the study can show whether CD 0.5 and CD 0.95 detect mode collapse, imbalance, and reduced within-cluster variation. Fifth, use at least 2,048 samples where computationally feasible for real-world validation, while retaining the 2^5 to 2^14 sweep to identify the minimum reliable sample size, and report hardware, random seeds, clustering implementation, and repetition counts to improve reproducibility, following the sample-size and metric-reporting gaps in [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515), [(Kong et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/706374d6db5eb6aa3fe8d857f43e5e62d5853337), and [(Luo et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37355fe82b7a9cf96ead194018b1775eec9af605).

## Sample Size Sensitivity Analysis: We will systematically evaluate metric behavior across different sample sizes (2^5 to 2^14) and perturbation levels to determine minimum sample size requirements for reliable estimates. This addresses the practical question of how many generated sequences are needed for trustworthy metric estimates.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the most recent protein-domain benchmark, which used a fixed sample of 50 generated sequences per model and target length of 100, 200, 300, and 500 residues and reported nontrivial standard deviations across repeated experiments, directly motivating the proposed sweep from 2^5 to 2^14 and perturbation-level analysis in the research plan. In [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef), DPLM pLDDT was 85.38±14.20 at length 100, 93.54±3.73 at length 200, 93.07±5.77 at length 300, and 87.73±11.61 at length 500, while ProGen2 pLDDT was 64.00±21.26 at length 100 and 61.45±20.17 at length 500; these large spreads at n=50 show that the sample size already inside the proposed range may be insufficient for stable quality estimates, especially for pLDDT, perplexity, diversity, and novelty. The same work also reported diversity and novelty variability, with DPLM pairwise TM-score 0.50±0.20 to 0.57±0.25, maximum cluster value 0.63 to 0.80, perplexity 6.21±3.10 to 3.33±1.80, EvoDiff pLDDT 50.20±10.27 to 43.14±5.16, ESM3 pLDDT 54.26±15.35 to 52.17±10.52, and maximum TM-score to PDB 0.54±0.03 to 0.80±0.01, supporting the plan's need to test quality, diversity, and distributional metrics under controlled sample sizes. A second, domain-specific support comes from [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f), which evaluated Mistral-7B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Gemma-7B protein LLMs trained on a UniRef50 Homo sapiens subset filtered to 60,000 sequences below 512 tokens, with 42,000 training and 1,480 test sequences on four Nvidia A6000 GPUs, sequence length 512, 2,000 steps, batch size 1, gradient accumulation 16, learning rate 5e-5, cosine scheduler, weight decay 0.01, 150 warm-up steps, bfloat16, DeepSpeed, and FlashAttention2; evaluation used 250 generated sequences of 70 to 140 amino acids for mean AlphaFold2 pLDDT across five models per sequence, and only 20 structures per model for Foldseek TM-score, intra-RMSD, PyMOL RMSD, and Rosetta-Relax REU, with natural and random baselines. Although it did not vary sample size, its fixed-sample design, the reported REU optimal range of -100 to -300, and the claim that P-Mistral outperformed other models across pLDDT, RMSD, TM-score, and REU illustrate the exact finite-sample evaluation gap that the proposed method would address, particularly because 20 structures is below the proposed 2^5=32 lower bound and 250 is near 2^8=256. The earliest methodological support is [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), which used a 500-sentence Chinese-to-English corpus from 40 general news stories, 20 blocks of 25 sentences, five systems, mean and variance of block BLEU, and paired t-statistics with a cited 95% threshold of 1.7 and an example t-statistic of 6 between adjacent systems; it also noted that a human translator scored 0.3468 against four references and 0.2571 against two references, showing reference-dependence. This supports the proposed use of finite-sample blocks, variance, and significance testing to determine when metric rankings become reliable, but it is weaker support because it is NLP, uses only one block size, and does not sweep 2^5 to 2^14 or introduce amino-acid perturbation levels. Read iteratively, Papineni established the finite-sample metric-stability idea, Zeinalipour moved it to protein LLMs but kept fixed samples, and Ye further showed variability at n=50, making the proposed systematic sweep a natural next step.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is that the newer protein work appears to move away from the older finite-sample caution by using very small samples without uncertainty quantification, which could be misread as evidence that the proposed minimum-sample-size analysis is unnecessary. In [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f), despite a 1,480-sequence test set, 250 generated sequences were used for pLDDT and only 20 generated structures per model were used for TM-score, intra-RMSD, inter-RMSD, and REU, yet no confidence intervals, variance across sample sizes, statistical significance tests, random seeds, or minimum-sample-size requirements were reported; the paper still claimed that P-Mistral consistently outperformed other models and that fine-tuned models matched or surpassed larger-dataset models. This does not prove that 20 or 250 samples are reliable, but it contradicts the proposed method's implicit assumption that the field currently lacks a clear minimum-sample-size standard by showing that small fixed samples are already being used as if sufficient. A weaker tension comes from [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), where 25-sentence blocks produced block-level BLEU means close to the aggregate 500-sentence scores and adjacent-system comparisons were reported as highly significant, with t=6 against a 1.7 threshold. This could be interpreted as evidence that small samples may be adequate when effect sizes are large, which would weaken the need for a universal minimum sample size; however, the evidence is limited to BLEU, one block size, one corpus of about 500 sentences from 40 news stories, and two human references, so it is not strong evidence against protein-specific metrics such as pLDDT, CD, IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, or EMD. The weakest tension is that [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) still produced interpretable mean±standard-deviation comparisons at only 50 sequences per model and length, which could be taken to support 50 as a practical benchmark size; nevertheless, the large standard deviations, especially DPLM pLDDT 85.38±14.20 at length 100 and ProGen2 pLDDT 64.00±21.26 at length 100, actually support the proposed sensitivity analysis rather than contradict it.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed sample-size sensitivity analysis should be converted from a descriptive sweep into a formal reliability protocol: for each sample size from 2^5=32 to 2^14=16384, compute bootstrap or block-bootstrap confidence intervals, paired permutation or t-tests for adjacent model rankings, rank-stability curves, and a pre-registered convergence criterion such as CI width below a practical threshold, stable top-k ranking, or achieved statistical power. This directly extends the paired t-statistic and variance logic of [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) to protein metrics and addresses the missing CIs and significance tests in [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f). Second, integrate the planned perturbation levels with the sample-size sweep by using the SwissProt synthetic corruption setup at 0%, 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30% amino-acid substitution, and by using the five-family cluster dataset for cluster elimination, cluster imbalance, and intra-cluster diversity reduction; report how the minimum sample size changes as perturbation increases, because [(Ye et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c6729bd4b2b3bdf013482b499c7401ab6de44ef) shows that variability is length- and model-dependent, so a single minimum sample size is unlikely. Third, make the analysis computationally feasible by generating a large pool of sequences once, then using nested or stratified subsamples for 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, and 16384, while caching AlphaFold2/ESMFold/OmegaFold pLDDT, MMseqs2 CD at 50% and 95%, perplexity/pseudoperplexity/scPerplexity, IPR, D&C, MMD with the planned RBF sigma analysis, FD, and EMD; given that [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f) used five AlphaFold2 models per sequence and only 250 sequences for pLDDT, the plan should report hardware, wall-clock time, and whether 16384 sequences are feasible or whether a cheaper proxy is used for the largest sizes. Fourth, include model-choice sensitivity as a first-class factor, because the plan already compares AlphaFold, ESMFold, OmegaFold, ESM-2/ProtT5, and ProtGPT2/ProGen2/RITA, and because [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) showed that BLEU depended on the number of references, analogous to protein metrics depending on the folding model, PLM, or clustering threshold. Finally, improve reproducibility by fixing random seeds for generation, subsampling, clustering, and structure prediction, reporting the number of generation runs, and using natural and random baselines similar to those in [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f), so that the minimum sample size recommendation is tied to a concrete evaluation protocol rather than an abstract sample-size curve.

## Model Comparison Studies: We will investigate the impact of different underlying models:
- Structure prediction models (AlphaFold, ESMFold, OmegaFold) for pLDDT calculations
- Protein language models (ESM-2 family, ProtT5) of varying sizes for distributional similarity metrics
- Autoregressive models (ProtGPT2, ProGen2, RITA) for perplexity calculations
This separates metric behavior from the choice of scoring or embedding model.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed model-comparison study is that perplexity and sequence-recovery metrics are not invariant to the underlying inverse-folding model, scale, or training data: [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) reports a CATH topology split with 16,153 training, 1,457 validation, and 1,797 test structures, where GVP-GNN trained only on CATH achieves 42.2 percent native sequence recovery, GVP-GNN-large trained only on CATH drops to 39.2 percent, adding 12 million AlphaFold2-predicted UniRef50 structures degrades the smaller model to 38.6 percent, while GVP-GNN-large improves to 50.8 percent and GVP-Transformer improves to 51.6 percent, with perplexity generally increasing with solvent-accessible surface area. This directly motivates the proposed comparison of ProtGPT2, ProGen2, and RITA for perplexity rather than treating perplexity as a universal quality score. A second strong support is that protein language model size changes distributional similarity and quality metrics: [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) evaluates 2,048 generated sequences per condition and finds pLDDT increasing from 74.3 for ESM-2 8M to 83.4 for ESM-2 3B, FD-seq decreasing from 0.560 to 0.314, and CD0.5 falling from 0.981 to 0.969, supporting the proposed comparison of ESM-2 family and ProtT5 for IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD. A further support is cross-predictor pLDDT behavior: [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) reports ESMFold pLDDT with a Pearson correlation of 0.79 to LDDT against AlphaFold2 predictions on about 4,000 metagenomic sequences, and uses mean pLDDT and pTM thresholds of 0.5, 0.7, and pLDDT greater than 0.9 across 617 million MGnify90 predictions, showing that pLDDT is informative but model-specific. Additional support comes from AlphaFold2 validation: [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) reports median GDT-TS of 92.4 on CASP14 and 91.3 on a time-separated PDB set, with pLDDT calibrated to local accuracy and inference using MSAs capped at 1000 sequences, 20 recycling steps, and 5 independently initialized models, which provides a reproducible AlphaFold pLDDT baseline. [(Dauparas et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe) support pLDDT as a foldability proxy for inverse-designed sequences, where ProteinMPNN designs had higher AlphaFold pLDDT than baselines and 89 of 100 de novo designs folded while 7 of 10 designed binders bound the target. Weaker but relevant support is that structure predictor choice affects runtime, success, and accuracy: [(Lai, and Chen, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c9b1885765a9bcd4539642de1295491f84fdb4) compares AlphaFold, RoseTTAFold, and ESMFold on 299 reference and 964 alternative ClinVar sequences, finding ESMFold fastest but with the worst TM-score, RoseTTAFold with better TM-score but fewer successes, and an ESMFold practical limit near 850 amino acids on 32 GB VRAM. Finally, [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) uses ESMFold pLDDT greater than 70 and 75 for DPLM foldability and novelty, and OmegaFold with motif-RMSD below 1 angstrom and pLDDT above 70 for motif-scaffolding, but without a controlled same-sequence comparison of AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is to any assumption that pLDDT can be interpreted as a model-independent quality score without reporting the predictor: [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) shows ESMFold pLDDT only correlates at 0.79 with LDDT to AlphaFold2 predictions, and its confidence categories are defined by ESMFold-specific mean pLDDT and pTM thresholds, so an ESMFold pLDDT above 70 is not automatically equivalent to an AlphaFold pLDDT above 70. A second contradiction is that predictor choice changes accuracy and success in ways that cannot be captured by a single pLDDT threshold: [(Lai, and Chen, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c9b1885765a9bcd4539642de1295491f84fdb4) finds ESMFold has the worst TM-score but more successful predictions, RoseTTAFold has better TM-score but fewer successes, and AlphaFold remains accurate after intersecting best PDB sets, contradicting a universal best predictor or universal threshold. A further contradiction is that perplexity is confounded by model scale and training data: [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) shows adding AlphaFold2-predicted UniRef50 structures degrades smaller GVP-GNN but improves larger GVP-GNN-large and GVP-Transformer, so perplexity comparisons across ProtGPT2, ProGen2, and RITA are not interpretable unless training data, tokenization, and architecture are controlled. A weaker contradiction is that [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) varies the generative encoder rather than only the metric scoring model, so its evidence that ESM-2 size changes pLDDT, FD-seq, and CD0.5 does not by itself isolate the effect of the embedding model used for distributional similarity. Another weaker contradiction is that [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ec09a9534fe41d3a5231693050150e0bbec8515) assigns ESMFold to unconditional pLDDT evaluation and OmegaFold to motif-scaffolding without a controlled same-sequence predictor comparison or statistical significance tests, illustrating the risk of ad hoc predictor assignment. Finally, although [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) and [(Dauparas et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe) strongly validate AlphaFold pLDDT, they do not establish interchangeability with ESMFold or OmegaFold, and they also contradict the expectation that existing benchmarks can be reused directly for the proposed three-way comparison because none compares AlphaFold, ESMFold, and OmegaFold on identical generated sequences.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed method should run a controlled same-sequence pLDDT benchmark in which identical generated sequences, synthetic perturbation sets, and real generator outputs are predicted by AlphaFold2, ESMFold, and OmegaFold under fixed settings, reporting per-sequence pLDDT, pTM, success rate, runtime, VRAM, MSA depth, recycling, and sequence length, with paired statistical tests and confidence intervals across sample sizes 2^5 to 2^14. The AlphaFold protocol should specify MSA depth, recycling, and model count, using the CASP14-style settings of 1000 MSA sequences, 20 recycling steps, and 5 independently initialized models from [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815), while ESMFold and OmegaFold should be tested for length limits, memory, and failure modes, motivated by the 32 GB VRAM and approximately 850 amino acid ESMFold limit reported by [(Lai, and Chen, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c9b1885765a9bcd4539642de1295491f84fdb4). The next priority is to calibrate pLDDT against structural agreement or experimental outcomes: use cross-predictor LDDT as in [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), TM-score where experimental structures are available as in [(Lai, and Chen, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c9b1885765a9bcd4539642de1295491f84fdb4), and foldability or binding assays where feasible, using the 89 of 100 folded ProteinMPNN designs and 7 of 10 binder successes from [(Dauparas et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/98926d43356e87c22c82efc132dcaaac1ff40ebe) as a validation target. For perplexity, compare ProtGPT2, ProGen2, and RITA on identical sequences while reporting tokenization, vocabulary, training data, sequence length, core versus surface composition, solvent-accessible surface area, and sampling temperature, and include native sequence recovery or inverse-folding perplexity where applicable, following the CATH topology split, leakage filtering, and TM-score-filtered subset used by [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f). For distributional similarity, compare ESM-2 sizes and ProtT5 on identical generated sets of at least 2,048 sequences, reporting embedding dimension, normalization, sample size, MMD RBF sigma, FD, EMD, IPR, D&C, and MMseqs2 CD0.5 and CD95, using the ESM-2 8M, 650M, and 3B results from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) as a baseline while adding statistical significance and sample-size sensitivity. Finally, the study should report full computational reproducibility, including GPU count, wall-clock time, MSA resources, random seeds, and threshold choices, and should either include RoseTTAFold or explicitly justify its omission, because [(Lai, and Chen, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c9b1885765a9bcd4539642de1295491f84fdb4) shows that predictor choice affects both accuracy and practical success.

## Parameter Optimization: We will conduct comprehensive analysis of the MMD RBF kernel parameter σ across three experimental settings: controlled corruption scenarios, GPT2 training progression, and evaluation of various protein generative models. This tests whether MMD results are sensitive to kernel tuning and whether a robust setting can be recommended.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Gretton et al., 2006-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bca4d7b932e0854c3325f1578cfd17341dd8ea8) because it directly studies two-sample MMD with a Gaussian RBF kernel and explicitly treats bandwidth sigma as a practical issue, noting that the empirical MMD is zero when sigma = 0 and approaches zero as sigma becomes very large, while using the median pairwise distance as a compromise. Its data-integration experiments on 4000 samples by 63 dimensions with 100 repetitions, 1000 samples by 100 dimensions with 1200 repetitions, 25 samples by 12600 dimensions with 1000 repetitions, and 25 samples by 2118 dimensions with 1000 repetitions show same-distribution acceptance of 94.4% to 100.0% and different-distribution acceptance values including 0.0%, 0.8%, 1.0%, 3.3%, 3.4%, and 42.0%, demonstrating that MMD can detect distributional differences when sigma is chosen, while also showing that a single median-distance setting is not a validated robust protein-specific parameter. This directly motivates the proposed sweep of sigma across controlled corruption, GPT2 training progression, and protein generative models. Next, [(Muandet et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0b9b5d6f79d62305f47ef560512bf53d17e38e2) provides theoretical support by defining MMD as the squared norm between kernel mean embeddings in an RKHS and establishing that under a characteristic kernel MMD is zero only when the two distributions are identical, which justifies using MMD to detect distributional differences in protein embeddings, although it supplies no empirical sigma grid, protein data, or sample-size protocol. Weaker but still relevant support is from [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456), which studies Kernel GANs and MMD-based kernel statistics on MOG, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA and explicitly analyzes kernel bandwidth and lower-dimensional autoencoder feature spaces, aligning with the proposed concern that bandwidth affects generative-model evaluation, but the supplied excerpt contains no quantitative bandwidth values, MMD scores, significance, or baseline comparisons, so it cannot quantify robustness. The weakest support is [(Li et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2904a9932f4cd0f0886121dc1f2d4aaac0455176), which uses MMD as a generative objective in GMMNs on MNIST and TFD and reports competitive reconstruction-related values, including GMMN 147 +/- 2 on MNIST versus adversarial nets 225 +/- 2 and GMMN 2085 +/- 25 on TFD versus adversarial nets 2057 +/- 26, showing that MMD-based generative modeling is viable, but it does not report sigma, protein sequences, corruption, or GPT2 progression.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension with the proposed method is that [(Gretton et al., 2006-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bca4d7b932e0854c3325f1578cfd17341dd8ea8) does not establish a universally robust sigma; it evaluates only the median pairwise distance and reports that test behavior depends strongly on dataset, sample size, dimensionality, and decision rule, with same-distribution acceptance of 94.4% to 100.0%, different-distribution acceptance ranging from 0.0% to 42.0%, uniform-convergence tests described as too conservative, bootstrap threshold less reliable on the low-sample 25-sample microarray data, and Pearson moment matching preferable in that setting. This contradicts the possibility of recommending one protein-wide sigma without conditioning on corruption level, sample size, embedding dimensionality, and thresholding rule. A second, weaker tension is from [(Li et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2904a9932f4cd0f0886121dc1f2d4aaac0455176), where MMD-based GMMN is not uniformly superior to baselines: DBN 138 +/- 2 and Stacked CAE 121 +/- 1.6 beat GMMN 147 +/- 2 on MNIST, and Deep GSN 1890 +/- 29 beats GMMN 2085 +/- 25 on TFD, suggesting that MMD-based generative modeling or evaluation should not be assumed dominant without model- and dataset-specific validation. A third, more theoretical caution is from [(Muandet et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0b9b5d6f79d62305f47ef560512bf53d17e38e2), which shows MMD is zero only under characteristic kernels and requires finite second moments and i.i.d. samples; if sigma is too small or too large, the induced RKHS may be too restrictive or too smooth, so the metric may miss differences, but the paper provides no empirical protein-specific contradiction. The weakest apparent contradiction is from [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456), which is directly relevant to kernel bandwidth in Kernel GANs but provides no quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpt, so it cannot refute the proposed sigma analysis and instead highlights a reporting gap.

### Suggested Action

Most important, use the median pairwise distance from [(Gretton et al., 2006-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bca4d7b932e0854c3325f1578cfd17341dd8ea8) as a mandatory baseline, but sweep sigma across a controlled grid such as 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, and 1.0 quantiles of pairwise distances plus extreme small and large values across the planned 0% to 30% corruption in 5% increments, sample sizes 2^5 to 2^14, GPT2 training checkpoints, and protein generative model outputs, then report MMD curves and recommend conditional settings rather than a single universal sigma. Second, adopt the statistical decision rules highlighted by [(Gretton et al., 2006-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bca4d7b932e0854c3325f1578cfd17341dd8ea8) by using unbiased MMD with Pearson moment-matching thresholds for low-sample protein embedding sets, avoiding overly conservative uniform-convergence thresholds, and using bootstrap thresholds only after validation, while reporting acceptance or rejection at alpha = 0.05, false positive rate, power, confidence intervals, and permutation p-values. Third, use the theoretical conditions from [(Muandet et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0b9b5d6f79d62305f47ef560512bf53d17e38e2) to validate the MMD setup by checking that the RBF kernel is characteristic, the kernel second moment is finite, samples are treated as i.i.d., and MMD is near zero for identical samples and increases monotonically with corruption, while reporting kernel-matrix condition, embedding norms, and bandwidth-induced smoothness. Fourth, incorporate the generative-model lessons from [(Li et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2904a9932f4cd0f0886121dc1f2d4aaac0455176) and [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456) by comparing MMD with IPR, D&C, FD, and EMD, testing both sequence-space and embedding-space MMD using ESM-2, ProtT5, or autoencoder feature spaces, and reporting computational cost, hardware, random seeds, and model-size effects. Finally, pre-register and report the implementation details missing in the related work, including exact sigma values, corruption protocol, GPT2 checkpoints, protein model outputs, random seeds, hardware and software, and statistical tests.

## Real-world Validation: We will test our findings on outputs from state-of-the-art protein generators to validate the practical applicability of our systematic analysis. This connects controlled metric diagnostics to actual protein generative model outputs.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed real-world validation comes from [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b), which evaluated actual fold-conditioned protein generator outputs from DiMA and RFDiffusion on 100 CATH S40 hold-out structures with 10 sequences per target, used TM-score with a TM-score > 0.5 success threshold, reported mean TM-score 0.93 versus 0.48, success rates 100% versus 41%, and best-attempt RMSD 2.6 A, and its broader framework used 2,048 generated sequences per metric with pLDDT, FD-seq, CD0.5, novelty, TM-score, scPerplexity, MMD, 1-Wasserstein distance, and perplexity, directly supporting the plan's goal of connecting controlled metric diagnostics to real generator outputs and its quality, diversity, and distributional metric suite. A next strongest support is [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f), which used actual outputs from fine-tuned Mistral-7B, Llama-2-7B, Llama-3-8B, and Gemma-7B protein LLMs trained on a Homo sapiens UniRef50 subset, generated sequences from a beginning-of-sequence token, evaluated 250 generated proteins per model with AlphaFold2 five predicted structures per sequence for mean pLDDT, FoldSeek TM-score and Intra RMSD, and Rosetta-Relax REU, and compared natural, random, ProGen, ProtGPT2, and ProLLaMA baselines, supporting real-world validation of pLDDT and structural/energetic metrics on state-of-the-art LLM outputs. [(Frey et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d82835c86f49ec507bf95495d2a958137b26ac6e) supports the distributional-similarity component by evaluating antibody dWJS outputs against diffusion and language-model baselines with a distributional conformity score analogous to FID and in silico/in vitro functional assays, although it is antibody-specific with length 297, 21-symbol vocabulary, one-hot dimension 6237, and empirical noise sigma 0.5 or higher. [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) supports applying metrics to actual ESM-family and ProtBert generated sequences in the CM2 family, using length 95 sequences, character-level tokenization, sequential, random 10% positions for 20 iterations, and burn-in sampling, with similarity to training set, HMM profile scores, first- and second-order statistics, second-order correlations, and GB-1 variant prediction. The oldest but methodologically relevant support is [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), which validated BLEU on real Chinese-to-English translation systems, compared metric rankings with human judgments, used a 500-sentence corpus, 25-sentence blocks, paired t-statistics, and reported 0.99 and 0.96 correlations with monolingual and bilingual human scores, demonstrating that real-system metric validation can detect small quality differences and ranking effects.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly comparable protein real-world validations do not establish statistical reliability of metric differences: [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) reported large DiMA versus RFDiffusion TM-score and success-rate gaps but did not report statistical significance tests, and its fold-conditioned sample was only 100 structures times 10 sequences, so the proposed real-world validation could overstate practical applicability if it does not add paired tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, and sample-size sensitivity. A closely related contradiction is [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f), which evaluated 250 generated proteins per model but only 20 per model for deeper TM-score, Intra RMSD, Inter RMSD, and REU analysis, reported no statistical significance or confidence intervals, and used a random-sequence baseline described as 20 amino acids long despite the main evaluation set being 70 to 140 amino acids, undermining the robustness of real-world metric comparisons. This is reinforced by older metric-validation evidence that metric values can be reference- and protocol-dependent: [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) showed BLEU scores changed with reference count, with a human translator scoring 0.3468 against four references and 0.2571 against two references, and its human evaluation used only 50 source sentences and 250 source-translation pairs rated by non-professional judges, warning that controlled diagnostics may not transfer without recalibration. Domain-specific constraints also contradict broad generalization: [(Frey et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d82835c86f49ec507bf95495d2a958137b26ac6e) used antibody-specific length 297, 21-symbol vocabulary, one-hot dimension 6237, and noise sigma 0.5 or higher, while [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) focused on CM2 length 95 sequences and did not report quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, hardware, or statistical significance, so neither establishes that the plan's full metric suite will behave identically across protein families, model scales, and generator architectures.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed real-world validation should add formal statistical inference and sample-size controls before claiming practical applicability: report paired or permutation tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, effect sizes, and significance thresholds for each metric, and explicitly test the planned 2^5 to 2^14 sample-size range on real generator outputs, following the block-wise variance and paired t-statistic logic in [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) and correcting the absence of significance tests in [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) and [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f). Second, the validation should use a multi-generator, multi-baseline protocol with full implementation details, including generator names, tokenizer/BPE, sequence length, sampling mode, number of generated sequences, number of AlphaFold2/ESMFold/OmegaFold structures per sequence, GPU hardware, training steps, batch size, learning rate, and random seeds, as partially demonstrated by [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f) with four Nvidia A6000 GPUs, 512-token sequences, 2,000 steps, batch size 1, gradient accumulation 16, learning rate 5e-5, bfloat16, DeepSpeed, and FlashAttention 2, and by [(Johnson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a07ff593c396078ee91e797d6f03a8c0517a401c) with sequential, random 10% position, and burn-in sampling. Third, the metric suite should retain the planned pLDDT, ppl, pppl, scPerplexity, CD 50% and 95% MMseqs2 clustering, IPR, D&C, MMD, FD, and EMD, while adding structural and energetic real-world diagnostics such as TM-score, RMSD, and Rosetta-Relax REU, using success thresholds such as TM-score > 0.5 and larger per-metric sample sizes such as 2,048 sequences, as in [(Meshchaninov et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e81434435fe1cbb032006334b089c70e863732b) and [(Zeinalipour et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54487bb7cb4945a4445c91add90bc8f45f28c53f). Fourth, the plan should add distributional and functional validation for at least one high-value family, using a distributional conformity or FID-like score and, where feasible, in silico/in vitro functional assays, as motivated by [(Frey et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d82835c86f49ec507bf95495d2a958137b26ac6e), while keeping the broader protein benchmark from CATH S40, UniRef50, SwissProt, and CM2 to avoid overfitting to one family. Finally, the authors should explicitly test reference- and model-dependence by varying the number of references, the structure-prediction model, the protein language model size, and the MMD RBF kernel sigma, because [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) showed reference-count sensitivity and the research plan already identifies pLDDT, perplexity, and MMD sigma as model-dependent.
