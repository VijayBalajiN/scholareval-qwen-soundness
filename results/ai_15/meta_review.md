## Dataset Construction Strategy: We will curate hardware-related content from diverse sources including GitHub repositories, silicon-proven projects, textbooks, and StackOverflow. A team of 10 domain experts will screen data for correctness, completeness, and diversity, with particular focus on sampling from silicon-proven projects. This grounds the benchmark in real, silicon-validated hardware content and reduces reliance on simplistic educational data.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the newer work of [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e), which advances the older synthetic-template line of [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) by constructing two curated hardware evaluation datasets: Set I, a hand-designed problem set spanning basic to advanced difficulty and low, medium, and high prompt detail with hand-designed testbenches, and Set II, an HDLBits-derived set with platform testbenches. Its quantitative results—CodeGen-16B-FT solving up to 74% of medium-complexity problems, GPT-3.5-turbo and GPT-4 solving about 75%, CodeGen-16B-FT scoring 0.436 versus 0.39 and 0.40 for GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-turbo on medium-description prompts, and GPT-3.5-turbo reaching 1.0 on Step_one and Zero—show that curated, difficulty-stratified, prompt-detail-aware problem sets can discriminate LLMs and that easy educational items can saturate, directly supporting GenBen's expert-screened 300-test construction, L1-L3 difficulty tiering, and move beyond simplistic educational data. A second, weaker line of support is [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), whose template-generated Task/Result corpus, trained versus non-trained template split, and multi-task prompts of two to four subtasks demonstrate that controlled data construction can measure generalization; its 52% trained-template multi-task and 41.2% non-trained-template multi-task results, manual inspection of variable/operator errors, and use of a 345M GPT-2 on a V100 with lr 1e-4 and 7500 steps support the need for explicit correctness screening, error-aware evaluation, and perturbation-like held-out phrasings in GenBen. The weakest but still relevant support is the newest work, [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7), whose compiler-error-driven error-correction dataset and VerilogFixEval align with GenBen's Debug category by pairing erroneous RTL, corrected RTL, instructions, and compiler messages, and by evaluating syntactic and functional rectification with pass@1; although it reports only qualitative comparability of OriGen to advanced closed-source LLMs and omits dataset size, it supports including compiler feedback and debug-specific cases in the curated benchmark.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the newer [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) shows hand-designed educational and HDLBits-derived datasets, without silicon-proven designs, multimodal inputs, or QoR metrics, can still produce meaningful model comparisons and high pass rates, including GPT-3.5-turbo 1.0 on Step_one and Zero, 0.6 in bug-fixing categories, CodeGen-16B-FT 0.653 on Multiplexers, and GPT-3.5-turbo 0.54, suggesting that the proposed emphasis on silicon-proven, multimodal, QoR-rich curation may be more than necessary for basic functional pass-rate evaluation and that simpler curated data may already be sufficient for some benchmark goals. A weaker contradiction is that the older [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) demonstrates that a fully synthetic, template-constrained corpus can be generated and evaluated with a 345M GPT-2, achieving 52% and 41.2% on trained and non-trained multi-task prompts, implying that large-scale expert curation of real silicon-proven content is not strictly required to obtain measurable benchmark signals when the target is syntax or functional generation rather than QoR. The weakest contradiction is [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7), whose compiler-error-driven synthetic dataset supports a debug/self-reflection benchmark without expert-curated silicon-proven projects and reports only qualitative comparability of OriGen to advanced closed-source LLMs, suggesting that synthetic compiler-feedback data may be sufficient for the Debug category; however, because it omits dataset size, pass rates, statistical significance, and QoR evaluation, this challenge to the proposed expert-curated, silicon-validated approach is limited.

### Suggested Action

Most important, operationalize silicon-proven curation with a documented provenance rubric: for each of the 300 tests, record source (GitHub, silicon-proven project, textbook, StackOverflow), design complexity, verification evidence (simulation, FPGA, tape-out, formal), testbench coverage, and expert screening decisions; use the 10 experts with double screening, inter-rater agreement, and a diversity matrix across Knowledge Master 75, Knowledge Transfer 69, Design 99, Debug 57, and Multimodal 60. Next, add contamination resistance to the dataset construction method itself: screen GitHub and StackOverflow items against public pretraining corpora using n-gram, MinHash, or embedding similarity, prefer private or newly generated variants, apply static and dynamic perturbations, and report contamination metrics, because related public datasets such as HDLBits in [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) are vulnerable to leakage. Then, ensure every curated item supports the plan's QoR and multimodal evaluation by including reference RTL, constraints, testbench, schematics or waveforms where applicable, and OpenLane, Yosys, and OpenSTA baselines, so that silicon-proven content is not only selected but also measurable. After that, calibrate L1-L3 difficulty and prompt detail using the low, medium, and high prompt levels and model pass-rate distributions in [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e), with expert ratings and iterative model-based difficulty validation. For the 57 Debug tests, incorporate the compiler-error message and erroneous/corrected RTL structure from [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7), but require expert validation of functional correctness and avoid module-declaration-only fixes. Finally, use the template and trained/non-trained approach from [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) only as an auxiliary augmentation for surface-level perturbations and multi-task prompts, with equivalence checking, error taxonomy, and statistical reporting including confidence intervals, significance, pass@5, temperature, max tokens, and compute to make the benchmark reproducible.

## Test Categories and Distribution: We will construct 300 tests distributed across: - Knowledge Master: 75 tests focusing on fundamental hardware concepts - Knowledge Transfer: 69 tests applying concepts to complex scenarios - Design: 99 tests with difficulty based on code complexity and design time - Debug: 57 tests for syntax/function/combination error correction - Multimodal: 60 tests incorporating textual and visual inputs. This defines the benchmark's scope and balances knowledge, design, debugging, and multimodal evaluation.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed 300-test distribution comes from [(Xu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/057db17f325d09ea85fadbce077a8c45c12bb233), which directly validates the 57-test Debug category by building an RTLLM-derived Verilog debugging benchmark with 15 modules, 8 syntax and 7 function error types, regex/GPT-4/manual injection, GPT-4 Turbo debug agent at temperature 0.7, scorer agent at 0.1, up to 10 iterations, ModelSim SE 10.7, and a Fix Rate averaged over 10 runs; its 93% syntax and 78% function Fix Rates, temperature peaks of 95% syntax at 0.5, 80% function at 0.9, and 87.30% overall at 0.7, support separating syntax, function, and combination errors and using repeated fix-rate evaluation rather than a single pass metric. This newer debug work builds on the simulation-based category evaluation introduced by [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860), which used 120 HDLBits text-only problems, Icarus Verilog, Pass@k at n=0,1,5,10, and feedback prompts, showing Claude 2 Pass@1 rising from 37.50% to 47.5% and GPT-3.5-turbo Pass@5 rising from 27.27% to 39% in succinct mode, while a GPT-3.5-turbo-plus-GPT-4 ensemble improved success from 63% to 79% with 60% less token use; its exclusion of 58 of 178 problems requiring waveform or state-diagram interpretation also supports the need for a 60-test Multimodal category, although it does not itself evaluate visual inputs. Thakur et al. in turn extend the older category-based template construction of [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), which used trained and non-trained English-to-Verilog templates, multi-task prompts, and a 345M GPT-2 fine-tuned for 7500 steps on a node with two Intel Xeon E5-2698 v4 cores, 20 GB RAM, and an NVIDIA V100 32 GB GPU, to show that category-based tests and unfamiliar templates can reduce rote memorization, with 52% trained multi-task and 41.2% non-trained multi-task correctness; this supports the Knowledge Master and Knowledge Transfer split but only weakly because the dataset is synthetic and small. The weakest but still relevant support comes from [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe), whose VerilogEval v2 156-problem text-only benchmark, code completion and specification-to-RTL prompts, pass@1 with 20-sample temperature 0.8/top_p 0.95 and 1-sample sets, and failure categories such as wire-versus-registered output confusion motivate including fundamental concept and runtime-logic tests in the 75 Knowledge Master and 99 Design categories.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction comes from the newest work, [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe), which complicates the older category-based pass-rate assumptions in [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) and [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) by showing prompt and in-context example sensitivity can change category-level outcomes, so a fixed 300-test distribution evaluated with standardized prompts and pass@5 may conflate model capability with prompt format: Llama2 70B improves from 0-shot to 1-shot in code completion but degrades with more examples, GPT-4o initially benefits but is hindered at 3-shot, and Llama3/3.1 70B and CodeLlama degrade with 1-shot, while specification-to-RTL reduces compile-time errors but leaves runtime logic errors; the excerpt also lacks numeric pass rates and statistical significance, weakening confidence in stable category scores. A second contradiction comes from [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), whose multi-task prompts produced only 52% correctness from trained templates and 41.2% from non-trained templates, were worse than individual templates, and often had correct syntax but wrong variable names or operators with high Average Error R-O; this challenges the 99-test Design category if it includes multi-assignment or multi-register prompts without separate scoring for each subtask, because aggregate pass rates may understate partial correctness and overstate difficulty. Third, [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) contradicts a simple no-feedback pass@5 interpretation by showing that feedback can improve Pass@k but also increase simulation error counts, with GPT-3.5-turbo succinct-mode Pass@5 improving from 27.27% to 39% while full-context mode improved only from 28% to 36.36%, and by reporting best Pass@k outcomes rather than mean performance, indicating that Debug and Design scores may depend on feedback context, token budget, and iteration count rather than only the static test distribution. Fourth, [(Xu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/057db17f325d09ea85fadbce077a8c45c12bb233) contradicts the assumption that debug performance transfers directly to the proposed Icarus Verilog workflow because its Fix Rates were measured in ModelSim SE 10.7 with GPT-4 Turbo, temperature-sensitive agents, and up to 10 iterations, so the proposed 57-test Debug category may not reproduce its 93% syntax and 78% function results without simulator, temperature, and iteration calibration. Finally, the related work collectively contradicts the assumption that the proposed 300-test distribution is already representative of real-world LAD because it relies on synthetic templates, 120 HDLBits problems, 15 RTLLM modules, and 156 VerilogEval problems, none of which provide silicon-proven, multimodal, or QoR evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to pilot-validate and operationalize the 300-test distribution before finalizing counts, using category-specific evidence: seed Knowledge Master and Knowledge Transfer with [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) trained/non-trained template categories and [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) HDLBits topic categories, seed Design with single-assignment and multi-assignment controls, seed Debug with [(Xu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/057db17f325d09ea85fadbce077a8c45c12bb233) 8 syntax and 7 function error types, and seed Multimodal with the waveform/state-diagram problems excluded by [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860); then adjust the 75/69/99/57/60 split if pilot pass rates show that multi-task Design or function Debug are disproportionately difficult. Second, adopt the [(Xu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/057db17f325d09ea85fadbce077a8c45c12bb233) debug protocol for the 57 Debug tests by separating syntax, function, and combination errors, reporting Fix Rate over at least 10 repeated runs, testing debug-agent temperatures around 0.5, 0.7, and 0.9, limiting iterations to 10, and cross-validating Icarus Verilog against ModelSim SE 10.7 where possible; also add [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) feedback ablations by comparing no feedback, succinct feedback, and full-context feedback while reporting token cost and iteration count. Third, add [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) prompt and sampling ablations to all categories by evaluating 0-shot, 1-shot, and 3-shot prompts, pass@1 and pass@5, and high-temperature sampling near temperature 0.8/top_p 0.95, while tracking compile errors, wire-versus-registered output errors, and runtime logic errors. Fourth, strengthen contamination control by using [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) non-trained templates, [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) HDLBits categories, [(Xu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/057db17f325d09ea85fadbce077a8c45c12bb233) error generator, and [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) failure modes as seeds for script-based static and dynamic perturbations, and audit sources from silicon-proven projects, textbooks, and StackOverflow to avoid relying only on open-source educational sets. Finally, because none of the related work validates QoR, run a small QoR pilot on a subset of Design and Debug outputs using OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA, normalize area/power/timing against reference designs, and only scale QoR to the full 300-test benchmark after confirming that functional pass rates and QoR metrics are not confounded by simulator, prompt, or feedback effects.

## Testbench Enhancement Process: We will employ constraint randomization and coverage-driven testbench generation methodologies to achieve point-to-point mapping between generated stimuli and functional coverage checklists, targeting >95% verification coverage. This directly addresses verification coverage gaps by tying stimuli to functional coverage points.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Testbench Enhancement Process is indirect but directly tied to the GenBen problem statement: [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) evaluates generated Verilog only with fixed functional testbenches, using a transparent custom problem set with hand-designed benches and an HDLBits-derived set with the platform built-in test bench suite, and reports compile rate, functional pass rate, Pass@k, and inference time, but no constraint-randomized stimuli, functional coverage checklists, coverage points, or coverage percentages. This omission is evidence that the baseline functional-test evaluation used in prior LLM hardware-generation work does not close the verification coverage gap that GenBen targets, especially given the plan's stated worst coverage scores of approximately 52.40% for RTLLM and 44.63% for VerilogEval. A second support is that the paper already uses Pass@k with k tied to problem count and completions, and specifically evaluates the expanded HDLBits set with five completions per problem at temperature 0.2, which aligns with GenBen's pass@5 strategy and suggests that multi-completion functional evaluation is feasible and informative. A third support is that the paper's quantitative results show evaluation outcomes are sensitive to prompt detail, sampling temperature, number of completions, and model choice: for example, a guided Verilog-autocomplete system prompt improved GPT-3.5-turbo over an unguided programming-assistant prompt by about 53% on basic problems, about 41% at low prompt detail, and about 34.3% at high prompt detail, while CodeGen-16B-FT reached up to 74% on medium-complexity custom problems and scored 0.436 on medium-description prompts versus 0.39 for GPT4 and 0.40 for GPT-3.5-turbo. These findings support GenBen's need for standardized prompt templates, difficulty tiers, and controlled sampling across nine models. The weakest but still relevant support is that the paper includes bug-fixing and verification-related HDLBits categories, where GPT-3.5-turbo reached 0.6, CodeGen-16B-FT had an edge on BugMux2, and PALM2 scored 0.42, indicating that verification-adjacent tasks are discriminative and should be represented in GenBen's Debug and Design categories.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no evidence that constraint randomization, coverage-driven testbench generation, or point-to-point stimulus-to-coverage mapping can be implemented in a way that supports the proposed >95% verification coverage target. [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) uses hand-designed custom testbenches and HDLBits built-in testbenches, with no coverage checklists, no coverage metrics, no coverage closure criteria, and no statistical significance tests, so the proposed method's central verification mechanism is not validated by this work. A second tension is that the paper treats fixed functional pass/fail testbenches as sufficient for evaluating generated Verilog, which may undercut the necessity of a universal coverage-driven enhancement unless GenBen shows that coverage-driven testbenches improve discriminative power over fixed benches. A third contradiction concerns feasibility and cost: the paper had to limit GPT4 to a single completion because of cost and web-interface access, skipped n=25 for J1-Large and PALM2, and used max tokens of 300 for most autocomplete models, 256 for J1-Large, and 900 for larger conversational models except GPT4, while GenBen plans pass@5 across nine models including multimodal models and an end-to-end flow with Icarus Verilog, OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA; this suggests that adding coverage-driven testbench generation and coverage collection may substantially increase simulation and evaluation cost. The weakest contradiction is that the paper's Pass@k results decrease as problem difficulty increases and prompts become more terse, and that some categories are easy for some models, such as GPT-3.5-turbo scoring 1.0 on Step_one and Zero getting-started categories, which implies that a single >95% coverage target may be unrealistic or poorly calibrated for L3-Tough, multimodal, or terse-prompt tests without per-tier coverage acceptance criteria.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to operationalize the coverage-driven testbench enhancement by defining explicit functional coverage checklists, cover points, cross coverage, and assertion or scoreboard criteria for each of GenBen's 300 tests, and by specifying how >95% verification coverage is measured, aggregated, and reported per test, category, and difficulty tier; because [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) reports no coverage data, GenBen must avoid an unsupported blanket target and instead publish coverage definitions, coverage databases, and closure thresholds. The second most important action is to run a controlled ablation comparing fixed hand-designed or HDLBits-style functional testbenches against constraint-randomized, coverage-driven testbenches on a representative subset of the 300 tests, measuring both coverage and pass@5, to demonstrate that the enhancement adds discriminative value rather than only increasing testbench complexity. Third, standardize and report the sampling and simulation protocol in the same granularity as the related work: temperature, top_p, max tokens, number of completions, random seeds, simulator version, and inference or simulation time, because the related work shows that temperature, completions, prompt detail, and model access mode materially affect Pass@k, with GPT4 limited to temperature 0.2 and one completion and HDLBits evaluated at temperature 0.2 with five completions. Fourth, calibrate coverage targets by difficulty and category, since the related work shows Pass@k decreases with difficulty and terse prompts and that some getting-started categories can be saturated, such as GPT-3.5-turbo scoring 1.0 on Step_one and Zero, so >95% coverage should be interpreted alongside pass rates and may need lower or separate targets for L3-Tough, Debug, and Multimodal tests. Fifth, add statistical reporting, such as confidence intervals or significance tests for pass@5 and coverage differences, because the related work reports point estimates without statistical significance. The least important but still useful action is to use the bug-fixing and verification-related categories highlighted in the related work, where GPT-3.5-turbo reached 0.6, CodeGen-16B-FT had an edge on BugMux2, and PALM2 scored 0.42, as a focused subset for validating that coverage-driven stimuli expose failure modes that fixed functional testbenches miss.

## End-to-End Workflow Implementation: We will integrate open-source tools including: - Icarus Verilog for simulation and functional correctness testing - OpenLane EDA flow for physical implementation - SkyWater 130nm PDK for consistent QoR evaluation - Yosys for synthesizability, area, and power extraction - OpenSTA for timing analysis. This creates a reproducible RTL-to-QoR pipeline for evaluating hardware designs.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed End-to-End Workflow Implementation comes from the newer [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33), which extends the earlier design-to-QoR evaluation idea in [(Liao et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5b3bf10c9a9818e4b5c09f4216502a55ad15ac) by coupling LLM-produced RTL with a fast verification pipeline and Yosys-based front-end QoR measurement using wire and cell counts, directly supporting the plan's use of Icarus Verilog for functional correctness and Yosys for synthesizability and area extraction. This is important for GenBen because the research plan requires pass@5 functional pass rates and QoR metrics across 300 tests, and Yao shows that verification filtering plus synthesis-level metrics can distinguish LLM-aided designs from compiler baselines, with GPT-4 and Claude3-Opus showing strong potential while VeriGen and RTLCoder hardly optimize RTL. A second, slightly weaker but still relevant support is [(Liao et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5b3bf10c9a9818e4b5c09f4216502a55ad15ac), which implements an end-to-end design-to-QoR chain collecting execution cycles, FF/LUT/slice/DSP/BRAM utilization, total power, and critical path delay, demonstrating that comprehensive QoR evaluation is necessary for LLM hardware generation and that QoR depends on EDA flow, target technology, benchmark complexity, and prompt interaction. This supports GenBen's goal of moving beyond syntax and pass rate to synthesizability, power, area, and timing, although Liao's commercial Vitis HLS/Vivado/Xilinx flow is not the proposed open-source stack. A third, weaker support is that both works motivate a standardized evaluation environment: Yao uses Yosys and Egg as open-source baselines, while Liao's mixed FPGA results show that ad hoc commercial flows can obscure model capability, reinforcing the plan's need for a reproducible Icarus/OpenLane/SkyWater 130nm/Yosys/OpenSTA pipeline for the nine evaluated models.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither paper validates the exact proposed open-source physical-implementation stack, which is central to the method. The newer [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33) explicitly does not report a PDK, physical implementation flow, OpenLane, OpenSTA, Icarus Verilog, or power metrics, and uses Yosys only for short-case wire/cell counts while using Vivado for long-case area and delay, so its evidence cannot confirm that the proposed SkyWater 130nm/OpenLane/OpenSTA workflow will produce consistent QoR for GenBen's 300 tests. The earlier [(Liao et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5b3bf10c9a9818e4b5c09f4216502a55ad15ac) contradicts the open-source reproducibility goal by using Vitis HLS 2023.2, Vivado 2023.2, and a Xilinx xc7a200tfbg484-1 FPGA, meaning its resource, power, and delay numbers are not directly transferable to SkyWater 130nm and may not reproduce with Icarus/OpenLane. A second contradiction is statistical and functional-evaluation weakness: Liao reports no functional pass-rate, no statistical significance tests, and prompt counts that vary with kernel complexity and researcher familiarity, with Verilog generation requiring the most prompts and syrk requiring more interaction than atax, which undermines the plan's pass@5 and normalized QoR claims unless GenBen adds standardized prompts, fixed sampling, and significance testing. A third contradiction is that Yao's reported outcomes are qualitative, with no exact area, delay, wire/cell reductions, or statistical tests, and its benchmark description has an inconsistency between 14 selected short cases and a table described as 16 cases, limiting confidence that small or inconsistently documented benchmarks can support the proposed 300-test, tiered, perturbed, multimodal evaluation. A weaker contradiction is that Liao's QoR results are flow-specific and mixed: LLM-generated Verilog used more resources for syrk and mvt except DSPs/BRAMs, while the remaining seven benchmarks showed average 38.67 percent resource and power reductions and 64 percent cycle reductions for LLM-based designs, yet HLS still had 28.82 percent shorter critical path on average, showing that normalized QoR against reference designs can be sensitive to the EDA flow and target technology and may require careful baseline selection in the proposed open-source pipeline.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to validate and document the proposed Icarus Verilog/Yosys/OpenLane/SkyWater 130nm/OpenSTA pipeline before using it for GenBen scoring, including tool versions, PDK version, reference RTL, golden testbenches, synthesis scripts, OpenSTA constraints, power extraction settings, and a clarification of whether power comes from Yosys netlists, OpenLane parasitics, or OpenSTA, because Liao's power metric is flow-specific and Yao does not report power. This directly addresses the gap identified in the newer [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33) and the earlier [(Liao et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5b3bf10c9a9818e4b5c09f4216502a55ad15ac), where QoR evaluation was either partial or tied to a commercial FPGA flow. The next most important action is to add functional pass-rate and statistical rigor to the end-to-end workflow: report Icarus pass@5 results, coverage metrics against the plan's >95% target, and paired statistical tests or confidence intervals for QoR differences across the 300 tests, difficulty tiers, and nine models, because neither related work provides significance testing and Liao's prompt-count variability shows that uncontrolled prompting can confound results. A third action is to incorporate verification techniques from [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33), such as automated test-case generation, fuzz testing, and program-analysis-based checks, into the Icarus-based testbench enhancement process to reduce false positives from weak testbenches and to measure verification runtime as a QoR-related cost. A fourth action is to standardize prompt interaction and log prompt counts by generated artifact type, retries, temperature, max tokens, model versions, and pass@5 sampling seeds, following the observation in [(Liao et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5b3bf10c9a9818e4b5c09f4216502a55ad15ac) that prompt effort varied by kernel complexity and researcher familiarity, which is critical for reproducible evaluation of GPT-4-turbo, GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo, Claude3.5, Llama3, QWEN-vl-max, QWEN-vl-plus, GLM-4V-plus, and GLM-4. A final, less urgent action is to define QoR normalization explicitly against reference designs generated through the same open-source flow, with separate reporting of synthesizability, area, timing, power, and runtime, so that GenBen can distinguish model capability from EDA-flow artifacts and support the plan's ablations on static and dynamic perturbations.

## Comprehensive Evaluation Framework: We will develop a multi-dimensional evaluation system encompassing: - Knowledge mastery and transfer assessment - Code generation and debugging capabilities - Quality-of-Results metrics including synthesizability, power, area, and timing. This defines the benchmark's assessment dimensions beyond simple pass rates.

### Support

The related work forms an iterative sequence from static functional evaluation to feedback-driven debugging and QoR, and the strongest support for the proposed Comprehensive Evaluation Framework is that QoR can be integrated after functional simulation: [(Thorat et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/501887c27f884ec1bd61f656b570d9dc49182375) generates Verilog, simulates with ICARUS, then uses Synopsys Design Compiler compile ultra on the ASAP 7nm predictive PDK and sweeps timing constraints, directly validating the proposed synthesizability, power, area, and timing dimensions, although it reports no quantitative PPA outcomes and uses RTLLM 29 designs plus VerilogEval 156 human and 108 machine designs with n=1, temperature 0.7, and 2048 context on an AMD EPYC 7543 32-core host and A100 80GB GPU. This is reinforced by [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33), which evaluates 55 engineer-crafted short Verilog cases and five long CPU, neural-network, and image-processing designs using Yosys wires/cells and Vivado area/delay, showing that LLM-aided hardware evaluation needs QoR and realistic complexity, although it omits power and generation-specific knowledge tests. For code generation and debugging, the strongest evidence is [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860), which uses 120 text-based HDLBits problems, Icarus Verilog, and feedback iterations, showing Claude 2 Pass@1 rises from 37.50% to 47.5% and GPT-3.5-turbo Pass@5 success rises from 27.27% to 39% with succinct feedback, while a GPT-3.5 plus GPT-4 ensemble raises success from 63% to 79%, supporting the proposed Debug dimension and the need to report compilation-error, simulation-error, and mismatch metrics. Earlier, [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) supports difficulty-tiered code generation by comparing low, medium, and high prompt detail on a custom set and HDLBits, using n=10 completions, temperatures 0.1 to 1, and Pass@(scenario*10), and finding that larger models and guided prompts improve compilation and functional pass rates, but it stops at functional correctness. Newer work further supports evaluation design: [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) shows that code-completion versus specification-to-RTL prompting and 0, 1, and 3-shot examples change compile-time and runtime failure modes on 156 VerilogEval problems with pass@1, 20-sample T=0.8 top_p=0.95, and 1-sample settings, supporting standardized prompts and failure analysis, while [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a) shows multi-step Chain-of-Descriptions improves VHDL Pass@1 by about 40% on 202 self-verifying problems, supporting structured generation evaluation, although it does not test QoR, knowledge transfer, or multimodal inputs.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is to the assumption that one standardized prompt template and pass@5 can fairly rank models: newer [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) reports that Llama2 70B improves from 0-shot to 1-shot but degrades with more examples, Llama3/3.1 70B and CodeLlama degrade with 1-shot, and GPT-4o is hindered at 3-shot, with specification-to-RTL reducing compile-time errors while runtime logic errors persist, so GenBen must stress-test prompt formats and sample counts, directly challenging the older static prompt comparisons in [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e). A second contradiction concerns debugging: [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) shows that feedback iterations and ensembles substantially improve functional success, so a one-shot Debug category would underestimate iterative debugging capability. Third, the proposed open-source QoR stack is less directly supported than commercial flows: [(Thorat et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/501887c27f884ec1bd61f656b570d9dc49182375) uses Synopsys Design Compiler and ASAP7, while [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33) uses Vivado for long-case area/delay and Yosys for short-case cells/wires, but neither validates OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA as a comparable PPA reference, and Thorat reports no quantitative PPA outcomes. Fourth, [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) shows prompt-detail effects and contains an inconsistent claim about minimal versus detailed prompts, with no statistical significance tests, warning that small problem sets and prompt wording can produce unstable difficulty-tier conclusions. Fifth, multimodal support is under-evidenced: [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860) removed 58 of 178 HDLBits problems requiring waveform or state-diagram interpretation, suggesting that the proposed 60 multimodal tests will require new visual-to-testbench validation methods. Finally, [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a) shows multi-step planning can improve Pass@1 by about 40%, implying that fixed zero-shot prompts may understate model capability, although this is weaker because it is VHDL and lacks QoR.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to validate the QoR flow before scaling to 300 tests: run a pilot on 20 to 30 designs with known reference QoR, report tool versions, synthesis constraints, area, timing, and power distributions, and cross-check OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA against commercial or reference flows where possible, using [(Thorat et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/501887c27f884ec1bd61f656b570d9dc49182375) and [(Yao et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4879d9461f89b2565dfb8216a8d596cce98c9b33) as methodological anchors; also clarify power extraction because Yosys alone does not provide power. Second, add prompt-sensitivity and statistical validation across all categories by testing 0, 1, and 3-shot examples, code-completion versus specification-to-RTL formats, and low, medium, and high prompt detail, reporting confidence intervals and significance tests, as motivated by [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) and [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e). Third, make the Debug category iterative by feeding Icarus Verilog compilation and simulation errors back for up to n iterations, reporting no-feedback versus feedback, compilation-error rate, simulation-error rate, and mismatch counts, following [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860). Fourth, construct and validate knowledge mastery and transfer instruments with expert review, concept mapping, near and far transfer, and item difficulty calibration, because no related work provides a validated instrument. Fifth, develop multimodal testbench generation for schematics, waveforms, and tables with deterministic visual-to-RTL mappings and coverage-driven checks, starting from textual equivalents, addressing the gap shown by [(Thakur et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05e6b94ada76f524016294678315363b3e5de860). Sixth, implement perturbation ablations that preserve functional coverage and QoR reference, measuring performance deltas between original and perturbed tests. Seventh, include planning or multi-step prompting ablations inspired by [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a) to ensure standardized prompts do not understate capability.

## Evaluation Metrics: We will measure performance using pass rates calculated as the percentage of test cases producing correct outputs, along with comprehensive QoR metrics normalized against reference designs. This operationalizes the benchmark's scoring with functional correctness and physical design quality.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Evaluation Metrics comes from [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), which directly operationalizes functional correctness as a pass-rate-like measure by comparing generated Verilog to canonical template-generated Verilog and reports 52% correct outputs for multi-task prompts built from trained templates and 41.2% for non-trained templates. This is directly relevant to GenBen's plan to calculate pass rates as the percentage of test cases producing correct outputs, and it shows that a correctness-based score can differentiate model performance across seen and unseen task structures. The same work also supports the research plan's perturbation and difficulty concerns because the drop from 52% to 41.2% on non-trained templates indicates that template memorization or narrow distribution can materially affect pass rates, motivating GenBen's static and dynamic perturbations and three-tier difficulty design. A secondary but important support for the QoR component is [(Fang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7198e5fdc450971c1acc0916fcefa472bf4f90dd), which evaluates MasterRTL using PPA estimation accuracy and compares estimates against baseline solutions and reference PPA values, thereby providing precedent for using physical-design quality metrics and reference-design comparison in hardware-generation evaluation. However, this support is weaker because the excerpt does not provide quantitative accuracy values, baseline deltas, statistical significance, PDK, EDA tool versions, or a normalization formula, so it validates the concept of PPA/QoR comparison but not the specific normalized QoR scoring proposed for GenBen.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that the available related work does not demonstrate that normalized QoR metrics can be reliably computed, compared, or interpreted in an LLM benchmark. [(Fang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7198e5fdc450971c1acc0916fcefa472bf4f90dd) reports PPA estimation accuracy and reference comparison, but the supplied excerpt contains no quantitative outcomes, accuracy values, baseline deltas, statistical significance, PDK, EDA tool versions, hardware, hyperparameters, or normalization formula; therefore it does not provide evidence that GenBen's plan to normalize QoR against reference designs using OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA will yield stable, reproducible, or statistically meaningful scores. A second contradiction concerns the sufficiency of pass-rate evaluation. [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) measured correctness only by equivalence to canonical template-generated Verilog, without testbench simulation, coverage-driven verification, or QoR, and still found that generated Verilog often had correct structure and syntax but incorrect variable names or operators, with a high average error ratio relative to answer length; this suggests that a simple pass rate can be brittle and may overstate functional adequacy if the test cases are narrow or if equivalence is checked against a single canonical implementation rather than behaviorally diverse stimuli. A weaker contradiction is external validity: the Pearce evaluation used a 345M-parameter GPT-2 fine-tuned for 7,500 steps on a single V100 32GB GPU with default decoding parameters, which is not comparable to GenBen's nine modern multimodal/language models and pass@5 protocol, so its absolute pass rates should not be treated as a baseline for the proposed model set.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed Evaluation Metrics should specify a reproducible QoR normalization protocol before running the nine-model evaluation: define the reference design set, PDK, EDA tool versions, synthesis constraints, timing corners, area/power extraction commands, and the exact normalization formula, such as ratio or percentage difference relative to a human or silicon-proven reference, with clipping or log-scaling if distributions are skewed. This is directly motivated by the missing quantitative PPA details in [(Fang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7198e5fdc450971c1acc0916fcefa472bf4f90dd). Second, the pass-rate metric should be implemented as simulation-based functional correctness with Icarus Verilog and coverage-driven testbenches, not merely textual or canonical equivalence, and should report both pass@1 and pass@5, per-category pass rates, and an error taxonomy covering syntax, variable/operator, functional, and timing failures to avoid the brittleness observed in [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39). Third, because Pearce found a 10.8-point drop from trained to non-trained templates, GenBen should include held-out, perturbed, and non-trained template tests in the 300-test suite and report confidence intervals or bootstrap significance for differences between models and between original and perturbed sets. Fourth, the benchmark should record computational and implementation details for reproducibility, including model prompts, decoding parameters, simulation timeouts, synthesis runtimes, and hardware/software versions, since the related work shows that compute and decoding settings can affect generated Verilog quality.

## Model Evaluation Setup: We will evaluate nine models comprising six multimodal and three language models (GPT-4-turbo, GPT-4o, GPT-3.5-turbo, Claude3.5, Llama3, QWEN-vl-max, QWEN-vl-plus, GLM-4V-plus, GLM-4) using a pass@5 evaluation strategy with standardized prompt templates. This standardizes cross-model comparison and captures multimodal and language-only capabilities.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Model Evaluation Setup comes from [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), which directly uses a multimodal Verilog benchmark built from four-element pairs of textual prompt, circuit structure image, testbench, and reference RTL, and evaluates GPT-4 and LLaMA series models by generating five samples per complexity category and selecting the best with pass@5. Although the supplied sections do not report quantitative pass rates, this methodological alignment is direct because it mirrors GenBen's planned comparison of multimodal and language-only models under standardized prompts and a pass@5 strategy, and its template coding for annotation-free hardware module images anticipates GenBen's need for reproducible multimodal prompt templates. The next strongest support is from [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7), which standardizes functional correctness for LLM-generated Verilog using unit tests and pass@k, generating n=10 samples per instruction on VerilogEval and RTLLM and treating a task solved if at least one of k samples passes; this supports the proposed use of pass-based functional evaluation, although it uses pass@k with n=10 and pass@1 for VerilogFixEval rather than pass@5. Supporting evidence also appears in [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe), which uses standardized code-completion and specification-to-RTL prompt styles on VerilogEval v2 and samples 20 outputs at T=0.8 and top_p=0.95, demonstrating that controlled prompt templates and sampling are necessary for cross-model comparison, and in [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a), which uses self-verifying testbenches on VHDL-Eval and ablates zero-shot, CoDes, single-step, and multi-step prompting, supporting GenBen's emphasis on standardized, testbench-verified pass-rate evaluation. Taken as an iterative sequence, the 2024-07 works establish pass@5/pass@k functional evaluation, while the 2024-08 and 2024-09 works strengthen the case for standardized prompts and testbench-based functional metrics, but they do not validate the exact nine-model set, dynamic perturbation, or QoR dimensions in GenBen.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed setup may be too narrow if it relies on a single standardized prompt template and pass@5 without ablations, because [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) shows that prompt style and in-context learning count materially change pass@1 outcomes and failure modes on VerilogEval v2: Llama2 70B improves from 0-shot to 1-shot in code completion but degrades at 3-shot, Llama3/Llama3.1 70B and CodeLlama degrade with 1-shot, GPT-4o improves initially but degrades at 3-shot, and specification-to-RTL prompting reduces compile-time wire/registered-output errors while runtime logic errors remain. This suggests that a single pass@5 score under one prompt template can mask model-specific prompt sensitivity. The next strongest contradiction is from [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a), where multi-step CoDes execution improves Pass@1 by approximately 40% over zero-shot on VHDL-Eval, indicating that fixed standardized prompting may understate model capability if planning, multi-step, or AST-based summarization prompts are not included as controlled variants. A further tension is that [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7) uses n=10 samples and pass@k, plus pass@1 for debugging, rather than pass@5, implying that pass@5 alone may not fully characterize best-of-n behavior or debugging performance, especially for the 57-test Debug category in GenBen. Finally, [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488) supports pass@5 but provides only qualitative outcomes, evaluates a narrower GPT-4/LLaMA set, and does not report QoR metrics, statistical significance, or contamination control, so it cannot by itself justify the nine-model, QoR-aware, perturbation-based evaluation proposed in GenBen. The iterative progression from pass@k in 2024-07 to prompt/ICL ablations in 2024-08 and planning ablations in 2024-09 therefore contradicts a minimal evaluation that fixes one prompt and one pass@5 metric.

### Suggested Action

Most important, retain pass@5 as the primary metric but add pass@1 and, where feasible, pass@10 or n=10 sampling with fixed temperature/top_p, because [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) and [(Cui et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d590275ab5738a8d771464b3da67ffc0014bbef7) show that pass@1, pass@k, and sampling parameters materially affect conclusions; report mean pass rate, best-of-five pass rate, and failure breakdowns. Next, expand standardized prompt templates into a controlled prompt matrix: code completion versus specification-to-RTL, zero/one/three-shot, single-step versus multi-step CoDes, and text-only versus multimodal variants, because [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) and [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a) show prompt style and planning steps can change pass rates and failure modes. Third, define exact multimodal prompt templates for circuit diagrams, architecture schematics, waveforms, and tables, using the four-element structure of prompt, image, testbench, and reference RTL from [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), and include text-only controls to isolate visual contribution. Fourth, pin all model versions, API dates, context windows, image resolutions, max tokens, decoding parameters, seeds, and prompt hashes for the nine models, and report compute/API cost, because none of the related works specify enough detail for full reproducibility. Fifth, integrate functional pass@5 with the GenBen end-to-end workflow by running Icarus Verilog simulation, Yosys/OpenLane/SkyWater 130nm QoR, and OpenSTA only on functionally passing samples, and report QoR normalized to reference designs, since [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488) uses siliconcompiler/OpenLane but does not report QoR in the supplied evaluation. Sixth, add statistical tests such as bootstrap confidence intervals or paired tests across the 300 tests and five samples, and use dynamic perturbations to compare original versus perturbed pass@5, because the related works do not provide significance tests or contamination mitigation. Finally, include a failure taxonomy and, where appropriate, sequential equivalence checking or self-consistency metrics inspired by [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) and [(Vijayaraghavan et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103aae8a701ebe08e43353be51bcb3f419cd462a) to distinguish syntax, logic, interface, and QoR failures.

## Perturbation Strategy: We will implement two types of perturbations to mitigate memorization bias: - Surface-level perturbations that alter phrasing without changing core meaning - Semantic perturbations that increase task difficulty by altering underlying requirements - Static perturbations applied during test construction - Dynamic perturbations applied during evaluation. This reduces memorization bias and tests robustness to varied specifications.

### Support

The strongest support is [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fee64692cf04ee22177726f6785f51ea814fba3b), the newest and most directly aligned work, because it formalizes original-versus-perturbed evaluation with knowledge-invariant content paraphrasing, option-position swapping, composite perturbations, and metrics ACC@Consist, PDR, and ROP on MMLU subsets (College Mathematics, World History, Professional Psychology, Professional Medicine), using Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on micro-PDR; its negative macro-PDR, significant negative micro-PDR, and 0.04-0.1 performance drops support GenBen's ablation plan to compare original and perturbed tests and quantify robustness rather than relying on a single static pass rate. A second strong support is [(Carlini et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28c7e583d90ccfc5c3078dfc1d6b80a9ad90248d), which quantifies exact-suffix memorization in GPT-Neo 125M, 1.3B, 2.7B, and 6B models on The Pile using 50-token suffixes, 50,000 random sequences, and about 500,000 duplicate-normalized sequences, showing that 6B extracts 7% of length-1000 suffixes versus 4% for 125M and 2% for GPT-2-XL, and that deduplication reduces low-frequency memorization from 3.6% to 1.2% with p<1e-15; this supports GenBen's motivation that static, high-frequency hardware benchmark content can leak into pretraining and that dynamic or non-exact perturbations are needed. A third support is [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), which earlier used template-based English-to-Verilog generation with trained and non-trained templates, 95/5 splits, 7500-step GPT-2 fine-tuning at learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.05, Adam epsilon 1e-8, and V100 32 GB hardware, showing that non-trained multi-task templates reduced correctness from 52% to 41.2% and therefore support static construction-time perturbation as a baseline for GenBen. A fourth support is [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e), which supports surface-level specification variation in Verilog by comparing low, medium, and high prompt detail and guided versus unguided system prompts with functional test benches and Pass@(scenario*n), reporting roughly 41% and 34.3% guided-prompt improvements for GPT-3.5-turbo at low and high description levels and model differences such as CodeGen-16B-FT 0.436 versus GPT4 0.39 on medium prompts. A fifth support is [(He et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e7cc95145665bae4fa98b7f81b9d551f1b1c021), which supports perturbation-based robustness testing through static adversarial NLI and paraphrase sets such as PAWS-QQP and X-ICL explanations, where human-written explanations improved SNLI by up to 6.1 points and concise 27-token zero-shot explanations improved average accuracy for Mistral, Zephyr, and Vicuna, although the evidence is less directly tied to hardware design. A final complementary support is [(Huang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/236c7dafea3df7ecffb5f18ec780d12f2f27d4b0), which adds a contamination-control method for C-EVAL by withholding test labels and using centralized submission with zero-shot and five-shot evaluation, supporting GenBen's need for private or controlled scoring even though it does not itself use perturbations.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that perturbations may not eliminate memorization if the underlying content remains high-frequency or exact-matchable. [(Carlini et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28c7e583d90ccfc5c3078dfc1d6b80a9ad90248d) shows that deduplication does not help sequences repeated at least 408 times, that beam search with 100 beams increases extraction by just under 2 percentage points, and that full-dataset testing is infeasible because a 6B model generates about one 100-token sequence per second on a V100 GPU, implying that surface paraphrase or static perturbation alone may be insufficient for GenBen unless it also controls duplicate frequency, exact suffix leakage, and high-frequency canaries. A second caution is that perturbation can change task difficulty or answer behavior rather than only memorization. [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fee64692cf04ee22177726f6785f51ea814fba3b) reports negative macro-PDR for all selected LLMs, significant negative micro-PDR, and gpt-4-turbo's extra incorrect options rising from 12% to 21.7%, so if GenBen's semantic perturbations alter underlying requirements, performance drops may reflect increased difficulty or ambiguity rather than reduced memorization unless ground truth, difficulty labels, and consistency metrics are tightly controlled. Third, [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) shows that prompt detail and system prompts strongly affect Verilog correctness, with guided prompts improving GPT-3.5-turbo by approximately 41% at low description and 34.3% at high description, and with an internally inconsistent 53% basic-difficulty figure, which contradicts the assumption that surface-level perturbations are neutral because phrasing, detail, and autocomplete framing can confound robustness with prompt engineering. Fourth, [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) indicates that even non-trained template variants expose weak generalization, with only 41.2% correctness and frequent correct structure but wrong variable names or operators, so static template perturbation may not be enough for silicon-proven, QoR-sensitive hardware tasks. Fifth, [(He et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e7cc95145665bae4fa98b7f81b9d551f1b1c021) shows that adversarial or paraphrase-based perturbations do not uniformly improve models: human X-ICL gains were limited to Mistral and GPT-3.5-turbo on adversarial NLI, zero-shot ChatGPT explanations were often worse than human explanations, and no statistical significance was reported, suggesting that static perturbation sets may have limited transfer to dynamic hardware benchmarking. Finally, [(Huang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/236c7dafea3df7ecffb5f18ec780d12f2f27d4b0) provides no quantitative outcomes for its private-test-split protocol, so its contamination-control benefit is plausible but not empirically quantified in the excerpt, and centralized submission may be less compatible with GenBen's need for per-session dynamic test generation and QoR evaluation; moreover, none of the cited works directly validates perturbations for multimodal hardware inputs or QoR evaluation, so that part of the proposed method remains under-evidenced.

### Suggested Action

Most important, GenBen should add explicit memorization and contamination controls before claiming perturbations reduce bias: include exact/near-exact suffix extraction tests, n-gram or embedding overlap against training corpora, duplicate-frequency audits, canary strings, and high-frequency monitoring, following [(Carlini et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28c7e583d90ccfc5c3078dfc1d6b80a9ad90248d), and report whether dynamic perturbations reduce extractability relative to static originals. Second, formalize the perturbation taxonomy with invariance certificates: surface-level paraphrases must preserve module interface, functional requirements, and difficulty tier; semantic perturbations must have known altered requirements, reference solutions, and difficulty labels; format/instruction perturbations should vary prompt detail and system prompts while keeping task intent fixed, using original-versus-perturbed comparisons, PDR, ROP, ACC@Consist, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests as in [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fee64692cf04ee22177726f6785f51ea814fba3b). Third, implement a template/generative pipeline with trained, held-out, and dynamically seeded template categories, random argument filling, multi-task sets, and per-session seeds, extending [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) and [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) to hardware, while deduplicating prompts and limiting high-frequency instances. Fourth, standardize the evaluation harness: fixed prompt templates, system prompts, decoding parameters, pass@5, functional testbenches with >95% coverage, Icarus Verilog, OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA, and report QoR normalized to reference designs, because [(Thakur et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b69882cb6c7367ea91224c27982277c5e643862e) shows prompt and testbench choices materially change pass rates. Fifth, extend perturbation controls to multimodal inputs by defining visual invariance and semantic visual changes, such as schematic label swaps, waveform timing shifts, and table parameter changes, and verify functional and QoR ground truth, because the related work does not directly validate multimodal hardware perturbations. Sixth, add a private or centralized scoring channel for held-out canary and dynamic tests, inspired by [(Huang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/236c7dafea3df7ecffb5f18ec780d12f2f27d4b0), to reduce leakage while still allowing local QoR evaluation. Seventh, include ablations separating static versus dynamic, surface versus semantic, and original versus perturbed performance, with confidence intervals and significance tests, and consider concise explanation or few-shot explanation variants only as secondary robustness probes based on [(He et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e7cc95145665bae4fa98b7f81b9d551f1b1c021).

## Generative Benchmark Infrastructure: We will implement script-based test generation to prevent automated RTL code extraction by crawlers, with both static and dynamic perturbation mechanisms to ensure each evaluation session uses slightly varied test instances while maintaining consistency. This addresses test set contamination by making static public RTL extraction less useful.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the earlier template-generation work of [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), which shows that script/template-based generation can produce varied English-to-Verilog task/result pairs and that held-out non-trained templates reduce DAVE's multi-task accuracy from 52% on trained-template instances to 41.2% on non-trained-template instances. This quantitative drop, although measured on a 345M-parameter GPT-2 fine-tuned for 7500 steps with learning rate 1e-4, weight decay 0.05, Adam epsilon 1e-8, and decoded with temperature 0.7 and top_p 0.9, supports the proposed method's premise that perturbing task syntax/structure can probe generalization and reduce reliance on memorized examples. The newer [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) extends the static-benchmark line by evaluating a 156-problem VerilogEval v2 setup under 0-shot, 1-shot, and 3-shot conditions with pass@1 and a 20-sample high-temperature set at T=0.8 and top_p=0.95, and reports model-specific in-context learning sensitivity such as Llama2 70B improving from 0-shot to 1-shot but degrading at 3-shot, GPT-4o initially benefiting then hindering at 3-shot, and Llama3/3.1 70B and CodeLlama degrading with 1-shot. This supports the research plan's motivation for session-specific, dynamically perturbed instances because static prompt and benchmark conditions can produce unstable, model-specific failure modes. The weakest support is methodological feasibility: [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) demonstrates that template metastructures, variables, operators, and multi-task concatenation can be generated programmatically, which is directly analogous to GenBen's script-based static perturbation, but it does not demonstrate dynamic per-session perturbation or crawler resistance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither the older template-generation work nor the newer static-benchmark work provides evidence that script-based generation prevents automated RTL extraction by crawlers. [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) explicitly uses held-out non-trained templates but is not a dynamic per-session perturbation system and does not explicitly prevent crawler extraction; its 10.8 percentage-point drop from 52% to 41.2% on multi-task instances is not statistically tested and could reflect unfamiliar syntax rather than contamination mitigation. The newer [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) uses a static public 156-problem benchmark with no reported contamination-mitigation mechanism, so it does not validate the proposed claim that dynamic perturbation makes static public RTL extraction less useful. A second contradiction is that the evidence for perturbation effects is limited in scale and complexity: [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) uses 5000 multi-task trained instances built from two to four registers or assignments, a 95/5 train/holdout split, and manual qualitative inspection for non-equivalent outputs, without baseline comparisons or significance tests, which is insufficient to support robust conclusions for GenBen's L1-L3 silicon-proven, QoR-evaluated benchmark. A third, weaker contradiction is that dynamic perturbation may introduce uncontrolled variance: [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) shows that prompt format and in-context examples already change outcomes, with specification-to-RTL reducing compile-time errors such as 'Reg Declared as Wire' while runtime logic errors remain, so adding dynamic perturbations without equivalence checking and controlled ablations could confound model capability with perturbation sensitivity.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the anti-crawler claim testable by building on the older template-generation idea in [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) and the newer static-benchmark limitations in [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe): implement session-specific dynamic perturbation with non-public generation templates, random seeds, canary tokens, and watermarking, then measure whether public static extraction or regenerated instances predict performance on held-out private sessions. The next most important action is to quantify perturbation effects with statistical rigor, using multiple random seeds, pass@1 and pass@5, confidence intervals, and ablations comparing original, static-perturbed, and dynamic-perturbed tests while controlling prompt format, in-context examples, temperature, top_p, and sample count, because [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) reported no significance tests and [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) provided only qualitative pass@1 observations. A third action is to preserve functional equivalence and difficulty under perturbation by integrating Icarus Verilog simulation, Yosys synthesis, OpenSTA timing, and OpenLane/SkyWater QoR checks, with automatic equivalence or coverage-driven testbench validation, so that surface-level and semantic perturbations do not silently change the intended task. A fourth action is to expand beyond the simple register/assignment templates in [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39) and the static 156-problem VerilogEval setup in [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) by generating L1-L3 silicon-proven, multimodal designs and reporting QoR-normalized metrics, since neither related work evaluates synthesizability, power, area, timing, or visual inputs. The least important but still useful action is to document reproducibility details at the level of [(Pearce et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bb4f4be3aad75da5a64a52b56e697ee6df71e39), including template counts, train/holdout splits, decoding parameters, hardware, and EDA tool versions, and to compare against static baselines such as VerilogEval v2 to isolate the contribution of dynamic perturbation.

## Multimodal Support: We will incorporate both textual and visual inputs including circuit diagrams, design architecture schematics, waveform diagrams, and tables to simulate real-world design scenarios. This expands the benchmark beyond text-only specifications to match practical hardware design inputs.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), which directly validates the proposed multimodal benchmark direction by constructing hardware co-design benchmark items as four-element pairs consisting of Prompt_list.txt, Circuit_Structure.png, TestBench.v, and reference.v, using GPT-4 Vision with natural language plus a circuit structure image to generate Verilog, comparing against a text-only baseline, and evaluating with pass@5 across arithmetic, digital circuit, and advanced hardware categories using GPT-4 and LLaMA series models. This closely mirrors GenBen's planned 60 multimodal tests, six multimodal and three language models, pass@5, and end-to-end OpenLane-based functional and QoR evaluation, and it specifically supports using circuit diagrams or structure images as first-class visual inputs alongside text. As newer work, it operationalizes the broader multimodal EDA vision of [(Zhong et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beadfd41698c0382a5899077a8346c05ca6fe513) by turning visual and structural design artifacts into concrete benchmark items and an evaluation workflow, although it remains qualitative. It also supports separating frontend Verilog generation from backend open-source EDA analysis, analogous to GenBen's Icarus Verilog, OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA pipeline, and its use of PPA reports, GDSII layout, and functional analysis aligns with the plan's synthesizability, power, area, and timing metrics. Weaker but conceptually supportive evidence is [(Zhong et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beadfd41698c0382a5899077a8346c05ca6fe513), which proposes an EDA-guided large circuit model integrating textual specification documents, HLS code, RTL scripts, netlists, graphs, and images into a shared latent space across logic synthesis, physical design, verification, and analysis. This supports GenBen's premise that real-world hardware design inputs are multimodal and that visual or structural artifacts should not be reduced to text-only prompts, although it is a model-training vision rather than a benchmark construction or evaluation protocol and provides no quantitative validation of the proposed 60-test multimodal category.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the closest empirical work, [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), reports only qualitative case-study evidence that GPT-4 Vision captured intricate hardware details and outperformed text-only generation, with no numerical pass rates, functional accuracy scores, PPA or QoR values, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance; therefore it does not substantiate the proposed assumption that adding circuit diagrams, architecture schematics, waveform diagrams, and tables will reliably improve pass@5 or normalized QoR across nine models. This is especially important because GenBen's multimodal category is only 60 tests and its evaluation depends on pass@5 and QoR normalization, so an unquantified multimodal advantage is insufficient to justify the category's size or expected gains. A second contradiction is modality coverage: [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488) uses circuit structure images and text prompts, but does not include waveform diagrams or tables, so it does not test the broader visual input set proposed by GenBen; the claim that waveform diagrams and tables simulate real-world design scenarios remains unsupported by this benchmark. A third contradiction is reproducibility and evaluation specificity: [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488) does not specify dataset size, compute resources, exact prompt templates, number of benchmark items, or statistical tests, and its backend uses siliconcompiler and OpenLane rather than the full GenBen stack of Icarus Verilog, OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, and OpenSTA, leaving unclear whether its qualitative multimodal gains would transfer to GenBen's standardized QoR metrics. Weaker contradiction is [(Zhong et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beadfd41698c0382a5899077a8346c05ca6fe513), which provides no quantitative experimental outcomes, no dataset size, training hardware, model size, hyperparameters, evaluation tools, PDK, testbench coverage, or QoR normalization, and explicitly frames the multimodal EDA workflow as an ideal with a considerable journey ahead; this contradicts the strength of evidence needed to support GenBen's multimodal support as a validated method rather than a plausible extension.

### Suggested Action

Most important action is to add a controlled multimodal ablation in GenBen: for each of the 60 multimodal tests, create matched text-only, circuit-diagram-only, schematic-only, waveform-only, table-only, and full-multimodal variants, then report pass@5, syntax correctness, functional accuracy, and normalized QoR metrics including area, power, timing, and synthesizability for all nine models with confidence intervals or significance tests, so the benchmark can measure whether visual inputs actually improve performance rather than merely being available. Second, adopt the four-element test unit and template-coding approach from [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), consisting of a prompt file, standardized image, testbench, and reference Verilog, and extend it to waveform diagrams and tables by defining image templates, annotation-free rendering rules, and a distributional selection procedure that minimizes difference from broader hardware design data, ensuring the 60 multimodal tests are representative rather than anecdotal. Third, align the evaluation pipeline with the strongest existing open-source evidence by using Icarus Verilog for functional simulation, OpenLane or siliconcompiler for ASIC flow, SkyWater 130nm for consistent PDK, Yosys for area, power, and synthesizability, and OpenSTA for timing, while reporting exact prompt templates, model versions, image resolutions, random seeds, and compute resources to make the multimodal results reproducible. Fourth, increase statistical power by either expanding the multimodal category beyond 60 tests or pre-registering a power analysis for the expected effect size, and include difficulty-tiered multimodal tests across L1-Simple, L2-Intermediate, and L3-Tough so that visual-input gains can be separated from complexity effects. Finally, use [(Zhong et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/beadfd41698c0382a5899077a8346c05ca6fe513) as a long-term motivation to include structural artifacts such as netlists or graphs in future multimodal tests, but do not rely on it for validation because it lacks quantitative benchmark evidence.

## Difficulty Tiering Mechanism: We will categorize tests into three difficulty levels (L1-Simple, L2-Intermediate, L3-Tough) to enable fine-grained evaluation of LLM capabilities across different complexity levels. This enables analysis of where models succeed or fail as design complexity increases.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the newest work, [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), which extends the older controlled-complexity evaluation of [(Yang et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39b2d8b8233a53dc7eadb819c52213369dff8648) and the hardware difficulty-labeling idea of [(Huang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e78331cea1db959d1dfe0458eacb284a191f369) to LLM hardware code generation. Chang et al. construct a multi-modal Verilog benchmark with three complexity categories, arithmetic, digital circuit, and advanced hardware designs, progressing from simple to complex, which is structurally analogous to GenBen L1-Simple, L2-Intermediate, and L3-Tough. The benchmark uses four-element test pairs containing a prompt list, circuit structure image, testbench, and reference RTL file, evaluates GPT-4 and LLaMA series models with five generations and pass@5, and connects generation to siliconcompiler and OpenLane for PPA, GDSII, and functional analysis, closely matching GenBen multimodal and QoR-oriented plan. The earlier Yang et al. work supports the same principle in a different domain by partitioning BERT-based video QA evaluation into timestamp-annotated versus full-length inputs and token lengths 128, 256, and 512; full-length TVQA validation accuracy was 61.97 percent, 2.45 percentage points lower than the timestamp baseline and 6.59 percentage points lower than STAGE, while sequence-length accuracy improved monotonically from 128 to 256 to 512, showing that aggregate scores can hide complexity-dependent degradation. The weakest but still relevant support is Huang et al., which uses easy-to-observe and difficult-to-observe labels on a module-wire netlist graph to guide observation point insertion, reducing observation points by 11 percent and test patterns by 6 percent under similar fault coverage, thereby showing that difficulty labels can be useful in hardware test generation even though it does not evaluate LLMs or define benchmark tiers.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the newest work, [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488), does not validate the proposed three-tier mechanism quantitatively. Although it uses three complexity categories, it reports only qualitative illuminating results, with no per-category pass rates, no statistical significance, no number of tests per category, no exact model versions, no dataset size, and no explicit L1, L2, or L3 definitions. This contradicts the assumption that a three-tier label alone is sufficient for fine-grained evaluation unless the tiers are operationalized and measured. The next contradiction comes from the older [(Yang et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39b2d8b8233a53dc7eadb819c52213369dff8648), where complexity is confounded with input length and localization: full-length inputs performed worse than timestamp-annotated inputs, yet increasing maximum token length from 128 to 512 improved accuracy, and no statistical significance tests were reported. This warns GenBen that L3-Tough should not be equated with longer prompts or more context, and that per-tier claims require controlled definitions and statistical testing. The weakest contradiction is [(Huang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e78331cea1db959d1dfe0458eacb284a191f369), which uses difficulty labels but provides no benchmark circuits, technology node, training data size, GCN hyperparameters, or fault coverage values, and its difficulty is node-level observability rather than LLM task difficulty, limiting its ability to justify a general L1-L3 taxonomy for LLM-aided hardware design.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define L1-Simple, L2-Intermediate, and L3-Tough with explicit, measurable criteria before generating the 300 GenBen tests, using the newer hardware categories from [(Chang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1b472d616c4954f12ea783b3ecd197e10c4488) as the primary template and adding hardware-specific attributes such as RTL line count, module count, combinational depth, number of functional coverage points, design time, verification coverage target, and QoR constraints. Use expert annotation and inter-rater agreement to ensure the tiers are not subjective. Second, report per-tier results for all nine planned models using pass@5, syntax correctness, functional correctness, and QoR metrics from Icarus Verilog, Yosys, OpenSTA, and OpenLane with SkyWater 130nm, including confidence intervals or significance tests, following the controlled-complexity reporting style of [(Yang et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39b2d8b8233a53dc7eadb819c52213369dff8648) to show where aggregate pass rates mask tier-specific degradation. Third, incorporate hardware-specific difficulty signals inspired by [(Huang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e78331cea1db959d1dfe0458eacb284a191f369), such as netlist graph observability, fanout, module count, or fault-coverage contribution, to automatically calibrate or audit L2 and L3 tests and ensure they are genuinely harder for verification and QoR. Fourth, stratify the planned 300 tests across Knowledge Master, Knowledge Transfer, Design, Debug, and Multimodal so each tier has enough samples for stable pass@5 estimates, and include ablations comparing original versus perturbed tests within each tier. Finally, document reproducibility details missing in the related work, including exact model versions, prompt templates, random seeds, number of tests per tier and category, hardware resources, EDA settings, and perturbation parameters.

## Ablation Studies: We will conduct controlled experiments to assess the impact of dynamic perturbations on model performance, comparing results between original and perturbed test sets to evaluate model robustness and sensitivity to variations. This isolates the effect of perturbation and validates the benchmark's resistance to memorization.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed ablation comes from [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e), which uses a controlled prompt-condition ablation on CROSSCODEEVAL to isolate the effect of changing test inputs while holding decoding and post-processing fixed. It compares in-file-only, retrieved cross-file, and retrieved-with-reference prompts under zero-shot greedy decoding, maximum generation length 50, statement-level post-processing, and code exact match, edit similarity, identifier exact match, and identifier F1. Its quantitative results show large aggregate shifts, such as StarCoder-15.5B Python exact match moving from 8.82 to 15.72 to 21.01, and, more importantly for GenBen, non-monotonic sample-level changes, with 327 correct completions becoming incorrect and 468 incorrect completions becoming correct when moving from retrieval to retrieval-with-reference in Python. This directly supports GenBen's plan to compare original and dynamically perturbed test sets and to evaluate robustness through per-test sensitivity, not only average pass rate. Its filtering of examples exactly completed by starcoderbase-1B also supports the broader memorization-mitigation goal, although it is a static contamination control rather than a dynamic perturbation test. A weaker but domain-relevant support is [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe), which ablates prompt format and in-context examples on the 156-problem VerilogEval v2 dataset. It shows that code-completion versus specification-to-RTL prompting and 0-shot, 1-shot, and 3-shot conditions change compile-time and runtime failure modes, for example Llama2 70B code completion degrades with more examples and Reg Declared as Wire becomes pronounced at 3-shot, while specification-to-RTL improves compile-time errors. Read iteratively, this newer hardware-design work extends the older code-completion ablation evidence into the RTL domain and supports GenBen's need for controlled prompt-level ablations, but it is weaker because it is directional, uses pass@1, and does not test dynamic perturbations or memorization resistance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest related work does not validate the central claim that dynamic perturbations specifically reduce memorization; both are static prompt or context ablations. [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e) shows that changing context can improve performance even when the retrieval query is formed from the prompt plus the reference completion, and that sample-level outcomes are non-monotonic, so an original-versus-perturbed comparison in GenBen could conflate memorization, context quality, difficulty, and prompt format unless perturbations are difficulty-matched and leakage-controlled. A weaker but important contradiction is that the newer hardware-design work in [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) partially contradicts the generalizability of the older prompt-augmentation gains in [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e) by showing that prompt-level changes can introduce or amplify specific hardware failure modes, such as Reg Declared as Wire at 3-shot, and that adding in-context examples can degrade Llama2 70B code completion; this warns that GenBen's dynamic perturbations may alter task difficulty or prompt format rather than only memorization bias. It also reports no numeric pass rates, baselines, or statistical significance tests, which contradicts the assumption that ablation evidence can be treated as robust without quantitative significance testing. A further, weaker limitation is that the evaluation protocols differ from GenBen: [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e) uses zero-shot greedy decoding, maximum generation length 50, and exact-match or identifier metrics, while [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe) uses pass@1 with a 20-sample high-temperature set at temperature 0.8 and top_p 0.95 plus a 1-sample set; neither directly supports GenBen's pass@5, multimodal, or QoR evaluation, so the ablation design must not assume these protocols transfer unchanged.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the ablation a stratified, difficulty-matched comparison across GenBen's 300 tests and L1-L3 tiers, with at least four conditions: original, surface-level perturbation, semantic perturbation, and a control perturbation that changes wording but preserves functional requirements and testbench coverage; report aggregate pass@1 and pass@5, per-test flip rates, confidence intervals, and significance tests, using the sample-level flip analysis in [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e) as a template. A secondary action is to add explicit memorization and contamination controls, such as excluding or flagging tests where a small reference model exactly completes the prompt, measuring n-gram or retrieval overlap between original and perturbed tests, and comparing performance of models with and without presumed exposure, because the removal of examples exactly completed by starcoderbase-1B and the recent GitHub filtering in [(Ding et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1bd7ea3a63b78a60b5d90d91fdb4a1d7ac0de8e) show contamination control is feasible but not sufficient by itself. A further action is to ablate prompt format and in-context examples in the hardware setting, following the code-completion versus specification-to-RTL and 0/1/3-shot design in [(Pinckney et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0a3671a5fa891107c9974276bf80579b9347fe), while tracking compile-time and runtime failure categories such as Reg Declared as Wire, so that dynamic perturbations are not confounded with prompt style or ICL difficulty. Finally, report implementation details and EDA/QoR constraints, including Icarus Verilog, OpenLane, SkyWater 130nm, Yosys, OpenSTA, seeds, decoding settings, and coverage checklists, and validate that semantic perturbations preserve intended functional coverage and QoR reference designs; this makes the robustness and memorization-resistance claims reproducible and tied to GenBen's end-to-end evaluation.
