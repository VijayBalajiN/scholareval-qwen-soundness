## We propose scKGOT (single-cell Knowledge Graph Optimal Transport), a novel method that employs the Knowledge Graph Optimal Transport (KGOT) algorithm to model and quantify ligand-receptor-signaling networks between sender and receiver cells — This is the central proposed system that reframes intercellular signaling inference as pathway-aware optimal transport rather than simple ligand-receptor expression matching.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c), which extends earlier entropic OT ideas such as [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45) to structured graph comparison via Fused Gromov-Wasserstein, coupling feature distances with structure matrices and an alpha trade-off; this directly supports scKGOT's pathway-aware transport over a ligand-receptor-pathway graph. The earlier Cuturi result is also supportive because Sinkhorn distance outperformed EMD, Hellinger, chi-square, total variation, squared Euclidean/Gaussian, and independence-kernel distances on MNIST 20x20 400x400 grids, with lambda=9 selected in most folds and 20 fixed-point iterations, showing entropic OT can be practical and kernelizable. Newer single-cell work [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c) supports OT on high-dimensional scRNA-seq data, where transport-rank regularized FactoredOT achieved 14.10 +/- 4.44 mean misclassification versus 17.47 +/- 3.17 for plain OT and 18.58 +/- 6.57 for entropic OT in haematopoietic batch correction, indicating OT can be stable for single-cell alignment when rank/regularization is controlled. [(Hu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed238585b4dafdc6389eeac6f786e3ecd6c8d4d7) supports the biological sender-receiver framing by constructing intracellular functional gene interaction networks, integrated sender-receiver networks, and crosstalk scores, which aligns with scKGOT's pathway-aware objective. [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) supports the relevance of ligand-receptor expression and correlation in TCGA and single-cell data, providing a biologically grounded baseline that scKGOT must surpass. Finally, [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8) supports constructing a curated LRP-KG by providing a multidata_table schema for protein, complex, and interaction annotations that can be integrated with KEGG and Reactome.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the newest signaling-inference work, [(Hu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed238585b4dafdc6389eeac6f786e3ecd6c8d4d7), infers intercellular signaling through network crosstalk scores and de novo signaling networks without knowledge-graph optimal transport, and it is evaluated in spatial transcriptomics and scRNA-seq settings lacking receptor expression; this challenges the necessity of scKGOT's OT formulation unless it demonstrably outperforms such network-scoring baselines. [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) also contradicts the added complexity of pathway-aware OT by showing that ligand-receptor inference can be framed through differential expression, pairwise correlations, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests on TCGA and single-cell data, implying that expression/correlation baselines may already capture much of the signal. [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c) contradicts a naive scKGOT transport design because its scRNA-seq OT application is batch correction, not signaling inference, and it requires transport-rank regularization to improve stability over plain and entropic OT, suggesting that unregularized pairwise-distance transport over gene-expression marginals may be insufficient for sparse high-dimensional single-cell data. The older generic OT evidence from [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45) and the structured graph OT evidence from [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c) do not directly validate ligand-receptor-pathway signaling, and their supplied excerpts lack numerical significance or biological validation, so they support the mathematical toolkit but not the central biological claim. [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8) is a database schema rather than an inference method, so it does not contradict performance but also provides no evidence that structured LR annotations improve signaling prediction.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add CytoTalk and expression/correlation baselines to the planned comparisons, because [(Hu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed238585b4dafdc6389eeac6f786e3ecd6c8d4d7) and [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) show that network crosstalk and ligand-receptor correlation are strong non-OT alternatives; report MR, Hits@K, accuracy, percentile rank, effect sizes, permutation p-values, and Wilcoxon or paired significance tests across five seeds. Second, implement scKGOT with entropic and fused-structure regularization informed by [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45) and [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c), including explicit alpha/temperature/lambda selection, fixed-point or conditional-gradient solvers, convergence criteria, runtime, and kernelization if using SVM-style evaluation. Third, incorporate low-rank or transport-rank regularization from [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c) to handle sparse high-dimensional scRNA-seq and batch effects, and evaluate batch-corrected or aligned variants before signaling inference. Fourth, build the LRP-KG using the curated receptor-ligand schema from [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8) plus KEGG/Reactome, documenting species, version, edge provenance, and FactDrop/TypeDrop ablations. Finally, extend evaluation to spatial transcriptomics and missing-receptor settings as in [(Hu et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed238585b4dafdc6389eeac6f786e3ecd6c8d4d7), and report dataset sizes, cell counts, gene counts, hardware, software versions, and random seeds to make the method reproducible.

## Our approach reformulates the traditional ligand-receptor binding problem from a probabilistic classification task to a fine-grained transportation problem that considers signal transmission through multiple pathways — This conceptual shift is the key methodological move that allows scKGOT to use pathway structure instead of only gene correlation.

### Support

The strongest support for scKGOT's shift from probabilistic ligand-receptor classification to pathway-mediated transport comes from [(Browaeys et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4b22dada82dd5a7f953aad033d04ae305f3cdd), which uses MultiNicheNet/NicheNet-v2 to infer differential ligand-receptor-target communication from multi-sample scRNA-seq by aggregating cells into pseudobulk, performing differential expression, computing normalized pseudobulk expression, and applying an updated ligand-receptor database with NSGA-II-optimized model parameters. This directly supports the plan's premise that ligand-receptor inference should not stop at pair expression but should incorporate downstream target regulation and prior biological knowledge, although it does not use KEGG/Reactome pathway graphs or optimal transport. A weaker but still relevant support is [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22), whose GraphComm constructs a directed cell-to-ligand/receptor graph, uses OmniPath as a prior, and infers communication probabilities with a graph attention network; this supports the plan's use of graph structure and prior databases, but it remains a probabilistic graph-attention model rather than a fine-grained OT over pathway spaces. Further weaker support is [(Cang, and Nie, 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de63dfe83e384f97a2e46020e5cac0ca27477854), which infers intercellular gene-gene information flow and spatial cell-cell communication from spatial transcriptomics using known signaling genes; this supports the idea that gene-gene information flow beyond direct ligand-receptor expression is biologically meaningful, but it is spatial rather than scRNA-seq and does not specify pathway-level OT. The weakest support is [(Bassez et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17bda6d5b9cbc92818ce67b2a985b8f74b7e9827), which reports cell-to-cell communication in anti-PD-1 scRNA-seq with T-cell expansion and PD-L1/L2 correlations, demonstrating the biological relevance of CCI inference, but the excerpt lacks algorithm, database, metrics, and pathway structure, so it cannot substantiate the OT formulation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is indirect: none of the supplied works provides quantitative evidence that a pathway-level optimal transport formulation outperforms the probabilistic, differential, or graph-attention alternatives that the plan must beat. In particular, [(Browaeys et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4b22dada82dd5a7f953aad033d04ae305f3cdd) shows that differential ligand-receptor-target scoring with NicheNet-v2, pseudobulk aggregation, and condition contrasts can identify communication patterns and is supported by spatial co-localization and serum protein analyses, yet the excerpt reports no AUC, accuracy, rank, p-value, or effect size that would establish a performance gap for scKGOT. A next-level contradiction is methodological mismatch in evaluation: the plan proposes MR and Hits@K from knowledge graph link prediction plus accuracy/percentile rank for CCI methods, but the related work validates CCI through spatial co-localization, serum proteins, and biological trajectories rather than filtered KG link-predictive metrics, suggesting that MR/Hits@K may not capture pathway transmission or cell-cell communication accuracy. A weaker contradiction is that [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22) uses a graph attention network over a cell-to-ligand/receptor graph with OmniPath priors and randomisation experiments, implying that graph-based probabilistic inference may be a simpler competitive alternative to the proposed KEGG/Reactome LRP-KG OT, although no numerical comparison is provided. An even weaker contradiction is that [(Cang, and Nie, 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de63dfe83e384f97a2e46020e5cac0ca27477854) frames communication in spatial transcriptomics and known signaling genes rather than scRNA-seq pathway OT, so it does not directly challenge scKGOT but limits the generalizability of its evidence. The weakest contradiction is that [(Bassez et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17bda6d5b9cbc92818ce67b2a985b8f74b7e9827) lacks method details, dataset size, hyperparameters, and metrics, so it cannot be used to contradict the plan except by showing that CCI analyses in high-impact immune contexts may be reported without the pathway-OT formalism.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define a pathway-level ground truth and evaluation protocol that directly tests the proposed OT claim, because the related work lacks quantitative pathway-transport benchmarks. The plan should add perturbation/kinase-inhibitor or pathway-activity labels, spatial co-localization validation as in [(Browaeys et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4b22dada82dd5a7f953aad033d04ae305f3cdd) and [(Cang, and Nie, 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de63dfe83e384f97a2e46020e5cac0ca27477854), and report AUC, precision, recall, F1, rank, and permutation p-values alongside MR/Hits@K. The next most important action is to benchmark scKGOT against the exact alternatives represented in the related work: NicheNet/MultiNicheNet-style differential ligand-receptor-target scoring, CellChat/CellPhoneDB/SingleCellSignalR/CellCall, and graph-attention methods such as GraphComm [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22), using the same 6 human and 5 mouse datasets, five random seeds, 100 permutation iterations, and identical filtering. A further action is to incorporate differential condition contrasts and pseudobulk aggregation as in [(Browaeys et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb4b22dada82dd5a7f953aad033d04ae305f3cdd) to test whether pathway OT improves over condition-level ligand activity scoring, and to include spatial validation where possible as in [(Cang, and Nie, 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de63dfe83e384f97a2e46020e5cac0ca27477854). Additional actions include specifying the OT solver, distance matrix construction, regularization, marginal distributions, KEGG/Reactome edge types, and computational resources; integrating OmniPath or other curated interaction sources if it improves coverage; and adding ablations that remove pathway edges, pathway types, or OT constraints to show that the improvement comes from pathway structure rather than from added graph complexity.

## We define the problem as: 

ẑ = arg max_{z∈C} Σ_{w_n∈W} P(z|w_n,D) · P(w_n|D)

where W represents the space of pathways, and we factorize the probability into gene importance scores within pathways and pathway knowledge discrepancy (KD) — This objective specifies how scKGOT scores candidate signaling explanations by combining pathway likelihoods with gene-level importance and knowledge discrepancy.

### Support

The strongest support for scKGOT comes from [(Feng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14be81e003b6638090dd044b97488787a1583b98) because PathFinder is the closest prior work to the plan's pathway-level single-cell communication objective: it uses scRNA-seq from an Alzheimer's disease mouse cohort, builds a gene-gene interaction network from curated databases including a ligand-receptor database, samples candidate signaling paths with a Path Sampler, and learns path importance with a transformer/Graphormer-style architecture containing Node Encoder, Norm Project, Jumping Knowledge, Sigmoid, Graph Encoder, and Path Importance Learner with a Control Test; its qualitative identification of JAK-STAT signaling and APOE4-associated inter-cell networks supports the plan's premise that pathway priors and gene/pathway importance can explain intercellular signaling, although it does not use optimal transport or pathway knowledge discrepancy. The next strongest support is [(Feng et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d5a4a12a5fea08463286e6f293332fa9ecd3968) because deepSignalingLinkNet directly uses KEGG signaling interaction links and KEGG pathway evaluation, encoding one-hop STRING PPI subgraphs with GCN/GAT, TCGA expression/CNV features from 13 cancer types, 18,377 genes, GMM subgroups, PCA/t-SNE, 130 genomic features and 50 hallmark features, and reports qualitative outperformance in KEGG pathway inference; this supports the plan's use of KEGG pathway knowledge and graph-based link prediction, but it is bulk-tissue and does not validate single-cell sender-receiver optimal transport. Further support is provided by [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22) GraphComm, which uses a directed cell-to-ligand/receptor graph, OmniPath prior, cell and ligand/receptor embeddings, and a graph attention network, supporting graph-structured prior knowledge for CCI inference, but not pathway KD or OT. [(Tsuyuzaki et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85e3cc269e8f1f8c12d2b25e692b9c72b589e620) scTensor supports structured many-to-many CCI inference by constructing a ligand-receptor-CCI tensor and applying CANDECOMP/PARAFAC and Tucker decomposition with label permutation evaluation, which is relevant to the plan's sender/receiver spaces and multi-relational inference, but it does not model pathways. [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5620c313d3de7eb83b0a521106618b6dbd7c410e) NicheNet supports the plan's LR-to-downstream-gene focus by inferring cancer-associated fibroblast ligands regulating partial EMT target genes in human HNSCC scRNA-seq, including TGFB3 to TGFBI, LAMC2, and TNC, but it is hypothesis-driven and lacks benchmark metrics. [(Pan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6ce82762fef1f24c929b87088104e3fcdab0a0) supports robust LR database construction and statistical integration by combining three resources into 3,954 pairs, applying 31 human organ scRNA-seq, filtering genes in fewer than 3 cells and cells with fewer than 200 genes, and using Fisher's combined probability test with chi-square 2k degrees of freedom, which is useful for the plan's LRP-KG and baseline integration. Weaker but foundational support comes from [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) CellChat, which uses ligand-receptor databases, network-based sender/receiver/mediator/influencer identification, and method comparisons, and from [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) which uses curated LR pairs, LIMMA differential expression in TCGA normal/cancer, correlation coefficients, and Kolmogorov-Smirnov tests, establishing that LR expression and co-expression are a necessary but insufficient basis for the plan's pathway-aware objective.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the supplied related works provides quantitative evidence that optimal transport over sender/receiver spaces plus pathway knowledge discrepancy will outperform existing CCI methods; the closest pathway-based method, [(Feng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14be81e003b6638090dd044b97488787a1583b98), reports only qualitative separation of conditions and JAK-STAT/APOE4 networks without baseline comparisons, accuracy, rank, or significance, so the plan's central OT/KD claim is not directly supported. A second major contradiction is that several successful or widely used CCI methods rely primarily on LR expression, correlation, tensor completion, or graph attention rather than pathway KD, suggesting the added pathway objective may be unnecessary or may introduce confounding: [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) uses LIMMA and KS tests on LR correlations; [(Tsuyuzaki et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85e3cc269e8f1f8c12d2b25e692b9c72b589e620) uses CANDECOMP/PARAFAC and Tucker tensor decomposition; [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) uses network centrality and communication probability; [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22) uses GAT over a cell-LR graph; and [(Pan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6ce82762fef1f24c929b87088104e3fcdab0a0) integrates LR scores with Fisher's test. A third contradiction is the evaluation design: the plan compares scKGOT to KG embedding methods using Mean Rank and Hits@K while comparing CCI methods using accuracy and percentile rank, but the related works do not provide a unified ground truth for pathway activation or LR-pathway link prediction; [(Feng et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d5a4a12a5fea08463286e6f293332fa9ecd3968) evaluates KEGG links with a 50/50 positive split and random negatives, which is not equivalent to single-cell CCI accuracy, and [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5620c313d3de7eb83b0a521106618b6dbd7c410e) NicheNet reports only qualitative top interactions without rank or significance. The plan's dataset criterion of at least one ligand-receptor pair per dataset may also be too weak to support pathway-level claims. A fourth contradiction is that the plan's LRP-KG from KEGG and Reactome is not validated by the related works: [(Feng et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d5a4a12a5fea08463286e6f293332fa9ecd3968) uses KEGG but with bulk STRING PPI and TCGA genomic features, while [(Feng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14be81e003b6638090dd044b97488787a1583b98) uses a predefined path list and curated gene-gene network rather than a full KEGG/Reactome LRP-KG, so the scale and species transferability of the proposed graph remain unproven. Weaker contradictions concern reproducibility and statistical power: many related works omit dataset sizes, hyperparameters, random seeds, hardware, and permutation details, and the plan's 100-iteration permutation testing and five independent runs may be insufficient for stable MR/Hits@K or percentile-rank comparisons, especially when baselines such as [(Tsuyuzaki et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85e3cc269e8f1f8c12d2b25e692b9c72b589e620) already use label permutation and [(Pan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6ce82762fef1f24c929b87088104e3fcdab0a0) uses Fisher combination without reporting benchmark significance.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize the scKGOT objective and its connection to optimal transport: define z, C, w_n, D, the factorization of P(z|w_n,D) into gene importance scores and pathway knowledge discrepancy, the construction of P(w_n|D), the cost matrix, marginal distributions, and the loss function, and provide a differentiable or scoring procedure that can be trained or calibrated on labeled LR-pathway or pathway-activation data. The next most important action is to create a unified benchmark with explicit ground truth, using curated LR-pathway pairs, pathway activity labels from perturbation/CRISPR or simulated OT, and the same metrics across baselines, including AUC, precision, recall, F1, MR, Hits@K, accuracy, and percentile rank, with more than 100 permutations, confidence intervals, and multiple random seeds. The plan should add direct baselines and ablations from the related work: compare against [(Feng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14be81e003b6638090dd044b97488787a1583b98) PathFinder, [(So et al., 2024-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93c62304b68e4dce4db66251bb28714c613a8b22) GraphComm, [(Tsuyuzaki et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85e3cc269e8f1f8c12d2b25e692b9c72b589e620) scTensor, [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) CellChat, [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5620c313d3de7eb83b0a521106618b6dbd7c410e) NicheNet, and the listed CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, and CellCall, and ablate pathway KD, gene importance, OT, KEGG-only, Reactome-only, FactDrop, TypeDrop, ExprDrop, and CellDrop. The LRP-KG construction should be specified with database versions, interaction types, node/edge counts, species mapping, confidence scores, and integration of LR resources such as the 3,954-pair database in [(Pan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6ce82762fef1f24c929b87088104e3fcdab0a0), while documenting how KEGG/Reactome pathways are mapped to single-cell sender/receiver genes. Dataset and preprocessing details should be fixed, including the 6 human and 5 mouse datasets, cell counts, QC thresholds analogous to [(Pan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6ce82762fef1f24c929b87088104e3fcdab0a0) genes in fewer than 3 cells and cells with fewer than 200 genes, normalization, batch correction, hardware, software versions, and random seeds. Finally, case studies in placenta, testis, and liver should be validated against independent pathway evidence and statistical tests, and the plan should report biological interpretability metrics such as pathway enrichment, gene importance stability, and comparison to known LR-pathway mechanisms.

## The core methodology involves constructing a Ligand-Receptor-Pathway Knowledge Graph (LRP-KG) that encompasses both intra-cellular and intercellular gene-gene interactions across different pathway types — This knowledge graph provides the prior biological structure that scKGOT uses to connect ligands, receptors, and downstream signaling pathways.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Lagger et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cea7f51aed8b859097ba1c5dad41254fc0c7b793), which directly couples curated ligand-receptor pairs with GO and KEGG pathway annotations in a scRNA-seq cell-cell communication pipeline, using scDiffCom to compute interaction scores from the geometric mean of ligand expression in sender cells and receptor expression in receiver cells and then differential analysis in a mouse aging atlas. This supports the proposed LRP-KG premise that ligand-receptor pairs and KEGG/GO pathway annotations can serve as prior biological structure for intercellular signaling, and it validates the scRNA-seq, cell-type, and pathway-annotation context of the plan. The next strongest support is [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61), whose GrowDAGs method reconstructs receptor-to-transcriptional-regulator signaling DAGs from a weighted directed interactome of 17,513 nodes and 577,617 edges across six NetPath pathways using super source and sink constraints, modified Dijkstra, topological sorting, and min edge cost or min paths cost objectives with k=200 iterations; the min paths cost variant captured ten Wnt signaling nodes with adjusted p-value 8.74e-5 and had 31 enriched Panther pathways, supporting the plan's need for explicit intra-cellular pathway edges connecting receptors to downstream regulators and providing concrete graph-construction, cost, and enrichment-evaluation details that can be adapted to LRP-KG. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8), which provides a receptor-ligand database schema with multidata_table, protein_table, complex_table, fields such as transmembrane, peripheral, secreted, receptor, integrin, and is_complex, supporting the need for a normalized, complex-aware metadata layer for ligand-receptor nodes, although it does not address pathways, KEGG/Reactome, or optimal transport. Taken iteratively, the 2020 schema, the 2021 scRNA-seq communication pipeline, and the 2022 pathway DAG reconstruction form a progression toward the proposed LRP-KG, but none directly validates KGOT.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method's central assumption is that [(Lagger et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cea7f51aed8b859097ba1c5dad41254fc0c7b793) uses KEGG/GO annotations and a simple geometric-mean ligand-receptor expression score in scDiffCom, yet the excerpt reports no accuracy, ranking, baseline comparison, effect size, or statistical significance, so it does not establish that adding pathway-level knowledge graph optimal transport is necessary or superior to expression-based communication scoring. The next strongest contradiction is from [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61), where pathway reconstruction is highly sensitive to the cost function and prior interactome: min edge cost and min paths cost produced different DAG topologies, and for Wnt at k=200 only 15% of predicted nodes were shared across DAG min edge cost, DAG min paths cost, and PathLinker, while an earlier k-shortest paths method captured only about 70% of known signaling interactions. This contradicts the assumption that a single static LRP-KG pathway prior will be stable or sufficient; pathway edges must be validated, ablated, and possibly reconstructed under multiple cost functions before being used to constrain optimal transport. The weakest contradiction is [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8), which provides only a database schema and no pathway-level gene-gene interactions, KEGG/Reactome facts, scRNA-seq evaluation, or quantitative outcomes, so it cannot support the central claim that pathway structure improves intercellular signaling inference.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define the LRP-KG as an explicit multi-relation graph with node types for ligand, receptor, complex, gene, pathway, and regulator, and edge types for intercellular ligand-receptor binding, intra-cellular receptor-to-pathway, pathway-to-gene, and pathway-to-regulator interactions; populate it from KEGG and Reactome with versioned releases and from receptor-ligand databases using a schema inspired by [(Efremova et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3b3e4a2eebc3fad3b03ee6d8228264247abbc8), including complex handling via is_complex and fields for transmembrane, peripheral, secreted, receptor, and integrin. Second, incorporate GrowDAGs-style pathway reconstruction and validation from [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61) by using a weighted directed interactome, negative-log evidence costs, super source and sink constraints, modified Dijkstra, topological sorting, and both min edge cost and min paths cost objectives, then compare reconstructed receptor-to-regulator DAGs by node overlap, enrichment adjusted p-values, and pathway-specific metrics before using them as LRP-KG priors. Third, use [(Lagger et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cea7f51aed8b859097ba1c5dad41254fc0c7b793) as a direct biological and methodological baseline by implementing or reproducing scDiffCom geometric-mean ligand-receptor scoring, differential cell-cell communication, and GO/KEGG annotation, and benchmark scKGOT against it on the same scRNA-seq datasets, reporting accuracy, percentile rank, effect sizes, and permutation p-values. Fourth, expand the evaluation beyond MR and Hits@K to include pathway-level precision/recall, enrichment adjusted p-values, overlap with known pathway nodes, and cell-cell communication metrics, while retaining five random seeds and 100 permutation iterations. Finally, document reproducibility constraints such as KEGG/Reactome versions, ligand-receptor database versions, dataset sizes, cell counts, tissue types, graph size, transport solver, distance matrix construction, marginal distributions, hyperparameters, hardware, runtime, and memory, and include FactDrop, TypeDrop, ExprDrop, and CellDrop ablations to test whether pathway facts and expression quality drive performance.

## We will use KEGG and Reactome databases to build this knowledge graph, incorporating thousands of pathways and millions of interaction records — This step grounds the LRP-KG in curated pathway databases and supplies the large-scale interaction evidence needed for pathway-aware inference.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed KEGG/Reactome-based LRP-KG construction comes from the newer work [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61), which demonstrates that pathway-aware graph inference can be performed on a large weighted directed interactome of 17,513 nodes and 577,617 directed edges. This supports the plausibility of scKGOT’s plan to use curated pathway and interaction evidence to constrain signaling inference, especially because GrowDAGs uses explicit graph structure, super source/sink endpoints, cost-weighted paths, DAG constraints, and k = 200 iterative growth to recover biologically meaningful pathways. The paper also supports the research plan’s emphasis on pathway-level evaluation: minimum all-paths-cost reconstructions produced 113 unique nodes and 31 enriched Panther pathways, including Wnt signaling with 10 nodes and an adjusted p-value of 8.74e-5, showing that pathway-aware graph methods can yield interpretable biological signals rather than only node-level predictions. This is directly relevant to scKGOT’s goal of moving beyond ligand-receptor pair prediction toward pathway-level intercellular signaling inference. The weaker support comes from the older work [(Shao et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7957b93e72b7775066da09cf9fb742eb2707b1f8), which uses SpaTalk to map genes associated with inferred ligand-receptor interactions to pathway and biological-process resources and evaluates their spatial enrichment in 10x Visium and TSK-stroma data. This supports the broader premise that pathway annotation can help interpret ligand-receptor signaling in spatial transcriptomics, including metabolic modulation between periportal and pericentral hepatocytes and signal transmission among glomerular cells. However, this support is conceptual rather than methodological: SpaTalk uses pathway enrichment to interpret already inferred ligand-receptor signals, not to construct a KEGG/Reactome LRP-KG or to train an optimal-transport model, and it provides no numerical benchmark values, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance measures.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not validate the specific claim that KEGG and Reactome can be combined into a large-scale LRP-KG that improves single-cell ligand-receptor inference. [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61) uses NetPath and a weighted directed interactome, not KEGG or Reactome, and shows that naive graph search is insufficient: the k-shortest-paths baseline captured only about 70% of known signaling interactions when using the first 20,000 shortest paths, and minimum edge-cost reconstructions tended to reuse the same nodes across paths. This contradicts a simplistic interpretation of the proposed method in which millions of KEGG/Reactome interaction records can be incorporated directly into an LRP-KG without explicit edge costs, pruning, DAG constraints, or transport constraints. If scKGOT builds a graph with thousands of pathways and millions of interaction records but does not specify how edges are weighted, how redundant pathways are handled, and how computational tractability is maintained, the related work suggests that the resulting graph may be noisy, redundant, or infeasible for optimal transport. The weaker contradiction comes from [(Shao et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7957b93e72b7775066da09cf9fb742eb2707b1f8), which does not provide evidence that pathway resources can be used as predictive graph structure for ligand-receptor inference. SpaTalk applies pathway enrichment after ligand-receptor inference and reports no quantitative performance outcome, baseline comparison, statistical significance, graph construction details, or optimal-transport evaluation. This tempers the proposed method’s assumption that KEGG/Reactome-based graph construction alone will improve precision and interpretability, because the related work shows pathway annotation is useful for interpretation but does not demonstrate that it can serve as the primary predictive substrate for scKGOT.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed method should define an explicit, reproducible KEGG/Reactome LRP-KG construction protocol before applying optimal transport, specifying database versions, species coverage, node and edge types, ligand-receptor mapping, pathway-to-gene mapping, evidence weights, filtering thresholds, and how millions of interaction records are pruned or costed. This should be informed by [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61), which shows that large-scale pathway inference benefits from negative-log transformed evidence weights, super source/sink constraints, DAG growth, and cost functions that reduce redundant node reuse. Second, the research plan should add quantitative benchmarks for the KG construction step itself, reporting node and edge counts, pathway coverage, redundancy, runtime, memory usage, and hardware specifications, and should compare KEGG-only, Reactome-only, and combined graphs to show that the larger graph improves downstream Hits@K, MR, accuracy, and percentile rank rather than merely increasing scale. Third, the method should incorporate pathway-level enrichment evaluation in the main benchmark, as done in [(Shao et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7957b93e72b7775066da09cf9fb742eb2707b1f8) and [(Köse et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3ddc4111caccdfab9958a2b093dc5a49fd7d61), by reporting enriched pathways, unique nodes, adjusted p-values, and comparisons to baselines, so that pathway interpretability is evaluated statistically rather than only qualitatively. Fourth, the ablation studies should include graph-constraint and cost-function ablations analogous to minimum edge-cost versus minimum all-paths-cost and k-shortest-paths baselines, to demonstrate that the LRP-KG’s pathway-aware constraints improve over simpler expression-based or shortest-path baselines. Finally, the plan should document computational resources, hyperparameters such as transport regularization or pathway growth iterations, and dataset sizes to ensure that the large-scale KEGG/Reactome graph step is reproducible and not an unsupported scalability claim.

## We define sender and receiver spaces using pairwise distance matrices derived from gene expression profiles, with marginal distributions representing relative gene abundance — This creates the single-cell expression representations that become the source and target distributions in the optimal transport model.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c), which applies regularized optimal transport to empirical single-cell RNA-seq distributions and reports quantitative gains in a haematopoietic batch-correction and label-transfer task. FactoredOT achieved 14.10% ± 4.44 mean misclassification, outperforming mutual nearest neighbors at 17.53% ± 5.09, plain optimal transport at 17.47% ± 3.17, entropically regularized optimal transport at 18.58% ± 6.57, group-lasso optimal transport at 15.47% ± 5.35, k-means centroid optimal transport at 15.37% ± 4.76, subspace alignment at 15.10% ± 3.14, transfer component analysis at 24.57% ± 7.04, and nearest-neighbor majority vote at 21.98% ± 4.90. This directly supports the feasibility of using optimal transport over single-cell expression-derived source and target distributions, although it uses empirical cell measures and 20-nearest-neighbor label transfer rather than relative gene-abundance marginals or pathway-aware signaling inference. A second, more structural support is [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c), which defines Fused Gromov-Wasserstein distances between graph measures using feature distances, shortest-path structure matrices, and histogram marginal weights. This is analogous to the proposed sender and receiver spaces built from pairwise distance matrices and marginal distributions, but the paper is limited to 12 graph-classification datasets, uses nested cross validation with 9 training folds and 1 test fold repeated 10 times, searches alpha over 15 logspace values in [0, 0.5] and symmetrically in [0.5, 1], gamma from 2^-10 to 2^10, and C from 1e-7 to 1e7, and the supplied excerpt does not provide numerical accuracies or significance values. The earliest support is [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45), which demonstrates an entropic optimal-transport distance using a ground cost matrix and marginal distributions, with 20 Sinkhorn-Knopp iterations, lambda selected from {5,7,9,11} x 1/q50(M), and a downstream SVM kernel exp(-d/t) with t chosen from {1, q10(d), q20(d), q50(d)} on MNIST 20x20 images. As an iterative scientific line, Cuturi supplies the entropic distance primitive, Vayer extends the idea to structured graph measures, and Forrow applies OT to scRNA-seq, collectively supporting the proposed representation while leaving pathway-specific ligand-receptor inference unvalidated.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is not a direct refutation but a performance contradiction: [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c) shows that plain optimal transport and entropically regularized optimal transport are outperformed by FactoredOT on scRNA-seq, with plain OT at 17.47% ± 3.17 and entropic OT at 18.58% ± 6.57 versus FactoredOT at 14.10% ± 4.44. This partially contradicts the earlier assumption in [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45) that a fixed-iteration entropic OT distance is sufficient as a general-purpose distance, and it warns that the proposed scKGOT method should not rely only on raw pairwise gene-expression distances and standard marginal constraints without additional structure or regularization. A weaker contradiction is that [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c) does not provide numerical classification outcomes in the supplied excerpt, uses an indefinite kernel with C-SVM, and evaluates graph classification rather than single-cell signaling, so it cannot substantiate the planned MR, Hits@K, accuracy, or permutation-based evaluation for intercellular communication. The weakest contradiction is that [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45) reports no numerical classification-error values, effect sizes, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests, uses an arbitrary 20 fixed-point iteration count, and evaluates MNIST rather than scRNA-seq, gene-abundance marginals, KEGG/Reactome pathways, or ligand-receptor pairs. None of the related works directly contradicts the use of pairwise distance matrices and marginal distributions, but they also do not validate the specific claim that relative gene-abundance marginals are the correct source and target measures for pathway-mediated signaling.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed method should explicitly benchmark the marginal and cost choices that define the sender and receiver spaces: compare relative gene-abundance marginals against empirical cell-count marginals, and compare raw gene-expression pairwise distances against pathway- or KG-informed distances, using the planned 6 human and 5 mouse scRNA-seq datasets, five random seeds, permutation testing, MR, Hits@K, and accuracy with percentile rank. Next, incorporate low-rank or transport-rank regularization, k-Wasserstein barycenters, or FactoredOT-style constraints, because [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c) shows that such regularization improves scRNA-seq alignment over plain and entropic OT. Then, adopt Sinkhorn divergence and rigorous lambda and iteration tuning with convergence diagnostics, following [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45), rather than fixing 20 iterations, and report the selected lambda, iteration count, runtime, and memory usage. Additionally, consider fused Gromov-Wasserstein or structure-preserving OT, following [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c), to preserve pathway and graph structure in the sender and receiver spaces, especially when using KEGG and Reactome-derived LRP-KG. Finally, include ablations for ExprDrop, CellDrop, FactDrop, and TypeDrop, and compare against NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, CellChat, and CellCall with statistical significance tests, to ensure that the pathway-aware OT model outperforms both generic OT and existing cell-cell communication baselines.

## The optimal transport framework will minimize a loss function that considers correlation distances between sender and receiver cells while respecting transport constraints — This loss operationalizes the transport problem by aligning sender and receiver gene signals under pathway-aware constraints.

### Support

Read as an iterative line of work, the strongest support comes from newer COMMOT, which formulates intercellular signaling as collective optimal transport between sender and receiver cells, uses expression-derived correlation or signal distances as transport cost, enforces transport constraints, and is benchmarked against CellChat, Giotto, and CellPhoneDB in spatial transcriptomics, including human epidermal development [(Cang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0947b6f97cae0c20e8f3e8302d837ee2c65f54b) and [(Cang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f154036c2e13e28e3a8aceb0676fa4dd406d6707). This directly supports the proposed loss that aligns sender and receiver gene signals under transport constraints, although it does not yet incorporate KEGG/Reactome pathway knowledge or LRP-KG constraints. A quantitative scRNA-seq precedent is FactoredOT, which achieved 14.10 mean misclassification with 4.44 standard deviation, outperforming entropic OT at 18.58 with 6.57, empirical OT at 17.47 with 3.17, group-lasso OT at 15.47 with 5.35, k-means centroid OT at 15.37 with 4.76, subspace alignment at 15.10 with 3.14, mutual nearest neighbors at 17.53 with 5.09, nearest-neighbor voting at 21.98 with 4.90, and transfer component analysis at 24.57 with 7.04, showing that constrained or regularized OT can improve high-dimensional single-cell alignment and downstream 20-nearest-neighbor label transfer [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c). Structured OT also supports adding pathway-like structure to the transport cost: Fused Gromov-Wasserstein aligns feature and structure spaces using l2 or Hamming/Weisfeiler-Lehman feature distances and shortest-path structure distances, solves the nonconvex quadratic problem with conditional gradient, and uses C-SVM with C cross-validated from 1e-7 to 1e7, gamma from 2^-10 to 2^10, and alpha over 15 logspace values in [0,0.5] and [0.5,1] [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c). The computational template for entropic OT is supported by Sinkhorn distance on MNIST, where a 20x20 pixel grid yields a 400x400 Euclidean metric, kernel bandwidth t is chosen from 1%, 10%, 20%, and 50% distance quantiles, entropic penalty lambda is chosen from {5,7,9,11} times 1/q50(M), 20 Sinkhorn iterations are used, and the method outperformed Hellinger, chi-square, total variation, squared Euclidean/Gaussian, independence, and EMD distances [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45). The weakest support is conceptual: spatial transcriptomic interaction analysis frames sender-receiver communication through gene-expression correlation and spatial range, but does not implement OT or pathway-aware constraints [(Liu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94f8a2bb7fbf6ea93036ebf8b2a66190e40ff8f9).

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is quantitative and newer than the original entropic OT optimism: in the haematopoietic scRNA-seq batch-correction experiment, plain entropic OT had 18.58 mean misclassification with 6.57 standard deviation, worse than FactoredOT at 14.10 with 4.44, subspace alignment at 15.10 with 3.14, k-means centroid OT at 15.37 with 4.76, group-lasso OT at 15.47 with 5.35, empirical OT at 17.47 with 3.17, and mutual nearest neighbors at 17.53 with 5.09, suggesting that a simple entropic OT loss over correlation distances may be insufficient for high-dimensional single-cell gene-expression alignment unless it includes low-rank, transport-rank, or other regularizing structure [(Forrow et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a851c9245c82a94ecac4eef87a196339eebf400c). A second tension is computational: adding pathway structure to OT is not guaranteed to be easy, because Fused Gromov-Wasserstein is a nonconvex quadratic transport problem that requires conditional gradient, a linear OT subproblem, constrained line search, and careful tuning of gamma and alpha, with no numeric accuracy reported in the excerpt [(Vayer et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f9de3cd77ac32ce00da3ba6b71e2360b5b2a81c). A third tension is domain transfer: the strongest entropic OT evidence on MNIST uses pixel-level 400x400 Euclidean distances, fixed 20 Sinkhorn iterations, and reports that Mahalanobis ground metrics of the form exp(-t M^2) and its inverse were not competitive, so the proposed correlation distance and pathway-aware cost must be explicitly validated rather than assumed to transfer from image pixels to gene signaling [(Cuturi, 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0080118b0eb02af581ff32b85a1bb6aed7081f45). A fourth tension is that COMMOT, although directly related, does not provide numeric performance values, statistical significance, dataset sizes, hyperparameters, or pathway-aware KG constraints, so it supports the OT formulation but does not prove that KEGG/Reactome pathway constraints improve over correlation-based signaling inference [(Cang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0947b6f97cae0c20e8f3e8302d837ee2c65f54b) and [(Cang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f154036c2e13e28e3a8aceb0676fa4dd406d6707). The weakest tension is that correlation-based sender-receiver interaction analysis is presented conceptually without an implemented algorithm, dataset-level evaluation, or statistical tests, so relying on correlation distances alone is under-supported [(Liu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94f8a2bb7fbf6ea93036ebf8b2a66190e40ff8f9).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the pathway-aware constraint explicit in the OT objective rather than leaving it as a general statement: define an LRP-KG-derived cost or regularizer, for example a pathway discrepancy term, pathway-specific marginal constraints, or a Fused Gromov-Wasserstein structure term built from KEGG/Reactome edges, and test it with FactDrop and TypeDrop ablations. The next most important action is to avoid relying on plain entropic OT by adding and benchmarking low-rank or transport-rank regularization, group-lasso OT, k-means centroid OT, subspace alignment, and FactoredOT-style couplings, because FactoredOT outperformed entropic OT in scRNA-seq alignment. The third action is to specify a reproducible solver and hyperparameter protocol: use the Python Optimal Transport toolbox or equivalent, choose entropic penalty and kernel bandwidth from distance quantiles as in Cuturi, avoid fixed 20 iterations without convergence checks, tune F GW gamma and alpha with nested cross-validation, and report runtime, convergence, random seeds, hardware, and software versions. The fourth action is to align evaluation with the strongest related baselines and the research plan: compare against CellChat, Giotto, CellPhoneDB, NicheNet, CellCall, SingleCellSignalR, and KG embedding baselines, and report MR, Hits@K for K=1,5,10,50, accuracy with percentile rank, downstream target genes, pathway enrichment, and permutation tests with 100 iterations across five seeds. The least important but still useful action is to include spatial transcriptomics or spatial coordinates where available, because COMMOT uses spatial resolution to infer signaling direction, and to validate correlation-distance design against expression distance, KG edge cost, and pathway activity scores before using it as the primary transport cost.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to expand the benchmark beyond the 6 human and 5 mouse at-least-one-LR-pair criterion by adopting the evaluation dimensions used in [(Luo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/077e018171fde37914036ecd2c9d8da5a87a8e53): add spatially informed positive and negative cell-pair controls, compute a differential LR correlation index where spatial transcriptomics are available, validate a subset of predicted LR interactions with CAGE or proteomics when possible, test robustness to reduced scRNA-seq sampling rates, and report running time and maximum memory usage for scKGOT and baselines. The second most important action is to standardize and document all baseline implementations, especially CellPhoneDB, CellChat, SingleCellSignalR, NicheNet, and CellCall, by fixing LR database versions, KEGG/Reactome pathway versions, significance thresholds, permutation counts, normalization, clustering, and software versions, and by reporting the five random seeds and hardware used, consistent with the reproducibility gaps noted in [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) and [(King et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/667bd85d2ff7652b626f75f6c50d15715ba56b62). The third action is to define a common evaluation protocol that maps KG link-prediction metrics, MR and Hits@K with K=1,5,10,50 under filtered settings, to cell-cell interaction metrics, accuracy and percentile rank, using the same positive and negative cell-pair labels, the same 100-iteration permutation testing, and reported p-values or confidence intervals across five independent runs. The fourth action is to enrich dataset reporting by listing accession numbers, species, tissue, cell counts, number of relevant LR pairs, cell-pair definitions, preprocessing, and filtering, so that the at-least-one-LR-pair criterion is not the only benchmarking justification. The least important but still useful action is to tie the planned ablations, FactDrop, TypeDrop, ExprDrop, and CellDrop, and case studies in placenta, testis, and liver, to the same quantitative metrics and permutation-based significance tests so that pathway-level interpretability claims are supported by reproducible benchmark evidence.

## Knowledge graph embedding methods (TransE, DistMult, RotatE, ComplEx) to evaluate our approach as a multi-relation link prediction problem — This baseline category tests whether scKGOT outperforms general knowledge graph link prediction methods on the same relational signaling task.

### Support

The strongest support for using TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx as the proposed multi-relation link-prediction baseline category comes from [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b), which evaluates exactly these four embedding methods, plus DeepWalk, on a heterogeneous biomedical KG with 3 entity types, 8 relation types, 604,903 training, 67,212 validation, and 168,029 test relations under a stratified 70/20/10 relation split. This is the closest available analogue to scKGOT's LRP-KG because it is biomedical, multi-relational, and uses the same baseline family, and it shows that RotatE (AUROC 0.793, AUPR 0.759, precision@1000 0.909), DistMult (AUROC 0.765, AUPR 0.696, precision@1000 0.656), ComplEx (AUROC 0.757, AUPR 0.699, precision@1000 0.622), and TransE (AUROC 0.667, AUPR 0.633, precision@1000 0.715) are nontrivial baselines on relation prediction. The next strongest support is [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935), which provides the filtered link-prediction protocol that the scKGOT plan explicitly adopts, using MR, MRR, and Hits@N on FB15k, WN18, FB15k-237, WN18RR, and Countries, and shows RotatE can outperform TransE, DistMult, ComplEx, HolE, and ConvE while also revealing relation-pattern dependence, e.g. RotatE Hits@10 0.922/0.923 for FB15k 1-to-1 head/tail and 0.602/0.961 for N-to-1 head/tail. This supports the plan's decision to benchmark scKGOT against RotatE in particular, because RotatE's phase-based rotations can capture symmetry, antisymmetry, inversion, and composition that may exist in ligand-receptor-pathway relations. The third supporting line is [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc), which extends the baseline family with ComplEx and demonstrates that filtered and raw ranking metrics diverge substantially, e.g. on FB15K ComplEx filtered MRR 0.692 versus raw MRR 0.242, and that negative sampling affects performance, with 50 negatives identified as a trade-off and 100 negatives improving filtered MRR to 0.737 before 200 negatives hurt. This directly supports the plan's filtered Hits@K protocol and the need to specify negative generation for sender/receiver/pathway triples. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Yang et al., 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86412306b777ee35aba71d4795b02915cb8a04c3), which established TransE and DistMult-style baselines on WordNet, FB15k, and FB15k-401, reporting improved TransE HITS@10 from 47.1% to 53.9% on FB15k and DistMult advantages on FB15k-401 relation categories, thereby showing that these older baselines remain meaningful reference points for multi-relational link prediction. Viewed iteratively, this line of work moves from Yang 2014's TransE/DistMult foundations, to Trouillon 2016's ComplEx and filtered/raw diagnostics, to Sun 2018's RotatE and self-adversarial sampling, and finally to Krix 2022's biomedical multi-relational evaluation, which collectively justify the proposed baseline category but also require careful adaptation to scKGOT's signaling graph.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or risk is that the same biomedical evidence that supports the baseline category also shows these generic KG embeddings can be very strong, so scKGOT must beat strong non-biological baselines to justify pathway-aware optimal transport. In [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b), RotatE reached AUROC 0.793 and precision@1000 0.909 for general relation prediction, and DistMult reached AUROC 0.963, AUPR 0.966, and precision@30 1.0 on drug-ADE prediction, indicating that a simple embedding baseline may already capture much of the signal in a biomedical multi-relational graph; if scKGOT is evaluated only on aggregate Hits@K without relation-specific or pathway-specific diagnostics, it may be difficult to demonstrate a meaningful advantage. The next contradiction is metric and protocol mismatch: Krix uses AUROC, AUPR, and precision@k with 1:1 uniform negative sampling, whereas the scKGOT plan uses filtered MR and Hits@K, so the strongest biomedical evidence does not directly validate the plan's exact evaluation setup. This also reflects an iterative progression in which newer work complicates older claims: [(Yang et al., 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86412306b777ee35aba71d4795b02915cb8a04c3) established TransE/DistMult but did not use filtered evaluation or report significance, [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc) showed that filtered and raw metrics can differ greatly, [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935) showed that RotatE can dominate but relation patterns matter, and [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b) transferred the baselines to a biomedical KG while changing the metric suite. The third contradiction is that filtered ranking can mask or amplify performance depending on candidate filtering and negative sampling. [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc) reports large gaps between filtered and raw metrics, e.g. ComplEx filtered MRR 0.692 but raw MRR 0.242 on FB15K, and shows that increasing negatives from 50 to 100 improved filtered MRR to 0.737 but 200 negatives degraded it, meaning the plan's filtered Hits@K results will be sensitive to how corrupted sender/receiver/pathway candidates are generated and filtered. The fourth contradiction is that baseline performance is relation-pattern dependent, so a single aggregate comparison may be misleading. [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935) shows RotatE is strong on symmetry/antisymmetry/inversion but TransE can slightly outperform RotatE on Countries S3 composition, and FB15k category results vary, e.g. RotatE Hits@10 0.602 head for N-to-1 versus 0.961 tail, so scKGOT's advantage may depend on the cardinality and directionality of ligand-receptor-pathway relations. The weakest contradiction is that none of the four papers directly validates these baselines on single-cell ligand-receptor-pathway signaling data, and several lack statistical significance or hardware details: [(Yang et al., 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86412306b777ee35aba71d4795b02915cb8a04c3) does not report filtered evaluation or significance, [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc) uses generic WN18/FB15K, [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935) does not report hardware, and [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b) uses a drug-protein-phenotype KG rather than a signaling LRP-KG.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the four baselines on the exact same LRP-KG train/validation/test split as scKGOT, using filtered ranking for MR and Hits@K (K=1,5,10,50) while also reporting AUROC, AUPR, and precision@k so that the results are comparable to the strongest biomedical evidence from [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b). The second most important action is to standardize negative sampling and candidate filtering: generate corrupted sender/receiver/pathway triples by replacing one endpoint, remove all known training/validation/test triples from the candidate list, and test negative ratios such as 1:1, 10:1, 50:1, 100:1, and 1000:1 because [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b) found AUROC/AUPR stable from 1:1 to 1000:1 and precision@30 stable up to 100:1, while [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc) found 50 negatives a practical trade-off and 100 negatives can improve filtered MRR. The third action is to tune and report baseline hyperparameters per method, including embedding dimension, batch size, optimizer, learning rate, margin, regularization, number of negatives, epochs, and early stopping, following the ranges used in the literature: dimension 100 and AdaGrad in [(Yang et al., 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86412306b777ee35aba71d4795b02915cb8a04c3), dimensions 10-200 and batch 100 in [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc), dimensions 125-1000, batch 512-2048, Adam, margin 3-30, and self-adversarial sampling in [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935). The fourth action is to report relation-specific and cardinality-specific results, because [(Sun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f096071a09701012c9c279aee2a88143a295935) and [(Krix et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dd13cff173b970b24eba00aacb3e0209722f8b5b) show that RotatE, DistMult, and ComplEx can dominate some relation types while TransE or other baselines can be competitive on others; scKGOT should therefore report Hits@K/MR by ligand-receptor, receptor-pathway, pathway-gene, and sender-receiver relation types, and by 1-to-1, 1-to-n, n-to-1, and n-to-n cardinality if applicable. The fifth action is to add statistical significance and reproducibility details: use the plan's five random seeds and 100-iteration permutation testing, but also report confidence intervals, paired comparisons against each baseline, and hardware/runtime information, because the related works generally omit significance or hardware details. The least important but still useful action is to include raw and filtered metrics, as in [(Trouillon et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2218e2e1df2c3adfb70e0def2e326a39928aacfc), to diagnose whether scKGOT's advantage is due to filtering, negative sampling, or genuine pathway-aware ranking.

## Specialized cell-cell interaction prediction methods (NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, CellChat, CellCall) — This baseline category tests scKGOT against existing tools designed specifically for ligand-receptor and cell-cell communication inference.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed baseline category is that [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1f49ef227875b57f0e4ef62c308d77b043ecca77) directly applies CellChat version 1.1.3, one of the five specialized cell-cell interaction tools named in the scKGOT plan, to Seurat objects built from preprocessed scRNA-seq matrices, showing that CellChat can infer ligand-receptor-based communication, rank signaling pathways, and compare EBV DNA Sero- versus EBV DNA Sero+ nasopharyngeal carcinoma samples. This supports the plan's decision to include CellChat among NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, and CellCall as a relevant baseline for intercellular signaling inference. A second, weaker but still useful support is that the study's preprocessing details—Seurat version 4.1.0, removal of cells with UMI below 200, expressed genes above 9000 or below 200, mitochondrial genes above 20 percent, normalization and scaling with cell-cycle score regression using the top 6000 highly variable genes—provide a concrete, reproducible input pipeline that scKGOT can mirror when benchmarking specialized tools. The weakest support is that the study's biological findings, including higher interaction abundance and intensity in EBV DNA Sero+ samples, macrophages having the most incoming and outgoing signaling, memory B cells showing strong incoming signaling only in EBV DNA Sero+ samples, and upregulated SPP1, BTLA, TNFSF12, and IL6 pathways, illustrate the kind of cell-, gene-, and pathway-level interpretation that the scKGOT plan aims to improve, but they do not provide quantitative ground truth for benchmarking.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the CellChat analysis in [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1f49ef227875b57f0e4ef62c308d77b043ecca77) is reported qualitatively, without exact interaction counts, pathway scores, p-values, effect sizes, or comparisons against other cell-cell communication tools, whereas the scKGOT plan requires rigorous benchmarking with accuracy, percentile rank, five random seeds, and 100-iteration permutation testing. This gap means the related work cannot directly validate the proposed method's claim that scKGOT will outperform specialized tools in precision and interpretability, and it also prevents positioning CellChat as an iterative improvement over older or newer specialized baselines. A second contradiction is that the reported pathway-level conclusions, such as upregulation of SPP1, BTLA, TNFSF12, and IL6 in EBV DNA Sero+ nasopharyngeal carcinoma, are not accompanied by numerical CellChat significance metrics, even though the paper generally defines statistical significance as two-sided P less than 0.05; this contradicts the assumption that existing specialized methods alone provide sufficiently quantified pathway evidence for scKGOT's pathway-centric optimal transport model. The weakest contradiction is that the study used only 10 EBV-related nasopharyngeal carcinoma samples and 1 chronic nasopharyngitis sample, with no hardware specifications or detailed CellChat hyperparameters beyond version 1.1.3, limiting reproducibility and generalizability to the broader 6 human and 5 mouse scRNA-seq benchmarking design proposed in the plan.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement CellChat version 1.1.3, or a documented current equivalent, as a quantitative baseline in the scKGOT benchmark by extracting interaction counts, pathway scores, p-values or FDR, and effect sizes, then compare these outputs to scKGOT using the plan's accuracy and percentile rank metrics, and where applicable MR and Hits@K, under the same five random seeds and 100-iteration permutation testing. The second most important action is to standardize preprocessing exactly as in [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1f49ef227875b57f0e4ef62c308d77b043ecca77) for fair comparison: use Seurat version 4.1.0 or a documented equivalent, apply the same QC filters (UMI below 200, expressed genes above 9000 or below 200, mitochondrial genes above 20 percent), normalize and scale with cell-cycle score regression, and use the top 6000 highly variable genes, while recording all hyperparameters, software versions, and hardware specifications. The third action is to use the 10 EBV-related nasopharyngeal carcinoma samples and 1 chronic nasopharyngitis sample as a case study, curating known ligand-receptor pairs and ground-truth interactions to test whether scKGOT recovers the reported macrophage, memory B cell, and dendritic cell communication patterns and SPP1, BTLA, TNFSF12, and IL6 pathway signals, especially the difference between EBV DNA Sero- and EBV DNA Sero+ samples. The fourth action is to extend the benchmark to the other named specialized tools—NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, and CellCall—across the planned 6 human and 5 mouse datasets, ensuring each dataset contains at least one validated ligand-receptor pair and that all methods receive identical Seurat objects and evaluation settings. The least important but still useful action is to explicitly report the limitations inherited from the related work, including small sample size, qualitative baseline reporting, and missing hardware details, so that scKGOT's conclusions are not overgeneralized from a single cancer immune-microenvironment study.

## We will report Mean Rank (MR) and Hits@K (K=1,5,10,50) based on filtered settings for knowledge graph methods, and accuracy with percentile rank for cell-cell interaction methods — These metrics define how scKGOT and baselines will be quantitatively compared across the two benchmark categories.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Bonner et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e712910ff1b81b6fe2a5e71515b68e91e679e79), which benchmarks TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx on biomedical knowledge graphs using a filtered left/right ranking protocol that removes corrupted triples already present in the graph, handles ties by averaging optimistic and pessimistic ranks, and reports AMR, MRR, Hits@1, and Hits@10. This is directly analogous to the proposed filtered MR and Hits@K for KG baselines, and its use of repeated random 10% splits, PyKEEN 1.0.6, PyTorch 1.7, CUDA 10.1, NVIDIA V100 32GB, and Optuna HPO supports the plan's emphasis on multiple seeds and robust benchmarking. It also shows that RotatE was the strongest and most stable baseline, DistMult was highly variable, and ComplEx was dataset-dependent, which supports including all four baselines but warns that fixed hyperparameters may not be fair. Next, [(Trouillon et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4dfb121275a6408d290b803baf8c9caeb23dc5b) supports the filtered evaluation of ComplEx by reporting filtered MRR and Hits@1/3/10 on WN18 and FB15K, showing large gaps between raw and filtered MRR, and providing concrete training details such as AdaGrad, early stopping, rank K, L2 regularization, negatives per positive, and single-GPU runtimes. This supports the proposed use of filtered ranking for ComplEx and the need to report tuning details. [(Zhang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86ac98157da100a529ca65fe6e1da064b0a651e8) further supports filtered ranking for TransE, TransH, and DistMult extensions, reporting MR, MRR, and Hits@1/3/10 under a filtered setting, and it highlights that MR can be affected by high-rank outliers, which supports reporting both MR and Hits@K. [(Nordsieck et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc5645dcbf7c0a32453ac96d0b6e77a841ac956) supports Hits@K-style evaluation and repeated runs, using hits@1/5/10, AMRI, 30 runs, PyKEEN, Adam, learning rate 4e-4, weight decay 1e-5, and method-specific epochs for TransE, ComplEx, RotatE, and DistMult. [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f442378ead6282024cf5b9046daa10422fe9fc5f) supports the use of MRR and Hits@K with multiple seeds and shows that negative sampling, especially personalized hard negatives, can substantially change rankings, reinforcing the need to specify the filtered/negative protocol. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Zhang et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38c4c08a873dea392d5a873560f71f8e8a77fd6f), which uses MRR and Hits@10 for KG baselines; [(Nordsieck et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f896b953b6096e190569250d1fb9f6901457373), which uses a Hits@K-like matches@k metric in an incomplete-ground-truth industrial KG; [(Guo et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d836af547c4cab3b6335d19f23d1a1fe04cba80), which reports Mean Rank, MRR, and Hits@1/3 for a KG link-prediction task; [(Zhu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86298b802961cb5dfcc9d7b9e60846641a99cba1), which provides a graph benchmark framework with MRR and Hits@1/10 and hard-negative evaluation; and [(Guo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/caba5f99e464d49fbfcb2eb732433aca8111dcaf), which includes permutation-based hypothesis testing, although it does not report the proposed metric suite.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that the related work shows filtered MR/Hits@K is not a single well-defined protocol unless negative sampling, tie handling, and candidate set are specified. [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f442378ead6282024cf5b9046daa10422fe9fc5f) shows that moving from global random negatives to HeaRT personalized hard negatives drops MRR from around 30-50 on Cora/Citeseer/Pubmed to around 10-25 and changes which methods rank best, so a generic filtered setting may produce metrics that are not comparable across studies or may be too easy if all entities are used without hard negatives. [(Bonner et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e712910ff1b81b6fe2a5e71515b68e91e679e79) contradicts the assumption that TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx can be compared with a simple fixed setup: it reports large variability for DistMult, dataset-dependent ComplEx, sensitivity to optimizer, loss, inverse relations, and HPO, and finds that task-specific HPO on sparse gene-disease edges improved ComplEx but reduced RotatE, meaning that globally tuned baselines may not be fair. It also reports AMR rather than MR, so the proposed MR must be defined carefully to avoid confusion with average mean rank or mean reciprocal rank. [(Zhang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86ac98157da100a529ca65fe6e1da064b0a651e8) shows that MR can be distorted by high-rank outliers, with 56 test triples ranked above 10000 causing more than 110 MR loss for TransE-middle-bottom and 37 triples above 7000 causing about 50 MR loss for DistMult-middle-bottom, so reporting MR without rank distributions or outlier analysis may overstate or understate performance. [(Nordsieck et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f896b953b6096e190569250d1fb9f6901457373) and [(Nordsieck et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc5645dcbf7c0a32453ac96d0b6e77a841ac956) use non-standard Hits@K-like metrics, such as matches@k with K=3 and in-sample subgraph evaluation, and do not report filtered MR or Hits@50, which limits direct comparability of the proposed Hits@50 to prior industrial KG work. [(Guo et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d836af547c4cab3b6335d19f23d1a1fe04cba80) reports Mean Rank 1.89, Hits@1 0.46, Hits@3 0.92, and MRR 0.68 on MovieLens without stating a filtered protocol or baseline comparison, illustrating that unfiltered or under-specified MR/Hits can be optimistic and hard to interpret. [(Zhu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86298b802961cb5dfcc9d7b9e60846641a99cba1) uses hard valid/test negatives and MRR/Hits@1/10 rather than the proposed filtered MR/Hits@50, suggesting that the proposed metric suite may omit a difficulty-controlled negative sampling component. Finally, none of the related work directly validates accuracy with percentile rank for cell-cell interaction methods, so that part of the proposed method has weaker empirical support than the KG ranking metrics.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define the filtered KG evaluation protocol in full: for each held-out LRP-KG triple, corrupt head and tail, remove all triples present in the training, validation, and test graphs before ranking, specify whether inverse relations are included, specify tie handling, and report both raw and filtered MR/Hits@K. Because [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f442378ead6282024cf5b9046daa10422fe9fc5f) and [(Zhu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86298b802961cb5dfcc9d7b9e60846641a99cba1) show that negative sampling strongly affects MRR and Hits@K, the plan should add a hard-negative sensitivity analysis, for example sampling a fixed number of plausible negative entities or using a HeaRT-like personalized hard-negative procedure, while keeping the main filtered all-entity protocol for comparability. Second, following [(Bonner et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e712910ff1b81b6fe2a5e71515b68e91e679e79) and [(Trouillon et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4dfb121275a6408d290b803baf8c9caeb23dc5b), the KG baselines should be tuned with a fixed validation split and a reproducible HPO procedure, reporting optimizer, loss, embedding dimension, negative sampling rate, margin or regularization, epochs, early stopping, software versions, hardware, and random seeds; the plan should not assume default TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx settings are sufficient, especially because DistMult was highly variable and biomedical KGs often require larger dimensions and more negatives. Third, to address the MR outlier issue in [(Zhang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86ac98157da100a529ca65fe6e1da064b0a651e8), report MR together with median rank, rank percentiles, the number of high-rank failures, and Hits@1/5/10/50, and use the planned five seeds and 100-iteration permutation tests to report confidence intervals and significance. Fourth, for cell-cell interaction methods, define accuracy and percentile rank precisely: specify ground-truth ligand-receptor/pathway positives, how negatives are constructed, whether filtering removes known interactions, how percentile rank is computed across sender-receiver pairs or datasets, and how thresholds are chosen; if possible, also report rank-based metrics for cell-cell methods to make the two benchmark categories more comparable. Finally, include detailed reproducibility information, including dataset sizes, species/tissue, split strategy, number of cells/genes, LRP-KG edge counts, baseline hyperparameters, and ablation settings, as emphasized by [(Nordsieck et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1dc5645dcbf7c0a32453ac96d0b6e77a841ac956), [(Bonner et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e712910ff1b81b6fe2a5e71515b68e91e679e79), and [(Guo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/caba5f99e464d49fbfcb2eb732433aca8111dcaf).

## All results will be derived from five independent runs using different random seeds, with permutation testing using 100 iterations — This statistical protocol ensures that reported performance is reproducible and that observed differences are not due to random variation.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed five-seed and 100-permutation protocol comes from [(Dimitrov et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/147b83171b2d5a6a05f7384af6cccd43de20df24), which shows that cell-cell communication inference is highly sensitive to both algorithm and resource, with methods outweighing resources, and that only a few biological patterns were robust across tools in a colorectal cancer scRNA-seq benchmark of 65,362 cells from 23 patients, analyzed on a 42,544-cell, 18-cell-type subset. This directly motivates repeated independent runs and permutation-based nulls to separate reproducible signal from random variation in scKGOT. A weaker but still relevant support is the same paper's benchmarking design, which retains top 500 interactions with sensitivity checks at 100, 250, and 1000, builds binary presence-absence matrices, computes Jaccard indices, clusters results, applies PCA to z-normalized average interaction ranks, and uses Fisher's exact tests against a union of resources; this supports the plan's need for rank-based, top-K, and enrichment-style evaluation alongside Mean Rank and Hits@K. The weakest, more recent support is [(Cang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0947b6f97cae0c20e8f3e8302d837ee2c65f54b), whose COMMOT optimal transport framework for spatial transcriptomics reports more robust identification of communication direction and downstream targets than CellChat, Giotto, and CellPhoneDB, supporting the broader optimal-transport premise of scKGOT, but the excerpt does not provide seed-run variability, permutation counts, exact metrics, or p-values, so it does not directly validate the five-seed/100-permutation protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Dimitrov et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/147b83171b2d5a6a05f7384af6cccd43de20df24) found p-value-based ranking infeasible because CellChat significant hits ranged from 237 to 12,971 depending on resource, with 3,041 significant hits using its inbuilt resource, forcing filtering at p-value <= 0.05 followed by probability-score ranking; this suggests that a fixed 100-iteration permutation test may have too little resolution to produce stable p-values for many ligand-receptor-pathway interactions and could make scKGOT's reported significance sensitive to permutation count. A second contradiction is that the same paper does not report random seeds, number of independent runs, or 100-iteration permutation testing, so it provides no direct empirical evidence that five seeds and 100 permutations are sufficient for reproducibility in large scRNA-seq communication benchmarks; its pooled 23-patient analysis also limits patient-specific interpretation, which is relevant to the plan's multi-dataset benchmarking. The weaker, more recent contradiction is that [(Cang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0947b6f97cae0c20e8f3e8302d837ee2c65f54b) claims robustness and improved metric performance over baselines but the provided excerpt lacks exact numerical values, seed-run variability, permutation counts, p-values, dataset sizes, hardware, and hyperparameters, so its robustness claim cannot be used to justify the specific five-seed/100-permutation protocol or to confirm that such a protocol is adequate for scKGOT.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to strengthen the statistical protocol by increasing permutation iterations beyond 100, for example 1,000 or 10,000, or by using an exact/rank-based null model, and to report the minimum resolvable p-value, per-seed Mean Rank and Hits@K, mean and standard deviation across the five seeds, and permutation p-values for each seed, because [(Dimitrov et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/147b83171b2d5a6a05f7384af6cccd43de20df24) shows p-value-based ranking can be unstable across resources. Next, adopt the benchmarking controls from [(Dimitrov et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/147b83171b2d5a6a05f7384af6cccd43de20df24), including top 100, 250, 500, and 1000 interaction sensitivity, Jaccard overlap, PCA of z-normalized average interaction ranks, Fisher's exact enrichment against a union of resources, and a reshuffled negative control such as BiRewire, to test whether scKGOT's pathway-level signal is robust to resource and threshold variation. Then, align the evaluation with the research plan's Mean Rank, Hits@K, and accuracy percentile rank by reporting these metrics for scKGOT, KG baselines TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx, and cell-cell baselines NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, CellChat, and CellCall, while also adding top-K overlap and pathway-level enrichment metrics. Finally, specify all reproducibility details, including the five random seeds, software versions, hardware, dataset sizes, permutation null model, and whether permutations are performed per patient, per cell type, or per gene/pathway, so that the protocol can be compared with the statistical gaps identified in [(Dimitrov et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/147b83171b2d5a6a05f7384af6cccd43de20df24) and the under-specified robustness evidence in [(Cang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0947b6f97cae0c20e8f3e8302d837ee2c65f54b).

## We will design systematic ablation experiments to assess robustness by:
- Removing facts and pathway types from LRP-KG (FactDrop, TypeDrop)
- Reducing gene expression data by removing low-expression genes and cells (ExprDrop, CellDrop) — These ablations test how much scKGOT depends on pathway knowledge, knowledge graph completeness, and expression data quality.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed ablation design comes from [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0), which constructs a curated ligand-receptor interaction database and uses it to estimate communication probabilities between sender and receiver cell populations. This is directly analogous to the LRP-KG construction in scKGOT, because both methods depend on the completeness and curation of interaction facts before inferring cell-cell signaling. Therefore, FactDrop and TypeDrop are well motivated: removing facts or pathway types from the LRP-KG tests whether inferred communication is driven by the interaction database rather than by expression alone. A weaker but still relevant support is that [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) analyzes population-level signaling in a dermis example involving FIB-A, FIB-P, FIB-B, MYL, Immune, and MELA populations, showing that communication inference is sensitive to cell population definitions and expression inputs. This indirectly supports ExprDrop and CellDrop, because removing low-expression genes or cells could alter sender/receiver marginal distributions and communication probabilities. However, the supplied excerpt does not report quantitative benchmark values, statistical significance, or ablation outcomes, so the support remains conceptual rather than empirical.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0) provides no direct evidence for the proposed ablation claims. It does not report results for removing facts, removing pathway types, removing low-expression genes, or removing cells, nor does it provide quantitative comparisons, statistical significance, permutation settings, or ablation metrics. Therefore, it cannot substantiate the hypothesis that scKGOT robustness depends specifically on pathway knowledge, KG completeness, or expression data quality. A second contradiction is methodological: CellChat is a ligand-receptor probabilistic communication framework, not a pathway knowledge graph optimal transport method. It does not model pathway knowledge discrepancy, pathway-type-specific transport, or fine-grained gene-gene transportation, so its success does not validate the core scKGOT formulation. This weakens the assumption that pathway-level KG ablations are sufficient to demonstrate the necessity of scKGOT's pathway integration. A third contradiction is reproducibility: the excerpt does not specify dataset sizes, hardware, hyperparameters, random seeds, permutation iterations, or evaluation metrics, so it cannot be used to calibrate the proposed five independent runs, 100-iteration permutation testing, MR/Hits@K, or accuracy with percentile rank. A fourth, more indirect contradiction is that CellChat's reliance on a curated ligand-receptor database may mean that performance is driven by database curation and population annotation rather than by pathway activation inference. If scKGOT's FactDrop and TypeDrop are not aligned with the same curation logic, the ablations may conflate database incompleteness with method robustness.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make FactDrop and TypeDrop operationally comparable to the ligand-receptor database construction used by [(Jin et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3cd148f5795617b281a3ee1738abf19e2476bb0). The ablation protocol should define exactly which LRP-KG facts are removed, how pathway types are removed, how many interactions remain, and whether removed facts correspond to ligand-receptor pairs, intra-cellular pathway edges, or intercellular signaling edges. scKGOT should then be benchmarked against CellChat on the same curated ligand-receptor database, the same sender/receiver populations, and the same evaluation metrics, including MR and Hits@K for KG-style link prediction and accuracy with percentile rank for cell-cell interaction prediction, using five random seeds and 100 permutation iterations. The next most important action is to specify ExprDrop and CellDrop thresholds and preprocessing so that they mirror the expression inputs used by CellChat, such as low-expression gene filtering, cell quality filtering, and population annotation. The ablation should report the number of genes and cells removed, the effect on sender/receiver marginal distributions, and the change in communication probabilities or transport plans. A further action is to use CellChat's network analyses as secondary diagnostics, including dominant senders, receivers, mediators, influencers, major signals, similarity, and spatially colocalized signaling events when spatial data are available, to show whether scKGOT preserves or improves biologically interpretable communication structure. Finally, the research plan should document LRP-KG completeness, pathway-type counts, KEGG/Reactome interaction counts, and any curation biases, so that FactDrop and TypeDrop can be interpreted as tests of pathway knowledge rather than as tests of database curation alone.

## We will demonstrate practical applicability through detailed analysis of specific biological contexts, including placenta, testis, and liver datasets, to showcase the method's ability to uncover biologically relevant pathway interactions and cellular heterogeneity patterns — These case studies validate the method in concrete biological settings and illustrate its interpretability.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed placenta, testis, and liver case-study method comes from [(Noël et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/576bec2385c76a42b2ac7f72c982eff0ff5c21f1), which used a concrete biological setting, LPS-activated monocyte-derived dendritic cells from adult PBMCs, to test whether inferred intercellular communication is biologically meaningful by combining communication scores with DC-T cell coculture assays, surface and adhesion molecule measurements, and target-cell activation scores. This directly supports the proposed plan's emphasis on case studies that connect inferred signaling to interpretable biological behavior, although it does not use KGOT, KEGG/Reactome pathway knowledge, or the proposed placenta, testis, and liver datasets. A second, more methodological support comes from [(Luo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/077e018171fde37914036ecd2c9d8da5a87a8e53), which benchmarked 18 ligand-receptor inference methods on spatial transcriptomics data using the ESICCC/CCCbank framework and a differential LR correlation index comparing spatially close versus distant cell pairs. This supports the proposed case-study goal of revealing cellular heterogeneity and tissue-specific signaling by showing that spatial proximity can be used as a quantitative validation signal for inferred LR interactions, but it does not evaluate scKGOT or the proposed tissues. The weakest support is from [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9), which analyzed TCGA normal and cancer transcriptomes and single-cell data with a curated ligand-receptor database, LIMMA-based differential expression, LR correlation coefficients, and a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. This supports the feasibility of LR-based communication inference from transcriptomic data, but it is weaker because the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, effect sizes, p-values, or baseline comparisons, and it does not address pathway-level optimal transport or the proposed case-study tissues.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed case-study method is that none of the related work provides quantitative, statistically validated evidence that pathway-level KGOT outperforms existing LR inference methods in placenta, testis, or liver, so the plan's claim that these case studies will validate scKGOT is not directly supported. [(Noël et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/576bec2385c76a42b2ac7f72c982eff0ff5c21f1) validates IL-10-dependent channels experimentally, but in LPS-activated dendritic cells rather than the proposed tissues, and the excerpt lacks p-values, effect sizes, and baseline comparisons. [(Luo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/077e018171fde37914036ecd2c9d8da5a87a8e53) provides a benchmarking metric for 18 LR methods, but the supplied sections contain no numeric index values, rankings, p-values, or evidence that KGOT would rank favorably. [(Zhou et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95198116d59bed1aba8a68fa89db0f51301579f9) uses a simpler LR correlation and differential-expression framework, and its excerpt also lacks quantitative outcomes, which weakens the evidence that the proposed pathway-level transport model is necessary or superior. A secondary contradiction is tissue and modality mismatch: the related work uses cancer/normal TCGA, LPS-activated immune cells, and spatial transcriptomics, not the proposed human/mouse placenta, testis, and liver scRNA-seq datasets, so generalizability to those case studies is unproven. A weaker contradiction is reproducibility: the related work omits dataset sizes, sequencing depth, hardware, software versions, hyperparameters, random seeds, and permutation settings, whereas the proposed plan requires five independent runs, 100 permutation iterations, and reproducible KGOT case studies; the related work therefore does not demonstrate that such rigorous case-study validation is feasible without additional specification.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to convert the placenta, testis, and liver case studies from qualitative demonstrations into quantitative validation aligned with the proposed evaluation framework: for each tissue, define curated or literature-supported LR/pathway ground truth, report MR, Hits@K for K=1,5,10,50, accuracy with percentile rank, permutation p-values from 100 iterations, and compare scKGOT against NicheNet, CellPhoneDB, SingleCellSignalR, CellChat, CellCall, and KG embedding baselines such as TransE, DistMult, RotatE, and ComplEx. Second, adopt the spatial-coherence idea from [(Luo et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/077e018171fde37914036ecd2c9d8da5a87a8e53) by adding a differential LR correlation index or equivalent close-versus-distant cell-pair metric when spatial coordinates are available, and if the selected scRNA-seq datasets lack spatial information, include at least one spatial transcriptomics dataset for placenta, testis, or liver to test heterogeneity and spatial signaling. Third, follow the validation logic of [(Noël et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/576bec2385c76a42b2ac7f72c982eff0ff5c21f1) by prioritizing top scKGOT pathway interactions for independent biological validation, using public proteomic, ATAC, qPCR, or perturbation data where feasible, and reporting target-cell activation scores rather than only inferred LR pairs. Fourth, strengthen reproducibility by specifying dataset sizes, cell counts, species, batch structure, preprocessing, KEGG and Reactome versions, LRP-KG construction details, KGOT hyperparameters, random seeds, and computational resources. Fifth, ensure the case studies explicitly connect to the proposed multi-level analysis by reporting cell-level communication scores, gene-level pathway gene importance, and pathway-level knowledge discrepancy or transport mass, with ablations such as FactDrop, TypeDrop, ExprDrop, and CellDrop to show that pathway knowledge, not just LR expression correlation, drives the results.
