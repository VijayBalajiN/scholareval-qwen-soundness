## Context: Establishes a chronic-stage bradyzoite model to test compounds that may eradicate tissue cysts. Method: We will employ a recently developed human myotube-based culture system to generate mature T. gondii drug-tolerant bradyzoites for compound screening. For bradyzoite cultures, we will differentiate KD3 myoblasts into multinucleated myotubes and infect them under bicarbonate-depleted conditions, allowing four weeks for tissue cyst maturation.

### Support

The strongest support from [(Shiomi et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ac41625d5270c6cda76074bb1ed591e0a872ac2) is that human myogenic cells can be isolated, maintained, immortalized, and differentiated into multinucleated myotubes, which directly supports the proposed use of a human myotube-based host culture for differentiating KD3 myoblasts into multinucleated myotubes before T. gondii infection. A second, more limited support is that the paper demonstrates genetic control of myogenic cell growth and senescence through viral expression of E7 and Tet-Off-regulated p16INK4a, showing that stable human myogenic lines can be engineered and phenotypically characterized, a concept that could be adapted to maintain a reproducible KD3-derived myotube line for chronic bradyzoite culture. The weakest support is methodological: the paper uses growth-property assays, karyotyping, immunoblotting, immunofluorescence, and transplantation to verify myogenic identity and phenotype, providing a template for confirming that proposed KD3 myotubes remain myogenic and healthy before infection with Pru-Δhxgprt tdTomato parasites under bicarbonate-depleted conditions.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no evidence that human myotubes support T. gondii infection, bradyzoite conversion, or four-week tissue cyst maturation under bicarbonate-depleted conditions, so it does not validate the central proposed bradyzoite model or the downstream 10 μM seven-day MMV Pathogen Box treatment and 28-day regrowth readout. A second contradiction is that the myotubes in [(Shiomi et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ac41625d5270c6cda76074bb1ed591e0a872ac2) were generated with E7 and/or p16INK4a Tet-Off manipulation, which can alter cell-cycle control, senescence, and karyotype; this raises the possibility that the host niche, cyst-wall formation, or drug response could differ from proposed KD3 myoblast-derived myotubes, especially if KD3 cells are not identically immortalized or if p16INK4a/E7 status changes parasite maturation. The weakest contradiction is that the reported outcomes were qualitative, with no quantitative parasite viability, cyst counts, IC50/LC50, statistical significance, host cytotoxicity, or metabolomic data, so the work cannot support the proposed quantitative screening, resazurin cytotoxicity, LightBBB/SwissADME, LC-MS metabolomics, MitoTracker Deep Red FM, TEM, or ATP assay components.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to first validate the proposed KD3 myotube bradyzoite model before compound screening: infect differentiated KD3 myotubes with Pru-Δhxgprt tdTomato parasites under bicarbonate-depleted conditions, allow four weeks for cyst maturation, and quantify cyst number, tdTomato fluorescence, parasite viability, host-cell viability by resazurin, and 28-day tachyzoite regrowth after DMSO or active-compound removal, with statistical comparisons to established bradyzoite controls. The next action is to characterize the KD3 myotubes for myogenic markers, karyotype stability, senescence, and bicarbonate response, and to avoid or explicitly control for E7/p16INK4a-type immortalization effects by comparing unmanipulated KD3 myotubes with any immortalized lines to ensure that host-cell genetic manipulation does not confound bradyzoite maturation or drug response. The least critical but still useful action is to add quantitative method controls and reporting metrics, including dose-response IC50/LC50 curves, DMSO and host-only controls, time-course cyst maturation, and statistical power calculations, so that the proposed MMV Pathogen Box screen, metabolomics, MitoTracker, TEM, and ATP assays are anchored to a validated chronic bradyzoite culture.

## Context: Core phenotypic screen to identify compounds active against both acute and chronic parasite stages. Method: We will screen the MMV Pathogen Box, containing 400 compounds with known antiparasitic activities, against both tachyzoites and bradyzoites. We will conduct the compound screening by treating both tachyzoite and bradyzoite cultures with 10 μM of each Pathogen Box compound for seven days, using DMSO as a negative control. Parasite viability will be monitored using fluorescence measurements of tdTomato-expressing parasites. For bradyzoites, we will assess compound efficacy by removing inhibitors after treatment and monitoring for tachyzoite regrowth over 28 days.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 400-compound MMV Pathogen Box tachyzoite screen comes from Hayward et al., 2022-02, who screened the same 400-compound Pathogen Box against intact T. gondii in 96-well Seahorse XFe96 format with DMSO negative control and identified 7/400 compounds that reduced O2 consumption, then confirmed activity with fluorescence proliferation assays in T. gondii and P. falciparum, flow cytometry viability, and human cell proliferation assays [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f). This directly supports the feasibility of using the Pathogen Box, DMSO control, and a phenotypic tachyzoite viability readout, and provides an empirical hit rate (~1.75%) that can inform replicate number and hit selection for the proposed 10 uM, 7-day tdTomato screen. A second, weaker but relevant support is that Hayward et al. identified MMV688853 as an electron transport chain inhibitor in a TgCDPK1-independent manner at the Qi site of Complex III and showed limited cross-resistance in atovaquone-resistant T. gondii and P. falciparum and no cross-resistance in ELQ-300-resistant T. gondii, which supports the plan's emphasis on mitochondrial/ETC targets and comparative analysis with bc1 inhibitors such as atovaquone and buparvaquone. Viewed iteratively, the older Murata et al. 2017-06 framework is extended by Hayward's 2022 Pathogen Box tachyzoite screen, but Hayward does not test the bradyzoite washout endpoint. The weakest support is from Murata et al., 2017-06, whose section headings indicate a phenotypic anti-Toxoplasma compound screen, validation of the parasite growth assay with a pyrimethamine dose-response curve, host cell cytotoxicity testing, and a bradyzoite differentiation assay, and whose reported hits tanshinone IIA and hydroxyzine were described as inhibiting intracellular parasite growth and affecting bradyzoites [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9); this supports the general logic of combining tachyzoite screening, cytotoxicity controls, and bradyzoite-stage assessment, but it does not confirm the proposed Pathogen Box, 10 uM, 7-day, tdTomato, or 28-day washout regrowth conditions.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work validates the proposed bradyzoite-specific endpoint: the newer Hayward et al. 2022-02 work neither confirms nor refutes the older Murata et al. 2017-06 bradyzoite differentiation claim [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9), but Hayward used intact tachyzoites and oxygen consumption/proliferation readouts and did not include mature bradyzoites, tdTomato-specific fluorescence, or a 28-day inhibitor-washout regrowth assay [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f), so the central chronic-stage extension of the proposed method remains unsupported by direct evidence. A second contradiction is that Hayward's primary tachyzoite readout was Seahorse XFe96 oxygen consumption rather than tdTomato fluorescence, and the excerpt does not report the exact 10 uM, 7-day treatment, numeric IC50, OCR fold-change, or p-values; therefore the proposed 10 uM, 7-day tdTomato screen is not directly benchmarked against the closest Pathogen Box study and may miss or rank compounds differently if OCR and fluorescence proliferation are not concordant. A third, weaker contradiction is that Murata et al. 2017-06 provides only section headings and no quantitative IC50, LC50, percent inhibition, fold-change, or statistical significance, and does not specify compound concentration, treatment duration, parasite strain, cell line, fluorescence instrument, or washout period [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9); thus it cannot confirm that bradyzoite effects observed in that work would translate to the proposed 28-day regrowth metric. Finally, the related work does not address the proposed metabolomic, LightBBB/SwissADME, MitoTracker, TEM, or ATP-starvation components, so those downstream analyses remain methodologically unvalidated by these sources.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a bradyzoite validation arm that directly tests the proposed 28-day washout regrowth endpoint using the myotube-derived mature bradyzoites, with tdTomato fluorescence quantified at defined time points and a DMSO negative control, because neither Hayward et al. 2022-02 nor Murata et al. 2017-06 provides quantitative evidence for this endpoint [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f) [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9). Second, incorporate Hayward's tachyzoite readout strategy as a parallel or confirmatory assay: use 96-well Seahorse XFe96 OCR or an equivalent mitochondrial respiration assay alongside tdTomato fluorescence for the 400 Pathogen Box compounds, because Hayward identified 7/400 OCR inhibitors and linked MMV688853 to Complex III, which is directly relevant to the plan's mitochondrial/ETC focus [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f). Third, specify and report quantitative validation for all hits: serial dilution IC50 for tachyzoites and LC50 for bradyzoites, DMSO controls, replicate number, statistical tests, and cytotoxicity thresholds using resazurin or the human cell proliferation assays used by Hayward, to avoid host-cell toxicity false positives [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f). Fourth, use the older Murata framework to include a known active control such as pyrimethamine dose-response and a bradyzoite differentiation/cytotoxicity validation, but upgrade it with the proposed tdTomato and 28-day regrowth metrics so that the bradyzoite claim is quantitative rather than inferential [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9). Fifth, for compounds with ETC-like signatures, perform target-level or resistance-based confirmation analogous to Hayward's atovaquone- and ELQ-300-resistant comparisons and, where feasible, plasma membrane-permeabilized Complex III enzymatic assays, and compare isotope incorporation and metabolite abundance to atovaquone/buparvaquone to distinguish bc1/Complex III inhibition from broader mitochondrial toxicity [(Hayward et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91528e3922fc8dcbae597add91ec748fcfb9f60f).

## Context: Provides an acute-stage infection model for dual-activity comparison. Method: For tachyzoite cultures, we will infect human foreskin fibroblasts with Pru-Δhxgprt tdTomato parasites.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed acute-stage infection method comes from [(Sheiner et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/509405c603028d4e987f340a9f9891ef3e1e6803), which used tdTomato-expressing T. gondii tachyzoites in fibroblast cultures to maintain acute-stage parasites and track parasite viability by fluorescence. This directly parallels the plan to infect human foreskin fibroblasts with Pru-Δhxgprt tdTomato parasites and monitor tdTomato fluorescence during MMV Pathogen Box screening, because it demonstrates that a red fluorescent reporter can be used in a fibroblast-hosted tachyzoite system for acute-stage infection and readout. The work also supports the broader feasibility of using tachyzoite tissue-culture infection as a phenotypic assay platform, since it enabled conditional mutant isolation and apicoplast localization by microscopy, western blotting, and pulse-chase analyses, indicating that fluorescence and biochemical readouts can be combined in the same culture system. Older work by [(Mui et al., 2008-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16ee89289d7a068f9519bdf15662f95350128ea7) provides weaker but still relevant support by showing that an in vitro tissue-culture assay can be used to evaluate inhibitor effects on T. gondii tachyzoites and that JPC-2067-B was cidal for T. gondii, which is conceptually consistent with the plan to test 400 Pathogen Box compounds at 10 uM for seven days and to identify tachyzoite-active hits. However, the Mui excerpt contains only section headings and does not specify parasite strain, host cell line, multiplicity of infection, drug concentrations, treatment duration, DMSO controls, parasite quantitation, host-cell toxicity, or statistical analysis, so its support is limited to the general validity of tachyzoite tissue-culture inhibitor assays rather than the specific screening parameters in the research plan.

### Contradictions

There is no direct contradiction to the proposed use of tdTomato-expressing tachyzoites in fibroblasts, but the strongest limitation is that neither related work validates the proposed screening implementation. [(Sheiner et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/509405c603028d4e987f340a9f9891ef3e1e6803) used tdTomato fibroblast cultures primarily for genetic manipulation, apicoplast protein identification, and conditional mutant analysis, not for high-throughput compound screening, and the excerpt reports no IC50, LC50, fold-change, DMSO control, resazurin cytotoxicity, or statistical significance for drug treatment. Therefore, it does not provide evidence that a seven-day, 10 uM Pathogen Box screen with fluorescence readout and resazurin host-cell viability controls will be reproducible or that tdTomato fluorescence alone is sufficient to distinguish parasite death from host-cell toxicity. The next strongest limitation is that [(Mui et al., 2008-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16ee89289d7a068f9519bdf15662f95350128ea7) reports only that JPC-2067-B affected tachyzoites and was cidal, without quantitative dose-response, parasite reduction, host-cell toxicity, or statistical significance, so it cannot support the plan's requirement to determine IC50 for tachyzoites and LC50 for bradyzoites or to exclude compounds that act through host-cell damage. A weaker but important gap is that neither work addresses the bradyzoite-specific components of the plan, including KD3 myoblast differentiation into myotubes, bicarbonate-depleted induction, four-week tissue cyst maturation, 28-day regrowth readout, metabolomics, LightBBB/SwissADME predictions, MitoTracker, TEM, or ATP assays; thus the related work supports only the acute-stage fibroblast infection arm and does not validate the dual-activity comparison or the bradyzoite screen.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a pilot validation of the proposed tachyzoite screening protocol before full Pathogen Box screening: define the exact Pru-Δhxgprt tdTomato strain, human foreskin fibroblast passage and confluence, multiplicity of infection, DMSO concentration, 10 uM compound treatment duration, fluorescence acquisition settings, and a resazurin cytotoxicity cutoff, then test a small subset of compounds with known tachyzoite activity and known host-cell toxicity to confirm that tdTomato fluorescence and resazurin can separate parasite-specific inhibition from host-cell damage. The second priority is to use the Sheiner workflow as a template for reporter-based acute-stage infection but explicitly adapt it to drug screening by including dose-response controls, DMSO-only and vehicle-treated cultures, and statistical comparison of fluorescence or parasite counts against controls, because the related work demonstrates the reporter system but not the screening statistics. The third priority is to avoid overinterpreting the Mui cidal result as evidence for the proposed 10 uM seven-day screen; instead, treat it only as historical support for tachyzoite tissue-culture inhibitor assays and collect quantitative IC50 data for the proposed compounds. Finally, because neither related work supports the bradyzoite arm, the plan should include separate validation of KD3 myotube differentiation, bicarbonate-depleted bradyzoite induction, four-week cyst maturation, and 28-day regrowth readout, with parallel resazurin or equivalent host-cell viability controls, before using the acute-stage tdTomato fibroblast assay as the comparator for dual-activity compounds.

## Context: Confirms potency and parasite-specific activity of screening hits. Method: We will validate screening hits by determining half-inhibitory concentrations (IC50) for tachyzoites and half-maximal lethal concentrations (LC50) for bradyzoites using serial dilution assays. Host cell cytotoxicity will be assessed using resazurin viability assays to exclude compounds that suppress parasite growth through host cell damage.

### Support

The strongest support from [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9) is that the paper explicitly contains a validation-of-the-screening-method section and a host cell cytotoxicity assay section, which directly align with the proposed plan to confirm screening hits by dose-response or serial dilution assays and to exclude host-cell toxicity. This is more directly relevant than the later observation that tanshinone IIA and hydroxyzine were reported to inhibit intracellular parasite growth and affect bradyzoites, because the former establishes that the study used an assay-validation framework rather than only single-concentration screening. A further supporting point is that the supporting information title indicates a pyrimethamine dose-response curve was generated, implying use of a known anti-Toxoplasma reference compound to calibrate or validate the screening readout; this is conceptually consistent with the proposed use of serial dilution assays to derive IC50 values for tachyzoites and LC50 values for bradyzoites. The parasite growth assay and bradyzoite differentiation assay sections also support the biological logic of the proposed method, since the research plan requires distinguishing tachyzoite inhibition from bradyzoite persistence and requires a mature bradyzoite readout. However, the support is limited because the supplied excerpt does not report the actual dose-response parameters, cytotoxicity thresholds, or statistical validation, so it supports the general workflow but not the specific quantitative implementation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct refutation but a critical evidentiary gap: [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9) reports no numerical IC50, LC50, cytotoxicity, or statistical significance values in the provided text, which means it cannot substantiate the proposed method's central claim that hits will be validated by quantitative half-inhibitory and half-maximal lethal concentrations. The next major limitation is that the excerpt does not specify that resazurin viability assays were used, nor does it describe serial dilution formats, dilution series, incubation times, assay instruments, or statistical methods; therefore, the proposed resazurin-based host cytotoxicity exclusion and serial dilution validation are not directly evidenced by this work. A further limitation is that the paper identifies tanshinone IIA and hydroxyzine as compounds with effects on intracellular parasite growth and bradyzoites, but it does not quantify whether the bradyzoite effect is a true LC50, a reduction in differentiation, a delay in regrowth, or a host-cell toxicity artifact, which is precisely the distinction the proposed method seeks to make. Finally, the absence of parasite strain, host cell type, compound concentrations, and replicate or statistical details weakens the ability to generalize from this related work to the proposed human myotube-based bradyzoite culture and MMV Pathogen Box screening context.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the validation section quantitatively explicit and benchmarked: for each hit from the 10 μM, 7-day MMV Pathogen Box screen, perform a serial dilution dose-response assay in the same parasite and host-cell system, report IC50 for tachyzoites and LC50 for bradyzoites with 95% confidence intervals, and include a reference active compound such as pyrimethamine for tachyzoites and a bradyzoite-active comparator if available, as implied by the pyrimethamine dose-response validation in [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9). The next priority is to define the host cytotoxicity assay precisely: use resazurin viability on uninfected host cells at the same DMSO and compound concentrations, calculate selectivity indices as IC50 or LC50 divided by host CC50, and exclude hits whose parasite inhibition is explained by host-cell damage. For bradyzoites, the method should specify that LC50 is derived from a washout-regrowth endpoint, for example removal of inhibitor after treatment and monitoring tdTomato-positive regrowth over 28 days, rather than from a single viability measurement, because the related work only indicates bradyzoite effects without quantifying lethality. Additional actions should include reporting biological and technical replicates, DMSO controls, parasite strain and host cell identity, myotube maturation conditions, dilution scheme, incubation duration, fluorescence or resazurin instrument settings, and statistical tests with significance thresholds. These details would close the gap between the conceptual support in [(Murata et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/837e410f668638b1ac773178019675813e8082c9) and the proposed quantitative validation of tachyzoite and bradyzoite activity.

## Context: Reveals metabolic perturbations and candidate modes of action for active compounds. Method: To characterize compound modes of action, we will perform untargeted and stable isotope-resolved metabolomic analyses using liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry. We will treat parasites with active compounds and analyze their metabolic responses using both pHILIC and BEH-amide chromatography columns. For metabolomic analysis, we will treat infected cultures with confirmed active compounds at concentrations corresponding to 3-5 fold their respective IC50 values for three hours. We will isolate parasites by mechanical disruption and filtration, then extract metabolites using acetonitrile. We will analyze samples using high-resolution mass spectrometry with both positive and negative ionization modes.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Scalbert et al., 2009-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/729cb2d75bd5526f87313c16b909f5baf06fc69f), which directly addresses untargeted LC-MS/GC-MS metabolomics in positive, negative, or alternating polarity modes and high-resolution acquisition, and shows that data extraction is an experimental variable that can determine whether subtle pharmacological or nutritional perturbations are detected. Its recommendation of workflow optimization, QC samples, benchmarking of extraction tools, disclosure of processing parameters, permutation testing, blind validation, and overfitting assessment supports the proposed pHILIC and BEH-amide HRMS positive/negative metabolomics of tachyzoites and bradyzoites treated at 3-5x IC50 for 3 h, because such subtle drug-induced changes are exactly the type of signal vulnerable to extraction and modeling artifacts. A weaker, but biologically relevant, support is the newer [(Parker et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32129556d4eb50b84223917610a757bfb6ca5107), which demonstrates that stable isotope-resolved labeling can be applied to T. gondii cultures to infer metabolic flux and amino acid homeostasis, supporting the feasibility of isotope-resolved LC-MS in the same organism. However, this newer work is less directly supportive because it focuses on [13C] amino acid uptake and ApiAT transporter biology rather than untargeted central carbon or pyrimidine flux with 13C6-glucose and 15N-amide-glutamine, and it does not report quantitative incorporation, sample sizes, MS parameters, or bradyzoite compound treatment.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Scalbert et al., 2009-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/729cb2d75bd5526f87313c16b909f5baf06fc69f) found no single automated LC-MS or GC-MS extraction tool generic across data types; among approximately 20 tools, automated pipelines commonly produced missed peaks, noise integration, database mismatches, misalignment, and integration errors, especially at low signal-to-noise, while supervised target-based processing was cleaner but limited to a few hundred compounds and required roughly 2-4 weeks. It also reported that automated comprehensive extraction can generate 10,000-15,000 features per LC-MS file that correspond to fewer actual metabolites, and that PLS-DA can separate random classes, creating a major overfitting risk. The proposed method does not specify extraction software, peak detection, alignment, de-isotoping, adduct/fragment handling, normalization, QC, batch correction, independent validation, or statistical testing, so the planned 3 h, 3-5x IC50 metabolomic comparison of tachyzoites and bradyzoites may overinterpret technical artifacts or miss low-abundance drug-induced changes. A weaker contradiction is that the newer [(Parker et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32129556d4eb50b84223917610a757bfb6ca5107) does not provide numerical 13C incorporation, uptake rates, metabolite abundances, statistical significance, sample sizes, chromatography columns, mass spectrometer models, ionization modes, or labeling durations, despite using CRISPR/Cas9 parasite genetics, Xenopus oocyte expression, uptake/efflux/electrophysiology, and [13C] amino acid labeling; therefore it cannot substantiate the proposed quantitative isotope-resolved untargeted LC-MS workflow for compound-treated bradyzoites or tachyzoites.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define and benchmark a reproducible data-extraction and statistical-validation pipeline for the pHILIC and BEH-amide HRMS positive/negative datasets, including technical replicates, pooled QC, blank and host-control subtraction, isotope/adduct/charge-state handling, de-isotoping, alignment, feature filtering, normalization, batch-effect correction, and independent or blinded validation with permutation testing and multiple-testing correction; avoid relying on PLS-DA alone because [(Scalbert et al., 2009-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/729cb2d75bd5526f87313c16b909f5baf06fc69f) shows it can separate random classes. Next, specify the stable isotope-resolved design in detail: 13C6-glucose and 15N-amide-glutamine labeling duration, natural-abundance correction, isotopologue resolution, M+0/M+1/M+2 or equivalent reporting, flux modeling for central carbon and pyrimidine pathways, and targeted LC-MS validation of candidate metabolites, using [(Parker et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32129556d4eb50b84223917610a757bfb6ca5107) as evidence that isotope labeling is feasible in T. gondii but not as quantitative proof for the proposed endpoints. Then add experimental controls and reproducibility details: DMSO controls, parasite purity after mechanical disruption and filtration, acetonitrile extraction recovery, column-specific standards, sample size/power, biological replicates, and reporting of MS hardware, acquisition modes, and processing parameters.

## Context: Tests mitochondrial dysfunction as a mode of action in both parasite stages. Method: To investigate mitochondrial function, we will assess mitochondrial membrane potential using MitoTracker Deep Red FM staining and examine ultrastructural changes by transmission electron microscopy. We will measure ATP levels in glucose-starved parasites using a luciferase-based assay to determine the importance of mitochondrial ATP production in both parasite stages.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed mitochondrial dysfunction assay comes from [(Liu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e38e281e76211e80b7675e9c9eed8dd5bcbd0f9e), which directly pairs a mitochondrial membrane potential (ΔΨm) assay with transmission electron microscopy (TEM) in T. gondii tachyzoites treated with BAM15, in an in vitro study of RH and PRU strains that also examined proliferation, invasion, bradyzoite/cyst formation, ROS, and tachyzoite death. This is the closest precedent to the plan's use of ΔΨm and TEM to infer mitochondrial mode of action, although the excerpt provides only section headings and no dye identity, staining protocol, TEM instrument, treatment concentration, incubation time, fold change, IC50/LC50, or statistical significance. The next strongest support is [(Hargrave et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c76a6e0f555cac881976a4e2947527c5ebdf72b1), which used the same MitoTracker Deep Red FM probe and flow cytometry to assess mitochondrial membrane potential in T. gondii-infected murine BMDCs, comparing transfected Prugniaud-strain infection with LPS-stimulated and unstimulated controls within a broader workflow including RNA sequencing, LC-MS metabolomics, LDH activity, arginase-1 expression, and cytokine profiling; however, it measured host dendritic cell mitochondria rather than tachyzoite or bradyzoite mitochondria and did not report numeric fluorescence intensities, fold changes, MitoTracker concentration, incubation time, MOI, time post-infection, LPS dose, or flow cytometer settings. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Lavine, and Arrizabalaga, 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3f6768b907150c1cb212bca19d193f75319ece), which used electron microscopy to show reversible mitochondrial alteration in T. gondii after monensin exposure, establishing a TEM precedent for mitochondrial ultrastructure in the parasite, but it did not quantify changes, provide control comparisons, statistical significance, or examine bradyzoites, glucose starvation, or ATP production. The weakest direct support is [(Mühleip et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6de8cf608383d37be2edf1ca26f5396abf9a469), which used thin sectioning and conventional TEM to identify ATP synthase hexamers and pentagonal pyramid assembly in curved membrane regions, linking TEM to the ATP synthase machinery central to the plan's ATP-production question, but the excerpt lacks organism/tissue, fixation, section thickness, staining, microscope model, magnification, image analysis, sample size, and statistical analysis, and it does not address MitoTracker, glucose-starved ATP, or bradyzoites.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct negative result but a major evidence gap: none of the cited studies validates the proposed integrated mitochondrial assay in mature T. gondii bradyzoites, which is central to the research plan's goal of comparing tachyzoites and bradyzoites. [(Liu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e38e281e76211e80b7675e9c9eed8dd5bcbd0f9e) places ΔΨm and TEM in tachyzoites only, [(Hargrave et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c76a6e0f555cac881976a4e2947527c5ebdf72b1) measures MitoTracker Deep Red FM in host BMDCs rather than parasite stages, [(Lavine, and Arrizabalaga, 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3f6768b907150c1cb212bca19d193f75319ece) reports only monensin-induced reversible mitochondrial morphology without stage-specific or ATP-linked readouts, and [(Mühleip et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6de8cf608383d37be2edf1ca26f5396abf9a469) provides structural ATP synthase data without functional membrane potential or glucose-starvation ATP measurements. The next strongest tension is that all four studies lack the quantitative and statistical detail needed to support the plan's claim that mitochondrial dysfunction can be reliably detected and compared across drug treatments; none reports numeric MitoTracker fluorescence, fold changes, ATP concentrations, TEM morphometric measurements, sample sizes, replicates, or significance values, so the proposed method currently rests on qualitative precedent rather than validated effect sizes. A further contradiction to the plan's implicit assumption is that MitoTracker Deep Red FM signal in infected cultures may reflect host mitochondria, as shown by [(Hargrave et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c76a6e0f555cac881976a4e2947527c5ebdf72b1), and therefore cannot be assumed to report parasite tachyzoite or bradyzoite ΔΨm without parasite isolation, stage-specific controls, or parasite-specific mitochondrial markers. Finally, the TEM evidence is weaker for the proposed functional conclusion because [(Lavine, and Arrizabalaga, 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3f6768b907150c1cb212bca19d193f75319ece) and [(Mühleip et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6de8cf608383d37be2edf1ca26f5396abf9a469) describe ultrastructure or ATP synthase architecture qualitatively, without quantitative cristae, mitochondrial volume, ATP synthase density, or correlation to ATP output, which limits their ability to validate TEM as a quantitative readout of mitochondrial ATP production.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to first validate MitoTracker Deep Red FM in isolated tachyzoites and mature bradyzoites before applying it to screening hits, using DMSO, uncoupler (CCCP or FCCP), ETC inhibitor (oligomycin, antimycin A, or atovaquone/buparvaquone), and parasite-only preparations to distinguish parasite from host mitochondria; for bradyzoites in myotubes, optimize dye concentration, incubation time, cyst wall permeability, and readout by flow cytometry or fluorescence microscopy with parasite number normalization. The second priority is to establish the glucose-starvation luciferase ATP assay in both stages, defining glucose-free medium, starvation duration, ATP standard curve, spike-recovery, parasite isolation from fibroblasts or myotubes, and normalization to parasite or cyst number, with low-ATP sensitivity for bradyzoites and comparison to ETC inhibitors. The third priority is to convert TEM from qualitative observation to a quantitative ultrastructural assay by specifying fixation, section thickness, staining, microscope settings, image analysis metrics such as mitochondrial area, cristae density, membrane thickness, and ATP synthase density where feasible, and by reporting sample size, replicates, and statistical tests. The fourth priority is to integrate these mitochondrial readouts with the plan's metabolomic workflow by applying them to compounds with ETC-like signatures at 3-5 fold IC50/LC50 for 3 hours, comparing to atovaquone and buparvaquone, and using stable isotope incorporation and metabolite abundance to confirm target engagement. The least immediate but useful action is to add complementary mitochondrial mass or ETC gene/protein readouts, inspired by [(Hargrave et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c76a6e0f555cac881976a4e2947527c5ebdf72b1), to distinguish membrane-potential collapse from mitochondrial loss or biogenesis defects.

## Context: Confirms bc1/ETC target engagement for hits with consistent metabolic signatures. Method: For compounds showing metabolic signatures consistent with electron transport chain inhibition, we will perform comparative analysis with known bc1-complex inhibitors atovaquone and buparvaquone, using both metabolite abundance measurements and stable isotope incorporation patterns to confirm target identification.

### Support

The strongest support is that the newer [(Souza et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1939ffaa5cabdc13e6e11e10b46118f2d8e3eca) directly applies atovaquone, a bc1-complex inhibitor, to T. gondii and measures metabolite abundance, providing a positive-control framework for the proposed comparative analysis. As an iterative step, this newer work builds on the older [(Maclean et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a60e8a80ac027f3facc33dc97725c12718e6943) bc1 target validation by moving from complex integrity to atovaquone metabolite abundance, but it does not close the isotope gap. Souza et al. used five independent repetitions, atovaquone at 10, 50, and 100 nM, time points of 24 h, 48 h, 72 h, and 7 d, RH and ME49 strains, PMSF, five freeze-thaw cycles with liquid nitrogen and 90 C water bath, ion-exchange cleanup, centrifugation at 626 g for 5 min at 4 C, and targeted HPLC/UV on a BIORAD-Aminex HPX-87H exclusion column with 5 mM sulfuric acid eluent at 0.6 mL/min and 210 nm detection, calibrated for phosphoenolpyruvate, pyruvate, lactate, citrate, malate, oxaloacetate, fumarate, succinate, alpha-ketoglutarate, and glucose by spectrophotometry, with GraphPad Prism 8.2.1, normality and homogeneous variance checks, ANOVA followed by Dunn's posttest, and p<0.05. This supports using atovaquone as a reference inhibitor and metabolite abundance as a readout, although it does not test buparvaquone, untargeted LC-MS, or 13C6-glucose/15N-amide-glutamine isotope incorporation. The next strongest support is [(Maclean et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a60e8a80ac027f3facc33dc97725c12718e6943), which established that T. gondii complex III/bc1 subunits localize to mitochondria, co-migrate in a ~670 kDa complex, and are essential for parasite growth, mitochondrial membrane potential, and intact complex III, using mitochondrial isolation, blue/clear native PAGE, complexome profiling, SDS-PAGE, immunoblotting, immunoprecipitation, stable transfection, qRT-PCR, and JC-1 staining. This supports the proposed mitochondrial function assays, including MitoTracker Deep Red FM and ATP measurement, and the premise that bc1 target engagement should alter mitochondrial membrane potential and growth, but it does not provide metabolite abundance or isotope incorporation evidence.

### Contradictions

No related work directly refutes the proposed method, but the strongest limitation is that the newer [(Souza et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1939ffaa5cabdc13e6e11e10b46118f2d8e3eca), despite measuring atovaquone-treated metabolites, reports no quantitative changes in the HPLC-measured glycolytic or TCA metabolites; the only reported significant result was parasite counts at 7 days higher than at 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h, with p<0.05. This weakly contradicts the assumption that metabolite abundance alone will yield a clear atovaquone signature under the tested conditions, and it shows that a targeted HPLC/UV panel may be insufficient to validate the proposed untargeted LC-MS and isotope-resolved approach. It also used nM atovaquone and 24 h to 7 d time points rather than the proposed 3-5x IC50 for 3 h, so its conditions do not directly support the proposed acute comparative window. The weaker contradiction is that [(Maclean et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a60e8a80ac027f3facc33dc97725c12718e6943) does not measure metabolite abundance, stable isotope incorporation, IC50/LC50, fold changes, or statistical significance for membrane potential, and it relies on JC-1 rather than MitoTracker Deep Red FM; therefore it cannot validate the proposed isotope-resolved metabolomic confirmation of bc1 target engagement, leaving the central methodological claim unsupported by direct evidence.

### Suggested Action

Most important, generate matched positive-control datasets for atovaquone and buparvaquone under the exact proposed conditions, using 3-5x IC50 or LC50 concentrations, 3 h treatment, DMSO control, the same parasite isolation by mechanical disruption and filtration, acetonitrile extraction, pHILIC and BEH-amide LC-MS in positive and negative ionization modes, 13C6-glucose, and 15N-amide-glutamine, to define expected metabolite-abundance and isotope-incorporation signatures before classifying hits. Because [(Souza et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1939ffaa5cabdc13e6e11e10b46118f2d8e3eca) shows that targeted HPLC/UV alone did not report quantitative metabolite changes, add orthogonal targeted HPLC/UV for the Souza metabolite panel, including phosphoenolpyruvate, pyruvate, lactate, citrate, malate, oxaloacetate, fumarate, succinate, alpha-ketoglutarate, and glucose, and include PMSF, rapid quenching, five independent biological replicates, GraphPad Prism 8.2.1 or equivalent, normality and homogeneous variance checks, ANOVA followed by Dunn's posttest, and p<0.05 for targeted comparisons. Next, strengthen target-engagement evidence by measuring mitochondrial membrane potential with MitoTracker Deep Red FM and, where feasible, JC-1 as in [(Maclean et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a60e8a80ac027f3facc33dc97725c12718e6943), ATP in glucose-starved parasites by luciferase assay, and TEM ultrastructure; if resources allow, add complex III integrity or bc1 subunit co-IP/complexome profiling to directly link inhibitor effects to intact bc1. Finally, use buparvaquone as a second bc1 reference, perform dose-response to confirm IC50/LC50, apply QC, normalization, PCA or OPLS-DA, fold-change thresholds, and multiple-testing correction for untargeted metabolomics, and require concordant abundance, isotope, and mitochondrial phenotypes to assign ETC/bc1 target.
