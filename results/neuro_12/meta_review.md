## Context: The study requires direct spaceflight data to observe movement slowing under microgravity. Method: 'We will study twelve taikonauts from the China Space Station across multiple phases: pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight sessions.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Macaluso et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3cfc02cac9b750070c5469e65b890259c2934ac), which used the same core experimental paradigm: rapid right index finger reaching to tablet touchscreen targets in microgravity and neutral buoyancy, with foot straps and left-hand stabilization, and extracted the same feedforward and postural kinematic indices that the plan uses to distinguish conservative control from body-mass underestimation, including reaction time, movement duration, peak angular velocity, peak angular acceleration, time to peak angular acceleration, trunk displacement, and shoulder/hip forward displacement. Its qualitative pattern, namely that reaching accuracy remained within normogravity standards while microgravity changed focal movement timing in a way consistent with planned conservative control and postural displacement was reduced or reorganized, directly supports the feasibility of using a tablet-based reaching task to test planned-duration and postural effects in microgravity. The next strongest support is [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), which shows that touchscreen target acquisition can be tracked across pre, during, and post simulated microgravity phases and that performance is not uniformly slowed: time of measurement significantly affected throughput, F(5, 1675.2) = 45.16, p < 0.001, movement time, F(5, 1605.81) = 72.465, p < 0.001, error count, chi-square(5) = 15.10, p < 0.01, and touch duration, F(5, 1631.26) = 14.15, p < 0.001, with throughput increasing by about 0.49 bits/s relative to baseline and about 0.95 bits/s at 90 days after head-down tilt while error count and touch duration were elevated during head-down tilt. This supports the plan's use of repeated phases, a ground control group, and speed-accuracy trade-off measures rather than movement duration alone. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Thonnard et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/576fedf707528768a0d83b8bec20672d07f495ea), which documents repeated pre-, in-, and post-flight rapid finger and arm target tasks across 21 parabolic flight campaigns between 1999 and 2014 and ISS GRIP preparation, confirming that in-flight sessions can be structured around rapid discrete movements and that restraint or stabilization is essential for isolating limb kinematics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is methodological: [(Thonnard et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/576fedf707528768a0d83b8bec20672d07f495ea) found that pelvis restraint was strongly recommended and shoulder straps were important for stability during vertical and horizontal oscillations and vertical collisions, whereas the proposed method relies only on foot straps and left-hand tabletop stabilization. If parasitic body movements are not constrained, the plan's 100-Hz finger kinematics, peak acceleration timing, and submovement decomposition may be contaminated by trunk or shoulder motion, weakening the dissociation between conservative control and mass underestimation. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) shows repeated touchscreen measurements across simulated microgravity produce mixed signals, with speed-based throughput improving over time likely due to practice while error count and touch duration increase, and no clear early post-head-down-tilt drop at recovery day 4 or day 90. This contradicts the assumption that post-flight sessions will cleanly reveal a persistent microgravity-related slowing or recovery, and it suggests that a 12-taikonaut repeated-measures ANOVA may be vulnerable to practice, missing data, and non-sphericity, especially because Bieg et al. used linear mixed effects models and a zero-inflated Poisson model and had missing recovery-day 4 data for 12 of 24 participants. The weakest contradiction is that [(Macaluso et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3cfc02cac9b750070c5469e65b890259c2934ac), despite using the same tablet reaching paradigm, does not provide quantitative effect sizes, p-values, participant numbers, target geometry, sampling rate, or statistical details, so it cannot confirm that the proposed 12-cm targets, 45/90/135-degree anisotropic directions, 120 trials, 650-ms constraint, or 100-Hz sampling will yield sufficient signal for submovement decomposition and model-based mass tests.

### Suggested Action

Most important, strengthen the in-flight restraint and stability validation by adding pelvis or torso restraint and shoulder straps where feasible, and record trunk, shoulder, and hip displacement as in [(Macaluso et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b3cfc02cac9b750070c5469e65b890259c2934ac), so that trials with excessive body motion can be excluded or modeled as covariates. Second, replace or supplement repeated-measures ANOVA with linear mixed-effects models and a zero-inflated count model for errors, following [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), with random intercepts for participant and target or difficulty item, and pre-register handling of missing sessions; also conduct a power analysis for 12 taikonauts, 3 directions, and 3 phases, and consider adding practice blocks and counterbalanced target order to separate practice from physiological effects. Third, extend post-flight sampling beyond immediate recovery, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) did not detect an early drop at day 4 or day 90, and include longer recovery visits if mission constraints allow. Fourth, add speed-accuracy metrics beyond movement duration, such as error count, touch duration, success rate, and throughput or index of difficulty, to capture the mixed performance pattern reported in [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Finally, validate the two-joint arm model, movement utility theory, forward optimal controller, and submovement decomposition on the age-matched ground control data before in-flight use, and report smoothing, 100-Hz temporal resolution, and submovement detection thresholds to ensure that peak acceleration timing and corrective submovements are not artifacts.

## Context: The reaching task operationalizes the anisotropic inertia manipulation by requiring fast finger movements to directions with different effective limb masses. Method: 'The task will involve reaching from a start position to one of three targets located 12 cm away at 45°, 90°, and 135° counterclockwise from the horizontal axis.'

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) demonstrated that a tablet-based touchscreen target-acquisition task can be administered repeatedly before, during, and after a microgravity analog, specifically 60 days of -6 degree head-down tilt, and can detect phase-related changes in movement time, throughput, error count, and touch duration in 24 healthy adults aged 24 to 55 years, with 16 male and 8 female participants. This directly supports the proposed plan's use of a tablet touchscreen, repeated pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight sessions, and fast finger target acquisition with taikonauts, because the related protocol used a dominant-hand tapping task on a tablet while participants lay on a bed with elbows on a pad, a setup analogous to the proposed constrained in-flight reaching posture. A second, somewhat weaker support is statistical and trial-level: the related study used 1056 trials per participant across six measurement times, with 16 blocks and 11 targets in rotating order, and applied linear mixed models with subject and item random intercepts plus a zero-inflated Poisson model for errors, showing that repeated touchscreen motor data can yield significant phase effects, including throughput F(5,1675.2)=45.16, p<0.001; movement time F(5,1605.81)=72.465, p<0.001; error count chi-square(5)=15.10, p<0.01; and touch duration F(5,1631.26)=14.15, p<0.001. This supports the proposed 120-trial sessions as a plausible minimum, although the related work used approximately 176 trials per time point if evenly distributed, suggesting the proposed trial count should be validated for power. Weaker still, the related work supports the use of Fitts-law difficulty manipulation and repeated target selection, because it varied distance and width across 16 blocks with index of difficulty from 1.17 to 4.95 bits and found a highly similar movement-time versus index-of-difficulty relationship across head-down-tilt and non-head-down-tilt conditions, with a maximum R-squared difference of 0.01, indicating that target difficulty can be controlled and interpreted in a repeated-measurement touchscreen task. The weakest support is that the population and analog condition are not identical to the proposed taikonauts in actual spaceflight, so the evidence mainly establishes feasibility and sensitivity rather than the specific anisotropic-inertia hypothesis.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related microgravity analog did not show the movement slowing assumed in the research plan; instead, movement time decreased significantly from baseline through 21 days of head-down tilt and remained stable, while throughput increased by about 0.49 bits/s relative to baseline and about 0.95 bits/s at 90 days after head-down tilt. Although the proposed study uses actual spaceflight rather than head-down tilt, this finding cautions against assuming that a microgravity-related condition will uniformly produce slower reaching and suggests that phase effects may be task-specific, practice-related, or analog-specific. A second major contradiction is that the related task did not manipulate anisotropic inertia or use the proposed three 12 cm targets at 45, 90, and 135 degrees counterclockwise from the horizontal axis; it used 11 targets arranged in a circle in rotating order and varied distance and width across 16 blocks, so it provides no evidence that the proposed direction-specific effective-mass manipulation will produce the predicted kinematic dissociation between conservative control and body-mass underestimation. A third contradiction is that the related analysis did not extract peak acceleration, peak speed timing, or submovement decomposition; it measured aggregate Fitts-law indicators, namely movement time, throughput, error count or error rate, and touch duration, so it cannot support the proposed feedforward-control and corrective-submovement analyses. A fourth contradiction is that the related task did not impose the proposed 650 ms deadline or auditory beeps, so the planned speed-accuracy trade-off under a strict time constraint and reaction-time manipulation are not validated by this work. A fifth, weaker contradiction is that the related protocol used a different trial structure and target set, with 11 targets, rotating order, 16 difficulty combinations, and 1056 total trials, rather than 120 pseudorandom trials to three fixed directions, which may affect automation, learning, and direction-specific fatigue. A sixth, weaker contradiction is methodological: the related study used linear mixed models and a zero-inflated Poisson model for errors with interquartile-range outlier removal, whereas the proposed plan specifies repeated-measures ANOVAs, which may be less appropriate for non-normal error counts and repeated trial-level data. The weakest contradiction is operational: the related study had data corruption at the 4-day post measurement for 12 of 24 participants, indicating a risk in repeated constrained measurements that the proposed in-flight protocol must mitigate.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed method should add a ground pilot or biomechanical validation of the 45, 90, and 135 degree, 12 cm targets to confirm that they produce meaningfully different effective limb masses and that the resulting kinematics can distinguish conservative control from mass underestimation; this should include peak acceleration, peak speed timing, and submovement decomposition rather than only aggregate Fitts metrics. Second, the analysis plan should be upgraded from repeated-measures ANOVA to mixed-effects models with random intercepts for participant, session, block, and trial where appropriate, and a zero-inflated or negative-binomial model for error counts, following [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), with interquartile-range-based trial and block outlier removal and sensitivity analyses. Third, the trial count and difficulty should be calibrated: because the related work used approximately 176 trials per time point if evenly distributed and 16 difficulty combinations, the proposed 120 trials per session should be power-analyzed, and if possible increased to 150 to 200 trials or supplemented with practice blocks to separate learning from phase effects. Fourth, the 650 ms deadline and auditory beep condition should be piloted to ensure the task is not too difficult or too easy, and target width should be chosen so that the index of difficulty is comparable to the 1.17 to 4.95 bits range used in the related work. Fifth, the in-flight setup should explicitly control for posture and stabilization by documenting tablet position, foot straps, left-hand grasp, elbow support, and dominant hand, and should include a ground control group with repeated sessions to account for practice, as the related work showed throughput increased across the study. Sixth, data-quality safeguards should be implemented, including redundant recording, checksums, and backup storage, because the related study lost 4-day post data for half the sample. Weakest but still useful, the plan should report secondary Fitts-law metrics, including throughput, movement time, error rate, and touch duration, alongside peak kinematics, because these were sensitive to phase in the related work and can provide convergent evidence if peak-kinematic effects are small.

## Context: The computational models are used to predict how actual and misperceived limb mass should alter reaching kinematics. Method: 'These models will generate specific predictions about how actual and misperceived limb mass should affect reaching kinematics across different movement directions.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Wochner et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b80bce80ea49b57dd45a526f3666bd8f8940fe5), which used a two-joint biophysical arm model with six Hill-type muscles, fixed initial posture with elbow angle 90 degrees and shoulder angle 0 degrees, zero initial velocities, stimulation bounds from 0 to 1, and a vertical target manifold at 85 percent of total arm length to generate cost-based reaching trajectories. Its Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian process surrogate outperformed random testing over 600 iterations in three runs per condition, with a Student t-test rejecting equal means at p < 0.01 and best cost stabilizing near 8.09e-5 across 30 repeated runs, directly supporting the feasibility of using a two-joint cost-based forward model to predict reaching kinematics under task constraints. The next strongest support is [(Teka et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b60ab92485344ecbde5e05f62f7d3be9ce5c8), which used a forward computational biomechanical arm model with muscle force-length and force-velocity properties, afferent feedback, a spinal neuronal network, and a cortical controller to show direction-dependent modulation in reaching, including invariance in a shoulder-centered reference frame and dependence on movement distance; this supports the plan's use of direction-dependent effective mass across 45, 90, and 135 degree targets. Further support comes from [(Liao, and Kirsch, 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a706f0680cc85cb7c47cabcd9331282eb727d3a), which combined optimization-based submovement decomposition with a closed-loop computational model to predict submovement initiation time, amplitude, and duration in 3D reaching, supporting the proposed use of submovement decomposition to identify primary and corrective submovements. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Dehghani, and Bahrami, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bfd418fa377565a846f823d95cb801a7386054c), which indicates a model-based arm motor-planning approach using nonlinear predictive recruitment of motor synergies, an NPC parameter-setting procedure, and statistical validation, although the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative details.

### Contradictions

The strongest limitation or indirect contradiction is that the most methodologically aligned work, [(Wochner et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b80bce80ea49b57dd45a526f3666bd8f8940fe5), does not vary movement direction or limb mass and only optimizes endpoint cost on a vertical target manifold; its quantitative evidence therefore supports cost-based trajectory generation but does not validate the proposed prediction that actual versus misperceived limb mass will differentially alter peak acceleration, peak speed timing, duration, or submovements across directions. The next strongest gap is [(Teka et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b60ab92485344ecbde5e05f62f7d3be9ce5c8), which models direction-dependent biomechanical modulation but does not manipulate or model actual versus misperceived limb mass, and the supplied excerpt lacks numerical validation metrics, p-values, effect sizes, participant population, hardware, or statistical tests, so it cannot confirm that a forward optimal controller can predict mass-misperception effects. Further, [(Liao, and Kirsch, 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a706f0680cc85cb7c47cabcd9331282eb727d3a) supports submovement decomposition but does not address mass perception, and the excerpt provides no numerical accuracy, baseline comparison, participant size, hardware, target directions, mass conditions, or hyperparameters, limiting its ability to support the proposed mass-specific submovement predictions. The weakest contradiction is [(Dehghani, and Bahrami, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bfd418fa377565a846f823d95cb801a7386054c), which is too sparse to evaluate: it mentions nonlinear predictive motor synergies, NPC parameter-setting, and statistical validation but provides no quantitative outcomes, validation metrics, population, hardware, sampling rates, target directions, hyperparameters, or mass-related conditions.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to explicitly parameterize actual versus misperceived limb mass in the two-joint forward optimal controller, using the direction-dependent inertia or effective mass matrix for the 45, 90, and 135 degree, 12 cm targets, and to simulate feedforward trajectories under actual mass M and perceived mass M_hat before testing taikonaut data; this should be validated against ground-based mass perturbation data or simulated mass scaling, with pre-registered predictions for peak acceleration, peak speed timing, movement duration, and corrective submovements. Second, adopt the two-joint Hill-type musculoskeletal and cost-optimization framework from [(Wochner et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b80bce80ea49b57dd45a526f3666bd8f8940fe5), including explicit cost functions, stimulation or control bounds, initial posture, and, if gradients are unavailable, Bayesian optimization with a Gaussian process surrogate; report iterations, acquisition function, cost distributions, and significance tests to make the model reproducible. Third, incorporate the direction-dependent biomechanical details from [(Teka et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d2b60ab92485344ecbde5e05f62f7d3be9ce5c8), such as muscle force-length and force-velocity properties, afferent feedback, and spinal or cortical control layers, or at least direction-dependent impedance or effective mass, and test whether directional modulation is invariant in a shoulder-centered frame for the chosen targets. Fourth, use the closed-loop submovement decomposition approach from [(Liao, and Kirsch, 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a706f0680cc85cb7c47cabcd9331282eb727d3a) to predict and quantify primary and corrective submovements, including initiation time, amplitude, and duration, and calibrate decomposition thresholds against simulated submovements or known corrective trials, while checking whether 100 Hz touchscreen sampling is sufficient for acceleration and submovement detection. Finally, strengthen validation and statistical inference using the model-based planning and statistical validation logic implied by [(Dehghani, and Bahrami, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bfd418fa377565a846f823d95cb801a7386054c), by reporting effect sizes, confidence intervals, cross-validation or held-out validation, and mixed-effects or repeated-measures ANOVA assumptions for the small n = 12 taikonaut sample, three directions, 120 trials per session, and 650 ms time constraint.

## Context: In-flight conditions are controlled to ensure postural stability and consistent task geometry. Method: 'During in-flight sessions, participants will be secured with foot straps and will stabilize themselves by grasping the tabletop edge with their left hand.'

### Support

Strongest support for the in-flight foot-strap and left-hand tabletop-grasp method comes from [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), which shows that a controlled touchscreen target-acquisition protocol can be maintained across repeated measurement points in a microgravity analog by fixing body posture, tablet position, and reach geometry. In that study, 24 healthy adults aged 24 to 55 years, with mean age 33.33 years and standard deviation 9.38 years, 16 male and 8 female, completed 1056 FittsTouch trials per participant across six measurement points during 60 days of -6 degrees head-down tilt, with the tablet placed on a stand, elbows supported, and the photographed setup reproduced to keep the dominant-hand tapping geometry consistent; this directly supports the plan's emphasis on consistent tablet positioning and postural control when testing right-index-finger reaching on a tablet. A second, important support is that time of measurement significantly affected throughput, movement time, error count, and touch duration, with throughput F(5, 1675.2)=45.16, p<0.001; movement time F(5, 1605.81)=72.465, p<0.001; error count chi-square(5)=15.10, p<0.01; and touch duration F(5, 1631.26)=14.15, p<0.001, which supports the plan's pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight phase design and the need for age-matched ground controls to separate true microgravity effects from repeated-measurement drift. A third support is statistical: [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used linear mixed models with random intercepts for participant, target-distance-by-width item, and their interaction, plus a zero-inflated Poisson model for error count and Holm-adjusted pairwise contrasts, which is more appropriate than the proposed repeated-measures ANOVA for repeated trials, missing data, and non-normal error counts. Weaker but relevant support is that the strength of the movement-time versus index-of-difficulty relationship was highly similar in head-down-tilt and non-head-down-tilt conditions, with a maximum R-squared difference of 0.01, suggesting that when task geometry and posture are controlled, basic Fitts-law kinematics can be preserved; this supports the plan's goal of using consistent target geometry to isolate effective-mass effects, although it does not validate foot straps or tabletop grasping specifically.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) provides no direct evidence that foot straps and left-hand tabletop grasping are necessary, sufficient, or safe for in-flight touchscreen reaching; the study used a head-down-tilt bed-rest analog with a tablet stand and elbow support, not actual microgravity, and did not measure foot-strap tension, tabletop grasp force, body sway, or tablet motion. Therefore the proposed stabilization method remains unvalidated by this related work. A second contradiction is that the analog study did not show the movement slowing assumed in the research plan: movement time decreased significantly from baseline through 21 days of head-down tilt and showed no further substantial change afterward, while throughput increased, with one later measurement about 0.49 bits/s above baseline and the 90-day post-tilt measurement almost 0.95 bits/s above baseline. This suggests that controlled posture in a microgravity analog can be associated with faster, not slower, target acquisition, so foot-strap and tabletop stabilization alone should not be assumed to produce or reveal the hypothesized in-flight slowing. A third contradiction is that performance was not uniformly stable: error count was significantly higher during head-down tilt than baseline, and touch duration was significantly elevated at 5, 21, and 56 days of tilt, indicating that even with a controlled tablet setup, microgravity-analog conditions can alter error and contact behavior in ways that the plan's primary peak-kinematics and submovement measures may not capture. A fourth contradiction is methodological: the proposed repeated-measures ANOVA is less robust than the mixed-model approach used in [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), especially given repeated trials, item effects, and missing post-tilt data, because only 12 participants had the 4-day post-tilt measurement due to data corruption. A weaker contradiction is that the related work used a much richer task, with 16 distance-width combinations, index-of-difficulty values from 1.17 to 4.95, 11 targets per block, and 1056 trials per participant, than the plan's 120 trials across three fixed 12 cm targets with a 650 ms constraint, so the plan may have less power to detect direction-specific peak-kinematic or submovement changes and may not generalize across difficulty levels.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add an in-flight pilot or validation protocol for the foot-strap and left-hand tabletop-grasp method: standardize strap tightness, tabletop grasp position, tablet position, and body posture with photos or video, and measure or at least log tablet motion, body sway, strap tension, or grasp force if feasible, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) shows that consistent geometry is achievable but does not demonstrate that the proposed in-flight constraints are equivalent. Second, replace or supplement repeated-measures ANOVA with linear mixed models for continuous kinematic outcomes and a zero-inflated Poisson or negative-binomial model for error counts, including random intercepts for participant, target direction or item, phase, and interactions, with Holm-adjusted contrasts, mirroring [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) and better handling missing data and repeated trials. Third, increase the number of trials or provide a power analysis for 120 trials; [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used 1056 trials per participant across six measurement points, and submovement decomposition plus peak acceleration and peak speed timing will require sufficient trials per direction and phase to estimate primary and corrective submovements reliably. Fourth, use the dominant hand or restrict participants to right-handed individuals, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used dominant-hand tapping while the plan fixes the right index finger and left-hand stabilization, which could confound handedness with in-flight stabilization. Fifth, add throughput, error count or error rate, touch duration, and Fitts-law index-of-difficulty analyses alongside peak kinematics, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) showed these metrics are sensitive to measurement phase and can reveal performance changes that peak speed and acceleration alone may miss. Sixth, consider broadening target distances or widths, or validating the fixed 12 cm and 650 ms task, since the related work covered index-of-difficulty values from 1.17 to 4.95 and a wider difficulty range would improve generalizability and help distinguish conservative control from mass underestimation. Seventh, verify that 100 Hz sampling is adequate for peak acceleration and submovement decomposition; if possible, use higher sampling or validate the 100 Hz pipeline against higher-rate data, because acceleration and submovement timing are more sensitive to sampling than movement time or throughput.

## Context: Continuous finger tracking and kinematic analysis provide the feedforward control measures used to test the hypotheses. Method: 'We will record finger position continuously at 100 Hz and apply kinematic analyses to extract movement parameters including reaction time, movement duration, peak acceleration, peak speed, and their temporal characteristics.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed continuous finger-tracking method is that a longitudinal within-subjects touchscreen target-acquisition study in a -6 degree head-down-tilt microgravity analog demonstrated that tablet-based repeated measurements across pre, during, and post phases can detect significant phase effects on movement time, throughput, error count, and touch duration [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). This directly supports the feasibility of using a tablet touchscreen in a microgravity-analog setting and of comparing pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight phases, although it did not use 100 Hz continuous tracking. The study used 24 healthy adults aged 24 to 55 years, 60 days of head-down tilt, measurements 12 days before, on days 5, 21, and 56, and 4 and 90 days after, with 16 blocks per session and 11 circularly arranged targets in a rotating opposite-target pattern, 16 distance-width combinations, and index of difficulty from 1.17 to 4.95, corresponding to 176 trials per session and 1056 trials across the six measurement points per participant [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). This supports the proposed 120-trial session as a reasonable lower bound, but suggests that a larger trial count or block structure may improve stability. The statistical evidence is strong: time of measurement significantly affected throughput F(5, 1675.2)=45.16, p<0.001; movement time F(5, 1605.81)=72.465, p<0.001; error count chi-square(5)=15.10, p<0.01; and touch duration F(5, 1631.26)=14.15, p<0.001 [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). This supports the proposed repeated-measures phase design and the need to model practice effects. Weaker but still relevant support is that the study used linear mixed-effects models with subject, item, and subject-by-item random intercepts, a zero-inflated Poisson generalized linear mixed model for error count, trial-level and block-level interquartile-range outlier removal, and step-down familywise error-rate adjustment, which is more robust than the proposed repeated-measures ANOVA, especially given missing day-4 data for 12 participants [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). It also supports adding throughput, error rate, and touch duration as complementary metrics to the proposed reaction time, movement duration, peak acceleration, peak speed, temporal symmetry, and submovement analyses.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not record or analyze continuous finger position at 100 Hz and does not extract the proposed feedforward kinematic measures, including reaction time, peak acceleration, peak speed, temporal symmetry, or submovements [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). It only used touchscreen selection events to compute movement time, throughput, error count/error rate, and touch duration, so it cannot validate the proposed method's central claim that continuous tracking can distinguish conservative control from body-mass underestimation. The next major contradiction is that the task did not manipulate directional effective mass or anisotropic inertia; it used a multidirectional tapping task with 11 circularly arranged targets, a rotating opposite-target pattern, and 16 distance-width combinations, rather than the proposed three fixed 12 cm targets at 45, 90, and 135 degrees with a two-joint arm model [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Therefore it provides no direct evidence that the proposed direction-specific effective-mass design can dissociate mass underestimation from strategic control. A further contradiction is that the study had no ground-based control group, so practice effects may confound head-down-tilt-specific changes, whereas the proposed plan relies on an age-matched ground control group to separate phase effects from repeated-measurement effects [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). The related work also used a head-down-tilt analog in healthy adults rather than actual spaceflight in taikonauts, so its findings may not transfer to the proposed population and environment [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Finally, the observed pattern is not a simple uniform movement slowing: movement time decreased significantly from baseline through early head-down tilt with no further significant changes after day 21, throughput increased by about 0.49 bits/s relative to baseline and about 0.95 bits/s at 90 days post-head-down-tilt, error count was higher during head-down tilt, and touch duration was elevated on head-down-tilt days 5, 21, and 56 [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). This contradicts any assumption that phase effects will be captured only by slower movement duration and supports the need for multiple kinematic and error metrics. A weaker contradiction is that the movement-time versus index-of-difficulty relationship was highly similar between head-down-tilt and non-head-down-tilt conditions, with a maximum R-squared difference of 0.01, suggesting that basic Fitts-law scaling may be preserved and that the proposed mass-specific kinematic effects may be subtle.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement and validate a 100 Hz continuous finger-tracking pipeline before the spaceflight study, with explicit extraction of reaction time, movement duration, peak acceleration, peak speed, peak timing, temporal symmetry, and submovement decomposition, because the related work only validated selection-event metrics such as movement time, throughput, error count, and touch duration [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). The second most important action is to incorporate the related work's robust statistical framework: use linear mixed-effects models with subject, item, and subject-by-item random intercepts for continuous outcomes, a zero-inflated Poisson generalized linear mixed model for error count, trial-level and block-level interquartile-range outlier removal, and step-down familywise error-rate adjustment, rather than relying solely on repeated-measures ANOVA, especially because the related work had missing day-4 data for 12 of 24 participants [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Third, add throughput, error rate, and touch duration as secondary metrics alongside the proposed feedforward kinematics, because the related work showed significant phase effects on all four indicators, including throughput F(5, 1675.2)=45.16, p<0.001, movement time F(5, 1605.81)=72.465, p<0.001, error count chi-square(5)=15.10, p<0.01, and touch duration F(5, 1631.26)=14.15, p<0.001 [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Fourth, increase or justify the trial count by using a block structure similar to the related work, such as 16 blocks with 11 targets per block or at least 176 trials per session, to reduce noise and support mixed-model random effects, while retaining the proposed three-direction effective-mass design [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Fifth, explicitly model practice and repeated-measurement confounds by using the ground control group, counterbalancing target order, and including session, block, and trial as fixed or random effects, because the related work lacked a ground control and showed practice-related throughput increases [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591). Finally, validate tablet latency, sampling stability, and touch-event timing in a ground pilot with the same 650 ms constraint and auditory beep, so that reaction time and peak kinematics are not contaminated by device latency or task automation.

## Context: Feedback-based corrections are identified by decomposing speed profiles into primary and corrective submovements. Method: 'To identify feedback-based corrections, we will employ submovement decomposition methods to detect trials containing both primary and secondary submovements, which would indicate corrective actions during movement execution.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed submovement-decomposition method comes from [(Elliott et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a4899d4f0ae330dcc88b69ad3d76e7658c2aa5a), which directly operationalized the same logic by parsing kinematic trajectories into a primary distance-covering movement and corrective submovements using a second acceleration peak or velocity reversal. In adult rapid aiming and reaching with small targets, controlled movement amplitudes, and manipulations of vision, target size or position, effector mass, gravity, and practice, corrective submovements were associated with further reduction of endpoint error after the primary movement. The paper also provides quantitative practice and perturbation evidence: early high-difficulty aiming showed primary endpoints undershooting and hitting the target equally often with about 5% overshoots; after four days of practice, movement time decreased, primary endpoint variability decreased, primary endpoints landed on target about 70% of trials, undershoots fell to about 25%, and overshoots remained about 5%, indicating fewer and smaller corrections. In target-shift trials, corrective submovements toward the new target took about 250 to 290 ms and added only about 100 ms to movement time. This supports the plan's use of secondary submovements in 100 Hz finger reaches to 12 cm targets under a 650 ms constraint as a feasible marker of feedback-based correction, especially if the analysis records not only presence but timing, amplitude, and endpoint-error reduction. As an iterative extension of the broader submovement-parsing literature, the weaker but still relevant support is [(Meyer et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21fd368fce70680a9420c9e6b7b7a8423801181d), which used primary and secondary submovement decomposition in wrist-rotation pointing with visible and invisible cursor feedback and reported primary-submovement duration, primary endpoint variability, and secondary-submovement duration across target distance and width conditions. Although the supplied excerpt lacks numerical values, effect sizes, or statistical tests, it supports the methodological precedent that secondary submovements can be parsed from speed profiles and interpreted as feedback-related components, which is the same inferential step the research plan applies to altered effective limb mass in microgravity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not to feasibility but to the interpretive claim that any secondary submovement automatically indicates body-mass underestimation. [(Elliott et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a4899d4f0ae330dcc88b69ad3d76e7658c2aa5a) showed that corrective submovements also occur under vision, no-vision, target shifts, and practice, and that their frequency and amplitude change with task difficulty and feedback availability; for example, no-vision trials had larger endpoint error and variability but still showed corrective limb-target regulation, and target-shift corrections took 250 to 290 ms. Therefore, in the proposed taikonaut experiment, secondary submovements could reflect visual feedback, target uncertainty, speed-accuracy trade-offs, reaction-beep effects, or general conservative control rather than insufficient feedforward force from mass underestimation. A second, weaker contradiction is that the related evidence does not yet establish transfer to the proposed population, effector, and sampling constraints. [(Meyer et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21fd368fce70680a9420c9e6b7b7a8423801181d) used wrist-rotation pointing with visible and invisible cursor feedback and target distance and width manipulations, and the excerpt provides no numerical outcomes, effect sizes, or statistical tests, so it cannot confirm that the same primary and secondary parsing will yield reliable secondary-submovement counts in finger touchscreen reaches at 100 Hz. A third concern is that the proposed method, as stated, only detects trials containing primary and secondary submovements; this binary detection may miss small or truncated corrections, especially under the 650 ms completion constraint, and may be sensitive to noise in 100 Hz position data because acceleration-based submovement criteria require reliable velocity and acceleration estimates. The related work does not provide direct evidence that such a minimal detection rule is sufficient for distinguishing mass-related feedback corrections from ordinary corrective aiming behavior.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should pre-register an explicit submovement-decomposition algorithm and validation procedure before flight data are analyzed, using the second-acceleration-peak or velocity-reversal logic from [(Elliott et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a4899d4f0ae330dcc88b69ad3d76e7658c2aa5a) and the primary and secondary parsing framework from [(Meyer et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21fd368fce70680a9420c9e6b7b7a8423801181d). This should include smoothing or filtering for 100 Hz finger-position data, minimum amplitude and duration thresholds, a noise floor, and sensitivity analyses showing that secondary-submovement counts are stable across threshold choices. Next, the analysis should move beyond binary detection and quantify primary endpoint error, final endpoint error, secondary-submovement amplitude, duration, onset latency, and movement time, because Elliott's strongest evidence is that corrective submovements reduce endpoint error and change with practice and target shifts. Then, the statistical model should be aligned with the 12 taikonauts, 120 trials, three directions, and pre-flight, in-flight, and post-flight phases: use trial-level mixed-effects models or repeated-measures ANOVAs with planned contrasts for phase and direction, random intercepts for participant, and corrections for multiple comparisons, while reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals rather than only p-values. Finally, the method should explicitly test the mass-underestimation prediction by comparing secondary-submovement frequency and amplitude across directions with different effective masses, with the strongest predicted increase in high-effective-mass directions during in-flight relative to pre-flight and post-flight, and by using the forward optimal controller simulations to generate expected submovement patterns under correct versus misperceived mass.

## Context: Statistical testing compares spaceflight phases and movement directions to evaluate hypothesis-specific patterns. Method: 'We will analyze the data using repeated-measures ANOVAs with factors of experimental phase and movement direction.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed repeated-measures phase factor is that [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used a within-subjects 60-day head-down tilt design with 24 healthy adults and six repeated measurement phases on a touchscreen tapping task, and found significant phase effects for throughput F(5, 1675.2) = 45.16, p < 0.001, movement time F(5, 1605.81) = 72.465, p < 0.001, error count chi-square(5) = 15.10, p < 0.01, and touch duration F(5, 1631.26) = 14.15, p < 0.001. This directly supports treating experimental phase as a meaningful within-subject factor in tablet-based target acquisition. A second, weaker support is that the study used repeated touchscreen target selection with 16 blocks, 11 targets per block, 1056 trials per participant, and 16 target difficulty combinations from 1.17 to 4.95 bits, showing that repeated-measures touchscreen performance can be quantified with throughput, movement time, errors, and touch duration in a way that is compatible with the proposed tablet reaching task. A third, weaker support is that the multidirectional target arrangement and target difficulty manipulation suggest that target geometry and difficulty can influence performance, which is conceptually relevant to the proposed movement direction factor, although the study did not model direction as a statistical factor. The weakest support is that the head-down tilt analog and healthy adult sample provide only indirect evidence that phase-related changes can be detected in a microgravity-related setting, not direct evidence for actual spaceflight phases or for the proposed 12-taikonaut sample.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed repeated-measures ANOVA may be less appropriate than the statistical approach actually used in the closest related work, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used linear mixed-effects models for throughput, movement time, and touch duration and a zero-inflated Poisson generalized linear mixed model for error count, with random intercepts for participant, target difficulty item, and their interaction, plus Holm-corrected pairwise contrasts. This matters because the proposed plan includes repeated phases, possible missing data, non-normal error counts, and a small taikonaut sample, all of which make simple repeated-measures ANOVA vulnerable to assumption violations and listwise deletion. A second contradiction is that the related work did not include movement direction as a factor, so it provides no direct evidence that a phase-by-direction ANOVA will detect the hypothesized anisotropic inertia effects; its multidirectional tapping task used rotating opposite-target order and 16 difficulty combinations rather than three fixed 45, 90, and 135 degree directions. A third contradiction is that the related work did not analyze peak acceleration, peak speed timing, or submovement decomposition, so it does not support the proposed kinematic tests of conservative control versus body mass underestimation. A fourth contradiction is that the phase effects were metric-specific rather than a uniform slowing: movement time decreased from baseline through day 21, throughput increased by about 0.49 and 0.95 bits/s at later points, while error count was higher during head-down tilt and touch duration was elevated at days 5, 21, and 56, which contradicts a simple assumption that spaceflight phase will produce only slower movements. The weakest contradiction is that the study was a head-down tilt analog with 24 healthy adults aged 24 to 55, not 12 taikonauts in actual spaceflight, and recovery day 4 data were available for only 12 participants due to data corruption, limiting generalizability and highlighting data-loss risks.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace or supplement the planned repeated-measures ANOVA with linear mixed-effects models for continuous kinematic outcomes and a zero-inflated Poisson or negative binomial model for error counts, following [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), with fixed effects for experimental phase and movement direction, random intercepts for participant and, if feasible, target or direction item, and Holm-corrected contrasts for phase-by-direction comparisons. The next most important action is to increase statistical power and trial density because the related work used 1056 trials per participant across six phases, whereas the proposed plan uses only 120 trials per session, so a power analysis should determine whether 40 trials per direction per session is sufficient for peak acceleration, peak speed timing, and submovement frequency, and the protocol should consider more trials, more sessions, or block-level aggregation. A third action is to control target difficulty and direction confounds by explicitly modeling target difficulty or effective mass as a covariate or factor, because the related work varied 16 difficulty combinations and found target difficulty strongly related to movement time, while the proposed plan uses three fixed directions intended to vary effective mass. A fourth action is to add preprocessing and analysis details for peak acceleration, peak speed timing, and submovement decomposition, including 100 Hz smoothing, outlier removal using interquartile ranges as in the related work, and separate tests for primary versus corrective submovements. The least important but still useful action is to acknowledge that head-down tilt analog evidence is indirect and to use the age-matched ground control group to separate true spaceflight phase effects from repeated-measurement and practice effects.

## Context: A ground control group helps separate spaceflight effects from practice or repeated-measurement effects. Method: 'An age-matched ground control group will provide baseline comparisons to assess potential confounding effects of repeated measurements.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f60cb14f6bd8139a2d02dcf8c7110f53941d3), which can be read as an iterative refinement of the earlier ground-control comparison in [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9308a6e60979355fa1ced992d080de4438a1f2). The 2017-08 study used a separate healthy ground-control cohort of 12 subjects to provide a non-intervention repeated-measurement baseline while 18 head-down bed rest subjects, randomized to exercise and non-exercise groups, underwent 70 days of 6-degree head-down tilt and MRI/sensorimotor testing. The controls completed the same MRI and sensorimotor assessments at four time points over 90 days, and the authors used linear mixed models with random subject intercepts, time as continuous or factor, age as covariate, exercise group, group-by-time interactions, restricted maximum likelihood, and Bonferroni correction, while omitting first time points to account for practice effects. This directly supports the proposed use of a ground control group to separate spaceflight or intervention effects from repeated-measurement and practice effects in the taikonaut reaching study. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9308a6e60979355fa1ced992d080de4438a1f2), which used a ground control group to compare HDBR-related changes in diffusion MRI metrics FA and FW and functional mobility and balance outcomes, showing that a non-intervention ground comparison can be used to contextualize intervention-related brain and mobility changes. However, the provided excerpt for this work lacks participant numbers, age-matching details, scanner specifications, statistical thresholds, and quantitative outcomes, so it provides only conceptual support for the proposed ground-control method rather than direct evidence for reaching kinematics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the more detailed 2017-08 design, which extends the 2017-06 ground-control concept, did not explicitly state that the 12 healthy controls were age-matched to the 18 head-down bed rest participants, and their measurement time points (days 1, 7.5, 43.5, and 83.5 over 90 days) did not align with the bed-rest MRI and behavioral time points. This contradicts the proposed method's assumption that an age-matched ground control group alone will adequately control repeated-measurement confounding; session timing, number of sessions, task exposure, and practice effects also need to be matched. A second contradiction is that the same study used a relatively small control group (n=12) and reported brain-behavior associations that did not survive multiple-comparison correction, such as vestibular cortex b=6306.72, t(16)=2.69, p=.017 and cerebellar lobule V b=743.71, t(16)=2.90, p=.011, and a voxelwise peak-voxel Pearson correlation of .76 that survived FWE p<.05 but not an additional Bonferroni correction for four tests; the voxelwise analysis also used 15,000 permutations, 8 mm FWHM variance smoothing, and intracranial volume adjustment. This suggests that ground-control comparisons in small repeated-measurement studies may be underpowered and vulnerable to false-positive or unstable effects, which is relevant to the proposed 12-taikonaut design and submovement frequency analyses. A third contradiction is domain mismatch: both related works assessed HDBR-related balance, mobility, and MRI outcomes, not rapid touchscreen reaching, anisotropic limb inertia, peak acceleration/speed timing, or submovement decomposition, so they do not directly support the specific kinematic hypotheses in the research plan. The weakest contradiction is that [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9308a6e60979355fa1ced992d080de4438a1f2) provides no quantitative results or detailed reproducibility information in the supplied excerpt, limiting its ability to validate the proposed control-group approach.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify the ground control group as a repeated-measurement control that matches the taikonaut protocol in session number, session timing, task, and exposure, not only age. Controls should perform the same right-index-finger tablet reaching task with the same 12 cm targets at 45 degrees, 90 degrees, and 135 degrees, the same 120 trials per session, the same pseudorandom order, the same 650 ms time constraint, the same auditory beep in half trials, the same 100 Hz position recording, and the same tabletop stabilization and foot support as feasible on the ground. The second most important action is to replace or supplement the planned repeated-measures ANOVA with linear mixed models that include group, phase, direction, group-by-phase, group-by-direction, and group-by-phase-by-direction, random intercepts and random slopes for participants, baseline covariates such as age, sex, height, mass, handedness, and baseline movement duration, and appropriate multiple-comparison correction for peak acceleration, peak speed, their timing, and submovement frequency. The third action is to predefine a power analysis or minimum control-group size, ideally at least comparable to the 12 taikonauts and preferably larger, to detect group-by-phase interactions on the primary kinematic outcomes, because [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f60cb14f6bd8139a2d02dcf8c7110f53941d3) showed that a 12-subject control group can yield non-robust corrected results. The fourth action is to explicitly model practice effects by including initial practice trials or first-session data as a covariate, omitting or modeling early trials as in [(Koppelmans et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f60cb14f6bd8139a2d02dcf8c7110f53941d3), and using the control group to estimate expected practice-related changes in planned movement duration, peak kinematics, and submovement frequency before interpreting in-flight changes, and to calibrate movement utility theory and forward optimal controller baseline parameters for planned duration and effective mass perception.

## Context: Consistent tablet placement helps maintain the same task geometry across sessions. Method: 'The tablet will be positioned consistently across all sessions to maintain experimental conditions.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from the newer space-analog study by [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), which directly used a fixed tablet placement procedure as a within-subject control for repeated touchscreen target acquisition during 60 days of -6° head-down tilt. The tablet was mounted on a stand on the participant's bed, elbows were placed on a pad, the screen was adjusted so participants could see it without lifting the head and reach all screen areas without lifting the elbow, and the position was photographed and reproduced at later measurement points. With 24 healthy adults (24-55 years, 16 male, 8 female), 11 circular targets, 16 blocks, 1056 trials per participant, target distances of 294.88, 589.76, 884.64, and 1179.52 px, target widths of 39.32, 78.63, 157.27, and 235.90 px, and index of difficulty 1.17-4.95 in FittsTouch, the study obtained comparable tapping performance across six measurement points and a highly stable Fitts relationship, with maximum R-squared difference of 0.01 between head-down tilt and non-head-down tilt conditions. Significant time-of-measurement effects (throughput F(5, 1675.2)=45.16, p<0.001; movement time F(5, 1605.81)=72.465, p<0.001; error count chi-square(5)=15.10, p<0.01; touch duration F(5, 1631.26)=14.15, p<0.001) show that stable placement can preserve task geometry well enough to reveal phase-specific changes rather than placement artifacts. This directly supports the proposed plan's statement that the tablet will be positioned consistently across all sessions for 12 taikonauts performing directional touchscreen reaches, because it demonstrates that repeated spaceflight-analog sessions can be made comparable when screen-to-body geometry is controlled. As an iterative sequence, the older lab study by [(Kim, and Hyun, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/159667e42ba7bc3ae9b990a18e6dd5e768ef1d41) provides weaker but complementary support: it used a repeated-session touchscreen pointing task with fixed task geometry (20 mm cursor and target, 130 mm initial cursor-target distance, 35 px snap area, six conditions, five sessions of ten trials, randomized order) and showed learning stabilized by sessions four and five, with condition effects such as combined hardware/software snap fastest at 3768.8 ms (SD 2064.2) and lowest NASA-TLX workload. Although it did not explicitly control or report tablet placement, its fixed geometry and repeated-session design support the broader principle that stable task geometry is needed to interpret repeated touchscreen reaching data, and the newer Bieg study extends that principle to a spaceflight analog by adding explicit tablet placement control.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that the same newer space-analog evidence that supports consistent placement also shows the proposed method is under-specified if it relies only on procedural consistency. [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) reported that the tablet position was photographed and reproduced as accurately as possible, but did not report a numeric check of placement error, so consistency was procedural rather than directly verified. In the proposed plan, where targets are 12 cm away at 45°, 90°, and 135°, movement duration is constrained to 650 ms, finger position is sampled at 100 Hz, and submovement decomposition is used to infer feedforward versus feedback control, even small unmeasured shifts in tablet position, tilt, or screen-to-elbow geometry could change effective target distance, movement direction, and perceived limb mass, thereby confounding the repeated-measures ANOVA factors of phase and direction. The significant time-of-measurement effects in [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), including higher error counts during head-down tilt and elevated touch duration at 5, 21, and 56 days, also caution that consistent tablet placement is necessary but not sufficient to maintain identical experimental conditions; posture, head-down tilt, and recovery can still alter performance. A weaker contradiction is that the older study by [(Kim, and Hyun, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/159667e42ba7bc3ae9b990a18e6dd5e768ef1d41) did not explicitly report tablet placement or device positioning, so its fixed-geometry touchscreen pointing task cannot be taken as direct evidence that tablet placement itself was controlled. Its setup also differed from the proposed plan: it used a Dell Precision 5530 2-in-1 laptop, a key-press completion criterion, haptic joystick conditions, snap assistance, 12 participants, and 50 trials per condition, rather than 12 taikonauts, 120 trials per session, 100 Hz finger tracking, foot straps, left-hand tabletop stabilization, and three 12-cm directional targets. Thus, the newer Bieg work both supports and qualifies the older Kim fixed-geometry approach: it shows the principle is relevant to space analogs, but it also reveals that procedural consistency without numeric verification is a methodological gap.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add objective, quantified tablet-placement verification to the proposed method. The plan should specify fiducial markers or a camera-based calibration procedure to record tablet position, tilt, and screen-to-body geometry relative to the participant's torso, elbow, shoulder, and head, and should report placement error for each session with a predefined acceptance threshold, for example less than 1-2 mm in screen coordinates or less than 1 degree in tilt, before data are accepted. This directly addresses the main limitation in [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591), where placement was photographed and reproduced but not numerically verified, and it protects the proposed 12-cm target distances at 45°, 90°, and 135° from unmeasured drift. Second, the method should fully calibrate and report the task geometry: tablet resolution/DPI, physical target size, start position, target coordinates in both pixels and centimeters, coordinate system, 100 Hz finger tracking, 650 ms time constraint, and the left-hand tabletop stabilization and foot-strap setup, ideally using a validated touchscreen task platform such as FittsTouch or an equivalent implementation. This would make the proposed directional reaching task comparable to the Bieg space-analog protocol and to the fixed-geometry repeated-session design in [(Kim, and Hyun, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/159667e42ba7bc3ae9b990a18e6dd5e768ef1d41). Third, because [(Bieg et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3ff0bedae6143e46d2dcaca72405a0669643591) used 1056 trials per participant across 16 blocks while the proposed plan uses only 120 trials per session, the plan should include a power analysis or increase trials/blocks, especially for submovement decomposition and repeated-measures ANOVA with phase and direction; if placement error is recorded, it should be included as a covariate or used to exclude sessions that exceed the threshold. Finally, a pilot placement protocol with the age-matched ground control group should be used to standardize tablet mounting, elbow support, head position, and reachability, analogous to the Bieg procedure, so that in-flight sessions can be reproduced with minimal operator variability.
