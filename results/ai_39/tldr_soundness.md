## Strengths 
The strongest literature support is for the TUSZ v1.5.2 cross-patient benchmark, frequency-domain FFT log-amplitude inputs, patient-disjoint splits, weighted F1, and CNN/RNN/GNN baselines, especially [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), and [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a). A supervised head on EEG spatiotemporal features is also well supported by [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), [(Mirowski et al., 2009-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f94ed69179beb00b7d759b75f37e3b7f8d0fc937), and [(Zhong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7389f76fc63767154fa213fc436a13b47de00e7b). Self-supervised pretraining and DCRNN graph baselines are supported by [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), while 1-second spectral units and cross-site generalization are supported by [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307) and [(Craley et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cba777745196c164112f76e6c70de585c873b8f).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the need for and validation of the invariant mask and IRM components. A strong supervised CNN/attention Bi-LSTM baseline already reaches 87.62% patient-wise weighted F1 on TUSZ v1.5.2 without invariant masking, environment partitioning, or IRM, and its preprocessing differs from the plan, so direct comparison is not established: [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a). The eight-way class scheme is also problematic because myoclonic and nonspecific labels are rare or confusable, and prior TUSZ work used merged or reduced classes: [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), and [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6). Patient-level K-means environments are not validated by the cited EEG clustering work, which used segment-level K=4 clusters and no IRM: [(Kabir et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83cd22703663e87d91592000f967175d419c0254). Finally, the 60-second non-overlapped clip and 1-second FFT design are not directly anchored, because window length, overlap, and step size materially affect sensitivity and false positives: [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307), [(Pale et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9395220b698424b62eaa0a58783d47f1f4dc132), and [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Align the TUSZ evaluation with the strongest comparable protocols: use patient-disjoint splits, verify every seizure type appears in train/validation/test, handle rare classes by excluding/merging myoclonic and nonspecific labels or reporting them separately, and report weighted F1 with 95% confidence intervals, per-class precision/recall, macro F1, and confusion matrices, following [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), and [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6).

Add matched strong baselines and component ablations to prove ST-IRM: retrain MP-SeizNet or CNN+attention Bi-LSTM, self-supervised pre-trained Dist-DCRNN/Corr-DCRNN, and a stronger graph/S4 baseline under the same 19-channel, 200 Hz, 60-second, 1-second FFT protocol, then ablate the invariant mask, SSL temporal loss, K-means environments, gradient variance penalty, and window length/overlap, as motivated by [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), [(Tang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e475c5fda03556a2f09a7c6c11dfb6398c73b30), and [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307).

Make the patient-environment and invariant-mask design empirically validated: cluster patient-level EEG summaries or embeddings with k-means++ and multiple restarts, report silhouette/stability and check that clusters are not confounded with seizure type, electrode reference, or channel availability; normalize or include reference/channel variability as an environment; and validate the mask with sparsity/entropy diagnostics, channel/frequency-band ablations, and comparisons to unmasked features, following [(Kabir et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83cd22703663e87d91592000f967175d419c0254), [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893), and [(Mirowski et al., 2009-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f94ed69179beb00b7d759b75f37e3b7f8d0fc937).
