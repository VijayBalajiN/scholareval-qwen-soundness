## Context: To separate seizure-type-invariant EEG signals from patient-specific variation. Method: We will develop a mask function m(·) to decompose raw EEG feature representations φ(Xt) into two orthogonal components: invariant representation κ(Xt) = m(φ(Xt)) and variant representation ψ(Xt) = (1 - m(φ(Xt))) ⊙ φ(Xt), where m(Xt) ∈ [0,1]^(N×M).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed mask-based invariant representation comes from [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), which evaluates MP-SeizNet on TUSZ v1.5.2 with patient-disjoint 3-fold cross-validation and reports a patient-wise weighted F1-score of 87.62%, demonstrating that cross-patient seizure-type classification on the same corpus is feasible and that weighted F1 is an appropriate primary metric for the research plan. As an iterative progression, this 2022 work extends the earlier feature-based EEG seizure-detection landscape by providing quantitative patient-wise evidence rather than only qualitative method descriptions. The same paper uses 20-channel TCP montage EEG resampled to 250 Hz, 2-second non-overlapping segments, a CNN plus attention-based Bi-LSTM, cross-entropy loss, Adam, batch size 64, 100 epochs, initial learning rate 0.01 reduced to 1e-4, and early stopping after 10 epochs, providing a concrete supervised baseline that the proposed ST-IRM mask function must outperform under matched preprocessing. A secondary support is that the same paper reports wavelet-based features as important and seizure-wise weighted F1 of 98.01%, with attention improving Bi-LSTM by more than 10 percentage points, which supports the research plan's emphasis on temporal and frequency-domain EEG representations, although the plan uses 200 Hz, 60-second clips, and 1-second FFT log amplitudes rather than 250 Hz 2-second segments. The older review by [(Kim et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c088f7692baed52a3403d826425ae090b35fafab) provides weaker but relevant support by cataloguing time-domain, frequency-domain, and time-frequency EEG features such as FFT, STFT, autoregressive spectral estimation, and wavelet transform, and by noting that feature choice affects seizure detection performance; its mention of 1-35 Hz EEG processing and filtering is also relevant to the plan's FFT preprocessing, but it does not provide quantitative evidence for invariant masking or cross-patient generalization.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly comparable empirical work, [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), achieves patient-wise weighted F1 of 87.62% without an invariant mask, self-supervised invariant learning, environment partitioning, or invariant risk minimization, using only a supervised multi-path CNN and attention-based Bi-LSTM on TUSZ v1.5.2; this suggests that the proposed mask function m(·) must demonstrate a clear performance gain over a strong supervised temporal baseline, and that the claimed need for invariant decomposition is not yet established by the cited evidence. A second contradiction is preprocessing and evaluation mismatch: Albaqami uses 250 Hz sampling, 2-second segments, and patient-wise 3-fold CV with selected specific seizure labels, excluding non-specific labels and myoclonic seizures that had only three cases from two patients, whereas the research plan uses 200 Hz sampling, 60-second non-overlapping clips, 1-second FFT intervals, 19-electrode scalp EEG, and a fixed 179/22/34 patient split; therefore the 87.62% result cannot be directly compared to the proposed method unless preprocessing, class selection, and fold construction are aligned. A third contradiction is that the plan's fixed patient split may not guarantee that all eight seizure types are present in training, validation, and test, while Albaqami's protocol explicitly constrains patient-wise folds to classes recorded from at least three patients and subsamples test data when class imbalance is severe, indicating that the proposed evaluation could be unstable or invalid for rare seizure types. The review by [(Kim et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c088f7692baed52a3403d826425ae090b35fafab) provides weaker contradictory evidence in the sense that it describes conventional feature-based and supervised deep-learning pipelines without decomposing EEG representations into invariant and patient-specific variant components, and it reports no quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance values, so it does not support the assumption that standard EEG feature extraction alone resolves cross-patient distribution shift.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the research plan should align its cross-patient evaluation with the strongest comparable TUSZ evidence from [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) by using patient-disjoint folds, reporting weighted F1 with 95% confidence intervals from repeated randomized tests, and explicitly verifying that every seizure type appears in training, validation, and test; if rare classes such as myoclonic seizures have too few patients, the plan should exclude, merge, or report them separately rather than relying on a single fixed 179/22/34 split. Next, the plan should implement MP-SeizNet or an equivalent CNN plus attention-based Bi-LSTM baseline on the exact proposed preprocessing, namely 200 Hz, 60-second clips, 1-second FFT log amplitudes, and 19 electrodes, to test whether the invariant mask m(·), self-supervised temporal loss, environment partitioning, and gradient variance penalty improve over a strong supervised temporal model; if the preprocessing differs from Albaqami, the 87.62% patient-wise F1 should be cited only as context, not as a direct target. A further action is to incorporate temporal attention or Bi-LSTM modeling into the encoder φ(Xt) or the supervised head hT, because attention improved Bi-LSTM by more than 10 percentage points in seizure-wise evaluation, and to add ablations for segment length, sampling rate, and frequency features, including wavelet or time-frequency alternatives, since wavelet-based features were reported important and the older review by [(Kim et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c088f7692baed52a3403d826425ae090b35fafab) highlights FFT, STFT, autoregressive, and wavelet feature choices. Finally, the plan should improve reproducibility by specifying class counts, label filtering, random seeds, early stopping, learning-rate schedule, batch size, hardware/software versions, and ablations for the number of K-means patient environments, top-k graph neighbors, dropout, and penalty weight, while also adding a regularization or diagnostic to verify that κ(Xt) and ψ(Xt) are meaningfully separated rather than merely elementwise masked.

## Context: To enforce invariance across patient populations. Method: We will partition patients into different groups/environments using clustering methods (such as K-means) to create distinguishable environments, then apply invariant risk minimization across these environments.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed patient-grouping step is that [(Kabir et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83cd22703663e87d91592000f967175d419c0254) demonstrates that Lloyd's K-means can be applied to epileptic EEG time-series segments and that the resulting K=4 clusters can be converted into statistical features for downstream seizure-related classification. This is directly relevant to the proposed method's use of clustering to create distinguishable groups, because it shows K-means is a workable grouping operator on EEG data rather than an abstract choice. A weaker but still useful support is the paper's concrete EEG benchmark setup, with five sets of 100 single-channel 23.6-second signals sampled at 173.61 Hz, band-pass filtered at 0.53 to 40 Hz, and two-class evaluation such as Set A versus Set E, which indicates that EEG segmentation, feature extraction, and classifier evaluation can be operationalized in a reproducible pipeline. The weakest support is only methodological: the paper lists accuracy, sensitivity, false positive rate, precision, F-measure, kappa, ROC AUC, and mean absolute error as evaluation metrics, but the excerpt does not provide numerical values, so it cannot support the expected performance of clustering plus invariant risk minimization.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Kabir et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83cd22703663e87d91592000f967175d419c0254) does not partition patients into environments and does not apply invariant risk minimization, so it provides no direct evidence that K-means-derived groups can serve as IRM environments for cross-patient seizure classification. A further contradiction is that its K=4 clusters are formed over EEG segments within classes, not over patients, and use random partition initialization; therefore the clusters are not shown to correspond to patient-level distribution shifts, epileptogenic zones, or population-level covariates that the proposed ST-IRM framework requires. Another important contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes: the excerpt only states that Table 1 displays results for Case I, Set A versus Set E, without reporting accuracy, F1, ROC AUC, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance, so the method cannot be judged as effective. Additional mismatches weaken the evidence: the study is single-channel, two-class, and based on a public benchmark rather than the proposed TUSZ v1.5.2 multi-patient, 19-electrode, eight-seizure-type, cross-patient disjoint setup, and it uses default WEKA classifiers and LIBSVM 3.2 rather than a spatiotemporal invariant learning model.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the clustering step patient-level and explicitly validate that the resulting groups are distinguishable, stable, and non-trivial before applying IRM; this should go beyond the segment-level K=4 precedent in [(Kabir et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83cd22703663e87d91592000f967175d419c0254) by clustering patient embeddings or patient-level EEG summaries from TUSZ, reporting silhouette scores, cluster stability across restarts, and overlap with clinical or anatomical metadata. A secondary action is to use a more robust K-means implementation, such as k-means++ initialization and multiple restarts, and to ablate the number of patient groups rather than relying on an empirically chosen K=4, because the related work's random initialization and fixed K are insufficient for the proposed environment-based invariant learning. A further action is to couple the clustering output directly to the IRM objective and gradient variance penalty, then evaluate under the planned cross-patient disjoint split with weighted F1, precision, recall, and comparisons to DenseCNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and GNN baselines, so that the method is tested on the actual cross-patient problem rather than only on a two-class segment-level benchmark.

## Context: To ensure invariant representations are predictive of seizure type. Method: We will incorporate supervised signals to ensure preserved invariant information can predict seizure types using: Lsup = (1/|n|) Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ L(hT(m(φ(XᵢT))), yi).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed supervised loss Lsup = (1/|n|) sum L(hT(m(phi(XiT))), yi) comes from [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), which trained MP-SeizNet, a CNN plus attention-based Bi-LSTM classifier, with cross-entropy on TUSZ v1.5.2 for specific seizure-type classification and evaluated patient-wise with 3-fold cross-validation using disjoint patient sets, achieving a weighted F1 of 87.62 percent and seizure-wise F1 of 98.01 percent with 95 percent confidence intervals. This directly supports the plan's use of a supervised head on EEG-derived spatiotemporal features for cross-patient seizure-type classification and provides the closest baseline, dataset, metric, and disjoint-patient protocol for the proposed method. A second line of support is [(Mirowski et al., 2009-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f94ed69179beb00b7d759b75f37e3b7f8d0fc937), which used patient-specific supervised classification of bivariate synchronization features from six intracranial EEG channels, including wavelet coherence, phase-locking synchrony, and Lyapunov-based features, and achieved zero-false-alarm prediction in 15 of 21 patients with wavelet coherence and convolutional networks, 13 of 21 with phase-locking synchrony, and at least one feature-classifier combination for every patient. Its L1 regularization and post-hoc selection of only 3 or 4 channel pairs or a subset of high-frequency bands support the idea that a supervised head can be made predictive from a sparse, selected spatiotemporal representation, which is conceptually related to the proposed mask m(phi(X)). Third, [(Zhong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7389f76fc63767154fa213fc436a13b47de00e7b) supports the value of supervised classification over spatiotemporal EEG features and feature selection, reporting 98.01 percent accuracy, 0.96 AUC, and 3.83 percent false positive rate on Kaggle iEEG and 95.93 percent accuracy, 0.92 AUC, and 0.0473 per hour false positive rate on CHB-MIT, while showing that spatial-only features were weaker than combined spatiotemporal features. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Shen et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba96490735a090faf66c59dfe5a1ec0b2334feb6), which used a GA-SVM supervised pipeline on 1700 handcrafted EEG features and achieved 83.02 percent spike and 99.77 percent seizure accuracy on Data Set I, with 91.18 percent spike and 99.22 percent seizure accuracy on a smaller holdout Data Set II, supporting supervised feature-based seizure-related detection and feature selection, although it is less directly aligned with cross-patient seizure-type classification.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) achieved patient-wise weighted F1 of 87.62 percent on TUSZ v1.5.2 without an invariant mask, invariant risk minimization, self-supervised temporal loss, or patient-environment partitioning, which undermines the assumption that Lsup must be applied to a masked invariant representation to obtain cross-patient seizure-type performance. It also reports seizure-wise F1 of 98.01 percent when the same patient can appear in training, validation, and test folds, warning that high apparent performance may come from patient-specific information rather than invariant seizure-type components, so the proposed method must demonstrate gains over this standard supervised baseline under truly disjoint patients. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Mirowski et al., 2009-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f94ed69179beb00b7d759b75f37e3b7f8d0fc937) obtained its best zero-false-alarm results through patient-specific training, handcrafted bivariate synchronization features, and post-hoc L1-based feature selection, not through a single invariant mask trained across patient environments; this suggests that the predictive components may be patient-specific or that post-hoc adaptation is essential, which contradicts the proposed method's reliance on a shared invariant mask m(phi(X)) for cross-patient prediction. A further contradiction is that [(Shen et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba96490735a090faf66c59dfe5a1ec0b2334feb6) provides only limited evidence of cross-dataset generalization, with Data Set II containing only 5 inpatients, no reported statistical significance or confidence intervals, class imbalance, and a spike/seizure detection task rather than multi-class seizure-type classification; its seizure accuracy dropped from 99.77 percent to 99.22 percent and spike accuracy was 91.18 percent on the holdout set, indicating that supervised handcrafted features may not robustly generalize across patient groups without explicit invariance controls. The weakest contradiction is that [(Zhong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7389f76fc63767154fa213fc436a13b47de00e7b) is subject-specific binary preictal/interictal prediction rather than cross-patient seizure-type classification, uses no invariant mask or environment partition, and reports high subject-specific accuracy, 98.01 percent on Kaggle iEEG and 95.93 percent on CHB-MIT, which may overstate generalizability and does not support the proposed cross-patient invariant supervised head.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to benchmark the proposed Lsup on m(phi(X)) against Lsup on unmasked phi(X) and against the MP-SeizNet or CNN/Bi-LSTM supervised baseline from [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) on TUSZ v1.5.2 using the same patient-wise 3-fold disjoint-patient protocol, weighted F1 as the primary metric, 95 percent confidence intervals, per-class precision and recall, confusion matrices, and sensitivity/specificity, to show whether the invariant mask improves cross-patient seizure-type classification beyond standard supervised learning. The second most important action is to align preprocessing and evaluation with the most relevant TUSZ seizure-type setup, including deciding whether to use 2-second segments as in [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) or the plan's 60-second windows with 1-second FFT intervals, specifying the 19-electrode montage, handling class imbalance, excluding or separately reporting non-specific and myoclonic classes, and reporting hardware, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, batch size, early stopping, and seeds for reproducibility. Third, the mask should be validated using the feature-selection evidence from [(Mirowski et al., 2009-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f94ed69179beb00b7d759b75f37e3b7f8d0fc937) and [(Zhong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7389f76fc63767154fa213fc436a13b47de00e7b) by adding sparsity or entropy penalties, L1 regularization, channel and frequency-band ablations, high-frequency synchronization checks, and independence-plus-information feature scores to demonstrate that m(phi(X)) selects stable, predictive spatiotemporal components rather than patient-specific artifacts. Fourth, the environment-based invariant risk minimization component should be strengthened by ensuring K-means patient groups are not confounded with seizure type, reporting the number of patient groups, ablating the gradient variance penalty, and comparing against standard supervised, adversarial, and domain-adaptation baselines. The least important but still necessary action is to add statistical rigor and clinical interpretability, including multiple random seeds, significance tests, false positive rate, per-patient performance, and explicit reporting of computational resources and hyperparameters, so that any claimed advantage of the invariant supervised head is not attributable to leakage, class imbalance, or tuning, and to avoid the limited statistical reporting observed in [(Shen et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba96490735a090faf66c59dfe5a1ec0b2334feb6).

## Context: To provide a large multi-patient EEG benchmark. Method: We will conduct experiments on the Temple University Hospital EEG Seizure Corpus (TUSZ) dataset version v1.5.2, which contains 5,612 EEG signals and 3,050 annotated seizure events from over 300 patients, covering eight seizure types recorded using 19 electrodes from the standard 10-20 system.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed TUSZ v1.5.2 benchmark comes from [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), which uses the same 5,612-EEG, 3,050-seizure, 19-channel TUSZ v1.5.2 corpus, constructs frequency-domain inputs from log-amplitudes of FFTs, enforces patient-disjoint train/validation/test sets, and evaluates seizure detection/classification with AUROC and weighted F1 while comparing Dense-CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and DCRNN baselines; its finding that self-supervised pre-training improves rare-class classification and that frequency-domain inputs outperform time-domain inputs directly supports the plan's 200 Hz resampling, 60-second windows, 1-second FFT clips, disjoint patient splits, weighted F1, and baseline list. The next strongest support is [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), which also uses TUSZ v1.5.2 with more than 300 patients and more than 3,000 seizure events, reports weighted F1 under patient-wise 3-fold cross-validation, and shows MP-SeizNet achieving 87.62% patient-wise weighted F1, validating the proposed metric and cross-patient evaluation, although it uses 20 channels, 250 Hz, and 2-second segments. [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) further supports the benchmark by using TUSZ v1.5.2 with 3,050 annotated seizures, patient-wise 3-fold cross-validation, weighted F1, and FFT-based saliency spectrograms, reporting roughly 3 percentage-point patient-wise weighted F1 gains over prior methods, which supports frequency-domain multi-spectral features and ensemble baselines. Older work provides weaker but still relevant support: [(Thodoroff et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6622bdc1aed1f4d5719ca6cab80927e373c4f183) demonstrates cross-patient EEG seizure detection with transfer learning, negative subsampling from about 1000:1 to 80:20, probability rescaling, and a three-model ensemble, supporting the plan's concern with limited positives and patient variability, though it is binary CHB-MIT detection rather than TUSZ eight-way classification. [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630) supports the feasibility of 19-channel EEG seizure-type CNN baselines, with GoogLeNet transfer learning at 82.85% accuracy and InceptionV3 feature extraction with SVM at 88.30% accuracy, but its 70/30 split and accuracy metric are less aligned. [(Saputro et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9a19e288edd06d809cdd51a2c8783cba526bae) provides a classical same-corpus reference using Hjorth, MFCC, ICA features and SVM, with best 91.4% accuracy for MFCC plus Hjorth, useful as a non-deep baseline. The weakest support is [(Du et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/975a6b01942f3a5dccc948b1b3f91973086f2adc), whose MoE/expert and imbalanced-sampler structure is conceptually relevant to rare seizure types, but the excerpt lacks dataset, quantitative, and statistical evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that prior TUSZ v1.5.2 studies do not validate the proposed eight-way, 1-second-clip, fixed 179/22/34-patient protocol as a directly comparable benchmark. [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) excluded Myoclonic seizures because only three examples were available and evaluated seven selected types under patient-wise 3-fold cross-validation, while [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) excluded nonspecific labels and myoclonic seizures because nonspecific labels are not fully disjoint and myoclonic cases are extremely rare; this contradicts the assumption that all eight TUSZ seizure types can be treated as equally learnable in a cross-patient weighted-F1 benchmark. [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) further complicates the eight-way claim by refining the scheme to four classes, combined focal, generalized non-specific, absence, and combined tonic, because focal non-specific, simple partial, and complex partial seizures were not distinguishable by EEG alone and tonic/tonic-clonic seizures were rare, and it used 12-second or 60-second clips rather than 1-second intervals. The second strongest contradiction is preprocessing and split mismatch: [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) used 20 transverse central parietal channels, 250 Hz resampling, and 2-second non-overlapping segments, and [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) used patient-wise 3-fold cross-validation rather than the proposed fixed 179/22/34 patient split, so the proposed 19-electrode, 200 Hz, 60-second-window, 1-second-FFT, 1,925/450/521-clip design is not directly anchored by these results. [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) also used the official TUSZ train/test patient split with five overlapping patients removed, not the proposed 179/22/34 split, and reported AUROC, TPR at fixed FPR, and class accuracy rather than full weighted F1 in the provided excerpt. Weaker contradictions arise from non-TUSZ or non-cross-patient evidence: [(Thodoroff et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6622bdc1aed1f4d5719ca6cab80927e373c4f183) is binary CHB-MIT detection with no reported quantitative outcomes, so it cannot support eight-way TUSZ classification; [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630) uses a 70/30 split and accuracy without patient-disjoint validation or weighted F1; [(Saputro et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9a19e288edd06d809cdd51a2c8783cba526bae) does not specify TUSZ version, patient-disjoint split, or weighted F1; and [(Du et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/975a6b01942f3a5dccc948b1b3f91973086f2adc) provides no quantitative outcomes, dataset confirmation, or statistical significance, so it cannot substantiate the proposed benchmark or ST-IRM claims.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the TUSZ v1.5.2 class scheme and rare-class handling explicit and comparable: retain the proposed eight seizure types only with per-class counts, confidence intervals, and sensitivity analyses that either exclude Myoclonic seizures, merge ambiguous focal/non-specific/tonic classes, or use the four-class scheme from [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), because [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) show that myoclonic and nonspecific labels are problematic in cross-patient weighted-F1 evaluation. The next most important action is to validate the fixed 179/22/34 patient split and 1,925/450/521 clip split against standard TUSZ protocols by also reporting patient-wise 3-fold and seizure-wise 5-fold results, as done in [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), and by documenting patient-overlap checks similar to [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6). The method should include the strongest directly comparable baselines: Tang's distance/correlation DCRNN with and without self-supervised pre-training, Asif's SeizureNet saliency-spectrogram ensemble, Albaqami's MP-SeizNet CNN/attention Bi-LSTM, Raghu's transfer-learning and feature-extraction CNNs, Saputro's SVM feature baseline, and Thodoroff's ensemble/transfer-learning strategy, all evaluated under the same 19-channel, 200 Hz, 60-second-window, 1-second-FFT, patient-disjoint, weighted-F1 setup. Because the proposed 1-second FFT clips are not directly supported by prior TUSZ results, the plan should ablate clip granularity against 2-second segments from [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), 12-second/60-second clips from [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), and spectrogram/saliency encodings from [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630). The plan should also report statistical significance, 95% confidence intervals, and class-wise precision/recall, following the confidence-interval reporting in [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), and should address class imbalance using negative subsampling/probability rescaling or ensemble averaging as in [(Thodoroff et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6622bdc1aed1f4d5719ca6cab80927e373c4f183), while considering expert/MoE allocation for rare classes inspired by [(Du et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/975a6b01942f3a5dccc948b1b3f91973086f2adc). Finally, the ST-IRM components should be ablated separately from the benchmark: self-supervised temporal consistency, invariant mask, K-means patient environments, gradient variance penalty, and top-k/diffusion-step hyperparameters, with comparisons to the DCRNN and CNN/LSTM baselines named in [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) to show that gains are due to invariant learning rather than dataset or preprocessing choices, and the authors should document hardware, optimizer, batch size, and hyperparameters because several related works omit these reproducibility details.

## Context: To prepare TUSZ EEG for spatiotemporal invariant learning. Method: We will transform raw EEG signals into the frequency domain, resample recordings to 200Hz, and segment them into non-overlapped 60-second windows. Each clip will be further segmented into 1-second intervals, with Fast Fourier Transform applied to obtain logarithmic amplitudes of non-negative frequency components.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307), which used frequency-domain EEG with STFT, 1-second windows, 50% overlap, ICA artifact removal, and ConvLSTM on Temple University Hospital data (1,185 sessions, 592 patients, 2,370 seizures) and evaluated on unseen Royal Prince Alfred Hospital data (192 patients, 14,590 h, 536 seizures), reporting AUC 0.82, sensitivity 76.68%, and 56.22 false alarms/24 h; this directly supports the plan's premise that spectrotemporal frequency representations can generalize across sites and that 1-second spectral units are clinically useful. The next strongest support is [(Pale et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9395220b698424b62eaa0a58783d47f1f4dc132), which on CHB-MIT (982.9 h, 256 Hz, 24 subjects, 183 seizures) showed that fixed windowing, full temporal data, and episode/duration metrics materially affect false positives, precision, and sensitivity, supporting the plan's fixed 60-second clips and patient-disjoint evaluation as a more realistic alternative to random recording folds. [(Craley et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cba777745196c164112f76e6c70de585c873b8f) supports the exact 200 Hz sampling and 1-second log-amplitude spectral features, using 200 Hz EEG, 1-second windows with 250 ms overlap, log-summed STFT magnitudes in theta/delta/alpha/beta, and log line length for binary seizure detection on 90 recordings from 15 patients. [(Cooman et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e922f3d84e0756062c12cb79c4cd17a10e9e16c) supports the invariant/variant motivation by showing that a patient-independent model had 75% sensitivity and 3.0 false positives/h, while transfer-learning personalization reduced false positives to 1.9/h (p<0.001) with non-significant sensitivity loss (p=0.29) on 24 patients and 2,172 h, suggesting that separating stable seizure signals from patient-specific variation is valuable. Weaker support comes from [(Siddiqui et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec07a9b3a6641d2d1b38afb0ab79c4c4b25d20a5), which reports that frequency-domain features such as spectral power, entropy, peak/median/edge frequency, and total power are useful with shallow classifiers and F-measure for imbalanced data, and from [(Delorme, and Makeig, 2004-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76ad159a2887b008e5a7335d124c148c13e65465), which provides the foundational ICA/spectral decomposition context for separating neural and artifactual components, although it reports no classification performance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307) achieved its best reported cross-site detection with 12-second STFT segments, 250-sample windows described as 1 second, 50% overlap, 23 channels, and approximately 250 Hz, and reported a 6% average AUC improvement over a comparable prior method while using 12-second windows instead of 60-second windows; this challenges the proposed non-overlapped 60-second clip length and 200 Hz/1-second FFT design, especially because the proposed plan does not ablate window length, overlap, or sampling rate. The next strongest contradiction is [(Pale et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9395220b698424b62eaa0a58783d47f1f4dc132), which found that 4-second windows with step sizes from 0.5 to 4 seconds changed false-positive count, false-positive proportion, precision, and sensitivity, and that larger steps reduced false positives but decreased duration-level sensitivity, implying that the proposed 60-second non-overlapped clips with 1-second FFT intervals may either miss short ictal dynamics or create overly coarse labels unless window/step ablations are performed. [(Craley et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cba777745196c164112f76e6c70de585c873b8f) contradicts the assumed robustness of simple log-spectral features by showing that a coupled HMM transition prior improved contiguous seizure confidence but reduced specificity by associating post-seizure spectral artifacts with seizure, and that polynomial-kernel SVM performed poorly on noisy clinical EEG; this warns that the proposed FFT amplitudes may encode post-ictal and artifact variance that ST-IRM must explicitly suppress. [(Cooman et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e922f3d84e0756062c12cb79c4cd17a10e9e16c) also contradicts a purely patient-independent invariant model by showing that patient-specific adaptation was needed to reduce false detections, although its ECG heart-rate features and binary detection task are not direct evidence against EEG seizure-type classification. Weaker contradictions include [(Siddiqui et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec07a9b3a6641d2d1b38afb0ab79c4c4b25d20a5), whose reported 100% accuracies with decision forests and nine statistical features lack confidence intervals, p-values, and cross-patient TUSZ validation, making them insufficient to justify that shallow frequency features alone would suffice, and [(Delorme, and Makeig, 2004-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76ad159a2887b008e5a7335d124c148c13e65465), which provides no quantitative evidence that ICA-based spectral components improve cross-patient seizure-type classification and whose ICA assumptions (temporal independence, spatial stationarity, non-Gaussian sources) may not hold for 60-second TUSZ clips.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a windowing and segmentation ablation before finalizing the 60-second/1-second FFT pipeline, testing 60 s non-overlapped clips against 12 s, 30 s, and overlapped variants, and 1 s FFT intervals against 0.5 s, 2 s, and 4 s windows/steps, while reporting weighted F1, class-wise precision/recall, episode-level and duration-level sensitivity, false alarms per hour/day, and confidence intervals, because [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307) and [(Pale et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9395220b698424b62eaa0a58783d47f1f4dc132) show that window length, overlap, and step size materially affect generalization and false positives. Next, insert explicit artifact and line-noise handling before FFT, such as 60 Hz notch or adaptive filtering, bandpass filtering, and ICA or spatial source separation to remove eye/movement artifacts, and define a stable log-amplitude epsilon and per-band normalization for the 0-100 Hz non-negative FFT bins, because [(Craley et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cba777745196c164112f76e6c70de585c873b8f) used 1.6-50 Hz bandpass and 60 Hz notch, [(Yang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/314efc797c888a676381849c08d07509c870c307) used ICA with 19 components and FP1/FP2 correlation to remove eye artifacts, and [(Delorme, and Makeig, 2004-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76ad159a2887b008e5a7335d124c148c13e65465) demonstrates ICA scalp-map/spectral artifact identification. Third, strengthen the cross-patient evaluation by keeping patient-disjoint splits (179/22/34) but also reporting leave-one-patient-out or temporal CV results, and avoid random recording-level folds, because [(Craley et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cba777745196c164112f76e6c70de585c873b8f) used random 5-fold recording assignment and [(Pale et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9395220b698424b62eaa0a58783d47f1f4dc132) showed non-temporal or future-data training can overestimate performance. Fourth, incorporate patient-specific adaptation or environment-aware baselines, such as transfer learning, domain adaptation, or patient-specific fine-tuning, to test whether ST-IRM's invariant mask outperforms simpler personalization, motivated by [(Cooman et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e922f3d84e0756062c12cb79c4cd17a10e9e16c), where transfer learning reduced false positives from 3.0 to 1.9/h with p<0.001. Fifth, add handcrafted spectral-feature baselines using spectral power, entropy, peak/median/edge frequency, and total power with SVM, random forest, or logistic regression, because [(Siddiqui et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec07a9b3a6641d2d1b38afb0ab79c4c4b25d20a5) shows these features can be competitive and interpretable, and this will make the deep ST-IRM comparison more credible. Finally, report full reproducibility details missing in much of the literature, including software versions, random seeds, GPU/CPU specs, batch size 40, cosine annealing schedule, hyperparameter search ranges, and statistical significance tests, so that the proposed RTX 3090/Xeon Gold 6248R experiments can be compared against baselines such as DenseCNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, MSTGCN, Dist-DCRNN, Corr-DCRNN, NeuroGNN, and PANN-DCRNN.

## Context: To evaluate generalization across unseen patients. Method: We will divide the dataset ensuring patient sets are disjoint between training, validation, and test sets. The training set will contain clips from 179 patients, validation from 22 patients, and test from 34 patients, with 1,925, 450, and 521 clips respectively.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed patient-disjoint evaluation is that [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa) trained a seizure detector on SeizeIT1 from 42 patients and tested on SeizeIT2 from 39 unseen patients, showing that disjoint patient cohorts can be used to assess generalization in a clinically relevant EEG setting; their best automatically corrected behind-the-ear model reached 87.5 percent sensitivity and 1.93 false alarms per hour on 3199.9 hours of 250 Hz EEG using 2-second 50 percent overlap windows, SVM classification, and 8-of-10 post-processing. A second supporting point is that the same paper reported highly variable patient-level sensitivity, including 100 percent for some patients and 0 percent for patient 15 whose only seizure was short and low amplitude, which supports the research plan motivation that patient-specific EEG variation can undermine cross-patient seizure classification and motivates invariant spatiotemporal representations. The third, weaker support comes from the newer May 2022 TUSZ study [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893), which used TUSZ v1.5.2 with 3050 seizure events, 250 to 512 Hz EDF recordings, 10-20 zones, and short-time Fourier transform features from 2-second 50 percent overlap windows, demonstrating that frequency-domain TUSZ features are suitable for seizure classification; however, its fivefold cross-validation is less directly supportive of the proposed 179 training, 22 validation, and 34 test patient split because it does not enforce patient disjointness.

### Contradictions

The most direct contradiction is that the proposed preprocessing uses non-overlapped 60-second windows and 1-second intervals, whereas [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa) showed that even with 2-second 50 percent overlap windows and post-processing, a patient with only a short, low-amplitude seizure had 0 percent sensitivity, indicating that coarse non-overlapping 60-second clips and aggregate weighted F1 may miss short or low-amplitude seizure types and obscure per-patient failures. A second, and temporally newer, contradiction is that [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893) found amplitude statistics differed substantially across the three TUSZ electrode references while time and frequency correlation statistics remained broadly similar, and therefore classified separately under each reference; the proposed ST-IRM plan does not specify reference normalization, reference-specific environments, or reference-stratified evaluation, so reference-induced amplitude shifts could be mistaken for patient-specific variation or invariant seizure signal. A third, weaker contradiction is that [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa) required retraining after reducing three behind-the-ear training channels to two test channels, and [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893) notes variable channel counts in TUSZ, whereas the proposed method assumes 19 standard 10-20 electrodes without describing missing-channel handling, channel dropout, or hemisphere-specific channel selection. Finally, the related work does not provide numerical evidence that a multi-class TUSZ seizure-type classifier will generalize under the exact proposed split; [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893) used fivefold cross-validation and reported no numerical classification performance, so the proposed cross-patient advantage remains plausible but not yet validated by these works.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, retain the patient-disjoint 179 training, 22 validation, and 34 test split but add patient-level and event-level evaluation, including per-patient sensitivity, false alarms per hour, macro and weighted F1, confusion matrices, and sensitivity for short or low-amplitude seizures, because [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa) showed aggregate performance can hide 0 percent patient sensitivity; also test overlapping or shorter windows, such as 2-second 50 percent overlap or 1-second windows with overlap, inside the 60-second clips to reduce missed transient seizures. Second, explicitly control TUSZ electrode-reference and channel variability by normalizing amplitude per reference, including reference as an environment or covariate in ST-IRM, or evaluating separately per reference as in [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893), and by standardizing the 19-electrode layout, imputing or masking missing channels, and using channel dropout to address variable channel counts. Third, use clinician report keywords or clinical metadata to validate or constrain the K-means patient environments, aligning frequency bands and 10-20 zones with the feature mapping in [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893), so that invariant risk minimization is not driven by arbitrary cluster artifacts. Fourth, run all baselines, including DenseCNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and the listed GNN methods, under the identical patient-disjoint split, preprocessing, and RTX 3090 hardware, reporting hyperparameters, batch size 40, cosine annealing, and statistical significance or confidence intervals; use the reported RF, SVM, and MLP settings from [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893) as starting points if classical classifiers are included. Finally, ablate window length and overlap, number of patient groups, top-k neighbors, and penalty weights, with special attention to short-seizure sensitivity and reference or channel perturbations, and verify that resampling to 200 Hz does not remove relevant spectral content relative to the 250 Hz recordings in [(Zhang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff96e777632094d811afb0df79d16bacfb1665aa) and the 250 to 512 Hz recordings in [(Wang, and Mengoni, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c38bf4884b4d6b080e50aeadc5be92f10562893).

## Context: To benchmark cross-patient seizure classification performance. Method: We will compare our method against several baseline approaches including CNN-based methods (DenseCNN), RNN-based methods (LSTM), hybrid approaches (CNN-LSTM), and GNN-based methods (MSTGCN, Dist-DCRNN, Corr-DCRNN, NeuroGNN, PANN-DCRNN).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed baseline comparison comes from the two Tang DCRNN reports, [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) and [(Tang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c37eeef1a68434fcea64a974e15d509f9db014a), which evaluate Dense-CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, Dist-DCRNN, and Corr-DCRNN on TUSZ v1.5.2 with 5,612 EEGs, 3,050 seizures, 19 channels, log-amplitude FFT features, non-overlapping 12-s and 60-s clips, patient-disjoint train/validation/test construction, and weighted F1 or AUROC evaluation. Their results show that Dist-DCRNN and Corr-DCRNN are competitive with or better than the CNN/RNN baselines, and that self-supervised pre-training improves performance, for example pre-trained Dist-DCRNN reaches AUROC 0.875 on 60-s detection and 84.3% TPR at 25% FPR on 12-s detection versus 72.8% for Dense-CNN, 67.0% for LSTM, and 62.5% for CNN-LSTM, directly supporting the proposed inclusion of these baselines and the proposed self-supervised component. A second strong support is [(Tang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e475c5fda03556a2f09a7c6c11dfb6398c73b30), which uses the same TUSZ v1.5.2 60-s, 19-sensor, 200-Hz clip format and compares a graph/S4 model against Dist-DCRNN, supporting the relevance of graph-based baselines and the proposed 60-s frequency-domain protocol, although it targets binary detection rather than seizure-type classification. Next, [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) supports the hybrid CNN/RNN baseline category and patient-wise weighted F1 evaluation on TUSZ v1.5.2, reporting 87.62% patient-wise weighted F1 for MP-SeizNet and 98.01% seizure-wise weighted F1, with 95% confidence intervals from 20 randomized tests. Then, [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) supports the CNN baseline and patient-disjoint weighted F1 metric, showing SeizureNet improved patient-wise weighted F1 by about 3 points and seizure-wise weighted F1 by about 4 points on TUH-EEGSC v1.5.2 under 3-fold patient-wise cross-validation. The weakest support is [(O'Shea et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92988cf1e5b6bf7d876a0a0151211ea60dbb0746), which demonstrates cross-patient and cross-dataset generalization for FCN seizure detection on neonatal EEG, but uses AUC/AUC90, binary detection, 8-s windows, 32-Hz inputs, and a different population, so it supports the cross-patient motivation more than the exact TUSZ classification benchmark.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the Tang DCRNN classification results are based on a refined four-class scheme, not the eight TUSZ seizure types named in the research plan, because myoclonic seizures were excluded due to only three examples and tonic/tonic-clonic seizures were combined; [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) and [(Tang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c37eeef1a68434fcea64a974e15d509f9db014a) also report substantial label noise, with manual review of 32 misclassified generalized non-specific test seizures finding 27 were actually focal seizures mislabeled as generalized, which contradicts the assumption that an eight-type weighted F1 benchmark will be stable without class merging, label auditing, or rare-class handling. The same Tang works also show that self-supervised pre-trained Dist-DCRNN is already a strong baseline, with 74% accuracy on rare combined tonic seizures, 47 points above Dense-CNN and 48 points above non-pretrained Dist-DCRNN, so the proposed ST-IRM method must be compared against self-supervised pre-trained GNNs rather than only from-scratch baselines. A second important contradiction is that [(Tang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e475c5fda03556a2f09a7c6c11dfb6398c73b30) reports GraphS4mer outperforming pre-trained Dist-DCRNN by 3.1 AUROC points on TUSZ v1.5.2 60-s seizure detection, suggesting that Dist-DCRNN may not be the strongest available graph baseline and that the proposed baseline list should include dynamic graph or S4-style models if the goal is a state-of-the-art GNN comparison. Next, [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) uses a different TUSZ protocol, with 20-channel TCP montage, 250-Hz resampling, 2-s non-overlapping segments, exclusion of nonspecific focal/generalized labels and myoclonic seizures, and 3-fold patient-wise cross-validation, so its 87.62% patient-wise weighted F1 cannot be directly compared with the proposed 19-channel, 200-Hz, 60-s, eight-type, fixed 179/22/34 split without retraining or explicit protocol separation. Then, [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) provides only relative weighted F1 improvements, not absolute values, per-class metrics, confidence intervals, or significance tests, and uses 3-fold patient-wise cross-validation rather than the proposed fixed patient split, limiting its direct evidentiary value. The weakest contradiction is [(O'Shea et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92988cf1e5b6bf7d876a0a0151211ea60dbb0746), which evaluates AUC/AUC90 on neonatal binary detection rather than weighted F1 on TUSZ multi-class seizure-type classification, so it does not directly validate the proposed metric or class scheme.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to align the proposed eight-type TUSZ classification protocol with the evidence from [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) and [(Tang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c37eeef1a68434fcea64a974e15d509f9db014a) by reporting per-patient and per-class counts, explicitly excluding or merging extremely rare classes such as myoclonic and tonic/tonic-clonic if needed, and adding a label-noise audit or robust loss for confusable classes such as focal versus generalized non-specific. Second, the baseline suite should include self-supervised pre-trained Dist-DCRNN and Corr-DCRNN, as in the Tang works, and should add a stronger graph/S4 or dynamic-graph baseline such as GraphS4mer from [(Tang et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e475c5fda03556a2f09a7c6c11dfb6398c73b30) or an equivalent, because pre-trained Dist-DCRNN is already strong and may be outperformed by newer graph models; because MSTGCN, NeuroGNN, and PANN-DCRNN are not directly evaluated in the supplied related work, their implementations, training regimes, and hyperparameters should be documented to avoid unsupported comparisons. Third, the evaluation should standardize the cross-patient protocol: keep the proposed 179/22/34 patient-disjoint split but also report 3-fold patient-wise cross-validation or multiple random patient splits, with at least three seeds, 95% confidence intervals, and statistical significance tests, following the reporting style of [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) and the patient-wise validation in [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f). Fourth, all baselines should be retrained under the proposed 19-channel, 200-Hz, 60-s, 1-s FFT log-amplitude protocol rather than comparing to numbers from different preprocessing such as the 20-channel, 250-Hz, 2-s protocol in [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a); if external numbers are cited, they should be clearly separated from in-protocol baselines. Fifth, the metric suite should retain weighted F1 as primary but add per-class precision, recall, F1, macro F1, AUROC/AUPRC where applicable, and rare-class accuracy, because the Tang works show that aggregate weighted F1 can mask rare-class and label-noise effects. Sixth, the ST-IRM-specific ablations should directly test the value of invariant risk minimization over the strongest evidenced baselines by ablating the number of K-means patient environments, top-k correlation neighbors, maximum diffusion steps, dropout, penalty weight, and self-supervised loss, and by reporting hardware, batch size 40, cosine annealing schedule, and training epochs to make the proposed RTX 3090 setup reproducible. A final, lower-priority action is to add a mismatched patient-group or cross-dataset sanity check, inspired by [(O'Shea et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92988cf1e5b6bf7d876a0a0151211ea60dbb0746), to test whether invariant representations reduce performance drops under stronger distribution shift.

## Context: To quantify classification performance across seizure types. Method: We will use weighted F1-score as the primary evaluation metric, along with precision and recall, to measure classification performance across different seizure types.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation method comes from the most recent and most directly comparable work, [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a), which evaluated a multi-path seizure-type classifier on TUSZ v1.5.2, the same dataset version targeted by the research plan, and explicitly used weighted F1-score as the primary metric because of class imbalance. In patient-wise three-fold cross-validation with disjoint patient sets, the model achieved a weighted F1-score of 87.62%, while seizure-wise five-fold cross-validation reached 98.01%; the paper also reported confusion-matrix-derived sensitivity and positive predictive value, which correspond closely to the proposed recall and precision, and provided 95% confidence intervals from 20 randomized tests, e.g., F1 0.976 with CI 0.975-0.977 and F1 0.963 with CI 0.961-0.965. This directly supports the plan's decision to use weighted F1-score, precision, and recall under a disjoint patient split of 179 training, 22 validation, and 34 test patients. A second, older but still relevant line of evidence is [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), which evaluated SeizureNet on TUH-EEGSC v1.5.2 with 3050 seizures and used weighted F1-score under both seizure-level and patient-level cross-validation, reporting approximately 3 percentage-point patient-wise weighted F1 improvement and approximately 4 percentage-point seizure-wise improvement on v1.4.0. This supports the metric choice in the same corpus family and shows that patient-wise weighted F1 was already being treated as a meaningful cross-patient measure before the 2022 work, although it did not report absolute values, per-class precision/recall, or significance. The weakest support is from [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630), which used STFT spectrograms, transfer-learning CNNs, and SVM feature extraction on 19-channel EEG with a 70/30 split and 10 repeated runs, but reported only overall accuracy, with a best 88.30% from InceptionV3 features and 82.85% from GoogLeNet transfer learning. Although it does not validate weighted F1 directly, its lack of per-class metrics and its use of sample balancing make it useful as a contrast: it shows why the proposed plan should not rely on accuracy alone and should report precision and recall for individual seizure types.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is not with weighted F1 itself but with applying it unchanged to all seizure types in the proposed plan. Both [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) and [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) excluded myoclonic seizures, and Asif explicitly noted only three myoclonic examples in v1.5.2, while Albaqami also excluded nonspecific labels. If the plan evaluates eight seizure types without excluding or separately reporting myoclonic, weighted F1-score can be dominated by more frequent classes and the precision/recall for myoclonic may be statistically unstable, so the primary metric could overstate clinically useful generalization. This is a direct methodological contradiction to a naive all-class weighted F1 evaluation. A second, weaker contradiction is that the older work did not provide the statistical evidence needed to justify metric differences: [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) reported only approximate percentage-point improvements without absolute weighted F1 values, per-class precision/recall, or significance tests, and [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630) reported only average accuracy over 10 runs without confidence intervals, patient-disjoint splits, or per-class metrics. The newer [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) work partially corrects this by adding confidence intervals and confusion-matrix measures, but it still does not explicitly name precision and recall as separate metrics, so the proposed plan's full metric set is not fully validated by the literature. The weakest contradiction is that [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630) achieved high overall accuracy, up to 88.30%, after balancing training samples with an overlap technique, which could be misread as evidence that accuracy is sufficient; however, because it did not report weighted F1, precision, recall, or cross-patient disjoint evaluation, this is a weak and non-comparable challenge to the proposed metric.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the reporting standard demonstrated by [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a): report absolute patient-wise weighted F1-score, per-class precision and recall, confusion matrix, and uncertainty estimates such as 95% confidence intervals or bootstrap intervals across repeated patient-wise splits. Because the plan uses a fixed 179/22/34 patient split rather than three-fold patient-wise CV, the team should either add repeated random patient splits or bootstrap over patients/clips to quantify variance, and should report both the fixed-split result and the repeated-split distribution. The second most important action is to handle rare classes explicitly, following [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a): if myoclonic seizures have only a few examples, exclude them from the primary weighted F1-score or report them separately with minimum-support caveats, and include macro F1 or class-wise recall as secondary metrics so that rare-class failures are not hidden by the weighted score. Third, the plan should improve comparability with prior TUSZ results by documenting the effect of preprocessing differences: [(Albaqami et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1472efddbffc5691353a0bc3b1b2d6511f2ae36a) used 20-channel transverse central parietal EEG, 250 Hz resampling, and 2-second non-overlapping segments, whereas the plan uses 200 Hz resampling, 60-second clips, and 1-second intervals; an ablation with 2-second/250 Hz inputs or at least a sensitivity analysis would clarify whether metric differences are due to representation learning or segmentation. Fourth, the plan should keep weighted F1-score primary but add accuracy, macro F1, and micro F1 as secondary metrics, and avoid the sample-balancing strategy used in [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630) unless the original class distribution is also reported, because balancing can change the meaning of weighted F1 and precision/recall. Finally, statistical claims should be supported by significance tests or confidence intervals across folds/patients, rather than point estimates, to address the gap in [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and [(Raghu et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3da7fe00d8a1767726e865256b62b081b2b8630).

## Context: To understand the contribution of each ST-IRM component. Method: We will conduct in-depth analyses examining the effect of different numbers of patient groups, various top-k values, and different penalty weight configurations to understand the contribution of each component.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed ablation of patient-group count, top-k, and penalty weights is that [(Singh, and Kingsbury, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75878b7e7c1963f1c1a4d8bd6ba1e9a07dcbc5ff) explicitly evaluates how individual representation components and configuration choices change classification accuracy, which is the same scientific logic used in the ST-IRM plan. On CIFAR-10, the DTCWT ScatterNet pipeline showed measurable, directionally consistent effects: log transformation improved OLS-selected R1 accuracy from 80.7% to 81.6% and R2 from 80.9% to 81.8%; G-SVM accuracy on scale-specific coefficients rose from 62.7% at J=1 without log to 70.2% at J=4, and from 65.6% to 72.4% with log; m=1 coefficients achieved 70.4% without log and 72.5% with log; concatenating selected R1 and R2 dimensions reached 82.4%. This supports the proposed plan's assumption that component-level ablations can reveal whether a representation element contributes to performance, although the magnitude of effects was modest. A secondary support is that the paper used controlled hyperparameter and feature-selection decisions—G-SVM c=14 and gamma=0.00002 selected by 5-fold cross-validation, OLS selecting 108 dimensions per class for R1 and 1,200 dimensions per image for R2, and finding that selecting more dimensions reduced accuracy—which parallels the need to tune top-k neighbors, maximum diffusion steps, dropout, and penalty weights on the TUSZ validation set rather than assuming larger graphs or more penalty weight are better. A weaker but relevant support is the small-data result: with fewer than 10,000 training images the scattering pipeline outperformed LeNet and Network in Network, suggesting that invariant or structured representations can be useful when data are limited, which is conceptually aligned with cross-patient seizure classification where each patient contributes limited clips and distribution shift is high. The weakest support is computational and representation-efficiency evidence: R1/R2 extraction took 0.78 s versus 0.98 s for Mallat's R1-only network, OLS selection took 2.21 h versus 3.22 h, and the scattering vector had 44,796 dimensions versus 113,712 for Mallat's three-layer network while OLS selected 2,300 dimensions versus 1,080; this supports reporting computational cost and feature-dimensionality effects in the ST-IRM ablation, but it does not directly validate EEG-specific components.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Singh, and Kingsbury, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75878b7e7c1963f1c1a4d8bd6ba1e9a07dcbc5ff) does not test the specific ST-IRM mechanisms in the research plan: it uses CIFAR-10/100 images, DTCWT scattering, OLS feature selection, and G-SVM, not TUSZ EEG, cross-patient disjoint splits, patient-group environments, K-means clustering, top-k correlation graphs, or a gradient variance penalty toward the mask function. Therefore, its ablation evidence cannot directly support the claim that varying patient-group count, top-k, or penalty weight will improve cross-patient seizure classification. The next strongest contradiction is that the full-data supervised deep models outperformed the scattering pipeline by nearly 10% on CIFAR-10/100, with CIFAR-100 at 56.7%, indicating that invariant or handcrafted scattering representations may be less competitive than supervised deep models when sufficient data are available; the proposed plan must therefore show that ST-IRM beats DenseCNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and GNN baselines under the same cross-patient protocol, not merely that components change performance. A further contradiction is that the reported component effects were small and lacked explicit statistical significance: log transformation improved accuracy by only 0.9 percentage points for R1 and R2, and scale/m-order changes produced differences of a few points, so the proposed ablations may also yield marginal, non-significant effects if the mask, SSL, IRM, or penalty terms are weakly coupled to seizure-type information. The weakest contradiction is that increasing representation capacity or selected dimensions did not monotonically improve performance: OLS selected 2,300 dimensions from a 44,796-dimensional scattering vector, and selecting more dimensions reduced accuracy, while the pipeline's advantage over Mallat's network was partly attributed to feature richness rather than a guaranteed benefit from more components; this cautions against assuming that more patient groups, larger top-k, or stronger penalty weights will automatically improve weighted F1.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the proposed ablation a pre-registered, controlled matrix that directly mirrors the research plan's components: vary number of patient groups, for example 2, 4, 8, and 16; top-k graph neighbors, for example 3, 5, 10, and 20; and gradient variance penalty weights, for example 0, 0.1, 1, and 10, while holding batch size 40, cosine annealing, dropout, maximum diffusion steps, and the NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 with Intel Xeon Gold 6248R setup fixed, and report weighted F1, precision, recall, macro F1, per-seizure-type F1, and 95% confidence intervals or paired bootstrap tests over multiple seeds. The second most important action is to include component-removal baselines that isolate each ST-IRM element—no invariant mask, no self-supervised temporal loss, no environment-based IRM, no gradient variance penalty, no top-k correlation graph, and a standard supervised spatiotemporal model—so that the contribution of each term is measured against the same cross-patient TUSZ split of 1,925 training clips from 179 patients, 450 validation clips from 22 patients, and 521 test clips from 34 patients. Third, because [(Singh, and Kingsbury, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75878b7e7c1963f1c1a4d8bd6ba1e9a07dcbc5ff) showed that feature selection and hyperparameter choices can reduce accuracy when overfit or poorly tuned, the plan should use validation-set selection for top-k, penalty weight, and number of patient groups, with nested or repeated validation to avoid leakage, and should report the selected hyperparameters, search ranges, and number of trials. Fourth, the plan should add a small-data or patient-limited experiment analogous to the related work's fewer-than-10,000-image setting, using reduced patient subsets while preserving disjoint patient sets, to test whether ST-IRM's invariant representation advantage appears when per-patient data are scarce. Fifth, report computational and reproducibility details at the granularity of the related work: GPU hours, memory, epochs, learning-rate schedule, batch size, feature dimensionality, mask sparsity, gradient variance values, environment risk gaps, and runtime comparisons to baselines, because the related work quantified extraction time, OLS time, and feature dimensions. Finally, interpret ablation effects cautiously: if changes in patient-group count, top-k, or penalty weight are small, report them as sensitivity analyses rather than evidence of strong component contribution, and include statistical tests to distinguish meaningful improvements from noise.

## Context: To optimize model configuration on unseen-patient validation data. Method: We will tune hyperparameters including initial learning rate, top-k neighbors for correlation graphs, maximum diffusion steps, dropout probability, and training epochs on the validation set.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the newer TUSZ work [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), which builds on the earlier patient-level cross-validation of [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) and the earlier learning-rate-schedule evidence of [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86) by explicitly using a patient-disjoint validation split on TUSZ v1.5.2 with 5,612 EEGs, 3,050 annotated seizures, 19 channels, log-amplitude FFT clips, and a 90 percent training and 10 percent validation patient split while holding out the official test set after removing five overlapping patients. This directly validates the proposed plan's core procedure of tuning model configuration on unseen-patient validation data before final cross-patient evaluation, and its distance-graph and correlation-graph DCRNN baselines are the same model family for which the plan tunes top-k correlation neighbors, maximum diffusion steps, dropout probability, initial learning rate, and training epochs. The reported outcomes, including pre-trained Dist-DCRNN AUROC 0.875 for 60-second seizure detection, 84.3 percent TPR at 25 percent FPR for 12-second detection versus Dense-CNN 72.8 percent, LSTM 67.0 percent, and CNN-LSTM 62.5 percent, and large rare-class accuracy gains, show that patient-disjoint validation plus graph GNNs can yield clinically meaningful cross-patient performance. The next strongest support is [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), which evaluated SeizureNet on TUH-EEGSC v1.4.0 with 2,012 seizures and v1.5.2 with 3,050 seizures using patient-level 3-fold cross-validation for v1.5.2, matching the plan's cross-patient evaluation goal and weighted F1 metric. Its fixed training recipe, Adam with batch size 50, 400 epochs, initial learning rate 0.001 with factor-10 reductions at 50 percent and 75 percent of training, and weight decay 0.0005, provides a concrete seizure-domain baseline for the plan's initial learning rate and epoch budget, and its reported approximately 3-point patient-wise and 4-point seizure-wise weighted F1 improvements demonstrate that patient-disjoint evaluation can capture meaningful gains. Supporting evidence for the learning-rate and epoch components also comes from [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86), which tested SGDR with cosine-annealing restarts on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 WRN-28-10 and WRN-28-20, a motor-imagery EEG dataset, and downsampled ImageNet. It shows that initial learning rate and epoch budget materially affect performance: default SGD with initial learning rate 0.1 gave median test error 4.24 on CIFAR-10 and 20.33 on CIFAR-100, while SGDR T0=200 and Tmult=1 gave 3.86 and 19.98, and T0=10 and Tmult=2 gave 4.03 and 19.58; on EEG, SGDR matched final performance with better anytime performance and snapshot aggregation improved median reference error by 1 to 2 percent from one run and 2 to 3 percent from two hyperparameter settings. This supports tuning initial learning rate and epochs and using a cosine-annealing-style schedule, though it does not validate the plan's exact ST-IRM architecture. The weakest support is [(Haderlein et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57c88580ceb478d93bc0e33146c25576f4eea1f0), which used validation-based model selection for intracranial EEG seizure prediction, but only with within-patient temporal splits, linear regularized classifiers, 10-fold cross-validation, and no reported quantitative outcomes; it supports the general principle of validation-based tuning but provides little direct evidence for unseen-patient tuning of deep graph hyperparameters.

### Contradictions

The strongest quantitative contradiction is that [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f) achieved patient-level weighted F1 improvements on TUH-EEGSC v1.5.2 with a fixed hyperparameter configuration, Adam with batch size 50, 400 epochs, initial learning rate 0.001 with factor-10 reductions at 50 percent and 75 percent of training, and weight decay 0.0005, without reporting tuning on a separate unseen-patient validation set, which weakens the assumption that validation-based tuning of initial learning rate and epochs is necessary for cross-patient seizure classification. A second contradiction is that [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86) shows that tuning only initial learning rate and epochs with a cosine-annealing-style schedule may be insufficient: SGDR with restarts improved CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 median test error from 4.24 and 20.33 for default SGD to 3.86 and 19.98 for T0=200 and Tmult=1, and snapshot ensembles further improved performance, whereas the plan lists only cosine annealing and does not tune T0, Tmult, or snapshot aggregation. A third limitation is that the closest TUSZ work, [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), confirms patient-disjoint validation tuning but does not disclose the selected top-k, diffusion-step, dropout, learning-rate, or epoch values, and its largest gains are tied to self-supervised pre-training, for example pre-trained Dist-DCRNN 84.3 percent TPR at 25 percent FPR, so the plan's specific hyperparameter list is not independently validated. The weakest contradiction is [(Haderlein et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57c88580ceb478d93bc0e33146c25576f4eea1f0), which used validation-based tuning but with within-patient temporal splits and linear regularized classifiers on 15 intracranial EEG patients, with no reported quantitative outcomes; this suggests unseen-patient validation may not be the only appropriate strategy for small patient cohorts, but the absence of results makes it weak evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the validation protocol explicit and robust: before final evaluation, remove any patients appearing in both training and test sets as in [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), and because the plan uses only 22 validation patients, use repeated patient-disjoint splits or nested cross-validation to select initial learning rate, top-k correlation neighbors, maximum diffusion steps, dropout probability, and training epochs, reporting mean and variance of weighted F1, precision, and recall across splits. Second, expand the optimization search beyond initial learning rate and epochs to include the full learning-rate schedule, comparing cosine annealing with SGDR-style restarts using T0 and Tmult and with step-decay schedules such as the 0.001 initial learning rate with factor-10 reductions at 50 percent and 75 percent used by [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), while also evaluating snapshot ensembles or model averaging as suggested by [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86), since that work showed snapshot aggregation improved EEG median reference error by 1 to 2 percent from one run and 2 to 3 percent from two settings. Third, explicitly ablate the graph-specific hyperparameters on the validation set, including top-k correlation neighbors, maximum diffusion steps, dropout, and correlation versus distance graph construction, using the DCRNN and MSTGCN baselines in the plan and reporting the selected values, because [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) demonstrates the relevance of correlation-graph DCRNNs but does not disclose the tuned values. Fourth, incorporate self-supervised pre-training or invariant SSL as a separate ablation, since pre-trained Dist-DCRNN achieved 84.3 percent TPR at 25 percent FPR for 12-second detection and 0.875 AUROC for 60-second detection, and compare 60-second clips with 12-second clips to match the plan's 60-second segmentation while assessing sensitivity to clip length. Fifth, report per-class weighted F1, precision, recall, AUROC, and TPR at a fixed FPR, with confidence intervals and statistical significance tests, and handle rare seizure types transparently, following [(Asif et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/457931ded7106a4fa26bc84844e1323cecac231f), which excluded Myoclonic seizures due to only three examples. Finally, specify the computational budget and early-stopping rule for the RTX 3090 experiments, including batch size 40, maximum epochs, and whether validation-based early stopping will be used, and consider a temporal or leave-patient-out sensitivity analysis inspired by [(Haderlein et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57c88580ceb478d93bc0e33146c25576f4eea1f0) if patient-specific variability dominates the cross-patient signal.

## Context: To specify the training and computational setup. Method: We will use batch sizes of 40 EEG clips, employ cosine annealing learning rate scheduler, and conduct experiments on NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 with Intel Xeon Gold 6248R CPU.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed training and computational setup comes from [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), which uses the same TUSZ v1.5.2 corpus, 19 standard 10-20 channels, FFT log-amplitude frequency features, non-overlapping 60-second clips, patient-disjoint train/validation/test splits, weighted F1 for classification, and the same baseline family listed in the plan, including Dense-CNN, LSTM, CNN-LSTM, Dist-DCRNN, and Corr-DCRNN. Its self-supervised pretraining improves detection and classification, with pre-trained distance-graph DCRNN reaching AUROC 0.875 for 60-second seizure detection, 84.3 percent true positive rate at 25 percent false positive rate for 12-second detection versus 72.8, 67.0, and 62.5 percent for Dense-CNN, LSTM, and CNN-LSTM, and 74 percent accuracy on rare combined tonic seizures, 47 points above Dense-CNN and 48 points above non-pretrained DCRNN. This directly supports the plan's TUSZ preprocessing, cross-patient evaluation, baseline choices, and the value of a self-supervised component, although it does not report batch size, learning-rate schedule, or hardware. The next strongest support for the specific cosine annealing scheduler is [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86), which evaluated stochastic gradient descent with warm restarts on an EEG CNN with 14 subjects and roughly 1000 trials per subject, using a 30-epoch baseline that dropped the learning rate by a factor of 10 at epochs 10, 15, and 20. The warm-restart schedule achieved final performance similar to the best fixed-budget baseline while improving anytime performance, and snapshot ensembling improved median reference error by 1 to 2 percentage points from one run and by 2 to 3 percentage points from two hyperparameter settings, providing indirect EEG-specific support for cosine-style annealing and snapshot ensembles, but not for TUSZ, cross-patient seizure classification, batch size 40, or RTX 3090. A weaker but conceptually relevant support is [(Grill et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38f93092ece8eee9771e61c1edaf11b1293cae1b), whose BYOL self-supervised method uses an online encoder-predictor, an exponential moving average target encoder, projection and predictor MLPs, and no negative examples; the supplied excerpt reports that a fixed randomly initialized target yields 18.8 percent top-1 linear ImageNet accuracy versus 1.4 percent for the random network alone, supporting the use of a non-collapsing self-supervised target mechanism in the plan's SSL component, but it is image-based and does not validate EEG clip batches, cosine annealing, or the proposed hardware. Additional weaker support comes from [(Afzal et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dcfaacb4333afb240719af712366a059ccc67a3), which uses TUSZ with 5545 EEG files, 19 channels, 200 Hz resampling, FFT log amplitudes, z-normalization, and graph/RNN baselines, showing that such TUSZ graph seizure models are trainable with ADAM, batch size 128, five seeds, and a single A100, but it provides no numerical accuracy or F1 in the excerpt and therefore only weakly supports the proposed setup. The weakest support is [(Gijsen, and Ritter, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/915f5a6b0970b37f4dca99ddcdade054b2e6a96f), which applies a residual CNN EEG encoder and MLP projection heads to TUSZ with 5-second crops, 5-fold cross-validation, subject exclusion from pretraining, and temperature tau=0.3, but the excerpt contains no quantitative results, so it supports leakage-aware evaluation more than the proposed 60-second clip training setup.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is not a reported failure of batch size 40, cosine annealing, or RTX 3090, but the absence of direct validation for that exact setup in the closest TUSZ training protocol. [(Afzal et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dcfaacb4333afb240719af712366a059ccc67a3) trains comparable TUSZ graph seizure models with ADAM, batch size 128 EEG clips, five random seeds, and a single NVIDIA A100, whereas the proposed method uses batch size 40, cosine annealing, and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3090 with Intel Xeon Gold 6248R CPU; this mismatch means the proposed setup may be less stable, slower, or memory-constrained, and the excerpt also reports that MSE outperformed binary cross-entropy for seizure detection, which could contradict a binary detection loss if the plan ever uses BCE for clip-level seizure presence. A second tension is that [(Gijsen, and Ritter, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/915f5a6b0970b37f4dca99ddcdade054b2e6a96f) evaluates TUSZ seizure/background classification with 5-second crops and 5-fold cross-validation on train and dev sets, with subject exclusion from pretraining, rather than the plan's non-overlapping 60-second clips and fixed patient-disjoint 179/22/34 split; this does not prove the proposed split is wrong, but it shows that shorter crops and cross-validation are also used, and the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes to support the proposed 60-second setup. A third tension is that [(Grill et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38f93092ece8eee9771e61c1edaf11b1293cae1b) validates BYOL on ImageNet with ResNet-50, 224x224 crops, 2048-dimensional pooled features, and 4096-to-256 projection/predictor MLPs, but does not specify batch size, learning-rate schedule, or hardware, so it cannot support direct transfer of the proposed 40-clip EEG batch, cosine annealing, or RTX 3090/Xeon Gold 6248R setup. A fourth tension is that [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86) does not report batch size, hardware, exact warm-restart cycle parameters, or statistical significance for its EEG experiment, so its support for cosine annealing is indirect and insufficient to justify the proposed scheduler without additional ablation. Finally, [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) is the closest TUSZ evidence but also omits batch size, learning-rate schedule, epochs, and hardware, so it supports the scientific pipeline but not the specific computational choices.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to justify the proposed batch size, scheduler, and hardware with a TUSZ-specific pilot ablation. Following [(Afzal et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dcfaacb4333afb240719af712366a059ccc67a3) and [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6), train Dist-DCRNN, Corr-DCRNN, Dense-CNN, LSTM, and CNN-LSTM on the same 19-channel, 200 Hz, FFT log-amplitude, 60-second TUSZ v1.5.2 clips with patient-disjoint splits, then compare batch size 40 versus 128, cosine annealing versus step decay or constant learning rate, and RTX 3090 versus A100 if available, using at least five random seeds and reporting weighted F1, precision, recall, AUROC where applicable, variance, memory, and wall-clock time. If RTX 3090 memory limits batch size 128, use gradient accumulation to reach an effective batch size of 128 or explicitly report why batch size 40 is chosen. The next most important action is to specify the cosine annealing schedule using the EEG evidence from [(Loshchilov, and Hutter, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b022f2a277a4bf5f42382e86e4380b96340b9e86), including T_max, warm restarts, final learning rate, number of epochs, and whether snapshots at zero learning rate are ensembled; because that paper reported 1 to 2 percentage point improvements from single-run snapshots and 2 to 3 percentage point improvements from two hyperparameter settings without significance tests, the plan should add confidence intervals and significance tests for snapshot ensembling. Then, for the self-supervised component, adapt the BYOL design from [(Grill et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38f93092ece8eee9771e61c1edaf11b1293cae1b) to EEG frequency features by using an EMA target encoder, projection and predictor MLPs, and a collapse check, and include an ablation with a fixed randomly initialized target, since BYOL's fixed target still achieved 18.8 percent top-1 linear ImageNet accuracy versus 1.4 percent for the random network alone. Also incorporate the loss and preprocessing cautions from [(Afzal et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6dcfaacb4333afb240719af712366a059ccc67a3): use z-normalized FFT log amplitudes, a static distance graph with Gaussian kernel if graph baselines are used, cross-entropy for seizure-type classification, and MSE rather than binary cross-entropy if any binary seizure/background detection objective is included. Finally, address leakage and evaluation consistency using [(Gijsen, and Ritter, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/915f5a6b0970b37f4dca99ddcdade054b2e6a96f) by excluding TUSZ/TUEV subjects from any external pretraining, keeping the primary evaluation patient-disjoint, and optionally adding a 5-second crop ablation to test whether shorter crops improve rare seizure-type classification, while retaining the 60-second non-overlapping clips supported by [(Tang et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a4d5fdfaba62390b25c725badffee524bbcf0a6) as the primary setup.
