## Strengths 
The strongest support is for the POMDP speaker-listener formalization and regret-based metric: [(Ornik, and Topcu, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a250728df2774905652e62325bcb259be5c787c5) treats deception as sequential policy selection that affects an adversary's expected reward under constraints, and [(Alon et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3b6fdb088db514a8c69e97156f48d4b4b48bd26) shows reward degradation in an IPOMDP can signal harmful sender behavior. The conversational scenario and user-study design is supported by [(Chou et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e8c54f4bd1ad434f3ebde280e242a18c14a53c3) and [(Kuzio, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f214aebf2dc4aa3fc3c6128949a26b07ec5e6ad0), which show conversational deception can be studied with turn-level features and graded human judgments. LLM scenario generation is supported by [(Hagendorff, 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa5993501f4d0036d310e0292dea20e2c60b6c8f), and the dialogue-management and negotiation baselines are supported by [(Gasic et al., 2010-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04213b2da00939e9fb72efcd8b304c18b20b6f4a), [(Mell et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32d2236cc0394c2379828cb4de2a7998b8b197f0), and [(Hazra, and Majumder, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f459e09c1e0faff4628d79b43470a129be565).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the regret metric and human evaluation. [(Alon et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3b6fdb088db514a8c69e97156f48d4b4b48bd26) shows reward degradation can arise from monitoring, termination, or parameter sensitivity rather than deception, so a prior-only regret gap may produce false positives. [(Liu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66b2314d65a87af50588d16579c3610e485a109a) and [(Kuzio, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f214aebf2dc4aa3fc3c6128949a26b07ec5e6ad0) show human deception judgments can be noisy, suspicion-driven, or near chance, undermining simple Likert correlations. [(Hagendorff, 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa5993501f4d0036d310e0292dea20e2c60b6c8f) does not validate that LLMs can convert symbolic POMDP actions into dialogue while preserving hidden features and belief structure. [(Gasic et al., 2010-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04213b2da00939e9fb72efcd8b304c18b20b6f4a) and [(Zhang et al., 2001-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba659c62e93021a54500fd20089fe4c0d2060fb) do not show that randomly selecting max/min regret actions will yield human-perceivable deception. [(Mell et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32d2236cc0394c2379828cb4de2a7998b8b197f0) warns negotiation ratings may reflect toughness rather than regret, and 30 participants may be underpowered. [(Hazra, and Majumder, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f459e09c1e0faff4628d79b43470a129be565) shows LLM baselines can be weak and require careful benchmarking against human and chance performance.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Validate the regret formalism on small POMDP benchmarks before human studies: implement Tiger/RockSample-style speaker-listener tasks with prior-only, truthful, no-communication, and deceptive-kernel baselines; specify listener priors, action constraints, communication costs, task/belief rewards, horizon, solver, seeds, and report task regret, belief regret, combined regret, false-positive rates, and 95% CIs, following [(Ornik, and Topcu, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a250728df2774905652e62325bcb259be5c787c5), [(Zhang, and Zhu, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fa3753ffb04345109518f18e5cfb1231a873d89), and [(Alon et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3b6fdb088db514a8c69e97156f48d4b4b48bd26).

Redesign human studies with power analysis and uncertainty controls: add confidence/undecided options, honest/random/no-communication/deceptive baselines, manipulation checks, participant and scenario random effects, pre-registered mixed-effects or Bayesian models, and report correlations with CIs and p-values; use trust/distrust and interlocutor-judgment structure inspired by [(Kuzio, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f214aebf2dc4aa3fc3c6128949a26b07ec5e6ad0), [(Liu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66b2314d65a87af50588d16579c3610e485a109a), and [(Chou et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e8c54f4bd1ad434f3ebde280e242a18c14a53c3).

Add an LLM-dialogue validation and controlled baseline layer: back-translate or annotate generated dialogues to verify intended POMDP actions, revealed features, hidden features, priors, and listener goals; discard/regenerate failures; fix prompts, temperature, model versions, and seeds; pilot with a subset; then compare GPT-4, LLaMA/Llama-2, and Google Bard on the same scenarios against human ratings and chance baselines, reporting Spearman/Pearson correlations, accuracy/AUC where applicable, CIs, and multiple-comparison corrections, following [(Hagendorff, 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa5993501f4d0036d310e0292dea20e2c60b6c8f), [(Hazra, and Majumder, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a3f459e09c1e0faff4628d79b43470a129be565), and [(Mell et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32d2236cc0394c2379828cb4de2a7998b8b197f0).
