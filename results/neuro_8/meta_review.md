## Context: Core measurement of sub- and suprathreshold CA1PC activity during novel exploration. Method: We will use voltage imaging to investigate the temporal dynamics of sub- and suprathreshold neuronal activities in CA1PCs during novel exploration.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Piatkevich et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18198ac751aa950b5b6a03d39432756282664e0b), which used SomArchon-based in vivo optical voltage imaging to obtain single-cell, single-spike membrane potential dynamics with conventional wide-field microscopy, combined intracellular membrane potential recordings with LFPs in hippocampus, performed pair-wise coherence analysis between hippocampal neurons and LFPs, and included head-fixed voluntary movement and population imaging of spiking and subthreshold oscillations in hippocampal neurons. This is the newest and most directly relevant work in the provided set, and it extends the earlier 2014 GEVI sensor-development studies into in vivo hippocampal and head-fixed behavioral contexts, directly supporting the proposed plan to image sub- and suprathreshold CA1PC activity during head-fixed exploration while correlating spikes with LFP theta and ripple events, although it did not use Voltron2, 532-nm excitation, 2 kHz acquisition, or an air-lifted novel-exploration track. The next strongest support is [(Hochbaum et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1370fe973b2e4762dadf90b74efef68a8d04f60), which showed that Arch-based QuasAr GEVIs can report suprathreshold action potentials and subthreshold transients in primary neuronal cultures and organotypic hippocampal slices, with QuasAr2 reported as approximately threefold larger Delta F/F per 100 mV than ArcLight A242 and AP timing refined by subframe interpolation; this supports the feasibility of measuring both spike and subthreshold dynamics in hippocampal pyramidal-cell preparations, but it was in vitro and frame-rate limited. Supporting evidence then comes from [(Gong et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e337f440eeb76b53a684e5771c6b428352be249d), which demonstrated FRET-opsin voltage sensors MacN, MacQ, Mac-EEN, and Mac-EEQ using 633-nm excitation and near-infrared emission detection, and detected optical voltage transients from Purkinje neurons, supporting genetically encoded voltage imaging of neuronal membrane transients but not CA1PCs, 532-nm excitation, or 2 kHz imaging. Weaker support is provided by [(Zou et al., 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b23db9ca3f830819fe252166cbe17de5ffbebcc), which characterized eFRET GEVI linker variants containing QuasAr and mOrange2 using two-photon lifetime measurements to assess FRET efficiency, and by [(Flytzanis et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/024291e401b2ff335ae15e650db2d7278266ea33), which used mutant Arch voltage sensors in cultured neuronal networks and sensory neurons of behaving C. elegans; these establish sensor engineering and behavioral optical voltage imaging but do not quantify CA1PC-specific in vivo performance.

### Contradictions

The strongest caveat is that the closest in vivo precedent, [(Piatkevich et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18198ac751aa950b5b6a03d39432756282664e0b), used SomArchon and conventional wide-field microscopy rather than Voltron2, 532-nm excitation, 2 kHz high-spatial-resolution imaging, or CA1PC-specific expression during first-entry novel exploration; therefore it supports the general concept but does not validate the proposed acquisition parameters or place-field analysis. A more direct methodological contradiction is the frame-rate limitation reported in [(Hochbaum et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1370fe973b2e4762dadf90b74efef68a8d04f60), where fluorescence imaging was frame-rate limited and AP timing required subframe interpolation, implying that raw 2 kHz frames may still be insufficient for precise spike timing and subthreshold waveform reconstruction if SNR or kinetics are poor and showing that the 2014 in vitro foundation did not by itself solve high-frame-rate in vivo spike timing. Additional contradictions or gaps include [(Gong et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e337f440eeb76b53a684e5771c6b428352be249d), which used 633-nm excitation and near-infrared detection in Purkinje neurons rather than 532-nm CA1PC imaging, and did not report voltage sensitivity, SNR, or spike detection accuracy in the provided excerpt; [(Zou et al., 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b23db9ca3f830819fe252166cbe17de5ffbebcc), which focused on FRET efficiency of QuasAr-mOrange2 linker variants without reporting voltage sensitivity, SNR, or in vivo performance; and [(Flytzanis et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/024291e401b2ff335ae15e650db2d7278266ea33), which demonstrated Arch sensors in cultured networks and C. elegans sensory neurons but did not provide quantitative sensor performance, imaging hardware, frame rate, or mouse hippocampal validation. The weakest contradiction is the absence of any provided work that directly tests Voltron2 in CA1PCs at 2 kHz, so the proposed method remains extrapolated from related GEVI platforms.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a dedicated in vivo pilot in which Voltron2 is expressed specifically in CA1PCs, imaged with 532-nm excitation at 2 kHz, and validated against simultaneous electrophysiology or LFP-identified spikes to quantify Delta F/F per mV, SNR, spike detection accuracy, false-positive rate, motion artifacts, and subthreshold waveform fidelity, and to document the motion-correction and spike-detection pipeline before committing to the full novel-exploration experiment. Second, adopt the subframe interpolation and AP-timing refinement strategy demonstrated by [(Hochbaum et al., 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1370fe973b2e4762dadf90b74efef68a8d04f60), and use jittered spike-time controls as planned to test whether observed ensemble synchrony exceeds rate-matched chance levels. Third, implement the LFP coherence and oscillation alignment analyses used by [(Piatkevich et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18198ac751aa950b5b6a03d39432756282664e0b), including pair-wise coherence between imaged CA1PC spikes and contralateral hippocampal LFP theta and ripple events, to directly test the proposed relationship between synchronous ensembles and network oscillations. Fourth, characterize Voltron2 expression, FRET efficiency, kinetics, photostability, and voltage sensitivity using the sensor-validation logic of [(Zou et al., 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b23db9ca3f830819fe252166cbe17de5ffbebcc) and [(Gong et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e337f440eeb76b53a684e5771c6b428352be249d), for example by measuring FRET efficiency, calibration curves, and baseline fluorescence in cultured neurons or acute slices before in vivo use. Fifth, if 532-nm 2 kHz imaging yields insufficient SNR, consider alternative excitation wavelengths, higher photon budgets, motion correction, or a GEVI with better in vivo performance, while preserving the air-lifted track, contralateral LFP, and behavioral state segmentation. Finally, use the behavioral optical voltage imaging precedent of [(Flytzanis et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/024291e401b2ff335ae15e650db2d7278266ea33) to justify including locomotion versus immobility states, but require quantitative mouse CA1PC validation rather than relying on C. elegans or cultured-network demonstrations.

## Context: Genetic expression of a voltage indicator enabling simultaneous multi-cell sub- and suprathreshold measurements. Method: Our approach involves expressing the voltage indicator Voltron2 in CA1 pyramidal cells, which will allow us to measure both suprathreshold and subthreshold membrane potentials from multiple CA1PCs simultaneously.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Voltron2 CA1PC voltage-imaging method comes from the newer hippocampal circuit-level GEVI work by [(Tian et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1f7a06fabb8b6132a7fa24b312d2ebb7856758a2), which used engineered Arch-derived indicators QuasAr6a and QuasAr6b developed through directed evolution and photoselection-based pooled screening to image membrane potential dynamics, including spikes and subthreshold events, in hippocampal parvalbumin-positive interneurons and to probe synchrony and functional connectivity. This directly supports the plan's central assumption that genetically expressed voltage indicators can resolve sub- and suprathreshold dynamics in hippocampal ensembles and that such measurements can be used to study population synchrony, although it does not validate Voltron2, CA1 pyramidal cells, 532-nm excitation, 2-kHz acquisition, or the air-lifted novel-exploration paradigm. An older but still important line of support is [(Piatkevich et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18198ac751aa950b5b6a03d39432756282664e0b), which demonstrated in vivo optical voltage imaging with SomArchon using conventional wide-field microscopy, including single-cell single-spike precision during locomotion, population imaging of spiking and subthreshold oscillations, simultaneous LFP recording, and pair-wise coherence analysis that revealed phase relationships between hippocampal spikes and theta oscillations. This supports the plan's multi-modal design combining voltage imaging, LFP theta and ripple analysis, behavior, and ensemble synchrony metrics, and it shows that GEVI signals can be related to network oscillations and movement states. As an iterative sequence, Piatkevich et al. established the feasibility of in vivo GEVI with LFP and behavior, while Tian et al. extended the approach to engineered GEVIs and hippocampal circuit-level synchrony, making the proposed CA1PC ensemble analysis plausible but still requiring direct validation of the specific Voltron2, cell-type, and imaging-parameter combination.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct refutation but a validation gap: neither related work demonstrates that Voltron2 expressed in CA1 pyramidal cells will provide the required multi-cell, high-spatial-resolution, 2-kHz, 532-nm imaging of subthreshold membrane potentials during head-fixed exploration. [(Tian et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1f7a06fabb8b6132a7fa24b312d2ebb7856758a2) used QuasAr6a/b in PV interneurons, and [(Piatkevich et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18198ac751aa950b5b6a03d39432756282664e0b) used SomArchon with conventional wide-field microscopy, so their signal-to-noise, kinetics, photobleaching, motion tolerance, and cell-type specificity cannot be assumed to transfer to the proposed Voltron2 CA1PC experiment. A second limitation is that the related hippocampal synchrony evidence comes from PV interneurons, where gap junction-mediated spikelets and high synchrony may produce ensemble patterns that are not representative of CA1PC place cells during spatial memory acquisition; therefore, the plan's inference that synchronous CA1PC ensembles organize spatially tuned activity should not be generalized from PV interneuron circuit data. A third limitation is that both excerpts lack quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, statistical significance, baseline comparisons, imaging frame rates, laser power, expression levels, and exclusion criteria, so the support remains qualitative and does not confirm that the proposed jittered-control CCG analysis, theta/ripple alignment, or place-field comparisons will be statistically powered or artifact-free. Finally, the proposed contralateral LFP recording is not directly supported by the related works, which used local or simultaneous LFP recordings, and contralateral signals may be less suitable for resolving local CA1 theta phase or ripple events that are central to the plan.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a dedicated pilot validation of Voltron2 in CA1 pyramidal cells before the full novel-exploration experiment, measuring in vivo or acute-slice fluorescence at 532 nm and 2 kHz to quantify signal-to-noise, dynamic range, spike amplitude, subthreshold sensitivity, kinetics, photobleaching, phototoxicity, and motion artifacts, and to compare performance against QuasAr6a/b or SomArchon if available. The next priority is to strengthen the analysis and controls: implement rigorous motion correction, LFP artifact rejection, voltage calibration or voltage-clamp controls where feasible, and use jittered spike controls, grand average CCGs, ensemble-size distributions, and synchronization-strength metrics with explicit statistical tests and effect sizes, while also testing whether synchrony persists after controlling for theta phase, speed, and place-field overlap. The plan should also improve the LFP and behavioral design by recording ipsilateral or multi-site hippocampal LFP in addition to contralateral LFP, because local CA1 theta and ripple detection are central to the hypothesis, and by including habituation and novelty controls to ensure that the first-entry corner confinement does not confound spatial tuning. Finally, the team should address cell-type and circuit confounds by confirming CA1PC-specific expression, excluding PV or SST interneurons, and interpreting any gap-junction-like fast synchrony cautiously, while reporting detailed reproducibility information such as animal numbers, AAV or electroporation details, imaging hardware, laser power, frame rate, expression levels, and data exclusions.

## Context: Head-fixed spatial exploration system for imaging during novel environment acquisition. Method: We will employ an air-lifted plastic track system that allows mice to explore an environment while remaining head-fixed under a microscope.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed air-lifted plastic track comes from [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de), which directly demonstrated a head-fixed, air-table floating track platform for two-photon calcium imaging of hippocampal CA1 in mice. That system used a carbon-fiber circular track with an outer diameter of 32.5 cm, width of 5 cm, mass of 100 ± 2.8 g, and 4 cm walls, placed under a two-photon resonant scanning microscope, with magnet-based position tracking at 100 Hz; mice reached speeds up to 200 mm/s, completed more than 100 laps in 45-minute sessions, and in a 32.5 cm open field covered more than 90% of 2 × 2 cm bins, showing that head-fixed mice can explore a physical spatial environment while imaged. Viewed as an iterative progression, this newer work supports the older head-fixed imaging paradigm of [(Grosenick et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/283687587ba6e3834595946f74462c91e8085245) by extending stable head-fixed imaging from a virtual contextual learning task to a physical floating track, while retaining the central requirement of head fixation under a microscope. A second, slightly weaker but still important support is that [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) specified practical constraints and analysis parameters that can be transferred to the proposed plastic track, including track diameter being limited by the distance from the microscope objective to the back wall, a speed threshold of 20 mm/s for place-field analysis, 2 cm bins on the circular track and 2 × 2 cm bins in the open field, and place-cell definition using spatial information above shuffled chance plus occupancy thresholds. These details directly inform the proposed spatial tuning analysis and track geometry. The weakest but still relevant support is from [(Grosenick et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/283687587ba6e3834595946f74462c91e8085245), which showed that head-fixed mice can be imaged during a contextual learning behavior using SWIFT light-field microscopy, with single-shot volumetric acquisition at approximately 100 Hz over about 1 mm^3 and approximately 10 µm resolution, followed by deconvolution and sparse structured matrix factorization to extract cellular activity. Although that work used a virtual environment and fluorescence rather than an air-lifted physical track or Voltron2 voltage imaging, it supports the broader feasibility of head-fixed imaging during learning and demonstrates that high-speed volumetric acquisition and computational extraction of cellular signals are achievable in the same general paradigm.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction, or more precisely the strongest evidence that the proposed method is not yet validated, is that [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) used trained, familiar floating tracks and GCaMP6s calcium imaging rather than a genuinely novel recording room, corner release, Voltron2 voltage imaging, or LFP recording. Mice were water-restricted to 85% of pre-restriction weight, received 1-3 mL daily water, were trained twice daily for 45 minutes, and required 11-14 circular-track sessions or 16-18 open-field sessions before imaging; activity and average speed increased over training, with open-field coverage reaching more than 90% only after about 15 sessions. This contradicts the implicit assumption that a first exposure to the recording room after separate-room habituation will automatically produce stable, high-coverage exploration and mature place fields suitable for synchrony and spatial tuning analyses. The next strongest limitation is that the related work does not provide evidence for the proposed 2kHz Voltron2 voltage imaging in a head-fixed moving animal on an air-lifted track. [(Grosenick et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/283687587ba6e3834595946f74462c91e8085245) demonstrated approximately 100 Hz volumetric fluorescence imaging, not 2kHz voltage imaging, and did not report behavioral outcomes, ensemble sizes, jittered controls, or voltage dynamics; [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) used two-photon calcium imaging, which is slower and not equivalent to sub- and suprathreshold membrane potential measurements. Therefore, the proposed combination of 532-nm excitation, 2kHz acquisition, high spatial resolution, and Voltron2 in a head-fixed track remains untested in these works. A weaker but important contradiction is that the position tracking in [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) was 100 Hz, which may be insufficient for the proposed 10-30 ms population synchrony analyses and precise alignment of synchronous events with theta/ripple LFP and spatial tuning; the related work also did not record contralateral LFP, so it cannot support the proposed multi-modal correlation of synchrony with network oscillations. Finally, the related work used reward-based water delivery through a lick spout and peristaltic pump, with 4 µL rewards, whereas the proposed method does not specify reward, water restriction, or motivation; this is not a direct contradiction but leaves open whether the proposed novel exploration will generate comparable locomotion without the behavioral and motivational parameters that supported exploration in [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt and adapt the validated hardware and behavioral constraints from [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) for the proposed air-lifted plastic track: use a low-friction air-supported track with dimensions compatible with the microscope objective-to-back-wall distance, similar to the 32.5 cm outer diameter, 5 cm width, 100 ± 2.8 g mass, and 4 cm walls, and include visual/tactile cues to support orientation. The second most important action is to increase and synchronize position/speed tracking beyond the 100 Hz used in [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de), ideally to at least 200-500 Hz or matched to the 2kHz imaging frame rate, and to timestamp track position, LFP, and Voltron2 frames with sub-10 ms precision so that 10-30 ms synchronous ensembles, theta phases, and ripple events can be reliably aligned. The third most important action is to run a pilot validation of Voltron2 voltage imaging in the head-fixed track before the main experiment, measuring signal-to-noise, phototoxicity, motion artifacts, field of view, and spike/subthreshold detection at 532 nm and 2kHz, and to use acute slice or in vitro calibration if needed, because neither [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) nor [(Grosenick et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/283687587ba6e3834595946f74462c91e8085245) demonstrates this specific voltage-imaging setup. The fourth action is to incorporate a behavioral baseline and, if scientifically acceptable, a short acclimation or repeated first-exposure design to account for the fact that [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) required many sessions for stable exploration; if the goal is truly novel acquisition, collect multiple first exposures or early sessions and explicitly model the transition from immobility to locomotion rather than assuming trained-track behavior. The fifth action is to use the place-field analysis parameters from [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) as a baseline, including excluding speeds below 20 mm/s, using 2 cm bins on a circular track or 2 × 2 cm bins in an open field, and defining place cells by spatial information above shuffled chance plus occupancy thresholds, while adapting these thresholds for voltage-imaged ensembles and jittered spike controls. The sixth action is to specify water restriction, reward, and motivation parameters, such as the 85% body-weight restriction, 1-3 mL daily water, and 4 µL lick-spout rewards used in [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de), or to justify why they are unnecessary for the proposed novel exploration. The least important but still useful action is to borrow computational extraction and validation practices from [(Grosenick et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/283687587ba6e3834595946f74462c91e8085245), such as deconvolution-based volume reconstruction, sparse structured matrix factorization, and acute slice validation, to improve cell identification and signal extraction from the high-speed Voltron2 data.

## Context: Novelty control ensuring that imaging occurs during first exposure to the recording environment. Method: To ensure a genuinely novel experience, all pre-training and habituation will be performed in a separate room distinct from the recording setup. On imaging day, mice will be introduced to the recording room for the first time and initially confined to a specific corner of the track before being allowed to explore freely during imaging.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed novelty-control method is that [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) demonstrated that a head-fixed floating circular track or open field can support reliable position and speed tracking and CA1 place-field analysis, which is directly relevant to the proposed air-lifted plastic track, simultaneous position/speed monitoring, and spatial tuning assessment. That study used a 32.5 cm outer-diameter, 5 cm wide carbon-fiber track weighing 100 +/- 2.8 g on an air table, tracked position at 100 Hz, restricted analysis to running speed greater than 20 mm/s, used 2-cm spatial bins and approximately 30 ms time bins, and classified place cells using events in at least half of laps, at least 5% of moving time bins within the field, and spatial information above the 99th percentile of 1,000 shuffled time stamps; open-field maps used 2 x 2 cm bins, 2D Gaussian smoothing with standard deviation 1.5, and at least 2% of moving time bins. These details support the proposed use of spatial bins, speed gating, and shuffled or jittered controls, although the related work used GCaMP6s calcium event trains rather than Voltron2 voltage spikes. A second level of support is behavioral feasibility: mice reached speeds up to 200 mm/s, completed more than 100 laps in 45 min, covered more than 90% of the open field, and showed increased activity and speed after about 9 track sessions and 15 open-field sessions, supporting the plan to analyze locomotion versus immobility and to obtain enough running for ensemble and place-field statistics. A third, more indirect support is that the same-environment training produced session-dependent behavioral and activity changes, implying that familiarity can alter the recording environment and therefore motivating the proposed separate-room pre-training to preserve first-exposure novelty. The weakest support is that apparatus and training details such as dark infrared illumination, phosphorescent visual cues at 500 and 520 nm, tactile cues, water restriction to 85% of pre-restriction weight, and 4 uL water rewards can inform separate-room habituation, but they do not directly validate Voltron2 imaging at 2 kHz, 532-nm excitation, LFP recording, or corner confinement.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) did not implement the proposed novelty control: mice were trained and habituated in the same floating track or open-field setup before imaging, with 11-14 circular-track sessions and 16-18 open-field sessions, so the recorded spatial coding reflects a familiar environment rather than first exposure to the recording room. This directly conflicts with the proposed requirement that pre-training occur in a separate room and that imaging occur during first entry with initial corner confinement. A second contradiction is that the related work used two-photon GCaMP6s calcium imaging, motion correction, ROI segmentation, neuropil subtraction, and deconvolution into event trains, not Voltron2 voltage imaging at 2 kHz with 532-nm excitation; therefore it provides no direct evidence that subthreshold membrane dynamics, grand average cross-correlograms, jittered spike-timing controls, theta/ripple alignment, or 10-30 ms ensemble synchrony can be measured in this behavioral setup. A third contradiction is that the related work did not test initial corner confinement, so it cannot confirm that corner confinement preserves novelty without introducing stress, immobility, or altered exploration that could confound synchronous ensemble formation. A fourth contradiction is that the related work relied on water restriction and reward delivery, whereas the proposed plan does not specify these variables; adopting the reward schedule could create reward associations or motivation differences, while omitting it may reduce locomotion relative to the 200 mm/s speeds and 100 laps per 45 min observed in the related work. The weakest contradiction is that the related work did not report the number of place cells or quantitative stability metrics, and its track geometry was constrained by the microscope, so it provides limited evidence that the proposed plastic track and 2 kHz voltage imaging will yield comparable spatial tuning and synchrony measurements.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement and validate the separate-room novelty protocol before the main voltage-imaging experiments: use an identical track geometry, wall height, visual and tactile cues, reward schedule, and position-tracking system in a separate room, avoid any exposure to the recording room, and include a control group trained in the recording room to dissociate novelty from apparatus familiarity. Quantify novelty by comparing first-session speed, laps, coverage, immobility, and neural metrics against familiar controls, using the related work's behavioral targets such as speeds up to 200 mm/s, more than 100 laps in 45 min, and more than 90% open-field coverage as benchmarks. The next action is to define the corner-confinement procedure precisely, including corner dimensions, confinement duration, release criteria, and monitoring of stress or immobility, and to pilot the procedure to ensure that initial confinement does not create a separate place field or suppress exploration before free imaging. A third action is to adopt the spatial analysis parameters from [(Go et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ca87a196dfe6be7b80a87152a8394474a9f0de) where appropriate: use speed greater than 20 mm/s, linearize circular-track position, use 2-cm spatial bins and approximately 30 ms time bins, boxcar-smooth rate maps over three bins, and classify place cells using events in at least half of laps, at least 5% of moving time bins, and spatial information above the 99th percentile of 1,000 shuffled time stamps; for open-field data use 2 x 2 cm bins, 2D Gaussian smoothing with standard deviation 1.5, and at least 2% of moving time bins, while adapting the controls to Voltron2 spike times and using jittered spike-timing controls that preserve spike rates for synchrony. A fourth action is to validate the voltage-imaging hardware and illumination: confirm Voltron2 expression, 532-nm excitation, 2 kHz acquisition, motion correction, spike and subthreshold detection, and contralateral LFP recording, and ensure that the 532-nm illumination does not become a visual cue or stressor, given that the related work used dark infrared illumination and phosphorescent cues at 500 and 520 nm. The least important but still necessary action is to report reproducibility details and statistics, including session number, water restriction, reward locations, track mass and dimensions, position-tracking rate, exclusion criteria, jitter parameters, permutation tests, false-discovery-rate correction, and confidence intervals, noting that the related work reported activity rates as F/F.s^-1 with 90% confidence intervals because they were approximately log-normal.

## Context: Simultaneous behavioral and hippocampal network recording to link synchrony with behavior and oscillations. Method: We will simultaneously monitor animals' positions and speed within the track while recording local field potential (LFP) from the contralateral side of the hippocampus.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method is that Kemere et al. directly paired track-based position and speed measurements with hippocampal LFP and used spectral analyses of theta, gamma, and ripple oscillations, showing continuous LFP changes during exploration and speed-dependent gamma modulation during learning [(Kemere et al., 2013-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab80b0678481be5791f8dca05f86215a7f31bd71). This supports the plan's core decision to monitor position and speed while recording hippocampal LFP, because it demonstrates that behavioral state can be linked to oscillatory network activity in a spatial task. A second, still relevant support is the reported decrease in gamma modulation with increased environmental familiarity, which supports the plan's novelty control of performing pre-training and habituation in a separate room and introducing mice to the recording room for the first time. A third support is the paper's use of evoked field postsynaptic potentials and residual correlation analyses to assess pathway strength and correlated place-cell activity, which is conceptually aligned with the plan's goal of relating synchronous CA1 activity to spatial tuning and place-field similarity. The weakest support is methodological precedent for chronic microdrive LFP recording and camera-based speed estimation, but this does not directly validate the proposed head-fixed air-lifted track, contralateral LFP, or Voltron2 voltage imaging.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not test the proposed contralateral hippocampal LFP or head-fixed air-lifted track design; it used a microdrive and track-based LFP in animals exploring, so it cannot confirm that contralateral LFP will capture the same theta, gamma, or ripple dynamics relevant to the imaged hemisphere [(Kemere et al., 2013-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab80b0678481be5791f8dca05f86215a7f31bd71). A second contradiction is that the study did not record subthreshold membrane voltages or use voltage imaging, so it provides no direct evidence for the plan's central claim that Voltron2-based subVms will reveal synchronous CA1 ensembles during novel exploration. A third contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, effect sizes, statistical significance tests, sampling rates, filter bands, electrode configuration, track dimensions, and speed-estimation details, which limits the ability to use this work to justify the proposed 2 kHz imaging, 532 nm excitation, jittered-control comparisons, CCG thresholds, or ripple and theta detection parameters. The weakest contradiction is that the reported LFP-level findings on gamma modulation, ripples, and place-cell correlations do not address the plan's specific 10-30 ms population synchrony hypothesis or the proposed idea that theta oscillations may suppress shorter-timescale synchrony.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative validation and statistical plan that compensates for the missing details in the related work: specify animal numbers, power analysis, effect sizes, significance thresholds, jittered-control construction preserving spike rates and autocorrelation, CCG bin sizes, sliding-window durations, false-discovery or permutation controls, and metrics for ensemble size and synchronization strength. The second most important action is to justify and pilot-test the contralateral LFP and head-fixed air-lifted track by showing that contralateral theta and ripple events are coherent with the imaged hemisphere or by adding ipsilateral or bilateral LFP where feasible, because the related work only supports microdrive LFP in a track and does not establish contralateral recording validity [(Kemere et al., 2013-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab80b0678481be5791f8dca05f86215a7f31bd71). The third action is to incorporate the related work's speed-dependent gamma and familiarity findings by pre-registering novelty and speed covariates, measuring gamma power modulation by speed and environmental familiarity, and including gamma as a covariate when relating synchronous ensembles to theta and ripples. The fourth action is to define precise oscillation detection and alignment parameters, including theta band, ripple threshold, ripple duration, phase binning, and event alignment windows, so that the proposed 10-30 ms synchrony analyses are reproducible. ## Context: Primary analysis of population synchrony, ensemble size, and statistical significance. Method: Detection and analysis of synchronous ensembles by counting spikes from all neurons within sliding windows and comparing to jittered controls. We will compare original data with jittered controls where spike timings are randomly perturbed while maintaining spike rates. We will quantify ensemble sizes, synchronization strength between cell pairs, and the temporal relationship between synchronous events and both theta oscillations and ripple events.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(McHugh et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9b6af61fc96c5654ebe9da3a7c8a90120a61c93), which used spontaneous spiking cross-correlation, theta-coupling, and theta-paced ensemble co-firing or population sparsity analyses in multichannel hippocampal recordings from mice, directly supporting the proposed use of pairwise spike timing, theta alignment, and population-level ensemble metrics in CA1 during memory-related behavior. Although the excerpt does not report bin sizes, sliding-window criteria, jittering algorithm, ripple detection, effect sizes, or statistical significance, its focus on theta-paced ensemble co-firing and sparsity is the closest methodological precedent for quantifying ensemble activity and relating it to memory. Next, [(Kelemen, and Fenton, 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41031b6980ef992346069baceb565b35c7333015) supports the proposed grand average CCGs and pairwise synchronization strength by showing that cross-correlation of CA1 spike trains in rats can identify temporal coordination among same-function neural groups, which aligns with the plan's goal of linking synchronization to spatial tuning and place-cell function. The excerpt lacks bin width, lag window, significance thresholds, jitter controls, and numerical CCG amplitudes, so it supports the general CCG approach but not the specific 10-30 ms sliding-window ensemble-size test. Weakest support is from [(Kreuz et al., 2007-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1693bd5a891f520881bad018b06bf04fddeec1aa), which evaluated event synchronization and other spike-train distance measures on simulated Hindmarsh-Rose and in vitro cortical recordings; this supports using multiple pairwise synchrony metrics and benchmarking them, but it does not implement sliding-window spike counting, rate-preserving jitter controls, ensemble-size estimation, or alignment to hippocampal theta/ripple, and it provides no quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpt.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is a validation gap rather than a direct negative result: none of the cited methods implements the proposed rate-preserving jittered-control comparison for sliding-window spike counting, so the central significance test is not supported by the related work. [(McHugh et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9b6af61fc96c5654ebe9da3a7c8a90120a61c93) uses cross-correlation, theta coupling, and ensemble co-firing but does not specify jittered controls, and [(Kelemen, and Fenton, 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41031b6980ef992346069baceb565b35c7333015) uses cross-correlation without reported jittered nulls, meaning observed synchrony could be confounded by firing rate, theta phase, speed, or shared network drive. Next, the related methods do not establish that short 10-30 ms ensemble sizes can be reliably extracted from the proposed voltage-imaging data; [(McHugh et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9b6af61fc96c5654ebe9da3a7c8a90120a61c93) and [(Kelemen, and Fenton, 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41031b6980ef992346069baceb565b35c7333015) rely on electrophysiological spike trains and do not report bin widths, window sizes, spike-detection thresholds, false-positive rates, animal numbers, neuron counts, or electrode specifications, while the plan uses Voltron2 fluorescence at 2 kHz, where a 10-30 ms window contains only 20-60 samples and spike detection or subthreshold noise may distort spike-count distributions. Weakest contradiction is that [(Kreuz et al., 2007-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1693bd5a891f520881bad018b06bf04fddeec1aa) evaluates pairwise distance metrics in simulated and in vitro cortical data rather than in vivo hippocampal theta/ripple contexts, so its metrics may not capture ensemble-size dynamics or oscillation-locked synchrony; additionally, its lack of quantitative outcomes prevents direct benchmarking of event synchronization against the proposed spike-count ensemble metric.

### Suggested Action

Most important: define and validate the jittered-control null before interpreting ensemble significance; preserve not only instantaneous or binned spike rates but also theta phase, speed, subthreshold correlation structure, and spike-train autocorrelation, and report ensemble-size distributions, CCG peak amplitudes, and false-positive rates relative to null. Add explicit sliding-window parameters, for example 10, 20, and 30 ms windows with 1-5 ms steps, and threshold criteria for synchronous events, and benchmark the spike-count ensemble metric against pairwise synchrony measures from [(Kreuz et al., 2007-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1693bd5a891f520881bad018b06bf04fddeec1aa) such as event synchronization, Victor-Purpura, Van Rossum, Schreiber, and Hunter-Milton on simulated and, if available, electrophysiological ground-truth data. Next, incorporate the CCG and spatial-function logic of [(Kelemen, and Fenton, 2010-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41031b6980ef992346069baceb565b35c7333015) by reporting bin width, lag range, significance thresholds, and place-field similarity comparisons, and test whether pairwise synchronization strength predicts place-field overlap beyond chance. Finally, use the theta-paced ensemble and population-sparsity framework of [(McHugh et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9b6af61fc96c5654ebe9da3a7c8a90120a61c93) to separate theta-locked co-firing from short-timescale synchrony by binning synchronous events by theta phase, speed, and ripple presence, and by comparing ensemble sparsity or dimensionality across locomotion and immobility; also specify ripple detection thresholds and validate that ripple-aligned ensembles are not artifacts of high-rate offline periods.

## Context: Spatial memory analysis linking place-cell tuning to synchronous ensemble participation. Method: Assessment of spatial tuning properties to identify place cells and analyze their relationship to synchronous activity. Finally, we will examine the spatial coding properties of neurons participating in synchronous ensembles, particularly focusing on how synchronization strength relates to the similarity of place field tuning between neuron pairs.

### Support

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that [(Trettel et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41426d063e66b8b89ca4a123bde1b219158fb45b) does not provide quantitative evidence that place-field similarity predicts CA1 place-cell synchrony during memory acquisition; it reports grid-cell co-activation and relative spatial phase or distance effects, and it notes that preserved grid-cell correlation structure was not explained by hippocampal place-cell correlation structure during sleep, so the proposed place-cell ensemble hypothesis remains only indirectly supported by this source.
