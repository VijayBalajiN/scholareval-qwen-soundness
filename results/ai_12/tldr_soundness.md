## Strengths 
The strongest support is for a StarCraft II LLM benchmark that combines direct LLM-SC2 evidence, structured decision logging, and multi-opponent repeated evaluation. [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) supports moving beyond win rate by using Average Population Utilization, repeated 100-game samples, and a chain-of-summarization pipeline that separates high-level game-state interpretation from low-level command generation. [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) supports the two-level strategic/tactical architecture through scouting, threat response, reinforcement, and phase-specific production. [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) supports decision tracking, memory/reflection/planning ablations, and statistical comparison of agent behavior. [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb) supports tech-tree milestones and resource-threshold metrics, while [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd) and [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) support opponent-specific adaptation, repeated runs, and hierarchical macro/micro evaluation.

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the unvalidated real-time, adaptability, and fine-grained metric claims. [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) evaluates only fixed level-5 AI, uses win rate and Average Population Utilization, and finds that fine-tuned models can win while collapsing to mass-producing stalkers, so win rate and coarse economy metrics may mask strategic homogeneity. [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778) are largely qualitative and omit win rates, latency, statistical tests, and reproducibility, so they do not validate rapid tactical response or phase-based metrics. [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b) shows that sparse full-game rewards and easy fixed opponents can produce degenerate strategies such as trivial mining or building-lifting, warning that win-rate trends alone may measure avoidance rather than adaptation. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) and [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) show that difficulty-only variation and aggregate win rates can hide opponent-specific failures and high-variance transfer, so the proposed Macro/Rush/Random, synchronous/asynchronous, and phase protocol must be explicitly operationalized and statistically powered.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Implement an explicit two-level agent and structured decision log: maintain a high-level strategic summary containing resources, supply, technology, enemy intel, and objectives, and a low-level command queue, then log every decision in JSON or SQLite with timestamp, phase, model, prompt, raw output, action type, context, outcome, latency, and RPM/SUR/APM/EPM/APU, following the prompt fields in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), the scouting/defense/production categories in [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), and the memory-stream verification and hallucination-check logic in [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81).

Run a controlled opponent-and-phase matrix: create explicitly labeled Macro, Rush, and Random opponents, define Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late bins by objective triggers such as elapsed time, supply, base count, and tech level, and first run a 20-30 game pilot per model-opponent-mode condition to estimate latency and variance, then scale to at least 100 games for final comparisons; report per-strategy win rate, Elo, robustness as one minus minimum win rate, confidence intervals, and significance tests, extending the 100-game protocol in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), the robustness metric in [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), and the phase transitions observed in [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5).

Validate the proposed metrics with targeted probes and ablations: add rapid-response probes measuring time from threat/opportunity detection to first relevant action, tech-tree milestones such as first expansion, tech lab, and air units, and diversity metrics such as build-order sequence distance and strategy innovation rate to detect mass-production collapse; ablate memory, planning, summarization depth, and skill libraries, and compare against built-in AI, random, and tactical baselines using nonparametric or mixed-effects tests, following the ablation/statistical design in [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), the tech-tree milestones in [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb), and the diversity warning in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58).
