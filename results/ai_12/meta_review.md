## We will develop StarCraft II Arena, a comprehensive benchmark framework that evaluates LLMs across three core dimensions of strategic reasoning capability:
Synthesis: This is the central system design that creates the benchmark and frames the evaluation around strategic planning, real-time decision making, and adaptability.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), which directly evaluates LLMs in TextStarCraft II against built-in AI level 5 and shows that closed-source models can defeat level 5, while un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B failed to generate commands and fine-tuned open-source models defeated level 5 but collapsed into reduced strategic diversity, predominantly mass-producing stalkers; its data-quality analysis, where fine-tuning on top 25% Average Population Utilization games yielded 54/100 wins versus 28/100 on the full dataset, supports the proposed claim that win rate alone is insufficient and that resource/supply metrics such as Average Population Utilization should be part of a fine-grained benchmark. A second strong support is [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), which evaluates LLM generative agents in Smallville with controlled ablations, interview probes, memory-stream verification, TrueSkill ratings, Kruskal-Wallis tests, and Dunn post-hoc tests, demonstrating that LLM agent behavior can be decomposed into measurable components and that full architecture outperformed ablated conditions with TrueSkill mean 29.89 versus 21.21 for the fully ablated condition and significant differences across conditions; this supports the proposed decision tracking, qualitative analysis, and multi-dimensional evaluation, although the environment is cooperative rather than adversarial. A third support is [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797), which benchmarks GPT-3.5-turbo-16k, GPT-4, and LLaMa-2 across 20 social-dilemma scenarios with 300 initializations each, using logit regression, dominance analysis, and difference-in-proportions Z-tests, showing that LLM strategic choice can be measured with fine-grained behavioral metrics and statistical tests rather than a single success rate; this supports the proposed multi-LLM comparison and adaptability/context sensitivity, but only in static one-shot games. Additional support comes from [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb), which uses GPT-4 in Minecraft with a skill library and iterative prompting, reporting tech-tree mastery over three trials and map coverage, supporting the use of task-specific LLM agent metrics in game environments, though without opponents or real-time competition. SC2-specific RL work also supports the two-level POMDP framing: [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) uses a hierarchical controller and sub-policies in SC2LE, reaches 0.93 win rate against level-7 built-in AI, and shows that built-in AI difficulty ladders, curriculum, and iteration-level curves are useful evaluation scaffolds; [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd) trains a macro-level StarCraft II agent with Ape-X DQN and DRQN, achieves 83 percent win rate over 28 bots on 10 maps, and demonstrates that macro decisions at 10-second intervals can be evaluated against diverse opponents. Weaker but relevant support is provided by [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which show that LLM-generated high-level strategies and Chain of Summarization pipelines can be coupled to StarCraft II control, but they do not yet provide the quantitative benchmark evidence needed for the proposed arena.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is not that fine-grained metrics are unnecessary, but that existing LLM StarCraft II evidence is too narrow and brittle to support broad claims about strategic planning, real-time decision making, and adaptability. [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) shows that fine-tuned open-source models can defeat level-5 built-in AI but exhibit reduced strategic diversity by mass-producing stalkers, and that un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B could not understand task requirements, implying that LLM performance may depend heavily on prompt format, fine-tuning data quality, and model capability rather than general strategic reasoning; its evaluation also relies mainly on win rate and Average Population Utilization without the proposed RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, decision tracking, or statistical significance tests. A second contradiction is that LLM StarCraft II systems are often reported without reproducible quantitative outcomes: [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) describes 30 experiments on Automaton LE against Terran computer opponents but does not provide numeric win rates, ablations, latency, or fine-grained decision metrics, and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778) presents a Chain of Summarization architecture without model size, hardware, opponent types, map settings, or quantitative results, which challenges the assumption that current LLM SC2 agent work can be directly compared in a comprehensive arena. A third contradiction is that the strongest LLM-agent evaluation evidence comes from non-RTS or static settings: [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) evaluates a cooperative social sandbox with human interviews and TrueSkill, not real-time tactical execution or opponent adaptation; [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb) evaluates open-ended Minecraft exploration with high-level APIs and no opponent; and [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797) evaluates one-shot 2x2 games without sequential adaptation, imperfect information, or real-time constraints. Finally, SC2 RL work shows that robust strategic agents require substantial training and evaluation infrastructure: [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) reports full SC2 training runs of 2 to 3 days and uses 100 games per level repeated three times, while [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd) uses 1000 actors for 24 hours in a controlled case and 8000 games for broader evaluation; this does not contradict the proposed LLM benchmark, but it contradicts the feasibility of lightweight evaluation and highlights that the proposed arena must specify compute, opponent difficulty, map coverage, and statistical reliability to avoid underpowered comparisons.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to build the arena on a reproducible TextStarCraft II or SC2LE interface and immediately extend the coarse metrics from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) by logging the proposed RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, unit construction order, key building completion time, and decision context, while using built-in AI level 5 as a baseline and adding higher difficulties and opponent strategies such as Macro, Rush, and Random. The second priority is to adopt the ablation and statistical design of [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), including full versus no-planning, no-reflection, no-observation, and no-memory conditions, repeated runs, human or LLM judges, TrueSkill or equivalent ranking, Kruskal-Wallis tests, Dunn post-hoc tests, and effect sizes, so that the three proposed dimensions are not only named but causally isolated. Third, incorporate the controlled strategic-probe design of [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797) by creating SC2-specific game-theoretic or scenario probes with repeated initializations, context variables, and logit or dominance analyses to test whether models distinguish strategic contexts and adapt to opponent behavior. Fourth, use the SC2LE evaluation protocol from [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) to specify map, race, opponent difficulty ladder, number of games per condition, repeated trials, compute budget, and training or prompting curves, and report win-rate trends rather than only final win rates. Fifth, borrow the macro-level evaluation structure of [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd), including diverse opponent sets, multiple maps, and macro decision intervals, but replace RL replay memory with LLM decision logs and per-opponent adaptability metrics. Sixth, integrate the skill-library and iterative prompting ideas from [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb) to support real-time tactical execution and self-verification, while ensuring that the benchmark still measures opponent-facing behavior rather than only open-ended exploration. Finally, require the summarization and low-level controller components from [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778) to be evaluated with quantitative win rates, latency, action quality, and ablations, so that architectural claims are tied to the proposed fine-grained metrics.

## Our approach models the StarCraft II environment as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) with two-level inference: high-level strategic planning and low-level tactical execution.
Synthesis: This formalizes the StarCraft II setting and separates long-horizon strategy from immediate tactical execution.

### Support

The strongest support for modeling StarCraft II as a two-level POMDP comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), which evaluated LLM agents in TextStarCraft II using a Chain of Summarization pipeline that first performs single-frame and multi-frame summarization and then extracts an action queue, directly approximating the proposed separation between high-level strategic interpretation and low-level tactical command generation. That work also shows that closed-source LLMs and fine-tuned open-source models such as ChatGLM3 6B and Qwen 1.8B can defeat the level 5 built-in AI, while un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B failed to generate commands, and that fine-tuning on top 25% APU wins yielded 54/100 versus 28/100 on the full dataset, indicating that high-level game-state summarization and data quality matter for LLM strategic performance. The next strongest support is [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), whose SwarmBrain agent explicitly separates GPT-3.5-turbo strategic reasoning in the Overmind Brain from lower-level tactical execution through Swarm ReflexNet, and uses periodic scouting with Overlords or Zerglings to update prompts with enemy mineral-field intel, which aligns with the proposed POMDP belief-state and high-level planning components. [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) supports the strategic-planning dimension by modeling macro-management as sequence prediction over observation histories with a two-GRU network, linear layers of 1024 and 2048, GRU sizes 2048 and 512, and sigmoid win-probability output, trained on 36,619 filtered replays with APM above 10 and MMR above 1000, showing that high-level strategic state can be inferred from partial observations. [(Rashid et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffc211476f2e40e79466ffc198c919a97da3bb76) supports the low-level tactical execution dimension by showing that decentralized micromanagement policies can be evaluated separately on maps such as 3m, 5m, 8m, 3s5z3s5z, and 1c3s5z using 20 independent runs and 95% confidence intervals, which justifies treating tactical execution as a distinct capability. [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) supports the benchmarking context by providing BC and FT-BC reference agents trained on 21 billion internal game steps and 3.5 billion observations, evaluated against seven opponents with Elo and robustness defined as one minus the minimum win rate, and by showing that high-quality fine-tuning and action-step compression with delay features improve low-level action policies. Finally, [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b) supports the use of StarCraft II as a challenging sequential-decision environment and the value of mini-games such as MoveToBeacon, CollectMineralShards, FindAndDefeatZerglings, DefeatRoaches, DefeatZerglingsAndBanelings, CollectMineralsAndGas, and BuildMarines, while also documenting APM around 180, 600M agent steps, 64 asynchronous A3C threads, and 100 hyperparameter runs, which are useful constraints for the proposed benchmark.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most direct LLM evidence, [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), does not demonstrate that two-level inference produces robust strategic planning or adaptability; fine-tuned open-source models defeated the level 5 built-in AI but showed reduced strategic diversity, mainly mass-producing stalkers, and the evaluation relied on win rate and Average Population Utilization rather than the proposed fine-grained resource, supply, action, and decision-tracking metrics. This suggests that a high-level summarization plus low-level command pipeline may still collapse into narrow tactics unless explicitly constrained and measured. The next strongest limitation is [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b), where A3C agents with sparse ternary win/loss/tie rewards in the full game failed to develop viable strategies, and agents trained with Blizzard score rewards converged to trivial mineral-mining behavior, including in the economic mini-game; this contradicts the assumption that high-level strategic planning will emerge automatically from a POMDP formulation without explicit strategic rewards, mini-game decomposition, and fine-grained monitoring of APM, building completion, and resource collection. [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) also limits the proposed method by showing that many offline RL baselines did not beat BC and FT-BC, and that restricting training to fewer higher-quality episodes produced poorer performance, which implies that LLM or agent fine-tuning in StarCraft II is sensitive to data quality and that aggregate Elo/robustness metrics may not expose the strategic planning, real-time decision-making, and adaptability dimensions proposed in the research plan. [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) provides a strategic win-probability baseline but reports no quantitative accuracy, AUC, loss, or win-rate prediction performance in the supplied excerpt, so it does not provide strong evidence that high-level strategic state can be reliably inferred from partial observations. [(Rashid et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffc211476f2e40e79466ffc198c919a97da3bb76) contradicts the completeness of the proposed two-level model only by scope: it validates low-level micromanagement and win-rate evaluation but does not address high-level strategy, resource management, LLM inference, or fine-grained capability metrics. Finally, [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) reports 30 experiments on Automaton LE against Terran computer opponents from Very Easy to Hard but provides no numerical win rates, match times, baselines, or statistical significance, so its qualitative description of scouting, reinforcement, and late-game air-ground composition is insufficient to confirm that the proposed POMDP two-level inference improves measurable performance.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the two-level POMDP inference as an explicit architecture rather than an informal description: maintain a high-level strategic belief state containing resources, supply, technology, enemy intel, and macro objectives, and a low-level tactical policy for unit control, using a Chain of Summarization-style pipeline inspired by [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) and an Overmind/ReflexNet-style separation inspired by [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), while logging both high-level summaries and low-level commands to test whether the separation improves strategic coherence. Second, replace win-rate-only evaluation with the proposed fine-grained metrics and add APU from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), robustness from [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), and mini-game isolation from [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b); specifically report RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, unit construction order, key building completion time, strategy innovation rate, and decision-context/outcome logs, with statistical tests and 95% confidence intervals following the 20-run, 20-episode protocol in [(Rashid et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffc211476f2e40e79466ffc198c919a97da3bb76). Third, build a reference-agent ladder using BC/FT-BC opponents from [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), built-in AI levels from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) and [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), and QMIX-style tactical baselines from [(Rashid et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ffc211476f2e40e79466ffc198c919a97da3bb76), testing Macro, Rush, and Random opponents across Early, Mid, and Mid-to-Late phases. Fourth, use the macro-management replay pipeline from [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) to create a strategic-state evaluator or auxiliary reward signal, filtering replays for matches longer than 10,000 frames, APM above 10, and MMR above 1000, and training a GRU win-probability model with batch size 256, time-step size 20, Adam learning rate 0.001, and learning-rate halving per epoch. Fifth, address computational constraints by using action-step compression and delay features from [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), prompt complexity levels LV1 through LV6 from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), and scouting-based belief updates from [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), while explicitly ablating chain-of-thought or summarization depth to determine whether the two-level structure is causally responsible for performance gains.

## We will assess long-term resource management, technological advancement planning, and overall strategic coherence.
Synthesis: This operationalizes the strategic planning dimension by measuring long-horizon resource and technology choices.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb), because it directly operationalizes technological advancement planning and long-horizon resource acquisition in an LLM agent by measuring iterations to unlock Wooden, Stone, Iron, and Diamond Tool levels, with 3/3 success for early and mid levels, 1/3 success for Diamond Tool at 102 iterations, and 0/3 Diamond success without the skill library, while using GPT-4-0314, GPT-3.5-turbo-0301, text-embedding-ada-002, temperature 0 except curriculum at 0.1, and a maximum of 160 iterations over three runs; this validates the research plan emphasis on technology sequencing, resource thresholds, and strategic coherence rather than final win rate alone. Next, [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) supports the StarCraft II-specific relevance by evaluating LLM macro-strategic decision-making in TextStarCraft II against built-in AI level 5, using GPT3.5-turbo-16k, Llama2 70B, ChatGLM3 6B, and Qwen 1.8B, and showing that fine-tuning on top 25% Average Population Utilization wins produced 54/100 win rate versus 28/100 for the full dataset, indicating that macro resource and population metrics are informative even though the paper does not directly measure technology timing or strategic coherence. A further quantitative analog is [(Zhou et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06df762adcc4221162e83a737ea63cff47e65d), which uses programmatic resource metrics such as seeds, wood, dirt, travel distance, and digging depth over 10 trials with a 3,000-frame limit and command-switching tasks with 50 trials, and reports large multiplicative gains such as 64 times more seeds, 7 times more wood, and 41 times more dirt versus VPT(rl), supporting the proposed use of fine-grained resource and construction metrics rather than only success rates. Then, [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) provides direct StarCraft II LLM evidence of long-horizon resource management and technology planning in a Zerg agent using gpt-3.5-turbo, python-sc2, scouting, drone production, expansion, and air-ground technology, although it reports only 30 experiments on Automaton LE against Terran Computer difficulties and no quantitative win rates. Supporting this are [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) and [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8), which show that StarCraft macro-management can be represented through buildings, units, techniques, resources, observed enemy state, and phase-specific economic, military, and map-coverage features, with Wu et al. using 36,619 filtered replays from 64,396 original replays, PySC2, and a GRU global-state baseline with linear layers of 1024 and 2048, GRU layers of 2048 and 512, Adam learning rate 0.001, batch size 256, and timestep 20, while Erickson and Buro use about 400 amateur Protoss replays, a custom C++ BWAPI parser, logistic regression with game-level 10-fold cross-validation, 5-minute intervals, and accuracy plus average log-likelihood. Further analogical support comes from [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797), which demonstrates that LLM strategic coherence can be assessed statistically with 300 initializations per scenario, 18,000 total observations, logit regression, dominance analysis, and difference-in-proportions Z-tests, although in one-shot social dilemmas rather than real-time StarCraft. The weakest support is [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which aligns conceptually with multi-frame summarization for strategic decisions but provides no quantitative outcomes, baselines, or implementation details.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that fine-grained macro metrics may not capture strategic coherence if agents exploit narrow strategies: [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) found that fine-tuned open-source models defeated level 5 TextStarCraft II AI but relied predominantly on a mass-produce-stalkers strategy, and that top 25% Average Population Utilization training improved win rate from 28/100 to 54/100, suggesting that APU and win rate can reward exploitative macro behavior rather than broad strategic planning. A second strong contradiction is that long-horizon technology planning is brittle in LLM agents: [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb) achieved Diamond Tool only 1/3 with 102 iterations and 0/3 without the skill library, while AutoGPT required 92 plus or minus 72 iterations for Wooden Tool, 94 plus or minus 72 for Stone Tool, and 135 plus or minus 103 for Iron Tool, and ReAct and Reflexion failed all four tech tree levels, contradicting the assumption that LLMs will reliably demonstrate coherent technology advancement without scaffolding. Third, resource-collection gains do not automatically translate into long-horizon task success: [(Zhou et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06df762adcc4221162e83a737ea63cff47e65d) reported large multiplicative improvements in seeds, wood, dirt, travel, and digging, yet command-switching success was only 10 percent for MineDreamer versus 0 percent for VPT(rl) and STEVE-1(text) on the diamond task and 2 percent for Multi-Modal Memory, showing that RPM-like resource metrics can be decoupled from strategic completion. Fourth, direct StarCraft II LLM evidence remains under-quantified: [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) reports 30 experiments on Automaton LE against Terran Computer difficulties but no win rates, baselines, significance tests, hardware details, prompt hyperparameters, or fine-grained metrics, weakening confidence that the proposed metrics can be validated in real StarCraft II. Fifth, phase-specific strategic metrics can be unstable: [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8) found that late-game beta-variance lost accuracy because many late-game battles contained unit types unsupported by the simulator, that the UC feature was prone to overfitting with small training sets, and that final score was obfuscated, indicating that short-interval and late-game measurements need careful validation. Sixth, one-shot strategic coherence may not transfer to real-time long-horizon play: [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797) showed context-dependent cooperation and game-structure effects in 2x2 dilemmas with temperature 0.8, 300 initializations per scenario, and response caps, but it does not measure resource management, technology planning, or real-time adaptation. Seventh, large StarCraft macro baselines are not fully validated: [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) provides 36,619 filtered replays and a GRU architecture but no numerical baseline performance, exact split sizes, hardware, or LLM evaluation. The weakest contradiction is [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which lacks quantitative outcomes, model details, opponent types, maps, and evaluation metrics, so it cannot substantiate the proposed evaluation.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed method should convert technological advancement planning into explicit StarCraft II tech-tree milestones, following [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb): define required resource thresholds, building completion times, and unit or technology unlock events such as first expansion, tech lab, air units, and late-game units, then report time-to-milestone, actions-to-milestone, success over at least three runs, and ablations of memory or skill libraries to isolate strategic coherence. Second, it should adopt fine-grained programmatic resource and construction metrics inspired by [(Zhou et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06df762adcc4221162e83a737ea63cff47e65d) and [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa), including Resource Management Ability, Supply Utilization Rate, Average Population Utilization, mineral and vespene collection, building completion times, supply used, observed enemy state, and phase-specific early, mid, and late windows with multiple trials per scenario. Third, it should add statistical controls from [(Lorè, and Heydari, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e46db119b320df6ac4a5091e1561c54c5aece797): use repeated initializations, report confidence intervals, use logit or mixed-effects models, dominance analysis, and difference-in-proportions tests to compare models, opponents, and contexts, and avoid treating small differences as meaningful. Fourth, it should validate metrics against replay-derived baselines and avoid overfitting, using the feature schema and game-level cross-validation logic of [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) and [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8), including quality filters, phase intervals, sequence-model baselines for global state, and explicit handling of late-game unit-type coverage gaps. Fifth, it should incorporate LLM agent design lessons from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), such as chain-of-summarization, scouting, operational strategy, prompt complexity, and APU-based data quality, while adding diversity and innovation metrics to prevent narrow mass-production strategies. Finally, it should report reproducibility details missing in prior work: model versions, temperatures, hardware, map set, opponent difficulties, run counts, and failure modes, especially because [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778) lack quantitative validation.

## We will evaluate the model's ability to process dynamic information rapidly and adjust tactics in response to immediate threats or opportunities.
Synthesis: This operationalizes the real-time decision-making dimension by testing rapid tactical response under changing conditions.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), because it directly evaluates LLM agents in TextStarCraft II using a Chain of Summarization pipeline with single-frame summarization, multi-frame summarization, and action extraction for an action queue, showing that closed-source LLMs defeated the level-5 built-in AI and fine-tuned open-source models also defeated level-5 AI, with fine-tuning on top-25% Average Population Utilization wins reaching 54/100 versus 28/100 for the full dataset. This supports the proposed real-time decision-making method by demonstrating that LLMs can consume summarized dynamic observations and emit StarCraft commands, and that win rate alone can mask reduced strategic diversity, motivating the plan's fine-grained metrics such as APM, EPM, SUR, and decision tracking. Next, [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) provides a qualitative precedent for rapid tactical adjustment: SwarmBrain used GPT-3.5-turbo to scout, mobilized idle Roaches and Zerglings when enemy pressure threatened A2, reinforced A2 with Hydralisks after earlier losses, and later shifted to an air-ground composition and Ultralisk production, which aligns with the plan's threat/opportunity scenarios and decision-context tracking. Although it lacks latency, APM, EPM, and statistical tests, it supports the feasibility of evaluating tactical response under changing conditions. Weaker but still relevant support is [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), which shows that low-level tactical execution can be isolated in a hierarchical agent, where a combat sub-policy trained with PPO, entropy bonus, truncated GAE, per-network replay buffers, and curriculum learning improved win rate from 0.03 random macro baseline to 0.93 on level 7 and reached 0.79, 0.87, and 0.86 on cheating levels 8, 9, and 10, with best practices reaching 0.96, 0.97, and 0.94; this supports the plan's two-level POMDP framing and controlled difficulty/opponent scenarios, but it is RL rather than LLM and does not measure rapid response. The weakest support is [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which conceptually supports using chain-of-summarization to convert raw observations into single-frame and multi-frame summaries for command generation, but provides no quantitative outcomes, model specs, latency, or evaluation metrics.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the most direct LLM StarCraft evidence does not validate rapid tactical response and instead exposes brittleness: [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) evaluated LLMs only against level-5 built-in AI with win rate and Average Population Utilization, did not measure reaction latency, APM, EPM, or threat-response time, and found Llama2 70B failed to generate usable commands while fine-tuned open-source models won but reduced strategic diversity by mass-producing stalkers; this contradicts the assumption that LLMs can be assumed to process dynamic information rapidly and adapt tactically without explicit real-time instrumentation. Next, [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) contradicts quantitative claims of rapid decision-making because it reports only qualitative tactical sequences, no win rates, average match times, latency, action frequency, or statistical significance, so its examples cannot establish that GPT-3.5-turbo reliably responds to immediate threats under real-time constraints. Weaker contradiction comes from [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), which is not an LLM study but shows that strong tactical performance in StarCraft II can be obtained through a specialized hierarchical RL combat sub-policy, curriculum learning, and alternate updating, with win rate as the primary metric; this suggests that LLM-only evaluation may conflate strategic language ability with missing low-level tactical competence, and that win rate may be sufficient for some tactical comparisons, potentially challenging the plan's emphasis on process metrics unless the LLM is paired with or compared against a tactical execution baseline. The weakest contradiction is [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which offers no quantitative evidence that summarization-based LLM agents improve rapid tactical adjustment, making it insufficient to support the proposed real-time evaluation beyond conceptual motivation.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed method should add explicit rapid-response probes and latency-aware metrics: instrument time from threat/opportunity detection to first relevant action, action queue delay, inference latency, APM, EPM, and effective threat-response rate, and report these alongside win rate, because [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) and [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) show current LLM StarCraft evaluations lack these measures. Second, adopt and ablate a chain-of-summarization or multi-frame observation pipeline, as in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), but test whether summaries preserve threat-relevant cues such as enemy unit type, location, timing, and supply pressure, and compare prompt complexity against raw or compressed observations. Third, include baselines that separate LLM reasoning from tactical execution, such as built-in AI, random macro, a hierarchical RL combat sub-policy or PPO tactical agent inspired by [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), and fine-tuned LLMs, to avoid attributing failures to LLM reasoning when the issue is command generation or low-level execution; also report model-specific failures such as Llama2 70B's inability to generate usable commands. Fourth, use controlled threat/opportunity scenarios with known ground truth, multiple runs, and statistical tests, including opponent strategies Macro, Rush, and Random, difficulty levels, and synchronous/asynchronous modes, to quantify adaptability and avoid cherry-picked qualitative trajectories. Fifth, add diversity and decision-trajectory metrics, such as unit construction order, key building completion time, strategy innovation rate, and reduced-diversity checks, because [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) found fine-tuned models can win while collapsing to stalker mass production. Finally, document hardware, model size, token budget, action queue capacity, and latency constraints to ensure reproducibility, since none of the related works provide sufficient system-level detail for real-time LLM evaluation.

## We will measure the model's flexibility in adjusting strategies based on opponent behavior, environmental changes, and previous feedback.
Synthesis: This operationalizes the adaptability dimension by testing strategy revision in response to opponents and feedback.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd), because it operationalizes strategy revision in the same StarCraft II domain by training an Ape-X DQN with a Deep Recurrent Q-Network, opponent-specific replay memory, a 10-second LSTM interval, double Q-learning, prioritized replay, and a duel network, then evaluating against six bots on one map and 28 bots across 10 maps over 8000 games, achieving an 83% win rate and outperforming 26 of 28 opponents while showing distinct counterstrategies against Ximp versus Skynet. This directly supports the plan's use of multiple opponent strategies, opponent-specific context, win-rate trends, and qualitative strategy comparison. The next strongest support is [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), because it is the most directly LLM-relevant evidence that memory, reflection, planning, and prior experience can be measured with fine-grained behavioral probes rather than only final success. In its controlled stage, 100 human evaluators ranked responses from the full architecture, ablations, and a crowdworker baseline, yielding TrueSkill means of 29.89 for the full architecture, 26.88 for no-reflection, 25.64 for no-reflection-and-no-planning, 22.95 for crowdworkers, and 21.21 for the fully ablated condition, with Kruskal-Wallis H(4) = 150.29, p < 0.001, and Cohen's d of 8.16 versus no-memory; end-to-end, information diffusion rose from 4% to 32% for mayoral candidacy and from 4% to 52% for party knowledge, network density rose from 0.167 to 0.74, and only 1.3% of 453 awareness responses were hallucinated. This supports the plan's decision tracking, qualitative analysis, interviews, memory-stream verification, and ablation-based assessment of LLM adaptability, although the setting is a social sandbox rather than StarCraft II. The third support is [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), which extends the SC2 adaptability question to environmental changes and feedback by using a hierarchical PPO agent, curriculum learning, sparse outcome-only rewards, and cheating built-in AIs at levels 8, 9, and 10, reaching final win rates of 0.96, 0.97, and 0.94 and using best-model selection based on observed win-rate improvement. This supports the plan's inclusion of environmental changes, previous feedback, and trend-based evaluation. The weakest support is [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b), which shows that A3C agents in StarCraft II with sparse ternary win/loss/tie rewards, 64 asynchronous threads, 600M agent steps, and a fixed easiest AI did not develop a viable full-game strategy, with the best agent merely lifting buildings to avoid loss and Blizzard-score agents preserving trivial mineral mining. This supports the plan's need for fine-grained metrics, multiple opponents, and explicit tracking of strategic revision rather than relying on survival or ties.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction to the proposed method is [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), because it found that transfer from a non-cheating level-7 model to cheating levels 8, 9, and 10 was weak and sometimes degraded performance, while training from scratch on cheating levels produced an upward but high-variance trend, and that simultaneous updating could remain stuck near 0.4 win rate whereas alternate updating from a checkpoint reached 0.8. This contradicts the expectation that previous feedback or prior strategy will smoothly transfer when the environment changes adversarially, and it implies the plan must explicitly separate transfer, re-planning, and from-scratch adaptation rather than treating all improvement as flexibility. The next strongest contradiction is [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b), because it demonstrates that in StarCraft II, sparse win/loss/tie feedback and a fixed easy opponent can produce no viable strategy, only trivial avoidance behavior such as lifting buildings, and that Blizzard-score training converged to preserving initial mineral mining rather than building units or structures. This contradicts any assumption that a win-rate trend or error-rate trend alone, especially against a single easy opponent or with 30-minute ties, is sufficient evidence of strategic flexibility; if the LLM Arena uses similar sparse, fixed, or tie-prone conditions, it may measure avoidance heuristics rather than adaptation. The third contradiction is [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd), whose broader evaluation disabled opponents' online learning strategies and created games as soon as possible, with the authors noting potential performance drop in round-robin conditions; this suggests that adaptability measured against static bots may overestimate an agent's ability to adjust to non-stationary opponents, so the plan's Macro, Rush, and Random opponents should include dynamic or online-learning variants if feasible. The weakest contradiction is [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), because although it shows LLM agents can adjust social behavior through memory, reflection, and planning, it does not evaluate real-time strategic decision making, resource management, win rate, or competitive StarCraft II performance, so its positive LLM adaptability evidence cannot be directly extrapolated to the proposed Arena without domain-specific validation.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed method should implement an opponent-specific adaptability protocol that directly addresses the evidence from [(Xu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ceaee39e1d40bf781f54f4f99601f2e115a460bd) and [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b): test each LLM against multiple opponent strategies such as Macro, Rush, and Random, across multiple maps and repeated runs, with opponent-specific context or memory where the model is allowed to retain prior observations, and evaluate with a fixed sampling or zero-exploration equivalent rather than uncontrolled stochasticity. The evaluation should record win-rate trend, error-rate trend, RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, unit construction order, key building completion time, and strategy innovation rate, and it should explicitly compare counterstrategies across opponents, as Xu et al. did for Ximp versus Skynet, while avoiding the Vinyals et al. failure mode of treating building-lift avoidance or 30-minute ties as successful adaptation. Second, the method should add environmental-change and feedback conditions inspired by [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), including information asymmetries, resource advantages, or cheating-opponent analogues, and a feedback loop in which the model receives previous game outcomes and can update its prompt, memory, or strategy template; the plan should measure transfer from prior feedback separately from from-scratch adaptation, use best-model or best-checkpoint selection based on observed win-rate improvement, and report variance across runs because Liu et al. observed high variance and weak transfer. Third, the method should incorporate LLM-specific qualitative evaluation from [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), such as post-game interviews, memory-stream verification, ablations of memory, reflection, and planning, human ranking of strategic explanations, and hallucination checks on claimed awareness of opponent behavior; statistical reporting should include effect sizes and significance tests analogous to TrueSkill, Kruskal-Wallis, Dunn post-hoc tests, and Cohen's d. Fourth, the method should include mini-games or dense intermediate metrics to prevent trivial strategies, following the Vinyals et al. observation that sparse full-game rewards produced non-viable strategies, so the Arena should complement full-game win rates with isolated resource management, rush defense, expansion timing, and macro execution tasks. Finally, the plan should specify reproducibility details such as model versions, temperature, game speed, map set, opponent set, number of games, seeds, hardware or inference budget, synchronous versus asynchronous latency, and confidence intervals, because the related work shows that computational constraints, opponent selection, and evaluation protocol strongly affect measured adaptability.

## We will conduct comprehensive evaluations of multiple state-of-the-art LLMs, including both proprietary models (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, GPT-3.5 Turbo, Gemini 1.5 Flash) and open-source models (DeepSeek-V2.5, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct).
Synthesis: This is the main empirical comparison across model families and scales.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed multi-model StarCraft II Arena comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), which directly evaluates multiple LLMs in TextStarCraft II using multi-frame observations and a Chain of Summarization pipeline for single-frame summarization, multi-frame summarization, and action extraction. It compares closed-source LLMs, un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B, and fine-tuned ChatGLM3 6B and Qwen 1.8B against built-in AI level 5, using win rate and Average Population Utilization (APU). Its finding that closed-source models and fine-tuned open-source models defeated level 5 while un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B failed to generate commands supports the proposed plan's core premise that model family, scale, prompting, and training data produce materially different strategic-agent behavior. The APU metric is also directly relevant to the proposed Resource Management Ability and Supply Utilization Rate metrics, and the 100-game data-quality comparison, where fine-tuning on top 25 percent APU wins yielded 54/100 versus 28/100 on the full dataset, supports the need for repeated runs and quality-controlled evaluation. As a later StarCraft II-specific study, Ma extends the broader LLM-agent evaluation logic of [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb), which evaluated VOYAGER in MineDojo/Mineflayer Minecraft using GPT-4-0314, GPT-3.5-turbo-0301, text-embedding-ada-002, automatic curriculum, skill library, and iterative self-verified code generation. Although not StarCraft II, Wang's use of tech-tree mastery, map coverage, zero-shot generalization, three trials, and prompting-iteration efficiency up to 160 iterations supports the proposed shift from binary win rates to fine-grained behavioral and efficiency metrics. Its quantitative results, such as 3/3 success for wooden, stone, and iron tools with 6 +/- 2, 11 +/- 2, and 21 +/- 7 iterations versus 0/3 for ReAct and Reflexion, show that prompting architecture and self-verification can strongly affect task completion and should be controlled in the proposed Arena. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), which uses GPT-3.5 Turbo as the Overmind Intelligence Matrix in a python-sc2 StarCraft II agent playing Zerg against Terran on Automaton LE across Very Easy to Hard difficulties. Its qualitative reports of scouting, threat response, reinforcement, air-ground composition, and Ultralisk production support the feasibility of evaluating GPT-3.5 Turbo on the proposed strategic planning, real-time decision making, and adaptability dimensions, but it provides no numerical win rates or statistical comparisons.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or challenge comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), because it shows that un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B could not understand task requirements or generate commands in TextStarCraft II, while fine-tuned open-source models defeated level 5 but collapsed into reduced strategic diversity dominated by a mass-production stalker strategy. This challenges the proposed method's assumption that zero-shot evaluation of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct will be sufficient to assess open-source strategic capability, and it warns that win rate alone may mask strategic homogeneity. The same paper also limits evaluation to one built-in opponent level, uses win rate and APU rather than the proposed RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, decision tracking, synchronous/asynchronous modes, or opponent strategy variation, so it does not validate the proposed fine-grained metric suite and lacks reported statistical significance, hardware specifications, or exact prompt-level win rates. A secondary contradiction is that none of the three papers evaluates the exact proposed model set (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, Gemini 1.5 Flash, DeepSeek-V2.5, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, and Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct), and [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb) is not a direct multi-model benchmark across the proposed proprietary and open-source set, uses Minecraft rather than StarCraft II, reports only three trials per condition, does not report local hardware or model training, and focuses on a single VOYAGER architecture rather than isolating model-family effects. Wang's 1/3 diamond-tool success at 102 iterations and 0/3 for AutoGPT diamond tools show that even strong GPT-4-based agents can fail on harder tasks, but the small trial count and absence of significance tests weaken the evidence for generalizing to the proposed Arena. The weakest contradiction is [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), which provides only qualitative outcomes from 30 experiments, no numerical win rates, average match times, prompt hyperparameters, matches per difficulty, hardware, or fine-grained metrics such as resource management, supply utilization, APM, or decision tracking. This later single-model case study limits its ability to support the proposed quantitative benchmark and makes it difficult to compare GPT-3.5 Turbo against the other proposed models under controlled conditions.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the TextStarCraft II multi-LLM evaluation structure from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) while extending it to the proposed Arena: use at least 100 games per model and opponent condition, report win rate with confidence intervals or significance tests, include APU alongside the proposed RPM, SUR, APM, and EPM, and vary opponent strategies and difficulty rather than relying on a single built-in level. The second priority is to address the open-source model risk identified by [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) by including both zero-shot and fine-tuned or prompt-optimized variants of Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct, and DeepSeek-V2.5, using high-quality interaction logs filtered by APU or SUR, and measuring strategic diversity through unit construction order, key building completion time, and strategy innovation rate to detect mass-production collapse. A third priority is to standardize the agent architecture using lessons from [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f197bf0fc2f228483f6af3285000d54d8d97f9eb), such as iterative prompting, self-verification, execution-error feedback, and optional skill libraries, while recording prompting iterations, latency, error rates, and maximum iteration caps so that model capability is not confounded with prompting scaffolding. A fourth priority is to incorporate the StarCraft II implementation details from [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), including python-sc2, fixed map and starting positions, race selection, difficulty ladder, scouting reports, and decision categories such as early scouting, threat response, reinforcement, air-ground transition, and late-game production, so that the proposed decision tracking system can be operationalized. Finally, the plan should report API versions, temperatures, hardware or API rate limits, random seeds, number of runs, and statistical tests to improve reproducibility and allow the proposed multi-model comparison to be judged as a controlled benchmark rather than a collection of case studies.

## Each model will be tested across multiple scenarios with different opponent strategies (Macro, Rush, Random) in both synchronous and asynchronous operational modes. We will conduct multiple runs per model to ensure statistical reliability.
Synthesis: This experimental setup tests robustness to opponent behavior, timing constraints, and run-to-run variance.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), because it is the closest LLM-specific StarCraft II evaluation and uses repeated 100-game samples, multiple models, and a fine-grained Average Population Utilization metric; it shows closed-source LLMs and fine-tuned ChatGLM3 6B/Qwen 1.8B can defeat built-in level 5 AI, while un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B failed to generate usable commands, and that fine-tuning on top-quartile Average Population Utilization wins improved win rate from 28/100 to 54/100, directly supporting multiple runs, LLM comparison, and metrics beyond win rate. This work also iteratively extends the repeated-game protocols of [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) and [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) to LLMs, although it narrows opponent variation to a fixed built-in AI and removes chain-of-thought data due to compute constraints. The next strongest support is [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), which evaluates trained agents against a fixed panel of seven opponents across all maps with uniform race sampling and defines robustness as one minus the minimum win rate over reference agents, supporting the proposed emphasis on multiple opponents and robustness; its use of the AlphaStar Unplugged dataset with 3.5 billion observations derived from 21 billion internal steps, episode shortening by about 12 times, and dataset-size experiments with 10^10 frames, minibatch size 16,384, and learning rate 10^-3 shows that large-scale repeated evaluation is feasible, though it does not label opponents as Macro, Rush, or Random or test synchronous/asynchronous modes. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) supports repeated-run reliability in full StarCraft II by using 100 games per built-in difficulty level repeated three times on Simple64, with the hierarchical agent improving from 0.03 to 0.93 win rate on level 7 and cheating-level agents reaching 0.94 to 0.97, but it varies difficulty rather than strategy and reports training costs of 2 to 3 days for full SC2 runs and 8 hours supervised plus 1 day plus 3 days reinforcement learning for mini-AlphaStar. [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565) provides foundational support for multi-scenario repeated evaluation by running 32 test episodes every 10,000 timesteps across 14 SMAC scenarios and showing QMIX achieved the highest median win rate and exceeded 95% on Easy scenarios, validating scenario-level trends, but it evaluates MARL algorithms rather than LLMs and uses fixed opponents. The weakest support is [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), which tests SwarmBrain with GPT-3.5-turbo in 30 experiments across five built-in difficulty levels and considers win rate and average match time, supporting LLM repeated evaluation but offering no aggregate quantitative outcomes, single map/race, and no explicit strategy or timing conditions.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest LLM evidence does not validate the proposed opponent-strategy matrix or timing modes: [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) evaluates only built-in level 5 AI, reports no Macro/Rush/Random policies, no synchronous/asynchronous conditions, and no statistical significance, and it finds that fine-tuned open-source models show reduced strategic diversity by predominantly mass-producing stalkers, which contradicts the assumption that win rate against a fixed opponent reliably reveals adaptability or strategic planning. This also contradicts older RL-only win-rate protocols such as [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565) and [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) by showing that LLM agents can win while exhibiting narrow behavior, so binary success metrics may mask strategic limitations. The next strongest contradiction is that average win rates can mask opponent-specific weaknesses: [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) introduces robustness as one minus the minimum win rate over a fixed seven-opponent panel and reports that offline RL baselines such as deep offline Q-learning, SARSA, CRR, BCQ, R-BVE, CWP, RCBC, AWR, and PPO did not outperform behavior cloning and fine-tuned behavior cloning, implying that a proposed design that only reports aggregate win rates across multiple runs could hide opponent-specific failures, although it still lacks named strategy opponents and timing modes. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) then contradicts the use of built-in difficulty as a proxy for strategy by showing that transfer from level 7 to cheating levels was ineffective, that mini-AlphaStar could not obtain a win on level 7 despite 8 hours supervised learning plus 1 day and then 3 days reinforcement learning, and that high-level performance was limited by replay storage and compute, while its 100-game by three-repetition protocol still varies only difficulty. [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565) contradicts reliance on small repeated win-rate samples by showing high variance and poor performance on Hard and Super-Hard SMAC scenarios, with QMIX and VDN plateauing on 5m_vs_6m, only QMIX making meaningful progress on two of five Super-Hard scenarios, QTRAN failing on 3s5z and taking much longer on 2s3z, and RNN state encoding being crucial for kiting in 3s_vs_5z, indicating that win-rate trends alone may miss tactical adaptability. The weakest contradiction is [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), which reports 30 experiments across five difficulty levels but no aggregate win rates, match times, significance tests, hardware, latency, tick rate, random seeds, or explicit opponent strategies, contradicting the proposed statistical reliability and comprehensive scenario/timing design if used as a precedent.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement explicitly labeled Macro, Rush, and Random opponents, using scripted policies or trained agents, and to report per-strategy win rates, Elo, and robustness as one minus the minimum win rate, following [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) and addressing the fixed level 5 limitation in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) and the difficulty-only variation in [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) and [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5). The second most important action is to define synchronous and asynchronous operational modes with explicit tick rate, action deadline, latency logging, timeout policy, and EPM/APM, because no related work tests timing and [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) omits latency/tick settings while [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) uses a text-based environment; a pilot should verify LLM inference latency before committing to synchronous play. The third action is to increase statistical reliability by running at least 100 games per model, opponent strategy, and operational mode, repeated three times, as in [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) and the 100-game samples in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), and to report confidence intervals, paired significance tests, variance, and effect sizes, avoiding the underpowered 32-episode protocol in [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565) and the unreported 30-experiment protocol in [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5). The fourth action is to validate fine-grained metrics by correlating RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, average match time, key building completion, and decision-tracking categories with win rate and strategy labels, using Average Population Utilization from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) as a precedent and adding the proposed decision tracking. The fifth action is to expand scenarios to multiple maps, races, starting positions, and early/mid/mid-to-late phases, using all-map uniform race sampling in [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) and the 14-scenario SMAC suite in [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565) as lower bounds, while controlling fixed positions as in [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) for reproducibility. The final action is to document compute and reproducibility constraints, including model versions, prompt templates, random seeds, hardware/API limits, fine-tuning data quality such as top APU logs in [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), and baselines such as heuristic focus firing from [(Samvelyan et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82055eed2ba0d7156a54c586249742c848e5d565), built-in AI from [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), behavior cloning/offline RL references from [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), and SwarmBrain from [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5).

## We will implement quantitative metrics including:
- Resource Management Ability (RPM): Total minerals and vespene gas collected
- Supply Utilization Rate (SUR): Ratio of supply used to maximum capacity
- Actions Per Minute (APM): Total actions divided by game time
- Effective Actions Per Minute (EPM): Meaningful actions per minute
- Win Rate Trend and Error Rate Trend for adaptability assessment
Synthesis: These operational metrics implement fine-grained capability measurement by quantifying resource collection, supply use, action throughput, and adaptability trends.

### Support

Read as an iterative sequence, the strongest support for the proposed method is that LLM-agent evaluation can be made more informative by decomposing behavior into fine-grained measures and using statistical comparisons rather than relying only on success rates. [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) evaluated 25 generative agents in Smallville with ablated memory, planning, and reflection, used TrueSkill, Kruskal-Wallis H(4) = 150.29 p < 0.001, Dunn post-hoc p < 0.001, and found the full architecture mean 29.89 versus the fully ablated baseline 21.21 with d = 8.16; this supports the plan's move from binary win rates to capability-specific metrics, although it does not measure StarCraft II resource, supply, APM, EPM, or error trends. The next strongest support is domain-specific: [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) evaluated LLMs in TextStarCraft II against level-5 built-in AI using win rate and Average Population Utilization, and found fine-tuning on top-25% APU winning games yielded 54/100 wins versus 28/100 on the full dataset, showing that a coarse economy/population metric can be operationally useful for LLM StarCraft II evaluation and that 100-game evaluations are feasible. Older StarCraft II work provides methodological support for trend-based evaluation: [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) used 100 games repeated 3 times against Terran built-in AIs on Simple64, reported win-rate trends over training iterations, and showed a hierarchical RL agent improved level-7 win rate from 0.03 to 0.93, while alternate updating lifted a stuck plateau from near 0.4 to about 0.8, supporting the plan's emphasis on win-rate trend and repeated runs. More recent StarCraft II evaluation work supports robust multi-opponent comparison: [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) evaluated trained agents against seven opponents, aggregated win rates into Elo and robustness, and used a 21-billion-step offline dataset, indicating that StarCraft II benchmarks can be made more reliable through opponent diversity and repeated games, even though it does not implement RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, or error-rate trend. Finally, [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) supports the feasibility of LLM-based StarCraft II play by running GPT-3.5-turbo SwarmBrain for 30 matches across five difficulty levels on Automaton LE, but its qualitative reporting makes it the weakest support for the proposed quantitative metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is that the closest LLM-SC2 work does not validate the proposed fine-grained metrics and instead uses only win rate and APU. [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) found that fine-tuning on top-25% APU winning games improved win rate from 28/100 to 54/100, but it did not report RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, win-rate trend, or error-rate trend, and its LLMs showed reduced strategic diversity with a predominant stalker mass-production strategy, suggesting that win rate and a coarse APU proxy may not capture strategic planning or adaptability. A related tension is that established StarCraft II evaluation can be conducted with outcome-based metrics alone. [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) used win rate, Elo, and robustness against seven opponents, and found offline RL and behavior-cloning baselines strong, without measuring resource collection, supply utilization, action throughput, or decision-level adaptability; this challenges the assumption that the proposed operational metrics are necessary for StarCraft II agent comparison, although it does not address LLM-specific failure modes. Earlier StarCraft II RL work also shows that win-rate trends can already reveal learning progress and plateaus. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) reported final win rates and training-curve win rates, including a 0.03 to 0.93 improvement on level 7 and a plateau lift from near 0.4 to about 0.8 with alternate updating, which means a separate error-rate trend may be redundant unless it is defined to capture distinct decision errors. The sandbox LLM work provides no direct contradiction to StarCraft II metrics but limits the evidence base because it does not measure resource, supply, APM, EPM, or combat error trends. [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) measured believability, memory, planning, and reflection in Smallville, not StarCraft operational constraints, so its support for the proposed metrics is analogical. The most recent LLM-SC2 work is the weakest contradiction because it is largely qualitative and does not test whether quantitative metrics improve evaluation. [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) reported 30 matches and qualitative tactics but no win rates, baselines, statistical tests, or operational metrics, leaving the proposed method unvalidated in that setting.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed method should validate RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, win-rate trend, and error-rate trend as discriminative and non-redundant metrics by comparing them against outcome metrics and coarse proxies. Following [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e), include Elo and robustness across multiple opponents, and following [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), include APU as a sanity-check economy/population metric; report correlations, effect sizes, and statistical significance across multiple runs, using the statistical rigor of [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), such as nonparametric tests and ablation-style comparisons of metric subsets. Second, define operational metrics precisely to avoid interface confounds: RPM should be normalized by game time and starting resources, SUR should specify maximum supply and timing, APM should distinguish LLM-issued actions from game-generated events, and EPM should be grounded in a decision-tracking taxonomy of meaningful actions such as resource allocation, unit production, scouting, and combat response, similar to the decision categories in the research plan and the qualitative tactics reported by [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5). Third, use repeated games and difficulty variation to make trends reliable: adopt the 100-game repeated evaluation style of [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3) and the multi-difficulty, multi-opponent design of [(Mathieu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/154bd12c5fe96843cde84f2466333d2089050c9e) and [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), with Macro, Rush, and Random opponents as planned, and report win-rate and error-rate trends per phase. Fourth, if fine-tuning or prompt data are used, apply the data-quality lesson from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) by filtering interaction logs on high-APU winning games and reporting how data quality affects RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, and win rate. Finally, report implementation details that are missing in related work, including LLM versions, prompt templates, temperature, synchronous/asynchronous latency, hardware, number of runs, and statistical tests, to improve reproducibility and to ensure that APM/EPM differences are not artifacts of interface speed or prompt verbosity.

## We will implement a comprehensive behavioral analysis system that records key decisions throughout gameplay, including:
- Action Type: Specific decision categories (resource allocation, unit production, etc.)
- Decision Context: Game state and objectives at decision time
- Outcome: Consequences of decisions on gameplay
Synthesis: This logging system enables post-hoc analysis of what the LLM decided, when, and why it mattered.

### Support

The related work forms an iterative sequence from replay-based outcome prediction and aggregate behavioral metrics to LLM-specific context capture, and the strongest support for the proposed decision-tracking system is the Smallville generative-agent study, which used 25 agents over two in-game days, maintained a server-side JSON record of each agent's current location, current action, and object being interacted with, wrote observations, plans, and reflections into a memory stream, and used post-hoc interviews to verify affirmative claims against the log [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81). This directly validates the proposed synthesis of logging action type, decision context, and outcome for LLM agents, and its hallucination check (6 of 453 responses, 1.3 percent) plus knowledge-density increase from 0.167 to 0.74 shows that structured logs can make LLM claims auditable. A closely related and more domain-specific support is TextStarCraft II, where a structured prompt template containing Current Game Stage, Our Situation, Technology, Our Strategy, Enemy's Strategy, Key Information, and LLM Suggestions & Decisions captured decision-relevant context and produced interpretable strategy descriptions such as air dominance with Voidrays against a ground-focused enemy [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58). This supports the proposed Decision Context fields and the plan's qualitative analysis of unit construction order and strategy innovation, although it stops short of automated outcome attribution. The newest StarCraft-specific LLM evidence, SwarmBrain, also supports the proposed Action Type categories by narratively analyzing 30 experiments on Automaton LE across five difficulty levels, including scouting toward B1/B2, defensive mobilization at A2, reinforcement from A1, late-game air-ground composition, and Ultralisk production after combat losses [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), showing that such categories are meaningful in SC2. Older but methodologically strong support comes from Erickson and Buro, whose BWAPI C++ replay parser extracted timestamped per-player features and battle information from about 400 Protoss versus Protoss replays drawn from a larger 8000-replay collection, used 10-fold game-level cross-validation, 5-minute intervals with post-15-minute grouping, threshold 0.5 accuracy, and average log-likelihood, demonstrating that timestamped context and consequences can be turned into phase-specific evaluation [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8). This supports the proposed Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late analysis and outcome attribution, especially because late-game map coverage and skill features mattered. Hierarchical RL in StarCraft II LE further supports logging action and outcome by storing controller and sub-policy trajectories, using expert replays to derive expected unit/building counts, applying PPO with entropy regularization, and reporting win rates against built-in AIs (0.93 at level 7, final 0.96/0.97/0.94 at levels 8-10) [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3), which supports a macro/micro action taxonomy and quantitative outcome metrics. The SC2LE evaluation setup supports the proposed environment interface by defining 64x64 feature layers, actions every 8 steps (~180 APM), mini-games to isolate behaviors, and A3C baselines with 64 asynchronous threads, learning rates from 1e-5 to 1e-3, and entropy penalty 1e-3 [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b), making it feasible to log observations, actions, and rewards at a controlled action cadence. Earlier multi-metric Doom evaluation supports moving beyond win rates by using K/D, kills, objects gathered, deaths, and suicides to infer exploration, camping, and arbitrary shooting, with navigation increasing objects picked up more than threefold in limited deathmatch and co-training raising best K/D from below 2.0 to above 4.0 after 65 hours [(Lample, and Chaplot, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0b65d3839e3bf703d156b524d7db7a5e10a2623), and the QPLEX/QMIX StarCraft II work supports post-hoc qualitative strategy comparison and multi-seed reporting with median and 25-75 percentiles across 17 tasks, where QPLEX exceeded baselines by at least 10 percent in average median test win rate and had a greater than 30 percent gap on 5s10z [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/052c100d45f949c06e8419b504e319b442cb3f0a), though these are weaker because they are aggregate or qualitative rather than decision-level.

### Contradictions

The iterative literature shows that newer LLM work has moved from aggregate win rates toward context capture, but it has not yet closed the outcome-attribution gap. The strongest contradiction is that the closest LLM StarCraft II studies do not yet implement the proposed comprehensive decision-level outcome database. Ma et al. used Chain of Summarization and a structured prompt, but did not describe automated logging of every action type, timestamped decision context, or downstream outcome for each decision; evaluation relied on win rates, Average Population Utilization (top-25% APU fine-tuning 54/100 versus full dataset 28/100), and qualitative policy examples, with no statistical significance, hardware, or seed reporting [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58). This contradicts the assumption that prompt-based strategy summaries are sufficient for the proposed post-hoc analysis of what mattered. Shao et al. similarly provide only narrative post-hoc discussion of 30 experiments on Automaton LE, with no quantitative win rates, baselines, average match times, latency, prompt hyperparameters, or significance tests [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), so the proposed structured logging and outcome attribution remain unvalidated in SC2. The Smallville study, while strong for LLM logging, contradicts the proposed method's domain-specific outcome metrics because it measured social knowledge and party attendance rather than resource allocation, unit production, combat, or decision-level consequence attribution, and reported no significance tests [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81). Erickson and Buro also contradict the proposed method's scope by predicting aggregate game outcomes from amateur Protoss replays rather than LLM action types or rationales, and their excerpt lacks numeric accuracy/log-likelihood values, while noting overfitting in the UC feature and late-game beta-variance degradation due to unsupported unit types [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8). Vinyals et al. provide a caution that full-game SC2LE evaluation can produce degenerate behavior: none of 100 sparse ternary reward A3C agents developed a viable strategy over 600M agent steps, and Blizzard-score agents converged to trivial mineral mining, so logging decisions alone may not reveal meaningful strategic reasoning if rewards or baselines are poorly specified [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b). Liu et al. support logging but contradict the proposed LLM-specific post-hoc analysis because their hierarchical trajectories were used to train RL agents, not to audit LLM decisions, and evaluation was limited to Simple64, 100 games per level repeated three times, with 2-3 day training runs [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3). Lample and Chaplot used aggregate Doom metrics without decision logs or significance tests, despite detailed training settings such as RMSProp, minibatch 32, a 1M-frame replay buffer, and epsilon decay from 1 to 0.1 [(Lample, and Chaplot, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0b65d3839e3bf703d156b524d7db7a5e10a2623), and Wang et al. used qualitative strategy visualization without structured decision logging, hardware, or hyperparameter details [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/052c100d45f949c06e8419b504e319b442cb3f0a), making them weaker evidence for the proposed fine-grained system.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement a server-side structured decision log before any model comparison, using a JSON or SQLite schema that records game ID, timestamp/frame, game phase, model, prompt version, raw LLM output, parsed Action Type, Decision Context, and Outcome. This should combine Park et al.'s JSON server and memory-stream verification [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) with Ma et al.'s prompt fields Current Game Stage, Our Situation, Technology, Our Strategy, Enemy's Strategy, Key Information, and LLM Suggestions & Decisions [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), and Shao et al.'s StarCraft categories such as scouting, defense, reinforcement, force composition, and production [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5). The second priority is to define a reproducible action taxonomy and outcome attribution protocol: classify decisions into resource allocation, unit production, technology, expansion, scouting, defense, and combat; log SC2LE observations/actions/rewards at a fixed cadence such as every 8 game steps (~180 APM) as in Vinyals et al. [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b); and compute consequences as deltas in minerals, gas, supply, kills/deaths, building completion time, unit construction order, Average Population Utilization, and win/loss contribution, using replay features and game-level 10-fold cross-validation from Erickson and Buro [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8) to avoid leakage. The third priority is to align the evaluation protocol with the research plan's statistical reliability requirements: run multiple models (GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, GPT-3.5 Turbo, Gemini 1.5 Flash, DeepSeek-V2.5, Llama-3.1-8B/70B) across Macro, Rush, Random opponents, synchronous/asynchronous modes, and Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late phases; use at least 100 games per condition as in Liu et al. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f4049dd954a62c5aac55882c55c3e4df991b2f3); report median and 25-75 percentiles over at least six seeds as in Wang et al. [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/052c100d45f949c06e8419b504e319b442cb3f0a); and add statistical significance tests (for example, paired bootstrap or mixed-effects models) that are absent in Lample and Chaplot, Park et al., Ma et al., and Shao et al. [(Lample, and Chaplot, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0b65d3839e3bf703d156b524d7db7a5e10a2623) [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5). The fourth priority is to use controlled mini-games and built-in AI baselines to isolate decision categories, following Vinyals et al.'s mini-games and A3C baselines [(Vinyals et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6127b8dc39497a2388a0fce2512595e0dcb7121b), while avoiding trivial reward convergence by using sparse but meaningful rewards and reporting APM/EPM, RPM, SUR, Win Rate Trend, Error Rate Trend, and Strategy Innovation Rate. The fifth priority is to add qualitative post-hoc interviews and strategy visualizations, but quantify them: verify affirmative LLM claims against the decision log as in Park et al. [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), compute hallucination/error rates, and use Wang et al.'s visualization approach [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/052c100d45f949c06e8419b504e319b442cb3f0a) only as a complement to quantitative decision metrics. Finally, report full reproducibility details missing in the related work: hardware, action latency, prompt templates, hyperparameters, random seeds, dataset sizes, and computational budgets, because Ma et al. omitted hardware/seeds/significance [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), Shao et al. omitted quantitative baselines and latency [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), and Wang et al. omitted hardware and hyperparameters [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/052c100d45f949c06e8419b504e319b442cb3f0a).

## We will analyze decision trajectories through metrics such as:
- Unit Construction Order: Sequence and timing of unit production
- Key Building Completion Time: Timing of critical infrastructure
- Strategy Innovation Rate: Frequency of adopting new strategic approaches
Synthesis: This qualitative trajectory analysis captures strategic sequencing and innovation beyond aggregate performance.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58), which directly evaluates LLM agents in TextStarCraft II and shows that aggregate win rate is insufficient: closed-source LLMs defeated the level 5 built-in AI, un-fine-tuned Llama2 70B failed to generate valid commands, and fine-tuned open-source models defeated level 5 but reduced strategic diversity by predominantly mass-producing stalkers. Its APU metric, 100 games per condition, qualitative assessment of strategic behavior, and finding that fine-tuning on wins from the top 25% APU games produced a 54/100 win rate versus 28/100 with the full dataset support the proposed decision-trajectory analysis and the research plan’s goal of assessing strategic planning, real-time decision making, and adaptability beyond binary success; the paper also notes that chain-of-thought components were removed due computational constraints, leaving inputs and outputs, which highlights the need to log full decision trajectories when feasible. The next strongest support is [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa), which standardizes 36,619 high-quality SC2LE replays from 64,396 after filters for more than 10,000 frames, APM greater than 10, and MMR greater than 1000, and extracts PySC2 features including player-owned buildings, units, techniques, resources, and observed enemy state; its build-order prediction task and GRU global-state baseline, with linear layers of 1024 and 2048 ReLU, GRU layers of 2048 and 512, sigmoid binary cross-entropy, Adam learning rate 0.001 halved each epoch, batch size 256, and time-step size 20, provide a concrete pipeline for deriving unit construction order and key building completion time. Weaker but foundational support is [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8), which uses a custom BWAPI C++ parser on about 400 Protoss-versus-Protoss games from roughly 8000 replays, samples temporal economic, military, map-coverage, and skill features, and evaluates logistic regression with game-level 10-fold cross-validation, 0.5-threshold accuracy, and average log-likelihood across 5-minute phases; this supports phase-specific trajectory analysis and shows late-game map coverage and skill features can reflect earlier economic and army advantages, although the final score is not used as a feature because it is unavailable in imperfect-information play. Together, these works form an iterative progression: Erickson’s temporal phase features were later expanded by Wu’s standardized dataset and build-order task, and then by Ma’s LLM strategy-diversity evaluation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the proposed qualitative trajectory metrics are not yet validated as discriminative or statistically reliable, and the closest LLM evidence shows strategic behavior can collapse rather than diversify: [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) reports fine-tuned models predominantly mass-producing stalkers, uses only 100 games per condition, and does not report confidence intervals, hardware specs, or significance tests for the 54/100 versus 28/100 comparison, so a strategy innovation rate could be low, dataset-driven, or confounded by opponent level, prompt constraints, and the removal of chain-of-thought data. This newer LLM evidence also tempers older replay-based outcome-prediction assumptions that better aggregate prediction automatically implies better strategic breadth. The next contradiction is that the proposed construction-order and building-completion metrics may be under-specified and biased by replay quality: [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) filters out short, low-APM, and low-MMR games, which may make human-replay-derived build-order baselines less representative of LLM agents that may produce invalid commands, low APM, or atypical macro play, and the excerpt does not provide quantitative build-order prediction performance, F1, sequence accuracy, or baseline comparisons, so it cannot confirm that construction-order features are robust enough for LLM evaluation. Weaker contradiction comes from [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8), which uses only about 400 games, reports no numeric accuracy, log-likelihood, or statistical significance, notes later 5-minute intervals contain fewer games because game lengths vary, and warns that some features overfit when interval training sets are small; this cautions that phase-specific decision-trajectory metrics, especially late-game key building completion and innovation rate, may suffer from small samples and overfitting if the proposed arena does not stratify and report sample sizes.

### Suggested Action

Most important, operationalize the three qualitative metrics as replay-derived, testable quantities before running LLM comparisons: use a PySC2/BWAPI parser in the style of [(Wu et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28d43200d9945f74ee82c309d7fcdb6d103ad4aa) to log unit production events, building completion timestamps, resource and supply states, and action types, then define unit construction order as a sequence with edit-distance or sequence-accuracy comparison to expert build orders, key building completion time as frame or minute thresholds for race-specific critical structures, and strategy innovation rate as the number of novel macro/build-order transitions per game or per phase relative to a labeled strategy taxonomy, with bootstrap confidence intervals. Second, adopt the LLM evaluation lessons from [(Ma et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f22f66cacac8eebc5728cd0a28513b5e75a9a58) by reporting APU alongside win rate, using at least 100 games per model-opponent-mode condition and preferably more, stratifying by APU or game phase, and explicitly testing whether fine-tuning or prompting increases or collapses strategy diversity, including mass-production stalkers or other single-strategy convergence. Third, follow the phase-specific evaluation design of [(Erickson, and Buro, 2014-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7caa6a3a95df34db072e4cc8c5808dbf448cf9b8) by using game-level cross-validation or independent test games, reporting per-phase sample sizes, avoiding example-level leakage, and using probabilistic or ranking metrics such as log-likelihood, AUC, or sequence F1 rather than only 0.5-threshold accuracy. Finally, integrate these metrics with the research plan’s quantitative RPM, SUR, APM, EPM, and win-rate/error-rate trends, and use baseline classifiers or sequence models with reported hyperparameters to validate that construction order and building completion time predict strategic outcomes before using them as primary LLM capability indicators.

## Models will be evaluated across different game phases (Early, Mid, Mid-to-Late) to assess performance consistency and strategic evolution over time.
Synthesis: This phase-based testing checks whether LLM performance and strategy change as the game progresses.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), which directly examines an LLM StarCraft II agent, SwarmBrain, using gpt-3.5-turbo and python-sc2 on Automaton LE across 30 experiments against five computer difficulties, and reports phase-relevant behavioral shifts: early Overlord/Zergling scouting of enemy mineral fields, dispatching Overlords to B1/B2, A2 threat response with idle Roaches/Zerglings, and later air-ground composition with Overseers, Ultralisks, Mutalisks, Corruptors, and Brood Lords. This supports the proposed Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late phase testing because it shows that LLM strategy in StarCraft II can be observed as changing across match phases and that phase-oriented analysis is feasible in the same domain. The next strongest support is [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), which argues for combining outcome metrics such as win rates, survival rates, rewards, Normalized Relative Advantage, and TrueSkill with process metrics for opponent analysis and qualitative strategic behavior; this supports the research plan's move beyond a single final win rate and its emphasis on strategic evolution, although it does not itself define Early, Mid, or Mid-to-Late bins. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), which longitudinally evaluated 25 generative agents over two in-game days in Smallville and quantified temporal change: knowledge of Sam's mayoral candidacy rose from 1/25 (4%) to 8/25 (32%), knowledge of Isabella's party rose from 1/25 (4%) to 13/25 (52%), mutual-knowledge network density rose from 0.167 to 0.74, hallucinated knowledge among 453 knowing responses was 6/453 (1.3%), and party attendance was 5/12. This supports the broader premise that game-agent behavior can be measured over time with verified state, but it is not an RTS strategic benchmark and does not test resource management, supply, APM/EPM, or win-rate trends.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is methodological: [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5) provides only qualitative, example-driven phase observations for SwarmBrain, without pre-defined Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late bins, phase-specific win rates, average match time, resource metrics, APM/EPM, statistical tests, hardware/latency details, or multiple LLM comparisons, despite running 30 experiments against Very Easy, Easy, Medium, Medium Hard, and Hard on Automaton LE. This contradicts the proposed method's assumption that phase-based testing can establish performance consistency and strategic evolution unless phases are operationally defined and measured with repeated quantitative outcomes. The next contradiction is that [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778) does not provide a phase-based protocol or numerical experimental outcomes, and instead frames evaluation around outcome scoring, process accuracy under incomplete information, and qualitative strategic behavior across game types and difficulty levels; this suggests that phase segmentation alone may be insufficient if it is not coupled with opponent nonstationarity, process accuracy, and calibrated scoring. The weakest contradiction is domain mismatch: [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81) evaluated 25 agents in a social sandbox over two in-game days using interviews, memory-stream verification, yes/no coding, network density, and party attendance, not a competitive RTS with imperfect information, real-time action constraints, or strategic resource management; therefore its longitudinal design does not directly validate the proposed StarCraft II Early/Mid/Mid-to-Late strategic metrics.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to operationalize Early, Mid, and Mid-to-Late as pre-registered, objective game-state bins rather than qualitative descriptions, using triggers such as elapsed match time, supply used, base count, mineral/vespene thresholds, technology level, and opponent tech, while anchoring the bins to the kinds of transitions observed in [(Shao et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e0a513624426e017a125d0498235b8f32ce9aa5), such as early scouting and A2 defense versus later air-ground Ultralisk production. The next most important action is to report phase-specific quantitative outcomes for every model, mapping RPM and SUR to strategic planning, APM and EPM to real-time decision making, and win-rate and error-rate trends to adaptability, with calibrated comparisons using Normalized Relative Advantage or TrueSkill as suggested by [(Zhang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac8bc36c253c8593492afc2db620c01b152dc778), with baselines such as computer AI, scripted strategies, or non-LLM agents, and with confidence intervals, effect sizes, and statistical tests across multiple runs, opponent strategies, and difficulties. A further action is to add decision-level verification and qualitative coding inspired by [(Park et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5278a8eb2ba2429d4029745caf4e661080073c81), where claims were checked against memory streams and hallucination was measured at 1.3% across 453 responses; in StarCraft II Arena this should mean verifying LLM strategic claims against game logs, coding action type/context/outcome, measuring hallucinated or unsupported decisions, and reporting inter-rater reliability. Finally, the plan should specify reproducibility constraints that prior work omitted, including map, starting location, opponent difficulty, synchronous/asynchronous mode, API model versions, latency, hardware, random seeds, and the number of runs per phase, so that phase-to-phase consistency and strategic evolution can be tested rather than inferred from anecdotal match descriptions.
