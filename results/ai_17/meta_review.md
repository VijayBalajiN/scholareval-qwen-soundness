## Context: The proposal addresses RAG knowledge bases that contain incorrect or outdated information and asks how to systematically refine them using expert feedback. Method: We propose MAC-CAFE, a Multi-actor, Centralized Critic Architecture for Feedback-driven Editing that formulates KB editing as a state search problem.

### Support

Strongest support for MAC-CAFE comes from [(Putta et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b393f619a87c5b6aa63c7abc7118263205b6aa62), which uses test-time MCTS over web-page states, UCB1, K sampled actions, process-level AI feedback, and DPO, and reports large quantitative gains: on WebShop, base xLAM-v0.1-r 28.6% exact-product success, trajectory DPO 40.6%, test-time MCTS 48.4%, and MCTS after node-level DPO 50.5%; on OpenTable, Agent Q plus MCTS reaches 95.4% versus 62.6% GPT-4o zero-shot. This directly supports the plan's MCTS-over-states and critic-like feedback loop, although its states are web pages rather than KB documents. The next strongest support is [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b), which frames RAG query refinement as MCTS with a Query Proposer, a 5-point Result Evaluator, UCB selection, and a Self-Reflection baseline on BioASQ 2023 Task 11b using BM25 over abstracts; this supports the plan's LLM-as-judge feedback and MCTS over retrieval-related states, but the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes, MCTS depth, expansion width, iteration count, exploration constant, or hardware, so its evidential strength is weaker. Centralized critic evidence is provided by [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31), where a CodeT5 actor and separate critic use unit-test execution feedback, with APPS pass@1 2.69%, pass@5 6.81%, pass@1000 20.98%, filtered n@k 8.48% and 12.62%, and MBPP pass@80 63.0% versus GPT-137B 61.4%, trained on 16 A100-40G GPUs for about 21 days and fine-tuned with batch size 64 and learning rate 2e-5 to 1e-5; this supports the plan's use of a critic to convert execution or expert feedback into actor updates, especially for ARKS-Pony and ARKS-Ring. [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903) adds weaker support for execution-based PPO on APPS and zero-shot MBPP, reporting PPOCoder+CodeT5 pass@80 63% and GPT-137B 61.4%, but the excerpt contains an internal inconsistency in its comparison with CodeRL and omits PPO hyperparameters, hardware, and significance tests. Older centralized-critic work, [(Hu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7da41fd9949af81b84fb983524f850f7d47918fa), supports using a centralized critic and full Bellman backup with auxiliary conversion, continuation, and deal-price models for sparse rewards in TaoBao ranking, but the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes and is single-agent. Multi-actor coordination is supported by [(Zhao et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/410b5a5888d744601a866f3e4a943171650f94fd), where M-RMDP coordinates 1000 ad agents over 100 million auctions per day using DQN, replay memory, target network, 100 discrete bid actions, and epsilon decay, with single-agent RMDP reporting 35.04% PUR_AMT/COST, 23.7% CVR, and 21.38% ROI improvements; however, M-RMDP lacks a centralized critic, MCTS, and textual gradients. The static RAG baseline in [(Lewis et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/659bf9ce7175e1ec266ff54359e2bd76e0b7ff31) supports the motivation for editing a 21-million-document Wikipedia index with FAISS/HNSW and top-k retrieval, but it does not edit documents. Finally, [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a) and [(Akyürek et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf35cef5c249d90b40043fffa41f8802c27f132) provide weaker but relevant support: CIRL uses AST perturbation and execution feedback to prevent MRR drops on CodeNet subsets, and RL4F uses critique warm-starting and PPO with inverse Levenshtein reward on a 43K-word alphabetization task, showing that feedback signals can improve downstream performance. Taken iteratively, the literature moves from static RAG in Lewis 2020 to execution-feedback code RL in Gotmare 2022 and Shojaee 2023 to MCTS-based LLM feedback in Putta 2024 and Hu 2024, which collectively supports the plan's search-and-feedback framing.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that no supplied related work validates the full MAC-CAFE architecture: a multi-actor, centralized critic, ReACT document editors, textual gradients, and MCTS over persistent KB states. The closest MCTS works, [(Putta et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b393f619a87c5b6aa63c7abc7118263205b6aa62) and [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b), search over web-page histories or BM25 queries, not over document-level KB configurations, and neither tests whether edits improve RAG factuality, completeness, or coherence. This is a major gap because MAC-CAFE's state space is a collection of documents, where each edit can change retrieval, generation, and coherence globally. The next strongest contradiction is computational and methodological: the plan's MCTS settings, UCT depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, and exploration constant=2.5, are not validated by related work; [(Putta et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b393f619a87c5b6aa63c7abc7118263205b6aa62) does not report MCTS depth, K, exploration constant, or hardware, and [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) omits MCTS hyperparameters and quantitative results, so there is no evidence that such a shallow search can find optimal KB edits across ARKS and CLARKS-news. Centralized critic evidence is also limited: [(Hu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7da41fd9949af81b84fb983524f850f7d47918fa) and [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) use centralized critics in single-agent settings, not to coordinate multiple document-level actors, and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903) uses PPO with execution reward but contains an internally inconsistent comparison, claiming PPOCoder outperforms CodeRL by 5.2% while reporting CodeRL 68.2% versus PPOCoder 63% on MBPP pass@80. Multi-agent evidence contradicts the centralized-critic assumption: [(Zhao et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/410b5a5888d744601a866f3e4a943171650f94fd) coordinates many agents with DQN and a cooperative objective but does not use a centralized critic, MCTS, or textual gradients, and it reports no statistical significance. The RAG foundation in [(Lewis et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/659bf9ce7175e1ec266ff54359e2bd76e0b7ff31) contradicts the assumption that a static 21-million-document index can be treated as an editable state without additional retrieval-index maintenance, because it uses a fixed December 2018 Wikipedia dump, 100-word chunks, FAISS/HNSW, and top-k retrieval with no document editing or expert feedback. Code-specific related work also limits generalizability: [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a) shows heuristic AST perturbation can hurt MRR, and [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903) optimize program generation rather than KB documentation, while [(Akyürek et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf35cef5c249d90b40043fffa41f8802c27f132) evaluates critique-driven RL on a synthetic alphabetization task and Interscript ROUGE, not on RAG KB editing. Iteratively, older multi-agent RL in Zhao 2018 supported coordination, but newer MCTS LLM work in Putta 2024 and Hu 2024 shows search can work only in narrower state spaces, and newer code RL in Gotmare 2022 and Shojaee 2023 does not transfer to persistent KB editing.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add ablations that isolate each MAC-CAFE component: compare the full multi-actor centralized critic with MCTS against a single-actor centralized critic, a multi-actor system without centralized critic, a centralized critic without MCTS, a no-critic ReACT baseline, and the proposed PROMPT AGENT-E baseline, using the plan's 1:1:2 train/eval/test failure-case splits on ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, ARKS-TensorflowM, and CLARKS-news, and reporting train/eval/test accuracy, execution accuracy, G-Eval coherence with GPT-4 as judge, retrieval MRR or nDCG, and statistical significance. This is needed because [(Putta et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b393f619a87c5b6aa63c7abc7118263205b6aa62) and [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) show MCTS and LLM feedback can help sequential search, but they do not prove that multi-actor document editing is necessary. Second, constrain and report the MCTS state space: use document-level or section-level decoupling, log the number of documents, chunks, candidate edits, LLM calls, token budget, wall-clock time, hardware, model identifiers such as GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW and OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE, document chunking at 50 lines, and the 18000 token retrieval budget, and sweep UCT depth, expansion width, iterations, and exploration constant rather than fixing depth=3, width=3, 5 iterations, and 2.5 without evidence; adopt process-level value estimates and preference-based policy improvement from [(Putta et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b393f619a87c5b6aa63c7abc7118263205b6aa62) where node-level value gaps exceed a threshold, and use the Self-Reflection baseline design from [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) to test whether sibling-node feedback improves query or edit proposals. Third, for code KBs, incorporate execution and AST-aware feedback: use compiler or unit-test rewards as in [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903), and avoid naive heuristic perturbations by following [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a), which shows CIRL-style intervention can prevent MRR degradation while CI-ADV can hurt retrieval. Fourth, strengthen the centralized critic with auxiliary value models or full backup estimation for sparse expert feedback, as in [(Hu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7da41fd9949af81b84fb983524f850f7d47918fa), and with critique warm-starting or inverse-edit-distance rewards as in [(Akyürek et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf35cef5c249d90b40043fffa41f8802c27f132), while measuring textual-gradient quality rather than only final task performance. Fifth, include multi-agent baselines and retrieval baselines: compare against a Zhao-style decentralized multi-agent DQN baseline from [(Zhao et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/410b5a5888d744601a866f3e4a943171650f94fd) to test whether centralized coordination helps, and against a static RAG or append-only KB baseline inspired by [(Lewis et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/659bf9ce7175e1ec266ff54359e2bd76e0b7ff31) to show that editing outperforms simply adding documents.

## Context: MAC-CAFE must turn global expert feedback into coordinated document edits. Method: We employ a multi-actor, centralized critic reinforcement learning framework where a centralized critic analyzes global reward signals from expert feedback and generates textual gradients, individual actors, modeled as ReACT agents, are assigned to specific documents and perform structured edits, and the critic decomposes feedback into document-specific reflections and coordinates updates across actors.

### Support

The strongest support for MAC-CAFE's centralized-critic, decentralized-actor architecture comes from [(Iqbal, and Sha, 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/929bef0066bad871ba971b673c053112d055d29f), where MAAC uses decentralized actors and a centralized critic with attention over other agents in Cooperative Treasure Collection with eight agents, six treasure hunters, two banks, six respawning treasures, and Rover-Tower with eight agents, four rovers, four towers, and five discrete messages, using six random seeds and parameter-matched baselines including MADDPG, COMA, and a uniform-attention ablation; this directly validates that a global critic can coordinate local actors without each actor observing the full joint state, analogous to MAC-CAFE's critic decomposing global expert feedback into document-specific reflections while ReACT actors edit assigned documents. The next strongest support is [(Dong et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8ecaac57de593207c85461ccfc7d10c8807d78d), where PACE uses n = 4 actors and critics, textual critiques, practical metrics such as accuracy, BLEU, or BERTScore, over more than 40 tasks, improves from worst, medium, best, and Butter Fingers prompts with a 15 percent misspelling rate, reports up to 98 percent enhancement in the Butter Fingers setting, runs about 0.78 times the baseline time, and shows ablations where full PACE outperforms actor-only and critic-only variants; this supports MAC-CAFE's iterative actor-critic textual editing and practical task metrics, although PACE edits a single prompt rather than multiple KB documents. Next, [(Sha, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db9715d147baf3fdd04830cfb7c88e30c07658eb) supports the textual-gradient idea by freezing pretrained models, optimizing word representations with a differentiable loss combining fluency, semantic similarity, and lexical constraints, ranking edit positions, sampling edits, running 100 editing epochs on Quora with 140K paraphrase pairs and 260K non-paraphrase pairs, 3K validation and 30K test, using Kneser-Ney NLL and six human graders, and reporting significant constrained-generation differences with p < 0.05 and paraphrase iBLEU 14.46, BLEU 23.27, ROUGE-1 59.65, ROUGE-2 33.08, and NLL 6.12; this shows that a global score can be converted into effective local text edits, but only for a single sentence. Next, [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) supports critic-guided code feedback by using CodeT5 770M as actor, a critic predicting compile, runtime, failed, and passed unit-test outcomes, REINFORCE with a greedy baseline and cross-entropy imitation, critic sampling with at most one repair, APPS 10,000 problems, pass@1 2.69, pass@5 6.81, pass@1000 20.98, and MBPP pass@80 63.0 above GPT-137B 61.4, after pretraining on 16 A100-40G GPUs for about 21 days; this is relevant to ARKS compiler feedback but not multi-document editing. Next, [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903) supports PPO-based execution-reward RL and generalization, using CodeT5 770M, APPS 10k problems, MBPP 974 problems, pass@80 63 percent, and KL or trust-region regularization. Next, [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a) supports counterfactual execution feedback for code alignment by using AST subtree insertion, induced execution feedback, CodeNet and CodeSearchNet Python, MRR stress tests on accept-ratio subsets from 0.0-0.1 to 0.4-0.5, and preventing MRR drops that affected GraphCodeBERT and Contra-Code, while noting 97 percent of CodeSearchNet Python positive-negative pairs had low lexical and structural similarity. Next, [(Liu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99bf4d9a1df69a486c0921676dc7e92900d0fe84) supports reward-driven text refinement with Qrefine-PPO, using a word-level wording reward plus answer-correlation reward, Yahoo and CSU data split 80/10/10, TensorFlow on an Intel Xeon E5-2620 v4 CPU and NVIDIA 1080Ti GPU, Adam learning rate 0.001, batch size 64, and PPO converging more stably than REINFORCE. Next, [(Lowe et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c3ece1ba41c415d7e81cfa5ca33a8de66efd434) supports multi-actor robustness by showing approximate policies with lambda 0.001 can match true-policy success and ensembles with K = 3 or K = 2 sub-policies can outperform single policies. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Ranzato et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b7aee9dfb027d6061c6a653684c0fa9a9bba750d), where MIXER improves sequence-level generation by combining cross-entropy and REINFORCE, achieving ROUGE 16.22 versus XENT 13.01, translation BLEU 20.73 versus 17.74, and captioning BLEU 29.16 versus 27.8, with mini-batches of 32 and gradient norm rescaling above 10, and [(Zhao et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef08ad10d257fcf5043d27d7225d6ec14a677a84), where DEERS incorporates negative skipped-item feedback into a DQN state with N = 10 previous clicked and skipped items, rewards 0, 1, and 5, gamma 0.95, and a JD.com dataset of 1,000,000 sessions, supporting feedback-state design but not multi-actor textual editing.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution for MAC-CAFE comes from [(Lyu et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4509f46c875f48821d42f3c6a5a95a2c124f08f1), which shows that centralized critics in multi-agent actor-critic Dec-POMDPs can have the same expected gradient as decentralized baselines but at least as large variance, that centralized state critics may be biased, that history-state critics are unbiased but have the highest theoretical variance, and that the centralized joint-history critic was less stable in Antipodal, Cross, Find Treasure, and Cleaner, converging to suboptimal lazy-agent behavior in Cleaner across 20 runs; this directly challenges the assumption that a centralized critic will reliably coordinate ReACT document actors without variance control, local history-state critics, or MCTS decoupling. The next strongest caution is [(Ranzato et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b7aee9dfb027d6061c6a653684c0fa9a9bba750d), where pure REINFORCE did not converge within a reasonable time and MIXER required incremental cross-entropy, while metric alignment can harm other metrics, as ROUGE-trained MIXER had BLEU 5.80 versus XENT 8.16 and BLEU-trained MIXER had ROUGE 15.1 versus 16.22 for ROUGE-trained MIXER; this contradicts a naive policy-gradient textual-gradient approach and warns that optimizing completeness, generalization, and coherence may trade off against one another. Next, [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903) caution that critic-guided code RL can be expensive and not uniformly superior: CodeRL used 16 A100-40G GPUs for about 21 days, limited critic sampling to one repair round, and achieved only 2.69 percent pass@1 on APPS, while PPOCoder reports MBPP pass@80 63 percent but the excerpt also lists CodeRL at 68.2 percent and claims a 5.2 percent advantage, an inconsistency that undermines confidence in the reported superiority. Next, [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a) shows that intervened or counterfactual code snippets caused MRR drops for Contra-Code and GraphCodeBERT in a stress test over CodeNet accept-ratio subsets from 0.0-0.1 to 0.4-0.5, indicating that perturbed KB content can confuse retrieval if positive and negative examples are not carefully separated; MAC-CAFE's document edits could create analogous retrieval confusion if expert feedback is not decomposed into safe document-specific reflections. Next, [(Zhao et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef08ad10d257fcf5043d27d7225d6ec14a677a84) shows that a fully connected DQN variant DEERS-f performed worse than positive-only DEERS-p, suggesting that failing to separate positive and negative feedback in the state can degrade performance; MAC-CAFE must therefore separate helpful corrections from noisy or harmful expert feedback and preserve document-specific signal. Weaker cautions include [(Dong et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8ecaac57de593207c85461ccfc7d10c8807d78d), where PACE recommends fewer than three iterations because editing effects plateau, whereas MAC-CAFE specifies five MCTS iterations, and [(Liu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99bf4d9a1df69a486c0921676dc7e92900d0fe84), which uses automatic BLEU-1, ROUGE, and METEOR without human evaluation or significance tests, limiting the strength of its evidence for reward-driven text refinement.

### Suggested Action

The most important action, based on [(Lyu et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4509f46c875f48821d42f3c6a5a95a2c124f08f1), is to add variance and bias controls to the centralized critic: use document-specific history-state critics or local critics for each ReACT actor, keep the centralized critic for advantage decomposition, use PPO with GAE, KL or trust-region penalties, gradient clipping, multiple random seeds, and report mean and standard deviation; ablate centralized versus decentralized critics and joint-history, state, and history-state critics to verify that MCTS document decoupling reduces variance rather than merely adding search cost. Second, based on [(Ranzato et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b7aee9dfb027d6061c6a653684c0fa9a9bba750d), [(Liu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99bf4d9a1df69a486c0921676dc7e92900d0fe84), and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903), use PPO or another stabilized actor-critic objective with cross-entropy or imitation warm-start rather than pure REINFORCE, combine execution accuracy with edit likelihood, tune reward ratio c1, entropy regularization c2, discount lambda, and threshold epsilon on validation data, and monitor metric trade-offs among completeness, generalization, and coherence. Third, based on [(Sha, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db9715d147baf3fdd04830cfb7c88e30c07658eb) and [(Dong et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8ecaac57de593207c85461ccfc7d10c8807d78d), operationalize textual gradients as ranked local edit proposals: have the critic produce section-level critiques, rank edit positions, sample structured insert, replace, or delete actions, use fluency, semantic similarity, coherence, and task metrics such as execution accuracy for ARKS and answer-change accuracy for CLARKS-news as proxy losses, and limit iterations to three or use early stopping because PACE shows plateauing after a few iterations. Fourth, based on [(Gotmare et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6d994b4f5a46cd14e8f09f1e9e49120546b15e31) and [(Shojaee et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a6bc37a07a37e3573d36e10cc11669eca0ff903), for ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, and ARKS-TensorflowM, train a critic to predict compiler or unit-test outcomes, use critic sampling or repair rounds, evaluate pass@k and execution accuracy, and explicitly budget compute because CodeRL required 16 A100-40G GPUs for about 21 days. Fifth, based on [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e42c121e8991650899b89bb6e8f3545962371d2a), create counterfactual code snippets using AST subtree insertion and induced execution feedback, but also evaluate retrieval confusion with MRR or retrieval precision to ensure edited KB documents do not make negative snippets indistinguishable from positives. Sixth, based on [(Lowe et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c3ece1ba41c415d7e81cfa5ca33a8de66efd434), consider actor ensembles or approximate policies for document actors, with K = 2 or K = 3 sub-policies, to improve robustness and allow the centralized critic to tolerate imperfect document-specific models. Seventh, based on [(Zhao et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef08ad10d257fcf5043d27d7225d6ec14a677a84), include negative expert feedback in the actor state, separate positive and negative feedback embeddings, and tune state length N and regularization alpha because positive items were more sensitive than skipped items. Finally, report statistical significance, confidence intervals, random seeds, hardware, MCTS UCT depth, expansion width, iterations, exploration constant, G-Eval judge prompts, and retrieval budgets to address reproducibility gaps common in the related work.

## Context: The KB editing process must search over possible document configurations to improve RAG performance. Method: We use Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to explore the state space of possible KB configurations, with the objective of finding the optimal KB state that maximizes RAG system performance.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Wang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d0df3168870fd17b36ecd5575e406feb5a5a1b5), which directly treats RAG knowledge configuration as a decision problem and shows that learned agents selecting and refining knowledge partitions improve RAG outputs: on XSum and BigPatent with 4-bit Mixtral 8x7B, M-RAG improved R-1 by 8% and 11% over Selfmem, translation Es->En by 8%, dialogue B-1 by 12%, with t-test p<0.05; ablations showed removing Agent-S reduced XSum R-1 from 48.13 to 44.20 and replacing Agent-R with greedy reduced it to 45.75, while M=4 partitions and K=3 candidates were stable. This supports MAC-CAFE's core premise that searching over KB configurations can improve RAG performance, although Wang et al. use bandit/MDP policies over partitions and automatic metrics rather than MCTS over document edits or expert feedback. Next, [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f31ab3e13618b7d1bf1fe7b695f54f7c27154fc2) supports the MCTS component by applying MCTS to combinatorial synthesis configurations with delayed rewards and a learned retrieval-guided policy; it reports consistent gains over prior ML synthesis and its MCTS+Learning baseline, and an ablation shows the BERT encoder matters, with replacing it causing 2.51%, 4.04%, and 4.11% ADP reductions on MCNC, EPFL arithmetic, and EPFL random control, plus a -10.90% adverse change on R3. Its experimental details—56 netlists, 23/13/20 split, 512 simulations per synthesis level, 100 test runs, one RTX A4000 16GB for about 9 days—show that MCTS over large configuration spaces can be effective but requires substantial search budget and learned guidance. [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) is the most direct RAG-MCTS analogue, using UCB selection, LLM query proposers, and a five-point LLM rubric to search retrieval states on BioASQ 2023 Task 11b with BM25, but the supplied excerpt contains no quantitative outcomes, so it supports the architecture conceptually rather than empirically. [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240) provides foundational evidence for UCT: in random P-game trees with branching factor 8 and depth 8 over 200 trees and 200 runs, UCT reached zero failure at leaf-node counts comparable to alpha-beta, outperformed plain Monte Carlo, and in a sailing MDP required fewer simulator samples than ARTDP and PG-ID to reach policy error below 0.1 on grids from 2x2 to 40x40, supporting UCT as a stochastic tree-search choice for MAC-CAFE's UCT configuration. [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f3822eb380b5e753a6d579f31dfc3ec4c4a0820) supports LLM-based state-space search by showing Tree of Thoughts improves GPT-4 performance from 4% to 74% on Game of 24, 4.25 to 4.76 in creative writing, and 0% to 73.3% on mini crosswords, indicating that LLM-generated candidate states and evaluators can guide search, though the tasks are not KB editing or RAG. [(Ouyang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766bffc357127e0dc86dd69561d5aeb520d6f4c) supports feedback-derived centralized reward signals: 175B InstructGPT was preferred over GPT-3 85 ± 3% of the time and over few-shot GPT-3 71 ± 4%, reward models achieved 69.6 ± 0.9% held-out accuracy versus 72.4 ± 0.4% training accuracy, and PPO improved helpfulness/safety, supporting MAC-CAFE's centralized critic and expert-feedback reward, but it uses PPO on language models rather than MCTS over KB documents. [(Zou et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69d309d5da72df0f05559e8f38edae15a34c0e1c) supports document-level multi-agent optimization of a global ranking objective, with NDCG gains over RankNet, AdaRank, ListNet, and MDPRank on MQ2007 and OHSUMED using 5-fold cross-validation, sparse NDCG reward, 100-hidden-unit MLPs, discount 0.95, learning rate 4e-7, and top-10 stabilization after about 4 steps, but it does not edit documents or evaluate RAG. Finally, [(Zhang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb26c0cf930b04635540e4815c4b8ca0581155c) supports using tree-search-generated preference data and step-level optimization, improving reasoning by an average 4.3% and maximum 9.7% over CoT and running 57.5 times faster than Tree-of-Thought, while showing that full-path preference optimization decreased performance by 4.6% relative to step-level Chain of Preference Optimization, which supports MAC-CAFE's decoupling of document-level edits rather than optimizing whole-KB trajectories.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the proposed MCTS budget—UCT with depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, and exploration constant 2.5—is likely too small for stochastic, LLM-evaluated KB states. [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f31ab3e13618b7d1bf1fe7b695f54f7c27154fc2) uses 512 simulations per synthesis level and 100 test runs, with about 9 days on one RTX A4000 16GB, indicating that effective MCTS over combinatorial configurations often requires far more simulations than 5 iterations. [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240) shows UCT convergence depends on tree size and failure tolerance, with iterations scaling roughly as B^(D/2); for the proposed B=3 and D=3 this gives about 5.2, so 5 iterations is near the theoretical lower bound and leaves little room for noisy RAG rewards, expert-feedback variance, or document-level stochasticity. Next, the most direct RAG-MCTS evidence is weak: [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) describes UCB-based MCTS for BioASQ 2023 Task 11b with BM25 and an LLM five-point rubric, but the excerpt reports no quantitative retrieval accuracy, answer accuracy, baseline comparison, or statistical significance, so it does not empirically validate that MCTS improves RAG. [(Wang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d0df3168870fd17b36ecd5575e406feb5a5a1b5) contradicts the specific MCTS choice by showing that a simpler multi-agent MDP/bandit over partitions and memory refinement can improve RAG with automatic metrics and p<0.05, suggesting that full MCTS may be unnecessary or harder to justify unless it outperforms cheaper partition-selection baselines. [(Ouyang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766bffc357127e0dc86dd69561d5aeb520d6f4c) contradicts the feasibility of a lightweight centralized critic by showing that reliable human-feedback reward modeling required about 13k SFT prompts, 33k preference comparisons, 31k PPO prompts, 40 trained contractors, and large GPT-3 models, whereas MAC-CAFE assumes a centralized critic can produce useful textual gradients from expert feedback without specifying data volume, reward-model training, or calibration. [(Zhang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb26c0cf930b04635540e4815c4b8ca0581155c) contradicts naive full-state optimization: full-path preference optimization decreased performance by 4.6% relative to step-level optimization and even performed worse than supervised fine-tuning that ignored dispreferred data, implying that MAC-CAFE must avoid treating the entire KB trajectory as one monolithic preference target. [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f3822eb380b5e753a6d579f31dfc3ec4c4a0820) and [(Zou et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69d309d5da72df0f05559e8f38edae15a34c0e1c) provide weaker contradictions because their settings are not KB editing or RAG: ToT used GPT-4 with temperature 0.7 on puzzles and creative writing without MCTS hyperparameters or RAG metrics, and Zou et al. used document-level agents for ranking with sparse NDCG rewards, 5-fold cross-validation, and no KB editing, so they do not directly challenge MAC-CAFE but limit the transferability of their positive results.

### Suggested Action

Most important: replace the fixed 5-iteration MCTS budget with a validated search-budget ablation. Because [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240) shows UCT convergence scales with branching factor and depth, and [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f31ab3e13618b7d1bf1fe7b695f54f7c27154fc2) uses 512 simulations per level with 100 runs, MAC-CAFE should test iterations such as 5, 20, 50, 100, and 512, depths 2-5, widths 2-5, and UCT constants 1.0-3.0, reporting value convergence, edit success, execution accuracy, and wall-clock cost. Second, add learned guidance to MCTS rather than relying only on raw UCT: use the centralized critic to train a value function or policy over document-edit states, and use embedding similarity or novelty scores to prioritize documents, following [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f31ab3e13618b7d1bf1fe7b695f54f7c27154fc2)'s retrieval-guided alpha modulation and [(Wang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d0df3168870fd17b36ecd5575e406feb5a5a1b5)'s Agent-S/Agent-R partition selection. Third, optimize document-level or step-level preferences instead of full KB paths, because [(Zhang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3fb26c0cf930b04635540e4815c4b8ca0581155c) found full-path preference optimization reduced performance by 4.6% relative to step-level optimization; MAC-CAFE should form document-specific preferred/dispreferred edit pairs, include dispreferred edits, and evaluate whether local actor updates generalize. Fourth, strengthen evaluation with multiple metrics and statistical tests: use execution accuracy for ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, and ARKS-TensorflowM, retrieval or answer accuracy for CLARKS-news, G-Eval coherence, and held-out human expert ratings, reporting confidence intervals and p-values as in [(Wang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d0df3168870fd17b36ecd5575e406feb5a5a1b5) and [(Ouyang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766bffc357127e0dc86dd69561d5aeb520d6f4c); do not rely solely on G-Eval. Fifth, build and validate the centralized critic as a reward model: collect enough expert feedback, split train/validation/test, measure held-out preference accuracy, and calibrate textual gradients, following [(Ouyang et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766bffc357127e0dc86dd69561d5aeb520d6f4c)'s held-out labeler evaluation. Sixth, include cheaper RAG baselines such as partition selection, top-k retrieval, iterative retrieval, and document addition, because [(Wang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d0df3168870fd17b36ecd5575e406feb5a5a1b5) shows partition-level agents can improve RAG and [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) suggests query/retrieval-state search may be a useful component. Seventh, use LLM evaluators and document-level actors carefully: validate LLM rubrics against human judgments as in [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b) and [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f3822eb380b5e753a6d579f31dfc3ec4c4a0820), and structure actor coordination with a shared critic or global reward as in [(Zou et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69d309d5da72df0f05559e8f38edae15a34c0e1c). Finally, report reproducibility details: model versions, prompt templates, random seeds, hardware, token budgets, chunking, retrieval settings, and MCTS logs, as emphasized by the detailed experimental constraints in [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f31ab3e13618b7d1bf1fe7b695f54f7c27154fc2) and the missing quantitative details in [(Hu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/79fa21c6d9ee706c694475b56be8612f21a3f04b).

## Context: The full KB state space is too large for direct search. Method: We decouple KB edits by isolating document-level modifications and breaking them into manageable sections.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed decoupling of KB edits into document-level sections is [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59c0daa1abe25f0039708f72225e0cb4a02b2c96), which updates a non-parametric RAG corpus on the LeKUBE Chinese legal dataset by splitting 180 updated statutes into nine chapter-level subsets, Criminal Law Chapters III, VI, VIII, IX and Civil Code Books 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, and reports best locality, good scalability, and good retainability across 642 updated-statute true/false questions, 164 non-updated true/false questions, and 180 multiple-choice scenario questions. This directly mirrors MAC-CAFE's plan to isolate document-level modifications, use section-level updates, and keep retrieval within a 18000-token budget. The next strongest support is [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a), which studies document-based knowledge editing on 2,000 Edit Success/Locality instances, 583 Reasoning instances, and 1,000 Cross-lingual Transfer instances using LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3.1-8B, InternLM-7B, and ChatGPT, and shows that converting documents into sentence or triple units before applying SEARC, MEMIT, or IKE can make document editing tractable when the extracted units are high quality. This supports MAC-CAFE's sectioning idea, but only if sections are precise and non-ambiguous. A further supporting signal is [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), where MeLLo's fact-level external memory with Contriever retrieval over MQUAKE-CF and MQUAKE-T degrades as edits scale, for example GPT-J 6B falls from 38.9 percent on MQUAKE-CF at one edit to 14.2 percent at 3000 edits and from 85.9 percent on MQUAKE-T to 30.7 percent at 1868 edits. This supports the motivation to move from a monolithic KB or fact-level memory to document-level sections, although it also warns that retrieval over many units can degrade. Older but relevant evidence that decoupling improves tractability includes [(Mitchell et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d650f1afd45c59ff907396fe8b678595dcb85ea), where SERAC decouples a T5-large editor from base models using a T5-small counterfactual model and distilBERT scope classifier, achieving QA success above 0.99 with drawdown below 0.01 and QA-hard success above 0.92 with drawdown below 0.03 after 10 edits, with overhead of 87 to 92 ms and 2.96 to 3.47 GB for QA. [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) supports isolated edit units by storing sequential edits as key-value memories on FEVER and ZsRE with BERT-base, BART-base, and GPT-2 XL, using thresholds t=0.9, t_d=0.15, t_s=0.05, Top-K=5, and a single A100, reducing T-Patcher's 2 to 3 days for 1000 edits to about 50 seconds per edit with 75 MB or 300 MB memory for 1000 edits. [(Meng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe1ac0b09cc0f50eb83eef6c7c6b45ac8b12413) supports decomposing large edit sets into localized units by distributing 10,000 zsRE edits across critical MLP layers in GPT-J 6B and GPT-NeoX 20B. Weaker support comes from local-edit search and gradient-guided editing: [(Kumar et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/724d182e2b0217ce10ff05e3ad0b2f548fb39b9f) shows discrete scored operations on WikiLarge and Newsela can control simplification, and [(Sha, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db9715d147baf3fdd04830cfb7c88e30c07658eb) shows localized edits under a learned score can improve constrained generation and Quora paraphrase generation with Wilcoxon p below 0.05. None of these directly validates MCTS, multi-actor coordination, or a centralized critic, so their support is indirect for MAC-CAFE's full architecture.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7), which shows that decomposing unstructured facts into extracted triplets did not uniformly improve editing and harmed retrieval or in-context methods because multiple related triplets can interfere. On COUNTERFACT, MQUAKE-CF, and WIKIUPDATE with GPT2-XL, GPT-J 6B, and Mistral 7B, Mistral MeLLo single-edit accuracy fell from 40.11 on structured facts to 27.68 on extracted triplets, GPT-J LoRA fell from 10.17 to 2.83, and ROME or MEMIT reached about 16 percent on COUNTERFACT and MQUAKE-CF but dropped to about 2.3 percent on WIKIUPDATE. This directly challenges MAC-CAFE's assumption that breaking KB edits into sections will automatically improve RAG performance, especially with embedding similarity retrieval and iterative retrieval, where related but wrong sections may be retrieved. The next strongest contradiction is [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a), where decomposition quality is a bottleneck: Tool-based triples caused SEARC to fall from 74.25 with gold triples to 25.50 on LLaMA2-7B, and IKE with raw documents scored 55.60 versus 87.30 with gold triples on LLaMA3.1-8B; sampled Tool triples had 42.74 percent Incorrect Subject errors and 33.87 percent Ambiguous Triple errors, while the LLM pipeline had higher recall but still produced incorrect relations. This contradicts a naive 50-line chunking strategy in MAC-CAFE unless sections are validated for completeness, specificity, and non-ambiguity. A further contradiction is [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), where even an external-memory fact-level method degrades at scale, with GPT-J 6B falling from 38.9 percent to 14.2 percent on MQUAKE-CF and from 85.9 percent to 30.7 percent on MQUAKE-T as edit batches grow from 1 to 3000 or 1868, and where retrieval accuracy is a key performance factor. If MAC-CAFE increases the number of sections, retrieval recall may drop and MCTS may explore noisy states. Weaker contradictions include [(Kumar et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/724d182e2b0217ce10ff05e3ad0b2f548fb39b9f), where adding reordering to RM+EX+LS did not improve SARI, dropping from 37.85 to 37.58 and BLEU from 73.62 to 70.15 on WikiLarge, showing that adding more local edit operations or finer decomposition does not automatically improve outcomes. [(Sha, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db9715d147baf3fdd04830cfb7c88e30c07658eb) and [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) show local or edit-level decoupling can work, but they do not provide statistical significance or document-level evidence, so they do not support the specific MCTS, multi-actor, and document-section claim. Finally, no related work validates the proposed UCT MCTS configuration with depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, and exploration constant=2.5 over document states; the closest search methods use local thresholds or gradient steps, leaving the MCTS component under-evidenced.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a section-quality and retrieval-interference evaluation before relying on MCTS. Following [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7) and [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a), MAC-CAFE should compare 50-line chunks against semantic or triple-validated sections, report section precision/recall, retrieval precision@k, false-section retrieval rate, and downstream RAG accuracy, and use gold sections or gold triples as an upper bound. It should also deduplicate related sections, add scope constraints, and reject sections with ambiguous subject/relation or incomplete context, using metrics analogous to the 42.74 percent Incorrect Subject and 33.87 percent Ambiguous Triple error analysis. The second most important action is to test scalability explicitly, because [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) shows fact-level external memory degrades from 38.9 percent to 14.2 percent on MQUAKE-CF and from 85.9 percent to 30.7 percent on MQUAKE-T as edits increase, while [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59c0daa1abe25f0039708f72225e0cb4a02b2c96) shows chapter-level RAG updates can retain locality and retainability. MAC-CAFE should evaluate increasing numbers of sections or edits, for example 1, 10, 100, and 1000, and report retrieval recall, MCTS node expansions, wall-clock time, API or GPU cost, and RAG accuracy, and consider hierarchical section indexing or section-level routing instead of searching the full KB state. Third, adopt a decoupled actor/editor design with external section memory and a scope classifier, inspired by [(Mitchell et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d650f1afd45c59ff907396fe8b678595dcb85ea) and [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3), so each actor stores section-level edit descriptors and the system can route queries to affected sections without re-optimizing the whole KB; report latency and memory overhead as in SERAC's 87 to 92 ms and 2.96 to 3.47 GB measurements and RASE's 75 MB or 300 MB memory for 1000 edits. Fourth, define structured local edit operations such as insert, delete, replace, merge, and split, with acceptance thresholds and ablations, following [(Kumar et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/724d182e2b0217ce10ff05e3ad0b2f548fb39b9f) and [(Sha, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db9715d147baf3fdd04830cfb7c88e30c07658eb), and use G-Eval coherence with GPT-4, execution accuracy, completeness, and generalization as reward signals rather than assuming MCTS alone will find good edits. Finally, strengthen the experimental design by adding baselines such as PROMPT AGENT-E, no-sectioning full-document editing, fact-level MeLLo or IKE, and parameter-editing baselines, reporting statistical significance or confidence intervals, and ablating MCTS depth, width, iterations, and exploration constant, because none of the related works directly validate the proposed MCTS, multi-actor, and centralized critic configuration.

## Context: The plan must test both incomplete and incorrect knowledge base settings. Method: We will evaluate MAC-CAFE on five datasets spanning incomplete and incorrect KB scenarios, including ARKS-Pony and ARKS-Ring for code generation with compiler feedback, ARKS-ScipyM and ARKS-TensorflowM for perturbed library documentation, and CLARKS-news for factual knowledge updates.

### Support

The strongest support for evaluating MAC-CAFE on the proposed five datasets comes from [(Su et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0874581dabb0af63971fa3026779d4e1e4ea5f61), which uses the same four ARKS code-generation settings corresponding to ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, and ARKS-TensorflowM and treats compiler or interpreter execution feedback as the knowledge source. Their evolving retrieval pipeline starts from the problem description, retrieves from a knowledge soup containing documentation, verified code snippets, and accumulated execution feedback, generates code with ChatGPT or CodeLlama, executes generated test inputs, and updates the KB with successful programs or failed programs paired with errors; using INSTRUCTOR-xl retrieval, a 4,096-token context, and token budgets from 4,000 to 24,000, it achieved a 16.1 percentage point absolute accuracy gain with ChatGPT and a 16.2 percentage point gain with CodeLlama over a documentation-only baseline, while iterative baselines without retrieval improved less than 2 percent on average and execution accuracy remained 0 on Ring. This directly supports the plan's incomplete and incorrect code-KB evaluation, its use of compiler feedback, and its focus on perturbed or outdated library documentation. A second strong line of support is [(Zhou et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a39442979d6e678dd36bb443ad529c14e86a86e), which shows that retrieval-conditioned code generation over a 400,000-paragraph Bash documentation pool and top-10 documents more than doubled command-name accuracy for T5, increased full-prediction charBLEU by more than 16 points, improved exact match by about 9 points, improved few-shot Codex charBLEU by 6.7 points, improved CodeT5 BLEU by 1.65 points, and raised unseen function-name recall from 9.03 to 18.30, with unigram overlap increasing from 12 percent to 24 percent; this supports the ARKS code datasets but assumes a complete documentation pool. A third strong line of support for CLARKS-news is [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), which supports external memory-based KB editing over parameter editing by showing MeLLo with Contriever embeddings outperformed MEMIT and MEND on MQUAKE-CF and MQUAKE-T, for example GPT-J multi-hop accuracy of 38.9, 20.3, 15.6, and 14.2 for 1, 100, 1,000, and 3,000 edited instances on MQUAKE-CF. Newer work by [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7) extends Zhong et al. by showing that in-context and retrieval-augmented methods are more robust than parameter editing on unstructured facts, such as IKE on Mistral declining 21 percent on COUNTERFACT versus 94 percent for other methods, which supports the plan's focus on unstructured factual updates. A further supporting line is [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa), which extends foundational RAG evidence by showing that BART-Large RAG-Token with dense passage retrieval, poly-encoder re-ranking, and end-to-end re-ranking reduced hallucination and improved knowledgeability in human evaluation of 100 Wizard of Wikipedia test-unseen responses. [(Borgeaud et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002c256d30d6be4b23d365a8de8ae0e67e4c9641) supports the need for leakage-aware evaluation in retrieval-enhanced settings by introducing filtered bits-per-byte bpb(alpha), using chunks of length at most 64, top-10 neighbours of length up to 128, and MinHash 13-gram Jaccard 0.8 filtering. The foundational RAG evidence from [(Lewis et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/659bf9ce7175e1ec266ff54359e2bd76e0b7ff31) supports the broader premise that external passages can condition generation, using RAG-Sequence and RAG-Token with DPR retrieval over 21 million 100-word Wikipedia chunks indexed with FAISS HNSW and k=5 or 10. The weakest but relevant support is [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e2ce535acf3a2ce8fd36d0f97e36c7777f65504), which shows that external KB editing can be implemented with a 743-item multilingual KB and LLaMA2-7b on a single A100 80GB GPU, with one-edit latencies of 0.70 to 1.35 seconds for ReMaKE. Overall, the related work supports the plan's choice of ARKS code datasets, compiler feedback, perturbed documentation, and factual-update evaluation, especially because the newest and most directly aligned work by Su et al. operates on the same ARKS settings.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan's assumption that document-level KB edits will remain coherent and generalizable is [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), which shows that retrieval-based memory editing can degrade sharply as the number of simultaneous edits increases: with GPT-J, MeLLo multi-hop accuracy on MQUAKE-CF fell from 38.9 for one edited instance to 20.3 for 100, 15.6 for 1,000, and 14.2 for 3,000, while on MQUAKE-T it fell from 85.9 for one edit to 45.7 for 100, 33.8 for 500, and 30.7 for 1,868; parameter-editing methods also collapsed on multi-hop questions, with MEMIT dropping from 40.5 to 7.0 on GPT-J and from 30.2 to 4.9 on Vicuna-7B on MQUAKE-CF. This directly challenges MAC-CAFE's MCTS-based document editing and its completeness/generalization metrics unless edit scale, locality, and multi-hop consistency are explicitly controlled. A second strong contradiction is [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7), which extends Zhong et al. by showing that unstructured and real-world factual updates are harder than isolated triplets: on WIKIUPDATE with GPT2-XL, IKE's decline grew from 9 percent on COUNTERFACT to 52 percent, and ROME and MEMIT with GPT-J fell from around 16.0 percent on counterfactual datasets to about 2.3 percent; MeLLo on GPT-J also declined from 29.10 to 15.54 on unstructured MQUAKE-CF. This contradicts the assumption that CLARKS-news factual updates will be handled robustly by retrieval-oriented KB editing without additional unstructured-fact, extracted-triplet, and temporal-cutoff controls. A third contradiction is [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa), which shows that more retrieved context is not always better: increasing RAG-Token documents from 5 to 25 produced only a small regular F1 gain from 22.5 to 22.6 and a perplexity drop from 13.4 to 13.0, but reduced Knowledge F1 from 26.0 to 24.7 on valid seen and from 22.7 to 21.1 on valid unseen, with higher human-evaluated hallucination; this warns that MAC-CAFE's 18,000-token retrieval budget and 50-line document chunks may introduce noise if re-ranking and budget ablations are omitted. A fourth contradiction is [(Borgeaud et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002c256d30d6be4b23d365a8de8ae0e67e4c9641), which shows that retrieval-enhanced models can exploit evaluation-set leakage, with negative filtered bpb(alpha) slopes for Retro on C4 and Wikitext103 attributed to copy-pasting retrieved training chunks; if perturbed ARKS documentation or CLARKS-news KB edits overlap evaluation items, execution accuracy or factual accuracy may reflect leakage rather than genuine KB refinement. A fifth, more design-level contradiction is that [(Su et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0874581dabb0af63971fa3026779d4e1e4ea5f61) strongly supports the evaluation setting but also weakly contradicts the necessity of MAC-CAFE's added MCTS and centralized critic because a simpler evolving retrieval-and-execution-feedback pipeline already achieved large gains over documentation-only retrieval. A sixth weaker limitation is that [(Zhou et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a39442979d6e678dd36bb443ad529c14e86a86e) demonstrates large documentation-retrieval gains only under a complete and relevant documentation pool, so its results do not guarantee that the same retrieval benefit will survive the incomplete and perturbed ARKS settings proposed here. The weakest contradiction is [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e2ce535acf3a2ce8fd36d0f97e36c7777f65504), which reports only one-edit latency for a 743-item KB with LLaMA2-7b on an A100 80GB GPU and no editing accuracy, generality, locality, or portability, so it cannot support the effectiveness of MAC-CAFE's editing objective.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add the directly comparable ARKS baselines and ablations from [(Su et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0874581dabb0af63971fa3026779d4e1e4ea5f61) before claiming that MAC-CAFE's MCTS, centralized critic, and multi-actor editing improve KB quality: implement documentation-only retrieval, iterative execution-feedback accumulation, query evolution, and combinations with MPSC, ExeDec, or Reflexion, alongside PROMPT AGENT-E, and report execution accuracy, token-budget curves from 4,000 to 24,000 tokens, and ablations for MCTS depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, exploration constant=2.5, document-wise actor isolation, and critic-generated textual gradients. A second priority is to add leakage-aware evaluation inspired by [(Borgeaud et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002c256d30d6be4b23d365a8de8ae0e67e4c9641), including n-gram or MinHash Jaccard filtering of KB documents against evaluation items, overlap-binned accuracy or bpb(alpha)-style curves, and held-out documentation or unseen function/command splits, so that gains on ARKS-ScipyM, ARKS-TensorflowM, and CLARKS-news are not caused by copying perturbed or overlapping content. A third priority is to adopt the scale, multi-hop, and unstructured-update metrics from [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) and [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7), such as edit-wise success, instance-wise accuracy, multi-hop accuracy, locality, portability, structured versus unstructured facts, and extracted-triplet variants, while testing increasing numbers of KB edits and reporting confidence intervals or significance tests. A fourth action is to tune retrieval and chunking using evidence from [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa), comparing 5 versus 25 retrieved documents, 50-line chunks versus 100-word chunks, TFIDF versus dense retrieval versus poly-encoder or end-to-end re-ranking, and 4,096-token versus 18,000-token budgets, with human evaluation of hallucination, knowledgeability, and consistency. A fifth action is to use code-specific metrics from [(Zhou et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0a39442979d6e678dd36bb443ad529c14e86a86e) and [(Su et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0874581dabb0af63971fa3026779d4e1e4ea5f61), including command-name accuracy, charBLEU, exact match, function-name recall, and execution-based accuracy on unseen commands or functions, to show that edits generalize beyond memorized examples. A sixth action is to make CLARKS-news evaluation more realistic by following [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7) with timestamp-aware factual updates, longer unstructured news facts, and controls for model training cutoff, and to complement G-Eval coherence scores with human coherence and hallucination ratings. The least important but still useful action is to report compute and latency details in the style of [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e2ce535acf3a2ce8fd36d0f97e36c7777f65504), including one-edit latency, MCTS search cost, GPU or API usage, GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW calls, OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE embedding costs, and token budget, so that the method is reproducible.

## Context: The coding experiments require a controlled protocol for measuring whether edits fix failures. Method: We will split failure cases into train/eval/test in 1:1:2 ratios, using execution accuracy as the success metric.

### Support

The strongest support for using execution accuracy in the MAC-CAFE coding protocol comes from [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5), which evaluated generated competitive-programming code with hidden tests, temporally separated CodeContests splits of 13,328 training, 117 validation, and 165 test problems, 9B and 41B models, sample budgets from 1k to 1M, optional clustering, and bootstrapped n@k and pass@k metrics; its 41B model reached 31.8 percent validation and 29.6 percent test solve rate at 10@100k, and 34.2 percent validation at 10@1M, showing that execution-based solve rates can measure generalization under train/validation/test separation. This newer execution-based protocol supersedes older general-accuracy code benchmarks such as [(Lu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/870ff1dde0c103c3d90be51880f984628e77a8d6), which used CodeBERT, CodeGPT, and encoder-decoder baselines on 10 tasks but did not report execution accuracy or failure-case splits. The next strongest support is [(Hendrycks et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd031f28dccfb226f6c0c588c93a97a50bf95f), which established APPS execution-based strict accuracy and average test-case pass rate on 10,000 problems, 131,777 test cases, and a 5,000/5,000 split, using beam width 5, AdamW, batch size 256, 10 epochs, and 8 A100 GPUs; GPT-Neo 2.7B achieved about 4 percent strict accuracy top-1 and over 5 percent top-5 on Introductory problems, while GPT-2 0.1B had top-5 average test-case score 10.75 and GPT-2 1.5B top-1 7.96, supporting execution as a binary success signal although not a failure-case repair protocol. [(Inala et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/075b6fb7d3787953164eecc1bd2e13f97c9f3c44) further supports execution-labeled train/eval/test evaluation by sampling 100 programs per APPS task, labeling CORRECT or WRONG via unit tests, training rankers on V100-32GB GPUs, and reporting pass@1, pass@5, pass@100, exec@1, and ranked metrics with an unbiased estimator; Codex pass@1 on APPS test rose from 3.8 percent to 4.7 percent, and a 125M generator plus 125M ranker outperformed a larger GPT-J model, showing that execution labels can drive held-out improvement. Newer work by [(Dong et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a95817df43516f5ff9cfc447209def1fcd569afc) supports execution-based correctness with APPS-Eval, MBPP-Eval, and HE-Eval train/dev/test partitions, 20 or more candidates, 100 or more extended tests, a single A100-40G GPU, five runs, and CodeScore correlations of 40.56 percent Kendall-Tau, 55.07 percent Spearman, and 58.87 percent Pearson over the best non-execution baseline, with human evaluation p-values below 0.005. [(Dong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba2f935d2578fbf77ec1aa79e26e3db396771e38) supports feedback-driven repair of failing code by using ChatGPT gpt-3.5-turbo-0301, temperature 0, max tokens 512, and up to four analyst-coder-tester interactions, achieving 74.4, 56.1, 68.2, and 49.5 Pass@1 on HumanEval, MBPP, HumanEval-ET, and MBPP-ET, and correcting 18.4 percent of HumanEval tasks, which aligns with MAC-CAFE expert-feedback editing. [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b6d4ef071a847c9b629bf4055838047ff670ff8) supports execution in repository-level settings with 1,874 nonstandalone and 706 standalone functions, Pytest environments, unbiased Pass@k, greedy decoding for k=1, and nucleus sampling with temperature 0.4, top-p 0.95, and max length 500 for k>1; gpt-4 Pass@1 was 17.40 without context and improved by 205 percent and 173 percent with local-file completion and infilling contexts, indicating execution accuracy is valid but context-dependent. [(Lian et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2926d1b33ef97d3d706944fb588a0e6d747810aa) supports failure-case identification using execution-based pass@1 on HumanEval+ and DS-1000 and exact match on CoNaLa, producing 3,125 annotated prompt-code pairs with 14 annotators, which is a precursor to the proposed failure-case split. [(Lin et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4eff164ceb0b80f57c2d0cbd10241799972fa9d0) is the closest RAG coding protocol, using GPT-4 with temperature 0, RAG retrieval, IKEC prompting, and planned executability and unit-test pass rate, but only preliminary 5-script results were reported, including 86.21 percent and 93.10 percent correct lines for one 29-line example and 73.33 percent average correct lines, so its support is weak.

### Contradictions

No related work directly refutes the 1:1:2 failure-case split, but several provide strong cautions that contradict a naive execution-accuracy protocol. The strongest contradiction is [(Dong et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a95817df43516f5ff9cfc447209def1fcd569afc), which found that execution-based metrics using only original small test sets had significantly lower correlation than with extended test cases, and on APPS-Eval, where test cases were rare or low-quality, CodeScore far exceeded execution-based baselines; it also reported that execution-based baselines were 20.7k, 28.7k, and 22.1k times slower, implying that MAC-CAFE execution accuracy can be unreliable and expensive if ARKS-Pony or ARKS-Ring test cases are sparse. This newer finding contradicts older reliance on raw execution outcomes in [(Hendrycks et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd031f28dccfb226f6c0c588c93a97a50bf95f) and [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5) by showing that test-case quality and coverage materially affect the metric. [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5) also contradicts the assumption that raw execution outcomes are clean by reporting false-positive rates of 62 percent for raw CodeContests, 60 percent for APPS, and 30 percent for HumanEval, versus 4 percent for cleaned CodeContests, so execution accuracy must be paired with validated hidden tests. [(Inala et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/075b6fb7d3787953164eecc1bd2e13f97c9f3c44) shows execution labels are highly imbalanced, with roughly 5 to 40 times more WRONG than CORRECT examples, and improvements on the harder APPS test set were smaller, suggesting that failure-case splits may contain many unrepairable or low-signal examples. [(Hendrycks et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd031f28dccfb226f6c0c588c93a97a50bf95f) reports high syntax-error rates, about 59 percent for GPT-3 on Introductory problems and about 3 percent for GPT-Neo, and does not report statistical significance tests, indicating that execution accuracy can be confounded by surface syntax and sampling variance. [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b6d4ef071a847c9b629bf4055838047ff670ff8) shows repository-level execution accuracy can be much lower than standard benchmarks, with gpt-4 Pass@1 17.40 versus 88.4 on HumanEval, so low-resource ARKS-Pony and ARKS-Ring tasks may yield sparse positive outcomes and require larger samples or k-sample estimators. [(Dong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba2f935d2578fbf77ec1aa79e26e3db396771e38) shows feedback repair is not guaranteed, with 36.5 percent of HumanEval tasks remaining unpassed after four interactions and tester errors mainly involving exception handling, which cautions against assuming MAC-CAFE edits will fix all failures. [(Lian et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2926d1b33ef97d3d706944fb588a0e6d747810aa) only identifies failures and does not measure edit-fix accuracy, so it cannot support the causal claim that KB edits repair failures. [(Lin et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4eff164ceb0b80f57c2d0cbd10241799972fa9d0) provides only preliminary 5-script results and no complete unit-test pass rates, executability rates, ablations, or significance, so it is weak evidence for a controlled protocol. [(Lu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/870ff1dde0c103c3d90be51880f984628e77a8d6) is the weakest contradiction because it relies on general accuracy rather than execution accuracy, but it also lacks failure-case splits and execution metrics, highlighting that the proposed protocol is not established by that older benchmark.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define a validated execution harness for ARKS-Pony and ARKS-Ring before applying the 1:1:2 split: use hidden unit tests or compiler/test feedback that are not exposed to the editing agents, filter false positives, include syntax, runtime, and functional tests, and report both strict pass@1 and average pass ratio, as motivated by [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5) and [(Dong et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a95817df43516f5ff9cfc447209def1fcd569afc). For ARKS-Pony and ARKS-Ring, distinguish compiler-feedback failures from hidden-test failures, use compiler feedback in actor prompts or training signals, but do not let it replace held-out execution accuracy. Second, implement the 1:1:2 failure-case split as a stratified, leakage-controlled protocol: stratify by language, difficulty, error type, and KB document, reserve the test partition for final evaluation only, use the eval partition for MCTS node selection, early stopping, and hyperparameter checks, and use the train partition only for actor/critic training or edit proposals; if the failure pool is too small, report a power analysis or use stratified cross-validation instead of pretending the 1:1:2 split is stable. Third, add statistical rigor: report bootstrap confidence intervals or paired significance tests for train/eval/test accuracy, as motivated by [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5) bootstrapping and [(Inala et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/075b6fb7d3787953164eecc1bd2e13f97c9f3c44) unbiased estimator, and include multiple seeds, top-1/top-5 or pass@k, syntax-error rates, and execution time. Fourth, because execution can be costly and test-case-sensitive, consider a learned surrogate or ranking signal for repeated MCTS evaluation, while retaining execution accuracy as the final ground truth, following [(Dong et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a95817df43516f5ff9cfc447209def1fcd569afc) and [(Inala et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/075b6fb7d3787953164eecc1bd2e13f97c9f3c44). Fifth, compare MAC-CAFE against PROMPT AGENT-E and a no-edit or document-addition baseline under identical retrieval settings, GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW, OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE, 18000-token budget, and MCTS depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, exploration constant=2.5, and ablate MCTS and document-wise actor coordination to show that the measured execution-accuracy gains come from KB edits rather than retrieval or sampling. Finally, document reproducibility details: dataset sizes, test-case counts, hardware, timeout/resource limits, random seeds, and the exact failure-case definition, because related work shows that dataset cleaning, test quality, and execution environment strongly affect outcomes, as in [(Li et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbe278b65a81602a864184bbca37de91448a5f5), [(Hendrycks et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ccd031f28dccfb226f6c0c588c93a97a50bf95f), and [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b6d4ef071a847c9b629bf4055838047ff670ff8).

## Context: A comparable baseline is needed to isolate the contribution of document-wise KB editing. Method: We will implement PROMPT AGENT-E as our baseline, extending PROMPT AGENT for KB editing by creating separate document-wise optimization agents.

### Support

The strongest support for implementing PROMPT AGENT-E as a document-wise extension of PromptAgent comes from [(Wang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1eb1a8c7f88de27af224153f43ecdd41774600f2), which formalizes prompt optimization as an MDP and uses MCTS with exploration weight 2.5, 12 iterations, error-based actions generated from sampled base-model errors, and a held-out reward; with GPT-3.5 as the base LLM and GPT-4 as the optimizer, it reached 0.802 average accuracy on six BIG-Bench Hard tasks versus 0.513 human zero-shot, 0.581 zero-shot chain-of-thought, and 0.690 APE, and outperformed baselines on five of six tasks, with chain-of-thought higher only on Object Counting at 0.960 versus 0.860. This directly validates the search-and-feedback template that the MAC-CAFE plan would reuse for KB document states, especially because the plan already specifies UCT, depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, and exploration constant=2.5. The next strongest support is [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f), which shows that prompt-only feedback-refine loops improve outputs when feedback is actionable and aspect-specific: GPT-4 code optimization improved from 27.3 percent to 36.0 percent, dialogue response preference improved from 25.4 percent to 74.6 percent, and three-iteration averages improved for code optimization, sentiment reversal, and constrained generation; actionable feedback outperformed generic or no feedback, and multi-aspect numerical scores were needed to select among iterations. This supports using document-specific, aspect-level feedback and scores for completeness, generalization, and coherence in the baseline. Weaker but relevant support is [(Musumeci et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/066686edd54bf49d763a83510746425f6aaa4ae7), which demonstrates section-wise prompt agents with an accumulated document state, specialized semantics, retrieval, and content roles, and explicit tokens such as [ALL_INFO] to detect missing information, although it used gpt-3.5-turbo-1106 with a 16,385-token context and 4,096 output tokens and provided only qualitative examples. Still weaker support is [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc10d108823630343484c5eecbb5c3011751282e), which compares a single-agent with instruction against multi-agent with and without instruction across GPT-3.5, GPT-4, and Claude-3 for analog layout design, supporting the idea of isolating agent decomposition, but the excerpt lacks numeric accuracy values. The weakest support is [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26e357f72ab72e8875ec8ee3f66e45c0b0094012), which only identifies PromptAgent as a baseline in REPROMPT experiments using GPT-4-turbo-1106-preview, temperature 0, seed 42, and inherited stopping criteria, without reporting PromptAgent-specific results.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the cited works directly validates the proposed KB-editing baseline: [(Wang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1eb1a8c7f88de27af224153f43ecdd41774600f2) optimizes a single task prompt, not separate KB documents, and does not use a multi-actor centralized critic, RAG retrieval, or document-level state decomposition; it also reports no statistical significance tests, so the 0.802 BIG-Bench Hard result cannot be treated as definitive evidence that the same MCTS template will transfer to KB editing. The next strongest limitation is [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f), which shows the feedback-refine loop is fragile when the model cannot follow structured feedback: Vicuna-13B often failed to produce correctly formatted feedback or refinement prompts, and math reasoning gains were modest because subtle errors were not detected; this contradicts an assumption that document-wise prompt agents will reliably produce high-quality KB edits without strong instruction-following and error detection. A further contradiction is that [(Musumeci et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/066686edd54bf49d763a83510746425f6aaa4ae7) provides no quantitative metrics, baseline comparisons, dataset size, or statistical significance, so its document-generation workflow cannot support the plan's claims about completeness, generalization, or coherence. Similarly, [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc10d108823630343484c5eecbb5c3011751282e) reports only that a sanity-check table and classification accuracy were used, without numeric outcomes, making it weak evidence that multi-agent decomposition improves performance. The weakest contradiction is [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26e357f72ab72e8875ec8ee3f66e45c0b0094012), which gives no algorithm, prompt, editing procedure, accuracy, ablation, or significance for its PromptAgent baseline, leaving the exact baseline implementation under-specified.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement PROMPT AGENT-E as a faithful, non-centralized document-wise extension of [(Wang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1eb1a8c7f88de27af224153f43ecdd41774600f2): instantiate one MCTS agent per KB document or section, define each state as the current document text, generate error-based actions from RAG failures on the train split, and use held-out train/eval reward rather than test accuracy; keep UCT exploration constant 2.5 but ablate the plan's 5 iterations against PromptAgent's 12 iterations and against Standard/Wide/Lite-style width/depth settings, because the cited method selected the best setting by reward. The next most important action is to adopt the actionable, aspect-specific feedback design from [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f): require each document agent to produce structured, document-specific feedback with numerical scores for completeness, generalization, and coherence, and use those scores to select among MCTS iterations, while avoiding generic feedback that degraded performance in the cited work. A third action is to borrow the section-wise accumulated-state and explicit missing-information tokens from [(Musumeci et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/066686edd54bf49d763a83510746425f6aaa4ae7): maintain a document-level context across edits, use tokens such as [ALL_INFO] or [MISSING_INFO] to detect incomplete KB content, and include few-shot examples of expected edit structure, since the cited work found examples dramatically improved agent responses. A fourth action is to add a single-agent versus multi-document-agent ablation inspired by [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc10d108823630343484c5eecbb5c3011751282e) to isolate whether document-wise decomposition itself helps, using the same GPT-4-1106-preview reasoning model, 50-line chunking, 18,000-token retrieval budget, and execution accuracy or G-Eval coherence scores. Finally, because [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26e357f72ab72e8875ec8ee3f66e45c0b0094012) leaves PromptAgent implementation details sparse, the plan should document the exact baseline prompts, MCTS hyperparameters, seeds, temperatures, API versions, and cost, and report paired significance tests or confidence intervals across multiple runs to address the missing statistical evidence in the related work.

## Context: The research questions require measuring whether edits are complete, generalizable, and coherent. Method: We will use evaluation metrics for completeness as train set accuracy, generalization as test set accuracy, and coherence as document-wise coherence scores using G-Eval with GPT-4 as judge.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed MAC-CAFE evaluation is that GPT-4-based judging can be a reliable open-ended quality instrument when validated against humans. [(Bai et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/378a545c3a1cf6c4aada8f9ee8820c0d8008220a) used GPT-4 as examiner for 10,000 open-ended QA questions across 1,000 domains, assigning 1-3 scores on accuracy, coherence, factuality, and comprehensiveness plus a 1-5 overall score, and used pairwise merge-sort ranking; its pairwise comparisons had over 85% agreement with 300 human annotations over 100 questions, and its Likert scores correlated more strongly with human overall scores than ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L, BLEU, and BERTScore F1. This directly supports using G-Eval with GPT-4 for document-wise coherence in MAC-CAFE, although the setting is QA rather than KB documents. A close second is [(Peng et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff7457e8e389e7f20448cf33d0bc6205daa47a71), which used GPT-4 to score event-level knowledge editing answers on correctness, coherence, comprehensiveness, and overall 1-5 quality, and found Spearman rho 74.4% and Kendall tau 69.8% against one author's 120 human-scored answers; this supports GPT-4 coherence scoring specifically in editing contexts, but the human validation is small and single-annotator. Supporting the completeness and generalization metrics, [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5c73d9e6641b018b633690102121f5605d34fb0) evaluated ROME, MEMIT, SERAC, MEND, KE, KN, CaliNet, T-Patcher, IKE, and FT-L on ZsRE and COUNTERFACT using reliability, generalization, and locality, showing that edit success can be decomposed into target-fact reliability, paraphrase or related-prompt generalization, and unrelated-knowledge preservation; this aligns with MAC-CAFE's train-set accuracy for completeness and test-set accuracy for generalization, but it does not validate document coherence. Further support for test-set generalization comes from [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), whose MQUAKE-CF and MQUAKE-T protocol shows that high single-hop edit recall can coexist with degraded multi-hop accuracy, justifying a held-out test set that probes downstream use rather than only direct recall. For RAG-specific generalization, [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) used seen and unseen Wizard of Wikipedia and CMU_DoG splits, automatic F1, Knowledge F1, perplexity, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, retrieval recall, and 100-response human evaluation, supporting held-out unseen evaluation and human checks for consistency and hallucination. Weaker but still relevant support for coherence scoring comes from trained automatic evaluators: [(Yuan et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6a7724763d8adba466519489b0b9d209e7f2d15) BARTScore, [(Zhong et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/041f5dbfcd07a3369ac44a6b902ee4b145eccf2b) T5-based multi-dimensional evaluation, [(Moon et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b01e6552cc929e1727fc145db82a252b3294c89) ELMo and word2vec coherence classifiers with 93.19% and 96.78% standard/inverse accuracy for ELMo and 90.42% and 95.27% for word2vec, and [(Jwalapuram et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91e265040ba64f765db09a3143ce23ef5b626a53) pairwise WSJ coherence learning with 1240, 138, and 1053 documents and 46,522 training instances all show that document or local coherence can be measured with model-based scores, but none directly validates G-Eval/GPT-4 on edited KB documents.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is direct evidence that G-Eval with GPT-4 is not uniformly the best coherence judge. [(Zhu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ffd59819bfd3bd908056e45c4ce2f932b70ecdf) compared G-Eval with GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-Turbo against COUDA, a 233M ALBERT-xxlarge coherence model trained on 31,778 synthetic coherent and incoherent samples with 30,000 training and 1,178 validation examples, batch size 32, learning rate 1e-5, and convergence within 3,000 steps; on SUMMEVAL, COUDA outperformed G-Eval with GPT-4 by 1.8 points in sample-level Spearman and 0.3 points in Kendall tau, and on INSTED pairwise intruder detection G-Eval with GPT-3.5-Turbo was only slightly above chance on the Wikipedia split while COUDA was best. This challenges MAC-CAFE's reliance on G-Eval/GPT-4 as the sole coherence metric for KB documents. A second contradiction is that GPT-4 judges can overestimate quality and require calibration: [(Peng et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff7457e8e389e7f20448cf33d0bc6205daa47a71) found GPT-4 average overall score 4.34 versus human 4.15, and its human validation used only 120 answers scored by one author, so the 74.4% Spearman and 69.8% Kendall correlations are promising but not sufficient to assume unbiased document-wise coherence scores. A third contradiction is that LLM-based factual or hallucination judging is uneven: [(Li et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0384ba36555232c587d4a80d527895a095a9001) reported hallucination-recognition accuracy ranging from 6.68% for Alpaca on QA to 80.42% for Davinci002 on general responses, with ChatGPT at 62.59% QA and 79.44% general, showing that LLM judges can be weak on some tasks and that GPT-4's coherence scores need task-specific validation. A fourth contradiction is that automatic output metrics can miss factual failure: [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) found RAG-Token fusion models more prone to knowledge hallucination than non-fusion models despite standard automatic metrics, and increasing retrieved documents to 25 lowered Knowledge F1 from 26.0 to 24.7 on valid seen and from 22.7 to 21.1 on valid unseen while raising F1 slightly, so MAC-CAFE's test-set accuracy and G-Eval coherence may not capture hallucination or retrieval-induced inconsistency. A fifth contradiction is that train/test accuracy may overstate generalization if it only measures direct edit recall: [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) showed MEMIT and ROME exceeded 90% single-hop edit-wise success but multi-hop accuracy dropped from 40.5% to 7.0% on GPT-J and from 30.2% to 4.9% on Vicuna-7B on MQUAKE-CF, and [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5c73d9e6641b018b633690102121f5605d34fb0) showed side-effect locality failures such as FT-L scoring 12.88 on other attributions and 9.48 on distract-neighborhood probes on GPT-J. Weaker contradictions include that fact-level false knowledge and general language behavior require additional probes: [(Dong et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7471cb40a33e9d971a922b5dff5ca9b4a73ca609) reduced false rate from 48.10% to 17.09% with CALINET but also showed perplexity increased on adversarial test while general test changed little, indicating that edit success and coherence are not the same as preservation of general knowledge; and [(Huang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e909e2a8cdacdcdff125ebcc566f37cb869a1c8) emphasizes long-tail, challenging factual questions, suggesting that MAC-CAFE's test set must avoid high-frequency memorized facts to be a valid generalization measure.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to validate the G-Eval/GPT-4 coherence metric on MAC-CAFE KB documents before using it as a primary outcome. Following [(Zhu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ffd59819bfd3bd908056e45c4ce2f932b70ecdf), collect human coherence ratings for a stratified sample of edited documents from ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, ARKS-TensorflowM, and CLARKS-news, then report Spearman, Kendall tau, Pearson, and accuracy against G-Eval/GPT-4, G-Eval/GPT-3.5, COUDA, [(Yuan et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6a7724763d8adba466519489b0b9d209e7f2d15) BARTScore, [(Zhong et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/041f5dbfcd07a3369ac44a6b902ee4b145eccf2b) UniEval, and trained pairwise coherence models; include confidence intervals, multiple random seeds, and calibration for overestimation as warned by [(Peng et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff7457e8e389e7f20448cf33d0bc6205daa47a71). Because [(Cui et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c0ec45aab03428f732c2191315a2a3af4ea05b3) found sentence-level divide-conquer evaluation produced more corrections and better precision/F1 than paragraph-level evaluation, MAC-CAFE should also test sentence-level or section-level G-Eval scoring and aggregate to document level, rather than relying only on whole-document scores. This is critical because the plan's coherence metric is currently under-specified and directly contradicted by [(Zhu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ffd59819bfd3bd908056e45c4ce2f932b70ecdf). The next most important action is to expand completeness and generalization beyond raw train/test accuracy. Adopt the reliability/generalization/locality structure from [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5c73d9e6641b018b633690102121f5605d34fb0) and [(Ifergan et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54a2e0dad730c1a955c66f3568a3fa0dca400225): define train-set completeness as exact or execution accuracy on the edited feedback items, generalization as accuracy on paraphrased prompts, related entities, unseen domains, and scaled edits, and locality as accuracy on unrelated facts or code APIs. For code KBs, use compiler/execution accuracy on held-out ARKS test cases and add paraphrase or variant prompts; for CLARKS-news, add temporal and long-tail questions following [(Huang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e909e2a8cdacdcdff125ebcc566f37cb869a1c8). Include multi-hop or downstream RAG probes inspired by [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), because high single-hop recall can mask multi-hop degradation, and include side-effect probes such as other attributions, distract-neighborhood, and other-task checks from [(Yao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5c73d9e6641b018b633690102121f5605d34fb0). Third, add RAG-specific and hallucination-specific evaluation. Following [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa), report retrieval recall, Knowledge F1 or equivalent groundedness, perplexity, BLEU/ROUGE only as secondary metrics, and human-evaluate a sample of generated answers for consistency, knowledgeability, and hallucination. Following [(Li et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0384ba36555232c587d4a80d527895a095a9001) and [(Huang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e909e2a8cdacdcdff125ebcc566f37cb869a1c8), include long-tail, dynamic, and adversarial questions, and use GPT-4 or human labels to detect hallucinated content rather than assuming coherence implies factual correctness. Fourth, add fact-level false-knowledge assessment for incorrect KB settings. Use contrastive knowledge assessment or similar prompt-probability scoring from [(Dong et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7471cb40a33e9d971a922b5dff5ca9b4a73ca609), with paraphrase templates and general or adversarial test perplexity, to ensure edits do not destroy general language behavior; for KB documents, operationalize this as before/after fact probes on edited triples, API names, or temporal facts. Finally, strengthen experimental rigor. Report baseline comparisons against PROMPT AGENT-E and, where feasible, parameter-editing or retrieval baselines; use multiple MCTS seeds and report variance; specify G-Eval prompts, temperature, max tokens, scoring scale, and aggregation; use statistical tests or confidence intervals for train/test accuracy and coherence correlations, as many related works omit significance tests. Also stratify results by domain, dynamism, and question type as in [(Yuan et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/51a12147e6030d1a4932b39a904ccdbc9b1da471), and for code datasets report execution accuracy separately from G-Eval coherence.

## Context: The proposed system must be reproducible and comparable across datasets. Method: We will configure MCTS with UCT algorithm, depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, exploration constant=2.5, and use a RAG system with embedding similarity retrieval, iterative retrieval for coding tasks, GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW as reasoning model, OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE for embeddings, document chunking at 50 lines for unstructured data, and a 18000 token budget for retrievals.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed MCTS/UCT component comes from [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240), which shows UCT can select actions in deterministic P-game trees with branching factor 8 and depth 8, reaching zero failure rate within leaf-node budgets comparable to alpha-beta, and in a stochastic sailing MDP requires fewer simulator samples than ARTDP and PG-ID to reach error below 0.1 on grids from 2x2 to 40x40, with P-game results averaged over 200 trees and 200 runs per tree; this directly supports using UCT as the selection rule for MAC-CAFE KB-edit states, although the paper does not test depth=3, expansion width=3, 5 iterations, c=2.5, RAG, or KB editing. Next, [(Zhou et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/700bd9681f1b9e9e2212e10415d27b11c7e6836b) supports MCTS for LLM agents, with LATS using an LM value function, reflection, and task-specific operations on programming, HotPotQA, WebShop, and Game of 24, achieving 0.44 success on Game of 24 versus RAP 0.40, ToT 0.20, Reflexion 0.12, and CoT 0.08, and its HotPotQA ablation used ReAct with n=5 children and k=50 trajectories, suggesting MCTS can improve LLM reasoning and acting but not validating KB editing. [(Shao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1675f47125aa409525c5f759b5e6bcc1c8831aa) supports iterative retrieval in RAG: ITER-RETGEN with T=2 used 2 LLM API calls and 10 retrieved paragraphs total, i.e. 5 paragraphs per iteration, and achieved significantly higher Acc† than retrieval-augmented baselines on HotPotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Bamboogle, and StrategyQA, while remaining competitive with Self-Ask on MuSiQue and Feverous, which supports the plan's iterative retrieval for coding tasks, though the paper uses text-davinci-003, Contriever-MSMARCO, top-5 paragraphs, 3-shot prompts, and greedy decoding rather than GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW, OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE, 50-line chunks, or a 18000-token budget. [(Xu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958e60eae99cf88a9138749e46881c837ca7f500) supports the premise that KB content materially affects LLM outputs: adding RAG to KOL profiles raised GPT-4o knowledge accuracy from 29.83 to 70.80 and tone accuracy from 60.41 to 88.20, with similar gains for Claude-3.5 Sonnect, Gemini-1.5 Pro, Qwen2-14B-Chat, Qwen2-72B-Chat, and GPT-3.5-Turbo, using 30 KOLs, 300 video transcripts per KOL, 500 multiple-choice questions per KOL, and GPT-4-scored five-round fan dialogues, which motivates MAC-CAFE's goal of editing KBs to improve RAG performance. [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) supports the confidence-bound selection principle underlying UCT, showing UCB1-TUNED substantially outperformed UCB1 on Bernoulli bandits over 100,000 plays averaged over 100 runs, and that UCB2 was relatively insensitive to small alpha, with alpha=0.001 used later. Weaker support comes from [(Sandberg et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/989f49b8855bed8d91f7e099379755db9b67a21e) and [(Hong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7faab5f9d92b565e0f3977485776ab88cda5b6cb), which use MCTS for scheduling and multiobjective optimization respectively but provide no quantitative outcomes in the excerpt and no evidence for the proposed RAG/KB-editing configuration.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the proposed 5-iteration, depth=3, expansion-width=3 UCT budget is far below the search budgets associated with convergence in the closest MCTS evidence. [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240) reports convergence over many leaf nodes on P-game trees with branching factor 8 and depth 8, with iterations needed to reach a fixed failure rate scaling roughly as B^(D/2), and in the sailing MDP compares sample efficiency over many simulator samples, not 5 iterations; it also modifies the UCT bias for deterministic trees, so c=2.5 is not validated. [(Zhou et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/700bd9681f1b9e9e2212e10415d27b11c7e6836b) uses LATS with n=5 children and k=50 trajectories for HotPotQA, indicating that effective MCTS over LLM trajectories may require more than 5 iterations, and its gains on Game of 24 (0.44 versus RAP 0.40, ToT 0.20, Reflexion 0.12, CoT 0.08) do not isolate the proposed depth=3, width=3, c=2.5 setting. Next, [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) contradicts the assumption that a fixed UCB1-style bonus with c=2.5 is optimal: UCB1-TUNED, which uses sample variance, performed substantially better than UCB1 on Bernoulli instances, but no regret bound is provided for the tuned variant, and the paper lacks numerical regret values, confidence intervals, or significance tests. [(Shao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1675f47125aa409525c5f759b5e6bcc1c8831aa) contradicts a naive iterative-retrieval design by showing that 65% of 20 random HotPotQA failure cases for ITER-RETGEN T=2 were retrieval-related, and 76.9% of those were caused by completely wrong first-iteration reasoning misleading retrieval; this warns that MAC-CAFE's iterative retrieval can amplify bad initial KB edits or reasoning, and the paper's retrieval stack (text-davinci-003, Contriever-MSMARCO, top-5 paragraphs, 3-shot greedy decoding) is not the proposed GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW, OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE, 50-line chunking, or 18000-token budget. [(Xu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958e60eae99cf88a9138749e46881c837ca7f500) provides a weaker contradiction that RAG alone is not sufficient: SFT without RAG outperformed profile-only RAG for Qwen2-14B-Chat on knowledge and tone (59.89 versus 55.25 and 78.93 versus 68.41), while the full MINDECHO RAG system still outperformed SFT (73.91 versus 59.89 knowledge and 89.15 versus 78.93 tone), showing that KB content helps but parameter adaptation can also matter; GPT-4 fan-centric scores improved more modestly, e.g. GPT-4o new-fan ALL 5.73 to 5.99 and old-fan ALL 6.50 to 7.14, suggesting style/coherence gains may be smaller than factual accuracy gains, and the paper does not report retrieval index details, embedding model, chunk size, or token budget. Finally, [(Sandberg et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/989f49b8855bed8d91f7e099379755db9b67a21e) and [(Hong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7faab5f9d92b565e0f3977485776ab88cda5b6cb) provide no quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or parameter values in the excerpt, so they cannot support the specific UCT depth, width, iterations, exploration constant, chunking, token budget, or multi-actor KB-editing claims.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace the fixed 5-iteration, depth=3, expansion-width=3, c=2.5 MCTS configuration with an ablation and convergence study: run UCT with increasing iterations (e.g. 5, 20, 50, 100), depths (3, 5, 8), expansion widths (3, 5), and exploration constants (1.0, 2.5, 4.0, variance-tuned UCB1-TUNED-style bonus or UCB2 alpha=0.001), and report selection regret, failure rate, edit acceptance rate, and RAG accuracy on ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, ARKS-TensorflowM, and CLARKS-news, following the convergence evidence in [(Kocsis, and Szepesvari, 2006-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e635d81a617d1239232a9c9a11a196c53dab8240) and the LATS search-budget evidence in [(Zhou et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/700bd9681f1b9e9e2212e10415d27b11c7e6836b). Second, make iterative retrieval robust to first-step reasoning errors by adding retrieval diagnostics and query refinement: log retrieval precision/recall, paragraph relevance, and failure taxonomy inspired by [(Shao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a1675f47125aa409525c5f759b5e6bcc1c8831aa), where 65% of T=2 HotPotQA failures were retrieval-related and 76.9% of those came from wrong first-iteration reasoning; compare single retrieval, T=2, and T=3 with the proposed 18000-token budget, 50-line chunks, and OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE, and consider top-k paragraph budgets analogous to the paper's 5 paragraphs per iteration. Third, add baselines and ablations that isolate MAC-CAFE's multi-actor centralized critic: compare against PROMPT AGENT-E, a single ReACT editor, no MCTS, no critic, no iterative retrieval, and a document-addition-only baseline, and report completeness (train accuracy), generalization (test accuracy), and coherence (G-Eval with GPT-4) with confidence intervals or significance tests. Fourth, because the plan optimizes multiple KB qualities, adopt a multiobjective or Pareto evaluation inspired by [(Hong et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7faab5f9d92b565e0f3977485776ab88cda5b6cb), reporting trade-offs among execution accuracy, factual accuracy, and coherence rather than a single aggregate score. Fifth, use the RAG evidence from [(Xu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958e60eae99cf88a9138749e46881c837ca7f500) to justify and validate KB edits with both task accuracy and style/coherence metrics: include GPT-4-scored dialogues or G-Eval coherence checks, human spot checks, and comparisons to profile-only or SFT baselines where appropriate, since that paper showed large knowledge/tone gains from RAG (e.g. GPT-4o knowledge 29.83 to 70.80, tone 60.41 to 88.20) but only modest fan-centric score gains. Finally, document reproducibility details absent from the related work: hardware/API costs, random seeds, token counts, retrieval index size, chunking behavior, embedding model version, number of documents, and statistical tests, so that the proposed 18000-token budget and 50-line chunking can be compared fairly.

## Context: The overall research goal is to show that feedback-driven KB editing improves RAG outcomes. Method: We will measure improvements in KB quality across the three defined characteristics and demonstrate enhanced RAG system performance through systematic comparison with our baseline approach.

### Support

The strongest support for MAC-CAFE comes from document-level RAG editing evidence that external KB content directly determines RAG outcomes and that improving document relevance can improve edit success. [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a) shows Standard RAG and CRAG on COUNTERFACT-derived instances with LLaMA3.1-8B achieve PS 54.00 and 56.05, comparable to document-based IKE(D) 55.60, while CRAG uses a T5 evaluator, 50-token chunks, and top-10 chunks, closely resembling MAC-CAFE's document chunking and retrieval-side optimization. This directly supports the plan's focus on KB documents rather than white-box parameters. A second strong line of support is that retrieval-augmented, non-parameter editing is more robust to unstructured real-world updates than parameter editing. [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7) reports IKE and MeLLo retain much more accuracy on unstructured facts than ROME or MEMIT, with Mistral IKE dropping 21 percent on COUNTERFACT while parameter methods drop 94 percent, and ROME/MEMIT falling from about 16 percent on COUNTERFACT/MQUAKE-CF to about 2.3 percent on WIKIUPDATE. This supports MAC-CAFE's hypothesis that KB editing is preferable for private, low-resource, or unstructured documentation such as ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, and ARKS-ScipyM. External editable memory also supports multi-hop and chained reasoning: [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) MeLLo with GPT-J outperforms MEMIT and MEND on MQUAKE-CF, reaching 38.9 versus 11.8 and 11.7 at one edit and 14.2 versus 5.4 and 6.1 at 3000 edits, which supports using an editable KB for code or factual chains where retrieval must supply multiple facts. The RAG literature also supports the need for systematic retrieval baselines: [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) shows retrieval augmentation outperforms non-retrieval BART-Large, DPR plus Poly-encoder re-ranking is best on automatic metrics, and document count changes alter F1, perplexity, Knowledge F1, and hallucination, which motivates MAC-CAFE's retrieval budget and baseline comparison. Retrieval-augmented sequential editing further supports the evaluation design: [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) uses contrastive fact and sentence encoders, shows RoBERTa with sentence-to-fact margin loss improves retrieval HIT for similar-but-different samples, and reports success, edit retain, generalization, training retain, and test retain metrics, aligning with MAC-CAFE's completeness, generalization, and repeated-edit concerns. Its cost comparison, about 50 seconds per RASE edit versus 2 to 3 days for 1000 T-Patcher edits on an A100, supports the plan's preference for document-level edits over expensive parameter editing. Newer black-box memory editing also supports targeted, span-preserving edits and a PROMPT-like baseline: [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3) postEdit with SBERT retrieval and a generative post-editor outperforms PROMPT, IKE, and SERAC on CounterFact and zsRE, with editing scores improving as the post-editor grows from 460M to 1.8B parameters and retention stabilizing after 1.3B. Older parameter-editing and retrieval-QA work provides useful evaluation scaffolding: [(Cao et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/240b0caabb415578bdea4da7d0a32bdff2e8163f) MEND uses KL constraints and reports success, retain, equivalence, and deterioration; [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/996445d847f06e99b0bd259345408a0cf1bce87e) ROME defines efficacy, paraphrase, neighborhood specificity, semantic consistency, and fluency on COUNTERFACT with GPT-2 XL 1.5B and GPT-J 6B; [(Meng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe1ac0b09cc0f50eb83eef6c7c6b45ac8b12413) MEMIT shows batch editing at 10,000 facts on 6B and 20B models; [(Press et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e070ff286709db28312e08b52b05539debe88146) Self-ask shows decomposition and search improve multi-hop QA with GPT-3 Davinci and InstructGPT; and [(Huang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9be51698e7c2247853b7b6f1f70fc4d6d7ef605) formalizes sequential editing with success, generalization, edit retain, training retain, and test retain. The iterative progression from white-box parameter editing in 2021-2022 to retrieval-memory and document-level RAG editing in 2023-2024 supports MAC-CAFE's core claim that KB-side editing and retrieval quality should be measured as a system, but the related work does not validate the specific multi-actor centralized critic RL or MCTS mechanism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that document-level RAG editing improves edit success but does not automatically preserve unrelated knowledge. [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a) reports that on LLaMA3.1-8B Standard RAG has PS 54.00 but NS 83.27, while IKE(D) has NS 85.29, and CRAG improves PS to 56.05 without a corresponding locality advantage. This contradicts the assumption that MAC-CAFE's document edits and G-Eval coherence will fully capture side effects; the plan needs explicit locality, out-of-scope, and retention metrics. A second major contradiction is scaling: external memory editing degrades as edits accumulate. [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) MeLLo with GPT-J drops from 38.9 to 14.2 on MQUAKE-CF as edits grow from 1 to 3000, and from 85.9 to 30.7 on MQUAKE-T from 1 to 1868, while MEMIT drops from 40.5 to 7.0 on GPT-J MQUAKE-CF; Vicuna-7B and GPT-3.5 show similar degradation. This challenges MAC-CAFE's MCTS setting of depth 3, expansion width 3, and only 5 iterations, because the search may be too shallow to coordinate many document edits or repair retrieval failures. Retrieval volume is another contradiction: [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) finds that increasing RAG-Token documents to 25 improves valid-seen F1 from 22.5 to 22.6 and lowers valid-unseen perplexity from 13.4 to 13.0, but lowers Knowledge F1 from 26.0 to 24.7 on valid seen and from 22.7 to 21.1 on valid unseen while increasing hallucination. This contradicts an uncritical 18000-token retrieval budget and iterative retrieval; more retrieved text can reduce knowledge precision. Unstructured KB content can also harm retrieval: [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7) shows extracted triplets can retrieve related but incorrect triplets, and IKE's unstructured decline grows from 9 percent on COUNTERFACT to 52 percent on WIKIUPDATE with GPT2-XL. This contradicts the plan's simple 50-line chunking and GPT-4 document edits unless the edits are validated against retrieval precision. Parameter editing also contradicts the blanket claim that KB editing is superior: [(Cao et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/240b0caabb415578bdea4da7d0a32bdff2e8163f) MEND estimates a 97 percent probability of outperforming baselines on FEVER and 88 percent on zsRE; [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/996445d847f06e99b0bd259345408a0cf1bce87e) ROME is rated 1.8 times more consistent than constrained fine-tuning by 15 human evaluators on 50 facts; and [(Meng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe1ac0b09cc0f50eb83eef6c7c6b45ac8b12413) MEMIT is best among tested editors at 10,000 edits. These results suggest MAC-CAFE must compare against strong parameter editors on the same RAG tasks, not assume superiority. The specific MAC-CAFE mechanism is also under-evidenced: none of the related works uses a centralized critic, multi-actor ReACT agents, textual gradients, expert-feedback rewards, or MCTS over KB states; [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) uses contrastive retrieval and stored edit operations, [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3) uses a trained post-editor, and [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) uses retrieval and self-check, but none validates the proposed RL coordination. Finally, the proposed coherence metric is weakly grounded: G-Eval with GPT-4 is not validated in the related work, whereas [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) uses human evaluation for consistency, engagingness, knowledgeability, and hallucination, and [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3) uses ROUGE and SBERT span retention, indicating that G-Eval alone may miss hallucination, span preservation, and user-facing quality.

### Suggested Action

Most important, expand the evaluation beyond completeness, generalization, and G-Eval coherence to include locality, retention, paraphrase, out-of-scope, multi-hop, hallucination, and execution metrics. Use neighborhood specificity and out-of-scope probes inspired by [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/996445d847f06e99b0bd259345408a0cf1bce87e) COUNTERFACT, edit retain and test retain from [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) and [(Huang et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9be51698e7c2247853b7b6f1f70fc4d6d7ef605), span retention with ROUGE/SBERT from [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3), and human or execution-based checks for ARKS-Pony, ARKS-Ring, ARKS-ScipyM, ARKS-TensorflowM, and CLARKS-news. Second, add strong baselines that isolate the KB-editing claim: Standard RAG, CRAG, IKE, MeLLo, postEdit/SERAC, PROMPT AGENT-E, and where feasible ROME, MEMIT, MEND, or LoRA on open-weight models, following [(Wu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/440ca16479ece9ed61461032b9b2fcde36c8ae7a), [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7), [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3), [(Cao et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/240b0caabb415578bdea4da7d0a32bdff2e8163f), and [(Meng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe1ac0b09cc0f50eb83eef6c7c6b45ac8b12413). Third, improve retrieval and KB structure before relying on MCTS: train or calibrate a contrastive retriever or re-ranker as in [(Han et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/646402e15a355a1e03e7f19ea70fdb31456f19f3) and [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa), ablate chunk sizes around 50 tokens or 50 lines, top-k, and the 18000-token budget, and measure retrieval HIT, similar-but-different discrimination, and Knowledge F1 to avoid the noise effects reported by [(Shuster et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2a7033a5a859e3a6e6f0a83018326400b4c5faa) and [(Wu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5040acef429108015991dc5480f1cedf6851f0b7). Fourth, validate the MAC-CAFE-specific architecture with ablations: centralized critic on/off, multi-actor versus single-agent, MCTS versus greedy, random, or PROMPT AGENT-E, and larger search budgets than depth 3, width 3, and 5 iterations, because no related work supports the current MCTS configuration and [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) shows edit accumulation can degrade performance; for ARKS, use execution success and compiler messages as reward signals and ablate whether the critic's textual gradients improve over direct execution-feedback prompts. Fifth, make coherence measurement more robust by combining G-Eval with human evaluation, contradiction self-checks as in [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3), span preservation metrics from [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3), and compiler or execution feedback for code datasets. Finally, report reproducibility details and statistical evidence: random seeds, confidence intervals, significance tests, hardware, GPT-4-1106-PREVIEW and OpenAI-TEXT-EMBEDDING-3-LARGE versions, prompt templates, retrieval logs, edit counts, and ablations over 1, 100, 1000, and 3000 edits as in [(Zhong et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56e952fd463accff09cf2e35432aaabd7c7c57f3) and [(Song et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6acd1576ed0abfb034035cb52bd34bf43abcfea3).
