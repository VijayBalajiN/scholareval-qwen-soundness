## Context: The central proposed solution for reducing privacy leakage in robust models. Method: We propose a novel approach that combines counterfactual explanations with adversarial example generation to create "counterfactual adversarial examples."

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82), which uses a trained C-CHVAE/CHVAE encoder-decoder, encodes a factual input, samples candidate latent points uniformly over lp-spheres around the encoded point, decodes them, and returns the closest decoded candidate that flips a pretrained classifier to the desired label. This is methodologically close to the plan's autoencoder latent-space generation of counterfactual-like perturbations, and its synthetic 10,000-observation experiment with the threshold rule x2 > 6 plus real Give Me Some Credit and HELOC credit evaluations show that latent counterfactuals can remain in high-density regions and score better on attainability than gradient or random baselines using local outlier factor and connectedness metrics. This supports the plan's claim that autoencoder latent search can produce more imperceptible and distributionally consistent perturbations for MNIST, although it does not test adversarial robustness or membership inference. A second strong support is [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6), which directly compares C-CHVAE counterfactual generation with NAE adversarial generation and SCFE with C-W/DeepFool on UCI Adult, COMPAS, and German credit, showing that both counterfactual and adversarial examples can be produced by constrained latent-space or gradient optimization and that empirical l2 distances remain within derived upper bounds, with tight bounds for the encoder-decoder comparison. This supports the plan's conceptual unification of counterfactual and adversarial generation and suggests that l2 distance, radius, and Lipschitz-based bounds should be reported for MNIST. A third line of support is [(Yang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6771af8f90c0e7b2ff5a7a2b0d40734a845f3e85), whose AIP method optimizes latent and attribute vectors with a weighted loss combining cross-entropy prediction loss and L2 perturbation losses, and reports lower LPR values than TextBugger and DeepWordBug on Yelp and Amazon, competitive flipping ratios against adversarial baselines, and improved CelebA accuracy when 2,000 generated counterfactuals are added to 20,000 samples, increasing accuracy from 87.32% to 88.85% and from 90.96% to 91.35%. This supports the plan's use of latent constraints, sparsity-like regularization, and data augmentation, but it uses VAE/AttGAN rather than a plain autoencoder and does not evaluate privacy. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae), which trains a WGAN on an unlabeled corpus and searches latent space for nearby decoded inputs that change a black-box classifier, validating latent adversarial generation and providing a conceptual analogue to the plan's AdvGAN baseline, though it lacks counterfactual constraints, autoencoders, sparsity, and MIA. The weakest support is conceptual: [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5) formalizes nearest counterfactual search with SMT, binary search over delta in [0,1], O(log(1/epsilon)) queries, and a lower-bound certificate, supporting the plan's nearest-neighbor cross-class idea but not its autoencoder or privacy objective; [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1) provides a causal recourse formulation showing that feasible counterfactual actions can have lower cost than naive feature differences, supporting the need for plausibility constraints but offering no empirical evidence for MNIST, CNNs, or MIA.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct negative privacy result but a quantitative tradeoff reported by [(Yang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6771af8f90c0e7b2ff5a7a2b0d40734a845f3e85): on CelebA, FGSM has the highest flipping ratio, while AIP ranks second and outperforms other counterfactual methods, and on text AIP achieves lower LPR than TextBugger and DeepWordBug but is not necessarily the strongest flipper. This contradicts the plan's assumption that counterfactual constraints can simultaneously preserve competitive adversarial effectiveness and improve privacy; if counterfactual constraints reduce flipping ratio, robust training may suffer unless the attack success is explicitly measured. A second major contradiction to the plan's autoencoder claim is [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6), which compares C-CHVAE with NAE, a Wasserstein-GAN-based adversarial method, and finds that empirical l2 distances remain within derived upper bounds and are tight across Adult, COMPAS, and German credit. This suggests GAN-based latent adversarial generation is a viable and comparable alternative to autoencoder-based counterfactual generation, and because the paper does not report MIA or privacy leakage, it provides no evidence that autoencoders are superior in privacy. A related contradiction is [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae), which shows that WGAN latent-space search can produce natural, valid adversarial examples for black-box classifiers on image and text data, further weakening the claim that autoencoders are inherently better for producing imperceptible adversaries; it also does not evaluate privacy, so the plan's privacy advantage remains unsupported. A methodological contradiction is in [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5), where nearest counterfactuals are defined relative to the factual input and certified by an unsatisfiable distance threshold, whereas the plan's reversed procedure starts from a nearest neighbor of a different class and generates a counterfactual of that neighbor. This may not guarantee that the final example is the nearest adversarial example to the original input, and the plan lacks a certificate or binary-search guarantee over the original-to-adversary distance. A weaker contradiction is [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1), which warns that treating raw feature differences as actions can be invalid under causal dependence and proposes minimal-intervention recourse in a structural causal model; although MNIST pixels are less obviously causally structured than tabular credit features, this still challenges the plan's reliance on pixel-level sparsity and nearest-neighbor features without feasibility or causal constraints. Finally, the weakest but most consequential gap is that none of the cited works evaluate black-box membership inference, robust-model MIA, decoupled generation and training, or sparsity constraints as a privacy mechanism, so the central hypothesis that counterfactual adversarial examples reduce MIA accuracy toward 50% remains unvalidated.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a direct black-box MIA evaluation that tests the plan's central privacy claim: train identical CNNs with three dropout layers on MNIST using original, PGD, AdvGAN, and proposed counterfactual adversarial examples, then evaluate state-of-the-art robust-model MIA attacks using only predictions, reporting MIA accuracy, confidence intervals, statistical significance, and whether accuracy approaches 50% while maintaining training and test accuracy. Second, formalize the reversed nearest-neighbor construction: define the distance metric, nearest-neighbor cross-class selection, and the condition that the counterfactual of the neighbor is adversarial to the original; add a binary search or SMT-style certificate over the original-to-adversary distance inspired by [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5), and compare against direct counterfactuals of the original and against PGD/AdvGAN. Third, do not assume autoencoder privacy superiority; run a controlled comparison of plain autoencoders, C-CHVAE/VAE, WGAN/AdvGAN/NAE on MNIST, reporting MIA, l1/l2 distance, flipping ratio, generation time, and distributional metrics such as local outlier factor and connectedness as in [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82) and [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6). Fourth, incorporate sparsity and plausibility constraints with ablations: use l0/l1 or latent L2 penalties, attribute or feature-group constraints, and tune the alpha tradeoff between prediction loss and perturbation loss as in [(Yang et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6771af8f90c0e7b2ff5a7a2b0d40734a845f3e85), while measuring whether sparsity reduces the generalization gap and MIA accuracy. Fifth, implement the decoupled generation/training protocol and vary the proportion of adversarial examples while controlling total data quantity, reporting robust accuracy, test accuracy, MIA accuracy, and t-SNE or quantitative distributional similarity between original and adversarial sets. Sixth, add feasibility or causal plausibility constraints where appropriate, or explicitly justify why pixel-level MNIST perturbations do not require the structural causal model proposed by [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1). Finally, report reproducibility details missing from the related work, including dataset splits, latent dimensions, search radius, number of latent samples, hyperparameters, hardware, and statistical tests.

## Context: The key algorithmic mechanism that makes the proposed adversarial examples class-informed and less distributionally distinct. Method: The core innovation lies in reversing the traditional generation process: instead of directly perturbing an original sample, we identify the nearest neighbor from a different class and generate counterfactual explanations of this neighbor sample to produce adversarial examples for the original.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed reversal is that latent-space counterfactual and adversarial perturbations can be made close and functionally related. [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6) compares C-CHVAE counterfactual search and NAE adversarial search, both encode inputs, search lp-norm latent balls, decode candidates, and select label-changing outputs; it derives l2 input-space bounds using L-Lipschitz generators and locally linear classifier approximations, and reports that empirical distances on UCI Adult, COMPAS, and German credit did not exceed the bounds, with tight C-CHVAE versus NAE bounds. This directly supports the plan's premise that counterfactual explanations can be converted into adversarial-like perturbations, although it does not test MNIST, CNNs, nearest-neighbor reversal, or MIA. 
A second strong support is class-informed counterfactual editing using a different-class sample. [(Goyal et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70dbe3e740a5e7927ccce00fd615365b08a6eaae) formalizes minimum-edit counterfactuals for query-distractor image pairs, replacing discriminative regions in the query with regions from a distractor to make the classifier match the distractor, and evaluates on SHAPES, MNIST, Omniglot, and CUB using greedy sequential exhaustive search and a continuous relaxation. This supports the proposed use of a different-class neighbor as a semantic anchor, but it is an interpretability method, not an adversarial or privacy method. 
Third, autoencoder latent-space counterfactual generation is well supported for plausibility. [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82) uses C-CHVAE, encodes observations, samples latent candidates on lp-spheres, decodes them, and returns the closest decoded point that flips the classifier; on 10,000 synthetic observations with the rule I(x2 > 6) and on Give Me Some Credit and HELOC credit datasets it reports higher attainability and connectedness than sparse baselines using local outlier factor and connectedness scores. This supports the plan's autoencoder latent-space step, but not the cross-class nearest-neighbor reversal or MIA. 
Fourth, latent adversarial generation is feasible. [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae) trains a Wasserstein-GAN-style generator and critic with a Wasserstein-1 objective on an unlabeled corpus and searches latent z for G(z*) that changes a black-box classifier prediction, supporting latent-space adversarial examples that remain near the data distribution, but it uses a GAN rather than an autoencoder and does not evaluate membership inference. 
Fifth, VAE counterfactual generation can preserve distributional structure. [(Xiang, and Lenskiy, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b407b8447f46da2023aa3769845813d2ee096706) pretrains a VAE, applies latent disturbance, and evaluates validity, constraint feasibility, Euclidean and Mahalanobis distance, and t-SNE on synthetic 20,000-sample linear and nonlinear datasets, a 10,000-sample 14-feature Bayesian network, and a diabetes subset reduced from 768 to 336 records with 7 attributes; this supports the plan's t-SNE and distribution-similarity analysis, but not adversarial robustness or privacy. 
Sixth, nearest-neighbor and density-aware counterfactual paths are supported. [(Poyiadzi et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f187ce8f89c243724aabd4bd29591c8c580ef851) builds KDE, k-NN, or epsilon graphs with density-weighted edges and uses Dijkstra with O(|E| + |V| log |V|) shortest-path complexity to find counterfactual paths on synthetic data and MNIST, supporting the use of nearest-neighbor structure and local density, but it operates in input space and does not address adversarial training or MIA. 
Seventh, distance-constrained nearest counterfactual search is supported. [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5) uses SMT formulas and binary search over normalized distance in [0, 1] with O(log(1/epsilon)) satisfiability calls to return a nearest counterfactual witness or certificate, which could be used to validate that the proposed neighbor-based counterfactual is close, but it searches from the original input rather than from a cross-class neighbor. 
Eighth, nearest-neighbor counterfactual baselines and generative counterfactual states are supported in a different domain. [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8) uses a Wasserstein autoencoder to generate counterfactual Atari states from an A3C encoder mapping four 80x80 frames to a 256-dimensional latent vector, compares against an NNCE baseline, and reports a 60-participant user study with a normal agent trained for 50 million frames and a flawed agent trained for 160 million steps; this supports NNCE-style explanations, but it does not use class labels, adversarial examples, or privacy metrics. 
Ninth, class-informed counterfactual generation on MNIST is supported. [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) uses RAT-SPN class-conditional models to flip predictions on MNIST contrastive pairs, German credit, Adult-Income, and CUB VGG-16 features, reporting high likelihood and visual appeal, with experiments on two Intel Xeon processors with 56 hyper-threaded cores and four GTX 1080 GPUs, RAT-SPN depth D=1, and 70/30 train-test splits; it does not use autoencoders, adversarial training, or MIA. 
Finally, constrained and causal counterfactual formulations provide weaker support for adding feasibility constraints. [(Ustun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86841a74f0fd99ba369f635715ecae3007f22611) and [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1) show that counterfactuals should respect action costs, immutable features, dataset shift, and causal interventions in additive-noise models, which is relevant to the plan's semantic meaningfulness goal but not to its core privacy-robustness mechanism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest evidence linking counterfactuals and adversarial examples validates distance closeness, not privacy reduction. [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6) shows C-CHVAE and NAE outputs can be close under L-Lipschitz and locally linear assumptions, but the evaluation is l2 distance on tabular Adult, COMPAS, and German credit, not MIA accuracy, and NAE is a Wasserstein GAN adversarial method. This undermines the plan's inference that autoencoder-based counterfactual adversarial examples will automatically reduce distributional distinctness or MIA risk, especially on MNIST with CNNs and dropout. 
A second contradiction is that counterfactual explanations are not necessarily imperceptible. [(Goyal et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70dbe3e740a5e7927ccce00fd615365b08a6eaae) creates minimum-edit counterfactuals by replacing regions from a distractor image, which can be visually salient and is evaluated for interpretability rather than adversarial imperceptibility; the plan requires imperceptible modifications, so CF plausibility alone does not guarantee adversarial validity. 
Third, dataset shift and action feasibility can invalidate nearest-neighbor counterfactuals. [(Ustun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86841a74f0fd99ba369f635715ecae3007f22611) studies recourse under dataset shift in credit scoring, where undersampling of young adults can change cost and feasibility, suggesting that a nearest neighbor from another class may not produce a valid or transferable counterfactual for the original sample. [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1) further argues that feature-wise differences do not directly correspond to actions and proposes causal minimal interventions, which contradicts the assumption that a nearest cross-class neighbor's counterfactual is automatically meaningful or actionable. 
Fourth, the proposed reversal may not minimize perturbation from the original sample. [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5) directly searches for the nearest counterfactual of the factual input with distance certificates, whereas the plan first selects a different-class neighbor and generates a counterfactual of that neighbor; this indirect route may increase input-space distance, reduce success rate, or produce examples that are not adversarial for the original without additional constraints. 
Fifth, plausibility-focused counterfactual methods can trade off success. [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) reports that a gradient-based baseline achieved success rate 1.0 while the RAT-SPN method had a slightly lower success rate, indicating that distribution-aware counterfactual generation may be less reliable than direct optimization; the plan must show that counterfactual adversarial examples maintain PGD- or AdvGAN-level robustness. 
Sixth, autoencoder counterfactual attainability does not imply privacy. [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82) shows C-CHVAE counterfactuals are more attainable and connected than sparse baselines, but it evaluates local outlier factor and connectedness, not membership inference, and it searches around the original observation rather than a cross-class neighbor. 
Seventh, GAN-based latent adversarial generation is already feasible, which weakens the claim that autoencoders are uniquely necessary. [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae) uses a Wasserstein-GAN latent search to generate adversarial examples for black-box image and text classifiers, but it does not evaluate MIA, so it neither supports nor refutes the autoencoder privacy advantage. 
Eighth, generative counterfactual states may be visually distinct rather than imperceptible. [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8) uses an adversarial loss hyperparameter lambda=100 to make generated counterfactual states visually distinct for human understanding, and its NNCE baseline is not explicitly class-informed, contradicting the plan's requirement for subtle, class-informed adversarial perturbations. 
Ninth, VAE counterfactuals can violate domain constraints. [(Xiang, and Lenskiy, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b407b8447f46da2023aa3769845813d2ee096706) reports that the VAE method sometimes decreased age and pregnancies despite a penalty on the diabetes dataset, showing that latent-space counterfactual generation may not preserve all unidirectional or semantic constraints; the plan's sparsity constraint alone may not prevent invalid or privacy-leaking perturbations.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a direct black-box MIA evaluation with statistical significance, because no related work validates the plan's central privacy claim. The experiment should report MIA accuracy and AUC for PGD, AdvGAN, and the proposed counterfactual adversarial method, with confidence intervals or paired tests, while varying the proportion of adversarial examples and controlling total data quantity. It should also report training accuracy, test accuracy, robust accuracy, and whether MIA approaches 50 percent, and it should ablate the decoupled generation-training design to show that separation, not just counterfactual generation, reduces leakage. 
Second, add quantitative distributional and imperceptibility metrics beyond t-SNE. Use L2 and Linf perturbation norms, sparsity, SSIM or pixel-wise difference, class-conditional likelihood, local outlier factor, connectedness, Euclidean and Mahalanobis distance, and MMD or KL estimates between original and adversarial sets, following [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82), [(Xiang, and Lenskiy, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b407b8447f46da2023aa3769845813d2ee096706), and [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6). This will test whether the proposed examples are both imperceptible and less distributionally distinct. 
Third, refine nearest-neighbor selection with density, likelihood, and distance constraints. Use cross-class kNN or epsilon-graph candidates, filter by local density and class-conditional likelihood as in [(Poyiadzi et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f187ce8f89c243724aabd4bd29591c8c580ef851), and optionally use SMT or binary-search distance certificates as in [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5) to ensure the generated counterfactual is close to the neighbor and the final adversarial example is close to the original. Evaluate success rate, distance, and MIA across neighbor radii. 
Fourth, implement the autoencoder latent-space search explicitly and compare it to GAN-based baselines. Use C-CHVAE-style lp-sphere sampling around the encoded neighbor, VAE latent disturbance with constraint penalties as in [(Xiang, and Lenskiy, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b407b8447f46da2023aa3769845813d2ee096706), and compare against NAE-style GAN adversarial search as in [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae) and [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6). This will test whether autoencoders truly provide better privacy than GANs. 
Fifth, derive or empirically check distance bounds between counterfactual and adversarial outputs on MNIST with CNNs. The plan should extend the L-Lipschitz and locally linear analysis from [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6) to the proposed CNN/dropout setting, reporting whether the proposed counterfactual adversarial examples remain within expected perturbation budgets and whether bound looseness correlates with MIA accuracy. 
Sixth, incorporate semantic feasibility constraints only where they do not harm adversarial success. Use action-cost or immutable-feature constraints from [(Ustun et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86841a74f0fd99ba369f635715ecae3007f22611) and causal minimal-intervention ideas from [(Karimi et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0367827a9162f981ee02c4b3130f58085fba93f1) to evaluate meaningfulness, but keep them optional and ablated because the primary goal is privacy-robustness on MNIST. 
Seventh, report full reproducibility details. Specify MNIST split sizes, CNN architecture, three dropout layers, optimizer, learning rate, epochs, latent dimension, sparsity penalty, PGD epsilon and steps, AdvGAN hyperparameters, MIA attack architecture, and hardware, following the level of detail in [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) and [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8). This will allow fair comparison and prevent overfitting or memorization confounds.

## Context: The planned model architecture and training constraints for generating private, imperceptible perturbations. Method: We will employ autoencoders for counterfactual explanation generation, as they provide superior privacy performance compared to generative models like GANs. The generation process operates in latent space to enhance privacy protection and produce more imperceptible perturbations. We will incorporate sparsity constraints to reduce the generalization gap between adversarial and original datasets, and decouple the adversarial generation process from model training to prevent the model from memorizing individual samples.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed autoencoder latent-space counterfactual adversarial plan comes from [(Samoilescu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c09fa0da9608f6e0c0112200bbda50892f00605), which is the closest methodological match: it trains a pretrained autoencoder, freezes the encoder, and uses a DDPG actor in latent space to output counterfactual embeddings that are decoded back to input space with clipping and categorical post-processing. On MNIST and CelebA with a CNN classifier at 99% and 81% test accuracy, it optimizes a sparse black-box prediction reward, a sparsity penalty over decoded features, and a consistency loss, using 300K iterations with sparsity coefficient 7.5 and consistency 0 on MNIST, and 100K iterations with sparsity 20.0 on CelebA; it reports valid, in-distribution counterfactuals generated within seconds, directly supporting the plan's autoencoder latent search, sparsity, and prediction-flip objective, although it does not evaluate MIA, adversarial robustness, or decoupled training. The next strongest support is [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82), whose C-CHVAE uses a Gaussian encoder/decoder with an isotropic prior, conditions on part of the input, and searches lp spheres around encoded latents to find decoded points that flip a pretrained classifier; on a 10,000-observation synthetic process and Give Me Some Credit/HELOC credit data, it reports counterfactuals in high-density regions and connected to original data, supporting latent autoencoder counterfactual generation and attainability, but without privacy, adversarial training, or reported hardware/hyperparameter details. Third, [(Mothilal et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2413fa296543159b32d16350d9e29f7db528790) provides quantitative support for sparsity and counterfactual validity: DiverseCF-Sparse on 500 test instances with lambda1=0.5, lambda2=1, ADAM lr 0.05, and k up to 10 achieves F1=0.44 versus LIME 0.19 for 1-NN boundary approximation on Adult-Income at k=4 and 0.5 MAD, and precision 0.61 versus 0.19 on LendingClub, showing sparse counterfactuals can improve local decision-boundary approximation, which is relevant to reducing distributional shift, though the setting is tabular and not MNIST/CNN/MIA and no statistical significance tests are reported. Fourth, [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6) supports the autoencoder-versus-GAN latent comparison by showing C-CHVAE counterfactuals and NAE Wasserstein-GAN adversarial examples searched in lp balls on Adult, COMPAS, and German credit remain within a tight theoretical L-Lipschitz distance bound, validating that autoencoder and GAN latent perturbations are comparable in distance geometry, but it does not establish privacy superiority and does not provide numeric bound values or privacy metrics. Fifth, [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae) supports latent-space adversarial generation and black-box robustness evaluation by training a Wasserstein GAN on unlabeled data and searching z-space for natural adversaries, with human evaluation and statistical analysis mentioned but no numerical privacy outcomes; this supports latent adversarial generation and black-box evaluation, but uses a GAN rather than an autoencoder. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Goyal et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70dbe3e740a5e7927ccce00fd615365b08a6eaae) for minimum-edit decision-changing counterfactuals on SHAPES, MNIST, Omniglot, and Birds; [(Poyiadzi et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f187ce8f89c243724aabd4bd29591c8c580ef851) for density-aware shortest-path counterfactuals on MNIST; [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5) for nearest-counterfactual search via SMT and binary search; [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8) for autoencoder-style counterfactual Atari states with an adversarial loss lambda=100 and a 60-participant user study; [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) for MNIST counterfactual success-rate evaluation against gradient, CEM, and FACE baselines on two Intel Xeon processors with 56 hyper-threaded cores and four GTX 1080 GPUs; and [(Farid et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb6db8c71ccdafff4546cb3ef9dfa9ea6089cf1) for latent generative counterfactual quality metrics on 256x256 ImageNet, CelebA-HQ, Flowers, and Pets, with qualitative and metric-based evaluation but no numeric table values in the excerpt. Read iteratively, the 2017 latent GAN adversarial work motivated the 2019 counterfactual latent/sparsity methods, which were then extended by 2021 autoencoder RL and autoencoder-GAN comparisons, and later by 2022/2023 generative counterfactual methods; the sequence supports latent counterfactual generation and sparsity, but not the privacy claim.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that none of the cited works directly validates the central claim that autoencoders provide superior privacy performance compared with GANs under black-box membership inference. The closest evidence, [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6), compares C-CHVAE autoencoder counterfactuals with NAE Wasserstein-GAN adversarial examples on Adult, COMPAS, and German credit and finds empirical l2 distances within a tight theoretical upper bound for L-Lipschitz generative models; this suggests GAN-based adversarial latent perturbations can be geometrically comparable to autoencoder counterfactuals, not that autoencoders are inherently more private. A second strong contradiction is [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae), which shows a GAN latent-space method can generate natural, semantically close black-box adversarial examples for images and text, challenging the assumption that GANs are necessarily less suitable for imperceptible adversarial generation; however, it does not evaluate MIA. Third, [(Farid et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb6db8c71ccdafff4546cb3ef9dfa9ea6089cf1) shows latent diffusion models can produce high-quality counterfactual images on 256x256 ImageNet, CelebA-HQ, Oxford Flowers 102, and Oxford Pets using ResNet-50, DenseNet-121, OpenCLIP-ViT-B/32, and DINO-ViT-S/8, with metrics such as FID, sFID, FVA, FS, MNAC, CD, and COUT, indicating that latent generative quality is not exclusive to autoencoders, although it does not test privacy. Fourth, the sparsity motivation is only indirectly supported: [(Mothilal et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2413fa296543159b32d16350d9e29f7db528790) and [(Samoilescu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c09fa0da9608f6e0c0112200bbda50892f00605) show sparsity improves counterfactual validity, proximity, or in-distributionness, but neither reports that sparsity reduces the adversarial-original generalization gap or lowers MIA accuracy. Fifth, the decoupling claim is unsupported: [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8) uses an adversarial loss term in the counterfactual model, and [(Samoilescu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c09fa0da9608f6e0c0112200bbda50892f00605) trains a generator with a black-box prediction reward, but neither evaluates whether separating generation from target-model training prevents memorization or membership leakage. Sixth, the plan's reliance on three dropout layers and t-SNE visualization is not evidenced by the related work; no cited study reports dropout as a privacy mechanism for robust CNNs or uses t-SNE as a statistically sufficient test of distributional similarity. Finally, several works lack quantitative outcomes or privacy metrics, such as [(Goyal et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70dbe3e740a5e7927ccce00fd615365b08a6eaae), [(Karimi et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/324d098c5294c69735ae1670707b2f21b605b8e5), and [(Poyiadzi et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f187ce8f89c243724aabd4bd29591c8c580ef851), so they cannot substantiate the proposed privacy-risk reduction. As an iterative sequence, the 2021 comparison partially undercuts a simple 2017-era GAN-versus-autoencoder privacy narrative by showing distance comparability, while the 2023 diffusion work further broadens the generative-model landscape without privacy evidence.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should add a direct black-box MIA comparison that tests the autoencoder counterfactual adversarial method against PGD, AdvGAN, and a GAN/diffusion latent baseline on the same MNIST CNN with three dropout layers, reporting MIA accuracy/AUC, 95% confidence intervals, multiple seeds, and statistical significance, because no cited work provides evidence for the autoencoder-privacy claim. The MIA should use state-of-the-art attacks for robust models, keep the target model fully trained, and compare coupled versus decoupled generation/training to isolate memorization effects. Second, implement sparsity as an ablation with explicit L0/L1/L2/Linf budgets and distributional metrics such as MMD, FID/sFID where applicable, and t-SNE only as visualization; compare against DiverseCF-Sparse-style sparsity [(Mothilal et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2413fa296543159b32d16350d9e29f7db528790) and Samoilescu sparsity coefficients [(Samoilescu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c09fa0da9608f6e0c0112200bbda50892f00605), and test whether sparsity reduces the adversarial-original gap and MIA accuracy. Third, formalize the nearest-neighbor reversal procedure: define the neighbor class, distance metric, latent radius, and success criterion, then report success rate, imperceptibility, and semantic meaningfulness using metrics from [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) success rate, [(Pawelczyk et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77c48f33c1fd633210dae784f3d311f69b47ed82) local outlier factor/connectedness, and [(Farid et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb6db8c71ccdafff4546cb3ef9dfa9ea6089cf1) FID/sFID/FVA/CD/COUT. Fourth, include an autoencoder-versus-GAN controlled comparison using C-CHVAE-style latent lp search and NAE/AdvGAN-style latent search, with radius hyperparameters and L-Lipschitz distance diagnostics inspired by [(Pawelczyk et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fd221aa10d0b0582c1cf1f186a0052d855472e6), to determine whether any privacy advantage is due to autoencoder structure, sparsity, or decoupling. Fifth, use a frozen pretrained autoencoder and consistency loss as in [(Samoilescu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c09fa0da9608f6e0c0112200bbda50892f00605) to ensure decoded counterfactuals re-encode stably and remain in-distribution, while training the generator on data separate from the target model's training set or using unlabeled data as in [(Zhao et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502b5ef1afb16f76bcae33db17179195bbcdaae) to support the decoupling claim. Sixth, report full reproducibility details: MNIST CNN architecture, dropout rates, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, hardware, latent dimensions, search iterations, sparsity coefficients, adversarial training proportions, and random seeds, following the level of detail in [(Shao, and Kersting, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb958b7e179ec7ee358d63d0e17fbcbb6f148e7) and [(Olson et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea8fcd754845fbf517a6b7dce6da32a5b48c14d8). Finally, evaluate semantic meaningfulness and imperceptibility on grayscale MNIST with PSNR/SSIM/Linf and human or class-conditional evaluation, because the related work shows counterfactual quality is not guaranteed by prediction flip alone.

## Context: The primary empirical benchmark for comparing the proposed method with existing adversarial training/generation baselines. Method: We will conduct experiments using CNN architectures with three dropout layers on the MNIST dataset to evaluate our approach against established methods including PGD and AdvGAN. The experimental framework will maintain consistent model architectures across generation and training processes, with all models fully trained without overfitting to minimize generalization gap impacts.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed MNIST CNN benchmark is [(Noack et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e088f262c96a957b0760b753d1f4d34d3b9923e1), which directly evaluates L2 PGD adversarial training on MNIST with epsilon 320/255 and 80/255, then tests PGD40 under L2 80/255 and 320/255 and L-infinity 4/255 and 8/255, reporting clean accuracy 95.00 for standard training, 76.04 and 90.34 for PGD-trained models, and adversarial accuracies such as 67.57, 58.29, 59.05, 38.4, 75.14, 20.44, 59.43, and 25.37; this validates MNIST as a controlled setting for comparing PGD baselines and motivates reporting clean, adversarial, and cross-norm metrics for the proposed counterfactual adversarial examples. Next, [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) supports using PGD as a strong first-order baseline on MNIST, with random-start PGD, varying iterations and restarts, Carlini-Wagner kappa 50, and black-box transfer from independently trained, naturally trained, or architecturally different networks, which aligns with the plan to compare PGD and AdvGAN under consistent architectures and black-box privacy evaluation. [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f) supports the benchmarking logic by evaluating L-BFGS, Fast Gradient Sign Method, JSMA, DeepFool, and custom L2, L0, and L-infinity attacks on 1000 random instances with average distortion, standard deviation, and success probability, providing a template for measuring whether counterfactual adversarial examples are imperceptible and successful. [(Sharma et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fe7a59cb317d9c0b1f3bbbab5fc92fe3e261cf4) supports the counterfactual core by showing that a black-box, model-agnostic genetic algorithm can search for a nearby feasible point that changes prediction or reaches a target class while minimizing distance, which is conceptually compatible with generating counterfactual adversarial examples, although it does not use autoencoders or privacy metrics. [(Rozsa, and Boult, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b7b8c5d5e57281f854c2b6a049ba5ca21ffe470) supports the MNIST robustness evaluation by comparing ReLU and tent-activation networks with PGD-style adversarial training on 55,000 training, 5,000 validation, and 10,000 test MNIST images, and by testing white-box and black-box transferability, which reinforces the need for transfer and clean/robust accuracy in the proposed benchmark. [(Roos, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ff0e181ed67f6ded0c7136cc705b0e6677b382d) supports reproducible adversarial-perturbation evaluation on MNIST by training 30 random-seed sets of eight model types and scoring 1000 adversarial images plus epsilon-scaled perturbations, which is useful for the proposed plan’s requirement to control generalization gap and report stable results. Finally, [(Chan et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/000829516229b87cafff052fe310604c85236671) provides only weak support because it mentions MNIST, SVHN, and CIFAR-10 adversarial robustness evaluation without architecture, PGD, AdvGAN, counterfactual, or MIA details.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is that [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) emphasizes sufficiently high capacity networks for adversarial training, whereas the proposed method fixes CNN architectures with three dropout layers; dropout can reduce effective capacity and may therefore undermine the robustness that the plan expects to compare against PGD and AdvGAN, and the paper does not provide evidence that dropout-specific architectures reduce membership inference risk. Second, [(Noack et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e088f262c96a957b0760b753d1f4d34d3b9923e1) shows that PGD adversarial training on MNIST can preserve robustness only with a substantial clean-accuracy cost, for example 76.04 clean accuracy at epsilon 320/255 versus 95.00 for standard training, and that cross-norm robustness is uneven, with 38.4 accuracy under L-infinity 8/255 after L2 training at epsilon 320/255; this challenges the proposed goal of maintaining competitive performance while reducing privacy risk unless the counterfactual method explicitly improves the accuracy-robustness-privacy tradeoff. Third, [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f) demonstrates that adversarial generation should be benchmarked with average distortion, standard deviation, and success probability over 1000 random instances, but the proposed method does not yet specify comparable distortion, success, or imperceptibility metrics for counterfactual adversarial examples, so the claim that latent-space counterfactual perturbations are more imperceptible is not yet evidenced. Fourth, [(Sharma et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fe7a59cb317d9c0b1f3bbbab5fc92fe3e261cf4) shows that counterfactual explanations can be generated by a black-box genetic algorithm without autoencoders, but it reports no quantitative outcomes, privacy metrics, or membership inference results, so it does not support the specific assumption that autoencoder latent-space generation will achieve MIA accuracy near random guessing. Fifth, [(Rozsa, and Boult, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b7b8c5d5e57281f854c2b6a049ba5ca21ffe470) suggests that robustness can be improved through architectural choices such as tent activations rather than dropout alone, which weakens the assumption that three dropout layers are sufficient to control generalization gap and privacy risk. Sixth, [(Roos, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ff0e181ed67f6ded0c7136cc705b0e6677b382d) proposes null-class training to reject non-digit, mixed-digit, shuffled-digit, and adversarial inputs, offering an alternative robustness strategy that does not address membership inference, so it does not validate the proposed privacy mechanism. Finally, [(Chan et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/000829516229b87cafff052fe310604c85236671) provides no architecture, attack, metric, or privacy detail, so it cannot support or refute the proposed method and leaves the AdvGAN comparison and MIA evaluation unsupported by the supplied related work.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt a quantitative benchmark protocol modeled on [(Noack et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e088f262c96a957b0760b753d1f4d34d3b9923e1) and [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386): define PGD epsilon in L2 and L-infinity, iteration counts, restarts, random initialization, and AdvGAN training details, then report clean accuracy, adversarial accuracy, attack success rate, average perturbation norm, and MIA accuracy with confidence intervals on MNIST. Second, resolve the dropout-capacity confound by including ablations with zero, one, two, and three dropout layers, capacity-matched non-dropout CNNs, and high-capacity variants inspired by [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), while reporting training and test loss to verify that models are fully trained without overfitting. Third, add counterfactual-specific metrics inspired by [(Sharma et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fe7a59cb317d9c0b1f3bbbab5fc92fe3e261cf4), including distance to the original, distance to the nearest neighbor class, target-class success, constraint feasibility, and semantic meaningfulness, and compare against a black-box genetic-algorithm counterfactual baseline to show that autoencoder latent-space generation is not merely a different search method. Fourth, implement the black-box MIA evaluation as a first-class metric, with membership labels, train/test split, attack-specific calibration, random-guessing 50 percent baseline, and statistical significance tests, because none of the supplied related work directly validates the privacy-risk hypothesis and this is also where decoupling generation from training should be tested. Fifth, broaden attack evaluation using [(Carlini, and Wagner, 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df40ce107a71b770c9d0354b78fdd8989da80d2f) and [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) by including Carlini-Wagner, JSMA, DeepFool, L0, L2, and L-infinity attacks, plus black-box transfer from independently trained or architecturally different models, and cross-norm attacks as in [(Noack et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e088f262c96a957b0760b753d1f4d34d3b9923e1). Sixth, improve reproducibility by reporting multiple random seeds, ideally in the spirit of [(Roos, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ff0e181ed67f6ded0c7136cc705b0e6677b382d), along with exact CNN architecture, dropout rates, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, hardware, normalization, and t-SNE parameters. Seventh, consider ablations for alternative robustness mechanisms such as null-class rejection from [(Roos, 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ff0e181ed67f6ded0c7136cc705b0e6677b382d) or activation-based robustness from [(Rozsa, and Boult, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b7b8c5d5e57281f854c2b6a049ba5ca21ffe470) to isolate the contribution of counterfactual adversarial examples and sparsity constraints.

## Context: The main privacy-risk evaluation protocol for measuring whether robust models leak membership information. Method: Privacy risk assessment will utilize membership inference attacks (MIA) under black-box settings, where attackers can only access prediction results. We will employ state-of-the-art attack methods specifically designed for robust models to evaluate privacy leakage. The separation of adversarial generation and training processes will be implemented across all methods for fair comparison.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed black-box robust-model MIA protocol comes from [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad), which queries only prediction confidence on benign and PGD adversarial inputs, compares natural, empirically robust, and verifiably robust models, and reports MIA accuracy and advantage over random guessing; this directly matches the plan's black-box prediction-only setting, PGD baseline, and need for robust-specific attack signals I_B and I_A. Next, [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d) supports the core MIA design by training shadow models on disjoint data, using only target prediction vectors, a logistic regression attack model, and a 50 percent random-guessing benchmark, with about 90 percent accuracy on MNIST and about 80 percent on UCI Adult, showing that overfitting and generalization gap drive leakage. [(Yeom et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f216f663660ff3bc195ecd3a8ad61f0ed1d9d7) further supports the plan's generalization-gap hypothesis by connecting model-query membership inference to overfitting and generalization error across linear regression, decision trees, and deep CNNs. [(Truex et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e569f9815ee8c6e5d2c37b5c5c4f16ad60381f5) supports the black-box API-probing and shadow-data evaluation setup, using scikit-learn targets, 10 runs, 10-fold cross-validation, and dataset-specific accuracies such as 61.75 percent on MNIST and 90.44 percent on CIFAR-10 for decision-tree targets. [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) supports PGD adversarial training as a baseline and black-box attack separation using an independently trained copy, a natural-trained copy, and a different convolutional architecture, although it does not measure MIA. [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) provides robust-training baselines and hyperparameters, including MNIST CNN with two convolutional and two fully connected layers, perturbation 0.1, step 0.01, 20 iterations, learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, 50 epochs, and robust accuracy around 57 percent under white-box attacks, which can inform the plan's PGD and robust-model comparisons even though it does not report MIA.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction to the plan's expectation that robust models can be made to approach 50 percent MIA accuracy without utility loss comes from [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad), where empirically robust models leaked more than natural models: on CIFAR10 a PGD-robust model reached 74.89 percent benign-confidence MIA and 75.67 percent adversarial-confidence MIA with 51.34 percent advantage, about 3.5 times the natural advantage, and verifiable Yale Face models exceeded 75 percent accuracy with 4.5 times the natural advantage, while verifiable Fashion-MNIST models did not improve because benign train accuracy fell below 90 percent and verified train accuracy fell below 80 percent. This directly challenges the assumption that robustness or verified robustness alone will reduce membership leakage and shows that adversarial-confidence probes can be more informative than benign probes. Next, [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d) contradicts the simultaneous goal of driving MIA toward random guessing while preserving performance by showing that coarsening the prediction vector reduces attack accuracy toward random guessing but also reduces the utility of prediction outputs. [(Yeom et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f216f663660ff3bc195ecd3a8ad61f0ed1d9d7) complicates the plan's reliance on minimizing overfitting by noting that deep CNNs may memorize training data while still generalizing well, so a small generalization gap is not guaranteed to eliminate leakage. [(Truex et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e569f9815ee8c6e5d2c37b5c5c4f16ad60381f5) weakens the idea that simple perturbation or noise will be sufficient, because adding small noise to target instances left attack accuracy close to the fully known case and shadow-data size had dataset-dependent effects, with CIFAR-10 and Purchases-50 improving as size increased while Purchases-10 and Purchases-20 plateaued earlier. Finally, [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) and [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) do not provide MIA evidence and use adversarial examples during training, so their robustness results cannot be taken as support that decoupled generation or robust training reduces privacy risk.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the robust-model MIA protocol from [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad): for each target model, query benign and PGD-generated adversarial prediction confidence under a fixed l_infty budget, compute I_B, I_A, and I_V only if verifiable certificates are used, and report MIA accuracy and advantage over 50 percent rather than accuracy alone; for MNIST use an explicit epsilon such as 8/255 or 0.1 depending on normalization, fixed PGD iterations/restarts, and a probe generator that is not used in target training. Second, enforce the plan's separation of adversarial generation and training across all methods by using the same target CNN, same MNIST split, same attack model, and same shadow-data distribution for counterfactual adversarial examples, PGD, AdvGAN, and TRADES-like baselines, while documenting when a baseline inherently couples generation and training; use [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) as a template for black-box fairness by testing transfer from an independently trained copy, a natural-trained copy, and a different architecture. Third, incorporate the evaluation controls from [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d), [(Yeom et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f216f663660ff3bc195ecd3a8ad61f0ed1d9d7), and [(Truex et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e569f9815ee8c6e5d2c37b5c5c4f16ad60381f5): train shadow models on disjoint data, use logistic regression or other black-box attack models, report generalization gap, overfitting, shadow-data synthesis strategy, 10 runs or 10-fold cross-validation, confidence intervals, and paired significance tests, and include output quantization or coarsening as an ablation while measuring utility loss. Fourth, use the robust-training hyperparameters from [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) and [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) to make baselines reproducible: for MNIST, specify CNN depth, perturbation 0.1, step 0.01, 20 PGD iterations, learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, 50 epochs, and TRADES lambda sweep, then adapt to the plan's three dropout layers and report train, test, and adversarial accuracy. Fifth, tie the counterfactual adversarial-example design to the privacy metric by evaluating l_inf/l2 imperceptibility, sparsity, nearest-neighbor class overlap, t-SNE separation, and semantic meaningfulness, and correlate each with MIA advantage; include noise ablations with sigma from 0 to 1 as in [(Truex et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e569f9815ee8c6e5d2c37b5c5c4f16ad60381f5) to show that the method is not merely adding noise. Finally, report full reproducibility details missing from the related work, including MNIST 60,000 training and 10,000 test sizes, dropout rates, autoencoder latent dimension, sparsity penalty, optimizer, seeds, hardware, and statistical significance, because the strongest related MIA studies do not report significance or compute resources.

## Context: The quantitative criteria used to judge whether the method achieves the target privacy-robustness-accuracy tradeoff. Method: The evaluation metrics will include training accuracy, test accuracy, and MIA accuracy, with the goal of achieving MIA accuracy approaching 50% (random guessing level) while maintaining competitive model performance. We will also analyze the semantic meaningfulness of generated perturbations and their alignment with adversarial example definitions across different dataset types.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation is [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad), which directly operationalizes the proposed metric suite by reporting training accuracy, test accuracy, benign-confidence MIA accuracy, adversarial-confidence MIA accuracy, and inference advantage for natural and robust CNN or wide ResNet models on Yale Face, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR10 under l-infinity PGD, distributional, and difference-based adversarial training. Its natural models are close to the proposed random-guessing target, with Yale Face benign and adversarial MIA accuracy of 55.85 and 54.27, Fashion-MNIST 57.12 and 50.95, and CIFAR10 57.43 and 50.86, validating MIA accuracy as a meaningful black-box privacy metric for robust models. The second strongest support is [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d), which established shadow-model MIA on UCI Adult, reported about 80 percent attack accuracy for a neural network target and about 70 percent for an SVM, showed that overfitting increases leakage, and showed that coarsening prediction precision can reduce attack accuracy toward random guessing at a utility cost; this supports the plan's generalization-gap hypothesis and the use of MIA accuracy as the primary privacy metric. Third, [(Yeom et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f216f663660ff3bc195ecd3a8ad61f0ed1d9d7) provides theoretical and empirical support that membership advantage scales with average generalization error, with a bounded-loss adversary advantage equal to average generalization error divided by the loss bound, directly supporting the plan's sparsity and decoupling aims to reduce the adversarial-original gap. Fourth, [(Rahman et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42d073d1217719fc8708ba4a5162b3f2034baeed) supports the DP-SGD and MIA evaluation design by training CIFAR-10 and MNIST with DP-SGD at epsilon 1, 2, 4, and 8 with delta 1e-5, using noise scale 6 and clipping 3 for CIFAR-10, noise scale 4, clipping 4, and PCA noise 7 for MNIST, balanced attack test sets of 15,000 and 17,500, and explicit baselines of 0.5 random-guess accuracy and 0.67 naive all-member F1, although it does not report numerical MIA outcomes. Fifth, [(Abadi et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9a986c8ff6c2f381d026fe014f6aaa865f34da7) supports the privacy training baseline by providing DP-SGD with a moments accountant, per-layer clipping and noise, and a concrete accounting comparison where lot size 0.01N, sigma 4, delta 1e-5, and T 10000 gives epsilon about 1.26 versus 9.34 under strong composition. Sixth, [(Tursynbek et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e10714c5179d551920662348c343f5785d86e633) supports the privacy-robustness tension by showing DP-SGD on LeNet MNIST with sigma 1.3 and clipping 1.0 increases the clean accuracy gap from 2.6 percent to 15.4 percent at FGSM epsilon 0.12 and reduces PGD iterations to failure, motivating a method that preserves robustness while lowering MIA. Seventh, [(Madry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), [(Schmidt et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/804fb9542f4f56e264dd2df57c255a9a2011c00f), and [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) support the robustness-accuracy side by providing PGD adversarial training, data-size scaling, and regularization baselines; Zhang reports MNIST robust accuracy increasing from 91.09 to 95.65 while natural accuracy falls from 99.41 to 99.16 as 1/lambda increases from 0.1 to 5.0, and CIFAR-10 robust accuracy increasing from 26.53 to 50.64 while natural accuracy falls from 91.31 to 81.72, showing that robustness can be tuned but at accuracy cost. Weaker support comes from [(Carlini et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/520ec00dc35475e0554dbb72f27bd2eeb6f4191d), whose canary exposure and shortest-path extraction can be used to test memorization, [(Song et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18cfd4b9e35fb12fbebedb0fdc3f7811090372bf), whose secret-encoding utility metrics define privacy-utility tradeoffs, [(Shejwalkar, and Houmansadr, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a8f6430be660a5fc34d589806622cc73c209731), whose DMP reference-set MIA setup compares privacy defenses with MIA accuracy, and [(Zhou et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d62c40a0122b8b2e271c313c04783d73055ee65), whose MNIST adversarial training and model inversion evaluation shows robustness can change privacy leakage and motivates semantic reconstruction checks.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad), which shows that standard robust models have MIA accuracy far above the proposed 50 percent target: Yale Face PGD robust models reach 61.69 benign-confidence and 68.83 adversarial-confidence MIA accuracy with inference advantage 37.66, about 3.2 times the natural model advantage; Fashion-MNIST PGD robust models reach 58.32 and 64.49 with advantage 28.98, about 2.0 times natural; CIFAR10 PGD robust models reach 74.89 and 75.67 with advantage 51.34, about 3.5 times natural; distributional and difference-based defenses also exceed natural baselines, and IBP-verified Yale Face models exceed 75 percent MIA accuracy. This directly contradicts the plan's assumption that adversarial training or robustness alone can be made to approach random-guess MIA, so the proposed counterfactual method must beat these baselines. The second strongest contradiction is [(Tursynbek et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e10714c5179d551920662348c343f5785d86e633), which shows that DP-SGD privacy training degrades robustness, with a clean accuracy gap of 2.6 percent between SGD and DP-SGD growing to 15.4 percent at FGSM epsilon 0.12 and fewer PGD iterations to failure at epsilon 0.1, 0.2, and 0.3, contradicting the goal of maintaining competitive robustness while using privacy-preserving training. Third, [(Schmidt et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/804fb9542f4f56e264dd2df57c255a9a2011c00f) shows that robust generalization requires substantially more data and that PGD robust training with 20 MNIST steps and 10 CIFAR-10 or SVHN steps, step size 2.5 epsilon/k, still leaves data-size and perturbation-strength tradeoffs; thresholding helps at epsilon 0.1 but does not address MIA, contradicting the idea that varying adversarial proportion alone will solve the tradeoff. Fourth, [(Zhou et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d62c40a0122b8b2e271c313c04783d73055ee65) provides mixed evidence that adversarial training reduces privacy leakage: at FGSM bound 0.02 it reduced model inversion attack accuracy and distorted reconstructions, but at 0.3 leakage increased and reconstructions resembled ground truth more, contradicting a monotonic robustness-reduces-privacy assumption. Fifth, [(Carlini et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/520ec00dc35475e0554dbb72f27bd2eeb6f4191d) contradicts the sufficiency of decoupled generation by showing memorized canaries can be extracted efficiently, with shortest-path search enumerating 3 to 5 orders of magnitude fewer nodes than brute force and GPU batching giving 50x to 500x speedups, so decoupling must be validated against canary extraction. Sixth, [(Song et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18cfd4b9e35fb12fbebedb0fdc3f7811090372bf) weakly contradicts simple privacy-utility separation by showing malicious training can encode secrets while preserving primary-task accuracy, with training accuracy over 98 percent and minor stochasticity, implying that utility-preserving training does not guarantee low membership or memorization risk. Weaker contradictory or limiting evidence includes [(Rahman et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42d073d1217719fc8708ba4a5162b3f2034baeed) and [(Abadi et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9a986c8ff6c2f381d026fe014f6aaa865f34da7), which support DP-SGD and MIA baselines but do not report numerical MIA outcomes or robustness results, and [(Shejwalkar, and Houmansadr, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a8f6430be660a5fc34d589806622cc73c209731), which proposes DMP but lacks numerical MIA results in the excerpt, leaving the claim that reference-set defenses can reach near-random MIA unverified.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the black-box prediction-based MIA protocol from [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad), using prediction confidence on benign examples and PGD adversarial examples, balanced member and non-member test sets, inference advantage over random guessing, and multiple random seeds, and to compare the proposed counterfactual adversarial examples against natural, PGD, distributional, difference-based, and verifiable robust baselines. The second most important action is to add DP-SGD and adversarial regularization baselines using the hyperparameters in [(Abadi et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9a986c8ff6c2f381d026fe014f6aaa865f34da7), [(Rahman et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42d073d1217719fc8708ba4a5162b3f2034baeed), and [(Tursynbek et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e10714c5179d551920662348c343f5785d86e633), including moments accountant, epsilon and delta, noise scale, clipping bound, lot size, and FGSM or PGD robustness evaluation with fixed epsilon budgets, so that the proposed method is judged against both privacy and robustness baselines. Third, explicitly measure and control the generalization gap using [(Yeom et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f216f663660ff3bc195ecd3a8ad61f0ed1d9d7) and [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d), by reporting training-test accuracy gap, overfitting curves, and testing whether sparsity, dropout, decoupled generation, and counterfactual perturbations reduce the gap and MIA advantage. Fourth, define semantic meaningfulness metrics beyond t-SNE, including L1 and L2 pixel distance, SSIM or structural similarity, class-conditional plausibility, human evaluation, and reconstruction-style checks inspired by [(Zhou et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d62c40a0122b8b2e271c313c04783d73055ee65), and compare PGD, AdvGAN, and counterfactual perturbations on MNIST and at least one color dataset. Fifth, add canary memorization tests from [(Carlini et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/520ec00dc35475e0554dbb72f27bd2eeb6f4191d) by inserting known canaries, measuring exposure, and running shortest-path extraction to verify that decoupled generation does not memorize training samples. Sixth, use robustness training details from [(Madry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), [(Schmidt et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/804fb9542f4f56e264dd2df57c255a9a2011c00f), and [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) to ensure fair baselines, including PGD random initialization, step size 2.5 epsilon/k, 10 to 20 steps, epsilon budgets, data-size scaling, and regularization lambda sweeps. Seventh, ablate the autoencoder latent-space claim and sparsity constraints, because the related work does not directly show autoencoders outperform GANs for MIA, and compare autoencoder, GAN, and direct PGD or AdvGAN generation under identical CNN, dropout, training data, and MIA evaluation. Eighth, include DMP reference-set and secret-encoding memorization baselines from [(Shejwalkar, and Houmansadr, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a8f6430be660a5fc34d589806622cc73c209731) and [(Song et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18cfd4b9e35fb12fbebedb0fdc3f7811090372bf) to distinguish membership risk from intentional memorization and to report utility metrics alongside privacy and robustness.

## Context: The ablation study for understanding how the amount of adversarial training data affects the robustness-privacy relationship. Method: To analyze the relationship between robustness and privacy, we will systematically vary the proportion of adversarial examples in training sets while controlling total data quantity.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed ablation is that adversarial training is directly linked to higher membership-inference leakage, which is exactly the robustness-privacy relationship the plan wants to probe by varying the proportion of adversarial examples while holding total data quantity fixed. [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad) shows that robust models have larger inference advantage than natural models on Yale Face, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR10, with PGD-trained CIFAR10 reaching I_B accuracy 74.89% and I_A accuracy 75.67% versus natural 57.43% and 50.86%, and that adversarial-confidence attacks often outperform benign-confidence attacks; this supports the plan's focus on black-box MIA and its target of moving MIA accuracy toward 50%. A secondary but important support is that the robustness-privacy tradeoff can be mitigated rather than assumed fixed: [(Lei et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d2443d65371b2cb8bc9f2ae46bbe7910fe07e39) reports that a first-layer Bayesian STF-BNN trained after PGD adversarial training at radii 0 to 10/255 improves adversarial accuracy and lowers threshold-based MIA accuracy relative to deterministic baselines on CIFAR-10/100 variants, although the excerpt is qualitative and varies radius rather than adversarial-data proportion. The earlier MIA methodology in [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d) supports the plan's evaluation design because it uses shadow models, logistic-regression attack classifiers, and ablations over training-data quantity, class balance, and overfitting on UCI Adult, showing MIA accuracy can rise to about 90% for overfitting targets and can be reduced toward random guessing by coarsening prediction precision; this provides a template for measuring how the fraction of adversarial examples changes memorization and leakage. [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14) supports the robustness side of the ablation by demonstrating a controlled TRADES regularization sweep on MNIST and CIFAR-10, with MNIST robust accuracy increasing from 91.09% to 95.65% as 1/lambda increases from 0.1 to 5.0 while natural accuracy falls only from 99.41% to 99.16%, and by providing reproducible MNIST/CNN hyperparameters (radius 0.1, inner step 0.01, 20 inner iterations, learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, 50 epochs) that match the plan's MNIST/CNN setting. Finally, [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) supports the use of PGD as the adversarial training and evaluation adversary on MNIST/CIFAR-10, with random-start PGD and multi-epoch training instead of many restarts, but it does not itself evaluate privacy or vary the fraction of adversarial examples, so its support is mainly methodological.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or challenge is that the same evidence showing robust models leak more also makes the plan's 50% MIA target difficult: [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad) finds that adversarially trained models, including PGD-only training, have substantially higher MIA advantage than natural models, with CIFAR10 robust inference advantage up to 51.34% and PGD I_A accuracy 75.67%, and that robust models are more sensitive to removed training points; this suggests that increasing the proportion of adversarial examples may increase, not decrease, leakage unless the counterfactual generation and decoupled training specifically suppress memorization. A second challenge comes from the robustness-accuracy tradeoff documented in [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14), where increasing TRADES regularization improves robust accuracy but reduces natural accuracy, especially on CIFAR-10 from 91.31% to 81.72% as 1/lambda increases from 0.1 to 5.0; this contradicts the assumption that the plan can simultaneously maintain competitive accuracy, strong robustness, and near-random MIA accuracy without explicit tradeoff management. A third, weaker contradiction is the privacy-utility tradeoff in [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d), where reducing MIA accuracy toward random guessing by coarsening target prediction precision also reduces target accuracy, implying that the plan's goal of MIA near 50% may require utility sacrifices if achieved through output smoothing or similar mechanisms rather than through counterfactual adversarial data. A fourth limitation is that [(Lei et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d2443d65371b2cb8bc9f2ae46bbe7910fe07e39) provides only qualitative evidence that STF-BNNs reduce MIA accuracy and improve robustness, without numerical MIA values, confidence intervals, or a controlled ablation over the proportion of adversarial examples, so it cannot strongly validate the proposed data-proportion mechanism. The weakest contradiction is that [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386) does not report privacy metrics, exact PGD step size, iteration count, perturbation budget, or training hyperparameters in the excerpt, leaving the robustness baseline insufficient to assess whether adversarial training alone changes membership leakage.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the robust-model MIA protocol from [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f39e93e1f53b573f3f1fb67921e3f7fa37b89ad): evaluate black-box membership inference with both benign-confidence I_B and adversarial-confidence I_A attacks, report inference advantage over random guessing, use l-infinity budgets comparable to the literature such as 8/255 for CIFAR-like settings and 0.1 for MNIST-like settings, and include a sensitivity analysis that removes one training point per class, retrains multiple times, and measures confidence changes, because Song shows I_A can outperform I_B for robust models and that robust models are more sensitive to individual training examples. The next most important action is to make the proposed proportion ablation directly comparable to existing robustness baselines by fixing total training size and varying the fraction of adversarial examples at several levels, for example 0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%, while replacing the removed natural examples with counterfactual adversarial examples or PGD/AdvGAN examples; include natural training, PGD adversarial-only training from [(Ma̧dry et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7aa38b85fa8cba64d6a4010543f6695dbf5f1386), TRADES-style regularization from [(Zhang et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c405d4b5dc41a86be05acd59c06ed19daf01d14), and the counterfactual method as baselines, and use the MNIST/CNN hyperparameters reported by Zhang (two convolutional and two fully connected layers, perturbation radius 0.1, inner step 0.01, 20 inner iterations, learning rate 0.01, batch size 128, 50 epochs) or explicitly justify deviations. Third, strengthen the privacy evaluation by borrowing the shadow-model ablation structure from [(Shokri et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0dcc9aa31dc9b31b836bcac1b140c8c94a2982d): train shadow models on matched approximations of the target training distribution, use logistic-regression attack classifiers, report accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and target accuracy, and ablate shadow-data quantity, class balance, and target overfitting so that a drop in MIA accuracy can be attributed to the adversarial-data proportion rather than to attack-model artifacts. Fourth, add quantitative distributional and statistical evidence beyond t-SNE: report multiple random seeds with mean and standard deviation as in Zhang, test significance where appropriate, and use quantitative measures such as MMD, KL divergence, nearest-neighbor class overlap, or generalization gap between natural and adversarial sets to support the claim that counterfactual examples reduce distributional memorization. Fifth, explicitly manage the privacy-utility tradeoff by reporting the accuracy cost of any mechanism that lowers MIA accuracy, including sparsity constraints, latent-space generation, dropout, or output coarsening, and consider an auxiliary ablation of Bayesian or variational regularization inspired by [(Lei et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d2443d65371b2cb8bc9f2ae46bbe7910fe07e39) while specifying hardware, seeds, epochs, and GPU/TPU resources to make the robustness-privacy results reproducible.

## Context: The explanatory analysis for diagnosing why the proposed method reduces distributional differences between original and adversarial data. Method: We will use t-SNE visualization to examine data distribution similarities between original and adversarial datasets, providing insights into why our method achieves better privacy performance.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the newest closest work, [(Valentim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6caa7354b2797b262880f12bd0fb7c7cfbd77bd), which applies t-SNE to clean and perturbed hidden representations of pretrained CNNs on CIFAR-10, uses a 5000-image validation split with 10 stratified random seeds, and computes a clean-versus-perturbed embedding metric for each target layer, directly extending earlier t-SNE visualizations into a layer-wise adversarial diagnostic. The next strongest support is the foundational t-SNE paper, [(Maaten, and Hinton, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c46943103bd7b7a2c7be86859995a4144d1938b), which provides a reproducible MNIST pipeline with PCA reduction from 784 to 30 dimensions, a k=20 nearest-neighbor graph over 60,000 images, 6,000 landmarks, random-walk affinities, 1,000 gradient-descent iterations, early exaggeration 4 for 50 iterations, momentum 0.5 for 250 iterations then 0.8, adaptive learning rate 100, and a 1-nearest-neighbor error of 5.13% versus 5.75% in original space, showing that t-SNE can preserve class structure on the same dataset used in the proposed plan. Further support is provided by [(Belkhouja, and Doppa, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0b2251db3ee55ce82c01dd355d2e6f5cf386f15), which uses t-SNE to compare original and adversarial time-series signals generated by TSA-STAT with beta_label=1 and beta_stat=1 and by PGD with a perturbation factor below 0.4, demonstrating that t-SNE can be used to inspect whether adversarial perturbations preserve statistical and semantic structure. Additional, weaker support comes from [(Xu et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00ed58fc0329a5f624e66d721c628504ace172ab), which uses distribution visualization to assess whether a generator aligns clean and adversarial feature-space distributions across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny-ImageNet, Cityscapes, VOC2012, and VOC07+12, supporting the explanatory role of distributional similarity even though the excerpt does not detail t-SNE hyperparameters. Weaker still are [(elShehaby, and Matrawy, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9e0173462e7be008c505823abe477fe33bea4c9), which reduces 50-dimensional CSE-CIC-IDS2018 intrusion-detection features to 2D t-SNE to visualize distribution and decision-boundary changes during retraining, and [(Dyrmishi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/942e507b2097762cb9a74e9a2563e6a9c751469a), which includes t-SNE plots of adversarial embeddings but provides no hyperparameters, dataset size, or quantitative outcome.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the newest closest work, [(Valentim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6caa7354b2797b262880f12bd0fb7c7cfbd77bd), reports no quantitative t-SNE outcome, statistical significance, or baseline comparison; it only states that 2D maps and a clean-versus-perturbed metric are generated for each validation split and layer, so t-SNE by itself does not demonstrate why privacy or robustness improves. A second major contradiction is that the foundational t-SNE method, [(Maaten, and Hinton, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c46943103bd7b7a2c7be86859995a4144d1938b), is a non-parametric visual diagnostic rather than a statistical test of distributional difference or privacy risk; its MNIST result, 5.13% 1-NN error versus 5.75% original, measures class separability, not membership-inference leakage. Third, [(Belkhouja, and Doppa, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0b2251db3ee55ce82c01dd355d2e6f5cf386f15) uses t-SNE only qualitatively and reports no distance score, significance test, or privacy metric, indicating that visual separation alone is insufficient to support claims about adversarial distributional similarity. Fourth, [(Xu et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00ed58fc0329a5f624e66d721c628504ace172ab) lacks actual visualization figures or numerical metrics in the excerpt, and its reported robustness gains are not tied to the distribution visualization, weakening the claim that such visual analysis explains performance. Fifth, [(elShehaby, and Matrawy, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9e0173462e7be008c505823abe477fe33bea4c9) provides no t-SNE hyperparameters, seeds, or quantitative similarity test, and its over-90% recovery result concerns stale adversarial attacks after retraining, not membership inference. Finally, [(Dyrmishi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/942e507b2097762cb9a74e9a2563e6a9c751469a) provides no embedding source, hyperparameters, dataset size, or quantitative result, making it too weak to support a reproducible t-SNE-based privacy explanation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to treat t-SNE only as a qualitative diagnostic and add quantitative distributional-similarity metrics and statistical tests that can be correlated with MIA accuracy, such as MMD, Wasserstein distance, Procrustes alignment, optimal-transport cost, cluster-overlap or nearest-neighbor cross-class distance, and bootstrap or permutation tests for significance. Second, specify a reproducible t-SNE protocol for MNIST and counterfactual adversarial examples, including whether t-SNE is applied to raw 784-dimensional pixels, autoencoder latent codes, or CNN layer activations; if using raw pixels, follow the MNIST recipe from [(Maaten, and Hinton, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c46943103bd7b7a2c7be86859995a4144d1938b) with PCA pre-reduction, k=20 graph, 6,000 landmarks, 1,000 iterations, early exaggeration 4 for 50 iterations, momentum 0.5 then 0.8, adaptive learning rate 100, and multiple random seeds. Third, adopt the layer-wise clean-versus-perturbed embedding comparison suggested by [(Valentim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6caa7354b2797b262880f12bd0fb7c7cfbd77bd) and the feature-space alignment motivation from [(Xu et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00ed58fc0329a5f624e66d721c628504ace172ab), reporting a numeric embedding metric for each layer, validation split, and attack method. Fourth, compare t-SNE maps and metrics across PGD, AdvGAN, and the counterfactual method while varying the proportion of adversarial examples, and include imperceptibility and semantic-preservation measures such as L2, L_inf, PSNR, SSIM, and statistical features like mean, standard deviation, skewness, kurtosis, and RMS as used in [(Belkhouja, and Doppa, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0b2251db3ee55ce82c01dd355d2e6f5cf386f15). Fifth, if decision-boundary interpretation is needed, add density or Voronoi-style boundary analysis as in [(elShehaby, and Matrawy, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9e0173462e7be008c505823abe477fe33bea4c9), but only after quantitative metrics are established. Finally, report all t-SNE hyperparameters, seeds, hardware, and software versions, and avoid overinterpreting stochastic local clusters, following the missing-detail lessons from [(Dyrmishi et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/942e507b2097762cb9a74e9a2563e6a9c751469a).
