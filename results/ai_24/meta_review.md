## We will evaluate ICAM on three routing problems: Traveling Salesman Problem (TSP), Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (CVRP), and Asymmetric Traveling Salesman Problem (ATSP). Test instances will be generated following established protocols, with scales ranging from 100 to 5,000 nodes for TSP/CVRP and up to 1,000 nodes for ATSP; this defines the primary problem coverage and scale range used to test cross-scale generalization.

### Support

The strongest support for evaluating ICAM as a constructive routing solver comes from [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937), which trains an actor-critic pointer network for CVRP with feasibility masking, greedy decoding, and beam search widths of 5 and 10, and evaluates 1,000 instances per size on VRP10, VRP20, VRP50, and VRP100. Its quantitative results, including roughly 95% of VRP10 instances within 10% of optimality, VRP20 within 13%, outliers within 20-25%, and RL-BS(10) winning 60.2% against OR-Tools versus 41.5% for RL-greedy, directly support the proposed plan's focus on constructive decoding, beam search, CVRP feasibility, and inference-time scaling. The next strongest support is [(Dai et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e819f533ef2bf5ca50a6b2008d96eaea2a2706e), whose S2V-DQN uses structure2vec with T=4 iterations, n-step Q-learning, fitted Q-iteration, and experience replay on DIMACS-style uniform and clustered Euclidean TSP graphs, and reports that learned greedy policies remain effective on larger graphs, directly motivating ICAM's TSP cross-scale evaluation, although the excerpt lacks numeric optimality gaps, significance values, and CVRP or ATSP results. [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997) provides methodological support for the plan's ablation and decoding analysis by training on TSP20-50 and testing zero-shot generalization to TSP200, showing that reinforcement learning is better under greedy decoding while supervised learning is better under beam search or sampling; this supports ICAM's planned reporting of single trajectory, multiple trajectory, and instance augmentation scenarios, but the evidence is qualitative and TSP-only. [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f) supports the evaluation design because it compares LKH-based variants using success count, average optimum gap, and computation time across same-size, different-distribution, different-size, and both-shifted tests, aligning with ICAM's planned LKH3-based gaps and cross-distribution testing, although the excerpt lacks numeric values. [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5) supports the CVRP branch and the importance of runtime by showing that a neural value function plus MIP policy iteration can reach an average final gap of 1.7% against OR-Tools on CVRPLIB instances with 32 to 78 cities after 100 policy iterations, while also highlighting why a constructive attention-free decoder is needed for larger scales. [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09) supports the broader claim that learned graph models can generalize to larger or different graphs, using a 20-layer GCN trained on 38,000 SATLIB-derived MIS graphs of about 1,200 vertices and tested on SNAP graphs up to about 100,000 vertices, but it addresses MIS and SAT rather than routing. [(Akpinar et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c77bafde0c9e34f94b03b74d42e4e1e80a33f715) provides weaker support for neural graph property learning, showing that RNN and LSTM models outperform feed-forward networks on Erdos-Renyi graphs with 10 to 18 vertices and deriving sample-complexity bounds, but the scale is far below ICAM's 100 to 5,000-node routing target. Finally, [(Pathan, and Shrivastava, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc412b3fcfb82d37f5961ae94639cb56a170079) offers the weakest support for instance-conditioned adaptation by showing that a pretrained policy can adapt to dynamic task-effort changes without retraining in small assignment, bin packing, and vehicle routing settings, but it reports no numeric CVRP values and does not test cross-scale generalization.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the cited constructive routing works quantitatively validates the proposed 100 to 5,000-node TSP/CVRP or up-to-1,000-node ATSP range. [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) only evaluates VRP10 through VRP100, and its comparisons against OR-Tools are not LKH3, Concorde, or HGS optimality gaps; [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997) only reaches TSP200 and reports qualitative outcomes; and [(Dai et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e819f533ef2bf5ca50a6b2008d96eaea2a2706e) claims larger-graph generalization but provides no node-count range, optimality gap, or statistical significance. This leaves ICAM's central large-scale claim under-evidenced. A second contradiction is that MIP-based neural routing does not scale: [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5) reports average SCIP solve times of about 3 seconds for 21 cities and about 240 seconds for 51 cities, with 51-city optima not obtained within 6 hours, contradicting any expectation that hybrid MIP policy iteration can serve as a scalable baseline or component at ICAM's target scale. Third, several works are too small or too different to support routing-specific scale adaptation: [(Akpinar et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c77bafde0c9e34f94b03b74d42e4e1e80a33f715) uses graphs of only 10 to 18 vertices because exact labels are intractable; [(Pathan, and Shrivastava, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc412b3fcfb82d37f5961ae94639cb56a170079) uses 10 to 50 task assignment instances with capacity 15 and no numeric CVRP results; and [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09) addresses MIS and SAT rather than TSP, CVRP, or ATSP. Fourth, no cited work directly validates ICAM's specific architectural choices, namely the adaptation function f(N, d_ij) = -alpha log2 N d_ij, the AAFM replacement of multi-head attention, or the 12-layer attention-free encoder; [(Dai et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e819f533ef2bf5ca50a6b2008d96eaea2a2706e) uses structure2vec graph embeddings, [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) uses pointer and actor-critic decoding, and [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997) ablates generic pipeline components, so the proposed attention-free and scale-bias mechanisms remain untested. Fifth, the related evaluation evidence is often statistically thin: [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) does not report significance tests or confidence intervals, [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09) and [(Akpinar et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c77bafde0c9e34f94b03b74d42e4e1e80a33f715) omit significance, and [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f) provides only metric names without values, so the plan's strong claims against Concorde, LKH3, HGS, POMO, MatNet, ELG, Pointerformer, MCTS, TAM, and GLOP require more rigorous evidence than the cited literature currently provides.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the 100 to 5,000-node TSP/CVRP and up-to-1,000-node ATSP evaluation explicit and reproducible: generate 1,000 instances per scale using DIMACS-style uniform and clustered protocols, CVRPLIB and TSPLIB benchmarks, and rotation or explosion distributions; report LKH3, Concorde, and HGS optimality gaps, objective values, inference times, memory, hardware, solver versions, time limits, multiple seeds, and statistical significance, with scaling curves at 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000, and 5,000 nodes. The next most important action is to add ICAM-specific ablations that isolate the claimed mechanisms: compare f(N, d_ij) with and without the log2 N term, sweep alpha, compare AAFM against standard multi-head attention at matched depth and compute, ablate the 12-layer encoder depth, and test each of the three training stages and the top-k loss against POMO, MatNet, ELG, Pointerformer, MCTS, TAM, and GLOP under identical training compute. Third, adopt the strongest evaluation practices from the related work: use greedy decoding, beam search widths 5 and 10, multiple trajectories, and CVRP feasibility masking as in [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937); report winning rates, gap distributions, and log(time/nodes) scaling; use LKH-based success, gap, and time reporting as in [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f); and include runtime comparisons against OR-Tools only as a secondary baseline because it is weaker than LKH3 or HGS. Fourth, include cross-distribution and dynamic adaptation tests: separate distribution shift, scale shift, and both, following [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f) and [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997), and add a small dynamic-change test inspired by [(Pathan, and Shrivastava, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc412b3fcfb82d37f5961ae94639cb56a170079) to show whether instance-conditioned adaptation helps without retraining. Finally, report reproducibility details missing from prior work, including batch size, learning rate, training episodes, beam width, sample paths, GPU or CPU specifications, and training time, while avoiding MIP-based components at large scale because [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5) shows that MIP solve times become prohibitive beyond about 50 cities.
