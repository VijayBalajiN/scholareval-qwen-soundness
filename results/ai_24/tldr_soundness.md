## Strengths 
The strongest support is for constructive RL decoding, CVRP feasibility masking, beam search, and inference-time scaling from [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937), which evaluated VRP10 through VRP100 and reported gap and win-rate evidence. Cross-scale TSP motivation is supported by [(Dai et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e819f533ef2bf5ca50a6b2008d96eaea2a2706e), while decoding and ablation practices are supported by [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997). LKH-based gap, time, and cross-distribution evaluation is supported by [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f), and CVRP runtime relevance is supported by [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions are that none of the cited constructive routing works quantitatively validates the proposed 100 to 5,000-node TSP/CVRP or up-to-1,000-node ATSP range: [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) only evaluates VRP10 through VRP100, [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997) only reaches TSP200 with qualitative outcomes, and [(Dai et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e819f533ef2bf5ca50a6b2008d96eaea2a2706e) lacks numeric scale, gap, and significance evidence. MIP-based neural routing does not scale, as [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5) reports about 240 seconds for 51 cities and no 51-city optima within 6 hours. The specific ICAM mechanisms, including f(N, d_ij), AAFM, and the 12-layer attention-free encoder, are not directly validated, and prior evidence is statistically thin, e.g., [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) lacks significance tests and confidence intervals, while [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f) provides metric names without values.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Make the large-scale evaluation explicit and reproducible: generate 1,000 instances per scale at 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000, and 5,000 nodes for TSP/CVRP and up to 1,000 nodes for ATSP using DIMACS-style uniform/clustered protocols, CVRPLIB/TSPLIB benchmarks, and rotation/explosion distributions; report LKH3, Concorde, and HGS optimality gaps, objective values, inference times, memory, hardware, solver versions, time limits, multiple seeds, and statistical significance, following LKH-based reporting in [(Liu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320967b68e932d23709834b5dbd2200b87fb257f) and constructive decoding/beam search practices in [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937).

Add ICAM-specific ablations under matched compute: compare f(N, d_ij) with and without the log2N term, sweep alpha, compare AAFM against standard multi-head attention at matched depth and FLOPs, ablate the 12-layer encoder depth, and test each of the three training stages and the top-k loss against POMO, MatNet, ELG, Pointerformer, MCTS, TAM, and GLOP; include greedy decoding, beam search widths 5 and 10, multiple trajectories, and CVRP feasibility masking as in [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937), with decoding analysis informed by [(Joshi et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d45afc1f12cba92a66da4e83a96380ac0a15997).

Separate distribution shift, scale shift, and both, and add a small dynamic-change/no-retraining test inspired by [(Pathan, and Shrivastava, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cdc412b3fcfb82d37f5961ae94639cb56a170079); report winning rates, gap distributions, log(time/nodes) scaling, and full reproducibility details such as batch size, learning rate, training episodes, beam width, sample paths, GPU/CPU specifications, and training time, while avoiding MIP-based components at large scale because [(Delarue et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7f0bac65336214c042920a378b7ecd511a10ce5) shows prohibitive solve times beyond about 50 cities.
