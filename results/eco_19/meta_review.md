## To test the central hypothesis that Bursicon signaling mediates seasonal polyphenism in C. chinensis: We will conduct RNAi experiments by feeding newly hatched 1st instar nymphs with dsRNAs targeting CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, CcBurs-R, or control dsEGFP. We will assess RNAi efficiency through qRT-PCR and evaluate functional outcomes through cuticle pigment extraction, chitin content determination, transmission electron microscopy for cuticle thickness measurement, and morphological transition percentage analysis.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed RNAi method comes from [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1), which used dsRNA-mediated knockdown of honeybee AmBurs α and AmBurs β and reported disturbed adult cuticle formation plus interference with structural cuticle protein and melanization/sclerotization enzyme expression. This is the closest methodological analogue to feeding C. chinensis 1st-instar nymphs with CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R dsRNAs and measuring cuticle pigment, chitin, and morph transition, because it directly links bursicon loss-of-function to cuticle structural and tanning-related outcomes. A second, less direct support is [(Loveall, and Deitcher, 2010-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c24d7e6bc15939026333be976ca99b64a6b60bfa), where ubiquitous UAS-burs RNAi in Drosophila obstructed advancement beyond the pharate adult stage and was paired with RT-PCR for rickets transcript levels, indicating that bursicon knockdown can perturb ecdysial/cuticle development and that transcript-level readouts are appropriate, although the study did not provide quantitative knockdown efficiency, sample sizes, or seasonal morph metrics. The weakest but still relevant support is [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4), which showed Bursicon subunits form heterodimers and homodimers and can activate downstream signaling, supporting the planned protein dimerization and downstream pathway analyses, but it used recombinant protein gain-of-function in Drosophila fat body rather than dsRNA feeding, cuticle pigment extraction, chitin quantification, TEM, or seasonal transition scoring.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or feasibility concern is from [(Loveall, and Deitcher, 2010-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c24d7e6bc15939026333be976ca99b64a6b60bfa), because ubiquitous UAS-burs RNAi blocked development beyond the pharate adult stage, implying that strong bursicon knockdown can cause ecdysial arrest or lethality. If C. chinensis CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R dsRNA feeding produces comparable systemic knockdown in newly hatched 1st instar nymphs, the planned morphological transition percentage could be confounded by failure to molt, reduced survival, or abnormal cuticle formation rather than a specific summer-to-winter polyphenism shift. A second, more conceptual contradiction is from [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4), which found that Burs a2a and Burs b2b homodimers induced antimicrobial peptide genes and activated Relish in isolated larval fat body without requiring the heterodimer receptor DLGR2. This challenges the assumption that CcBurs-R knockdown will fully recapitulate CcBurs-α or CcBurs-β knockdown, or that the receptor is the sole mediator of Bursicon signaling, although the evidence is from Drosophila immunity rather than cuticle tanning or seasonal polyphenism. The weakest contradiction is the absence of direct quantitative or seasonal evidence in all three studies: none reports C. chinensis, 1st-instar dsRNA feeding, 25°C versus 10°C, qRT-PCR fold-changes for CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R, pigment extraction values, chitin assay values, TEM thickness measurements, or statistical significance for morph transition percentage.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a pilot RNAi feasibility and dose-response experiment before the main seasonal assay, because [(Loveall, and Deitcher, 2010-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c24d7e6bc15939026333be976ca99b64a6b60bfa) shows bursicon RNAi can obstruct ecdysis and [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) does not specify dsRNA dose, delivery route, replicate number, or statistical tests. The pilot should test multiple dsRNA doses and feeding intervals in newly hatched 1st instar C. chinensis nymphs, include dsEGFP and non-targeting controls, measure survival, molting success, body size, and qRT-PCR knockdown at 3, 6, and 10 days post-treatment with validated reference genes, and select a dose that reduces CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R transcripts without preventing nymphal development. Second, the main experiment should explicitly combine RNAi with the planned 25°C versus 10°C treatments and use the planned n=3 biological replicates with at least 50 individuals per replicate, Student's t-test for pairwise comparisons, and ANOVA with Tukey's HSD for multiple comparisons, reporting qRT-PCR fold-changes, cuticle pigment absorbance or concentration, chitin content, TEM cuticle thickness from defined sections, and morph transition percentage with confidence intervals. Third, because [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) linked bursicon knockdown to structural cuticle proteins and melanization/sclerotization enzymes, the method should add qRT-PCR or RNA-seq validation of downstream cuticle and melanization genes, including CcTre1 and CcCHS1 from the research plan, to connect pigment, chitin, and thickness changes to a molecular cascade. Fourth, because [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4) demonstrated both heterodimer and homodimer signaling and receptor-independent activity, the rescue and protein interaction experiments should test CcBurs-α/CcBurs-β heterodimers and relevant homodimers, validate CcBurs-R expression and localization in the relevant tissue, and include receptor rescue or overexpression controls to distinguish receptor-dependent from receptor-independent effects. Finally, the method should specify technical details for reproducibility: dsRNA length and concentration, feeding protocol, qRT-PCR primers and reference genes, pigment extraction solvent and spectrophotometric wavelengths, chitin assay method, TEM sectioning and thickness measurement region, and statistical handling of missing or non-molting individuals.

## To establish the candidate Bursicon genes in C. chinensis: We will identify and characterize CcBurs-α and CcBurs-β as the two subunits of Bursicon in C. chinensis, along with their receptor CcBurs-R. We will use bioinformatics analysis, sequence alignment, phylogenetic analysis, and protein structure prediction to validate these genes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed C. chinensis gene-identification method comes from [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1), which used cDNA synthesis, cloning, sequencing, multiple sequence alignment, phylogenetic analysis, and RNA-seq to identify honeybee bursicon α, bursicon β, and receptor sequences and compare them with putative arthropod orthologues; this directly mirrors the proposed use of sequence alignment, phylogenetics, and bioinformatic validation for CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R, and its 20E-conditioned expression plus dsRNA knockdown of AmBurs α/β affecting adult cuticle formation supports the plan's later RNAi and cuticle endpoints, although the supplied summary does not specify sequencing platform, alignment or phylogenetic software, dsRNA dose, or sample sizes. This newer work also extends and supports the older honeybee result of [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc), which used FGENESH reannotation, bursicon-specific RT-PCR from larval total RNA, M-MLV reverse transcription with random hexamers at 37°C, RNase H treatment, pCR2.1 TOPO cloning, and DNA sequencing to recover two separate ORFs with secretory signals, rejecting a single fusion ORF and supporting a heterodimeric Bursicon organization; that evidence directly supports the proposed assumption that C. chinensis Bursicon should be characterized as two subunits rather than one multidomain protein, although it does not validate CcBurs-R and reports no qRT-PCR values, biological replicate counts, statistical tests, or functional assays. The third line of support is [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460), which identified CasBurs α and β in a crab, produced recombinant subunits and a heterodimer, and used RIA, Western blotting, and RP-HPLC to track expression in TGC/PO and hemolymph across the molt cycle, with qualitative associations to cuticle thickening/hardening and hemocyte recruitment/granulation; this supports the proposed heterodimer model and the plan's HEK293T protein-expression/rescue logic, but it is less directly relevant to the current method because it is crustacean, does not report C. chinensis, CcBurs-R, or the proposed alignment/phylogenetic/structure-prediction workflow, and the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance values.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that none of the related work provides direct C. chinensis evidence: [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) is honeybee, [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) is honeybee, and [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) is crab, so the proposed CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R candidates, their seasonal summer/winter expression, and their role in CcTRPM/low-temperature polyphenism remain unvalidated. A second limitation is that the older validation evidence is qualitative and under-quantified: [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) reported no qRT-PCR values, biological replicate counts, statistical tests, or functional assays, and [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) supplied no quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or significance values in the excerpt, so they cannot by themselves justify the plan's statistical framework of n=3, 50 individuals, Student's t-test, ANOVA, and Tukey HSD. A third gap is receptor-specific evidence: [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) identified a receptor and ecdysteroid-conditioned expression but the supplied summary does not show receptor knockdown, ligand-binding, or receptor-rescue data, while [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) and [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) do not address the receptor, so CcBurs-R validation is weaker than subunit validation. Finally, the related work does not support the proposed protein-structure-prediction or miRNA-targeting components: none of the three papers reports structure prediction, miRanda/Targetscan, dual-luciferase, RIP, FISH, agomir/antagomir, or temperature-specific Bursicon regulation, so those parts of the plan are extrapolations rather than evidence-backed steps.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the C. chinensis validation direct and quantitative before interpreting seasonal function: use the C. chinensis genome/transcriptome to predict CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R with explicit domain criteria such as cystine knot, secretory signal, and receptor leucine-rich or ligand-binding domains, then validate by RT-PCR/qRT-PCR across tissues, developmental stages, summer-form and winter-form, and 25°C vs 10°C time points, using the plan's n=3 and 50-individual replicates and t-test/ANOVA/Tukey HSD, because the related work shows the need for stronger quantification beyond the qualitative validation in [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) and [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460). Next, strengthen the bioinformatic validation by reporting alignment scores, orthology evidence, phylogenetic bootstrap or ULS support, outgroups, and structure-prediction confidence metrics, following the alignment/phylogenetic logic of [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) but adding the structure-prediction step that the related work lacks. Third, validate CcBurs-R functionally rather than only by sequence: include receptor RNAi, expression profiling, and, if feasible, cell-based ligand-binding or co-immunoprecipitation/rescue with recombinant heterodimer, extending the receptor identification in [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) and the recombinant heterodimer/cuticle evidence in [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460). Fourth, connect Bursicon manipulation to the plan's phenotypic endpoints by measuring cuticle pigment, chitin, TEM thickness, transition percentage, and CcTre1/CcCHS1 expression after RNAi, using the honeybee knockdown/cuticle evidence of [(Costa et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6825b2c91813dac9f2a92fe49b9d9515730311a1) and the crab cuticle-hardening evidence of [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) as baselines. Finally, treat the miRNA and CcTRPM/temperature components as exploratory: add target-site conservation, multiple prediction tools, negative controls, and CcTRPM knockdown/rescue or temperature time-course controls, because none of the related work directly supports miRNA targeting of Bursicon or low-temperature regulation of Bursicon in C. chinensis.

## To link the known low-temperature cue to Bursicon regulation: We will expose newly hatched 1st instar nymphs of summer-form to different temperature treatments (25°C vs 10°C) and measure the expression of Bursicon genes at 3, 6, and 10 days post-treatment to determine temperature responsiveness.

### Support

The strongest support from the related work is that newly hatched first instar nymphs are experimentally tractable and phenotypically responsive to low-temperature exposure, which is the same life-stage and general temperature-manipulation logic used in the proposed Bursicon time course. [(Keena et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6994432cc817231326f1be8ba3909721852ceb01) reported that first instar duration was significantly longer by 6 days under an alternating 20/5°C low-temperature treatment than under constant 15°C (F = 21.08, df = 1, 15, p < 0.0001), demonstrating that low temperature can measurably alter early nymphal development within a short post-hatch window. This supports the proposed plan’s assumption that 10°C exposure of newly hatched summer-form first instars will produce a biologically meaningful developmental state that could be reflected in Bursicon expression. A secondary but still relevant support is the experimental infrastructure: the study used 45 egg masses each from New Jersey and Pennsylvania, reared first instars in groups of 50–100 or 300–500, maintained 14:10 light:dark, 65% relative humidity, and mostly 25°C with some cages at 20–22°C, and included population as a random effect in gamma-distribution mixed models. These details support the feasibility of controlled first-instar rearing and the importance of replication, environmental standardization, and statistical adjustment for biological variation, all of which are relevant to the proposed qRT-PCR comparison of 25°C versus 10°C. The weakest support is conceptual rather than mechanistic: the related work provides a phenotypic analogue for temperature responsiveness in early instars, but it did not measure Bursicon, CcTRPM, miRNAs, cuticle pigmentation, or seasonal-form transition, so it cannot directly validate the proposed molecular endpoint.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the proposed method, but the strongest limitation is that the only available evidence does not test the proposed molecular mechanism. [(Keena et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6994432cc817231326f1be8ba3909721852ceb01) measured instar duration, survival, and molt timing in spotted lanternfly, not Bursicon gene expression, receptor expression, RNAi, qRT-PCR, or seasonal polyphenism in C. chinensis, so it cannot support the specific hypothesis that low temperature regulates CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R. A second, more methodologically important contradiction to the proposed sampling design is that low temperature prolonged first instar duration by 6 days, which means that fixed calendar time points at 3, 6, and 10 days post-treatment may not represent equivalent developmental stages between 25°C and 10°C. If 10°C slows development, Bursicon expression may be delayed rather than absent, and a fixed-day time course could misinterpret a stage-shifted response as a temperature effect. A third limitation is that the related work used constant 15°C and an alternating 20/5°C regime rather than the proposed constant 25°C versus 10°C treatments, so the magnitude, timing, and survival consequences of the proposed treatments remain unvalidated. Finally, the related work used Kaplan-Meier product-limit estimates and gamma-distribution mixed models with population as a random effect, whereas the proposed plan relies primarily on Student's t-test and ANOVA with Tukey's HSD; if low temperature increases mortality or developmental asynchrony, those simpler tests may be less appropriate than survival-aware or mixed-effects analyses.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a pilot temperature-exposure phase before the Bursicon expression time course to confirm that 25°C and 10°C are viable, to quantify first-instar duration and survival at each temperature, and to adjust the 3, 6, and 10-day sampling schedule to stage-matched developmental windows or to extend the time course if 10°C delays development. Based on the 6-day prolongation reported in [(Keena et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6994432cc817231326f1be8ba3909721852ceb01), the proposed design should not assume that day 10 at 10°C is developmentally equivalent to day 10 at 25°C; instead, it should include additional time points, developmental staging, or a second cohort sampled at matched instar progression. The next priority is to strengthen replication and statistical analysis by treating egg mass, population, or cage as a random effect and by using survival analysis or mixed models if mortality or censored molts occur, rather than relying only on t-tests and ANOVA. The plan should also increase biological replication beyond n=3 with 50 individuals per replicate where feasible, because the related work used 45 egg masses per population and large group sizes, indicating that population-level variation can be substantial. Additional improvements include adding a fluctuating low-temperature treatment such as 20/5°C as a biologically relevant comparator, standardizing humidity and photoperiod, monitoring RNA integrity and reference-gene stability, and measuring CcTRPM expression alongside Bursicon to directly link the temperature receptor to the proposed Bursicon response.

## To define baseline seasonal expression differences: We will analyze temporal and spatial expression patterns of CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R across different developmental stages and tissues using qRT-PCR. We will compare expression levels between summer-form and winter-form individuals to establish baseline differences.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed baseline seasonal qRT-PCR method is that [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) demonstrated, in honeybee larvae, that bursicon and bursicon-beta can be recovered as distinct RNA-to-cDNA products using total RNA isolation, first-strand cDNA synthesis with M-MLV reverse transcriptase and random hexamer priming at 37 C, RT-PCR, cloning into pCR2.1 TOPO, and DNA sequencing. As an earlier qualitative milestone in the iterative study of bursicon transcripts, this directly supports the premise that bursicon subunit transcripts are detectable in insect larval tissue and that a heterodimeric bursicon model is biologically plausible, which is relevant to the plan’s characterization of CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R. A weaker but still useful support is the methodological precedent for designing bursicon-specific primers from revised in silico gene predictions and validating amplified open reading frames by sequencing; this can be adapted to the proposed qRT-PCR workflow for CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R, especially for primer validation and transcript confirmation before quantitative seasonal profiling.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) did not perform qRT-PCR, reference-gene normalization, biological replicate quantification, or statistical comparison, so it cannot support the proposed method’s core claim of establishing baseline seasonal expression differences between summer-form and winter-form Cacopsylla chinensis. The next major limitation is population and experimental design: the study used honeybee larvae preserved in RNAlater, not summer-form or winter-form C. chinensis, and did not sample a defined panel of developmental stages or tissues, whereas the proposed method requires temporal and spatial expression profiling across developmental stages and tissues. A further limitation is that the reported outcome was qualitative amplification and sequencing of bursicon and bursicon-beta open reading frames, with no fold changes, expression rankings, seasonal-form comparisons, or metrics such as Cq, ΔCq, or ΔΔCq; therefore it does not provide evidence that the proposed qRT-PCR comparisons will be quantitatively informative or statistically robust.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to convert the qualitative RT-PCR precedent from [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) into a fully quantitative qRT-PCR design for CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R, using validated reference genes, no-RT and no-template controls, technical replicates, and the plan’s biological replicate structure (n=3 with at least 50 individuals per replicate), followed by Student’s t-test for pairwise summer/winter comparisons and ANOVA with Tukey’s HSD for multiple developmental-stage or tissue comparisons. The next priority is to define the sampling matrix explicitly: collect summer-form and winter-form individuals at matched developmental stages and relevant tissues, preserve RNA in RNAlater, perform on-column DNase treatment, and use random-primed cDNA synthesis as in the related work to reduce 3′ bias. Finally, before seasonal profiling, validate CcBurs-specific primers by melting-curve analysis and sequencing, and if transcript identity is uncertain, use the cloning/sequencing approach from [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) as a preliminary confirmation step for CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R.

## To test the proposed post-transcriptional regulation of Bursicon signaling by miRNAs: We will predict miRNAs targeting CcBurs-R using bioinformatics tools (miRanda and Targetscan), then validate target relationships through dual-luciferase reporter assays, RNA immunoprecipitation (RIP) assays, and fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH). We will also conduct functional studies using agomir and antagomir treatments.

### Support

The strongest and most current support comes from [(Zhu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d838fa4ab9dc44f57f2098e731689f3b2e00387), which used a nearly identical insect miRNA target-validation workflow: bioinformatic prediction of miRNAs targeting GSTu1 in P. xylostella, dual-luciferase reporter assays, RNA immunoprecipitation, FISH/RPA, miRNA injection, and Western blotting across susceptible and resistant populations designated SS, NIL, BL, and HK. This directly supports the proposed plan's use of miRanda and Targetscan predictions followed by dual-luciferase, RIP, FISH, and agomir/antagomir-like manipulation for CcBurs-R, because it demonstrates that the same assay chain can be applied to an insect target and can test whether a miRNA controls transcript accumulation or stability. The second strongest support is [(Peng et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d61d7b2be0ba16fb1783e84155efe477fea41b45), which used miRNA target prediction for miR-1-3p to nominate Bdtra and then assessed whether miR-1-3p controls Bdtra transcript accumulation in embryonic sex determination; although the excerpt does not specify prediction thresholds, reporter constructs, RIP, FISH, or agomir/antagomir details, it supports the general logic that computational target nomination should be followed by measurement of target transcript levels. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Hommers et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3e81168885ef189505b9f44ddccccc6b10d58c), which used luciferase reporter and promoter activity assays to show that hsa-miR-579-3p can regulate expression of anxiety-related candidate genes including SLC6A2 in a human panic disorder and trait anxiety context; this supports the utility of reporter-based assays for miRNA-mediated post-transcriptional regulation, but it is human, focuses on the MIR579 promoter SNP rs2910931 and candidate gene expression rather than direct 3'UTR target validation, and does not report RIP, FISH, agomir, or antagomir experiments for CcBurs-R. Chronologically, the related work forms an iterative progression: Hommers et al. 2018 provides older reporter-based evidence for miRNA regulation, Peng et al. 2020 extends the logic to target prediction and transcript accumulation, and Zhu et al. 2021 provides the newest and most methodologically complete support for the proposed integrated validation pipeline.

### Contradictions

The strongest evidence that contradicts or qualifies the proposed method comes from [(Zhu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d838fa4ab9dc44f57f2098e731689f3b2e00387): although the workflow is analogous, upregulation or downregulation of miR-8525-5p or miR-8530-5p alone did not affect GSTu1 expression, and lnc-GSTu1-AS enhanced GSTu1 stability by preventing miR-8525-5p-induced degradation, while miR-8530-5p was not involved in that lncRNA-mediated regulation. This directly challenges the proposed method's implicit assumption that agomir or antagomir treatment of predicted CcBurs-R-targeting miRNAs will produce a clear change in CcBurs-R mRNA or Bursicon signaling; if CcBurs-R is buffered by competing endogenous RNAs, lncRNAs, alternative target sites, or compensatory transcription, miRNA manipulation may yield null or weak phenotypes even when the miRNA-target interaction is real. The next caveat is that [(Peng et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d61d7b2be0ba16fb1783e84155efe477fea41b45) does not provide the prediction algorithm, input sequences, scoring thresholds, computational resources, or validation assays such as dual-luciferase, RIP, FISH, agomir, or antagomir, so it cannot support the proposed method's reliance on miRanda and Targetscan alone or on the full validation battery; it only shows that prediction can nominate a target whose transcript accumulation is then assessed, and as an older work it is qualified by the newer, more complex regulatory architecture revealed by Zhu et al. 2021. The weakest contradiction is that [(Hommers et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba3e81168885ef189505b9f44ddccccc6b10d58c) is a human genetics and reporter study of MIR579/SLC6A2 and does not provide evidence that miRNAs target the Bursicon receptor in Cacopsylla chinensis, nor does it report the insect-specific functional manipulations, RIP, FISH, or agomir/antagomir controls needed to confirm CcBurs-R regulation. Additionally, all three excerpts lack quantitative outcomes such as fold changes, p-values, sample sizes, effect sizes, replicate numbers, and statistical tests, so none provides direct statistical evidence that the proposed miRNA-CcBurs-R axis will be detectable with the planned n=3 and at least 50 individuals per replicate.

### Suggested Action

Most important, based on [(Zhu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d838fa4ab9dc44f57f2098e731689f3b2e00387), the method should not treat agomir or antagomir effects on CcBurs-R as the sole functional readout; it should add target-site mutation controls and a ceRNA/lncRNA screen for CcBurs-R, including dual-luciferase assays with wild-type and seed-mutant CcBurs-R 3'UTR, co-transfection of miR mimics or inhibitors, and tests for endogenous lncRNAs or transcripts that sequester the predicted miRNAs. Second, because [(Zhu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d838fa4ab9dc44f57f2098e731689f3b2e00387) and [(Peng et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d61d7b2be0ba16fb1783e84155efe477fea41b45) show that prediction and transcript accumulation are necessary but not sufficient, the bioinformatics step should be made transparent and insect-appropriate: report miRanda and Targetscan parameters, seed-match criteria, binding energy, 3'UTR conservation, expression correlation, software versions, and computational resources, and add at least one additional predictor or small RNA-seq/RNA-seq correlation for Cacopsylla chinensis, then rank candidates by multiple criteria rather than a single tool. Third, the validation assays should specify controls and quantitative outputs: dual-luciferase with Renilla normalization, negative miRNA controls, mutant target sites, biological and technical replicates; RIP with anti-Ago2 and input controls; FISH or RPA with probe specificity and negative tissue controls; and agomir/antagomir with scrambled controls, dose and time optimization, qRT-PCR for miRNA and CcBurs-R, Western blot or another protein-level assay, and phenotype readouts such as cuticle pigmentation, chitin content, cuticle thickness, and summer-to-winter transition percentage at 25°C and 10°C at 3, 6, and 10 days post-treatment. Fourth, the statistical plan should be strengthened beyond Student's t-test and ANOVA followed by Tukey's HSD by adding power analysis, multiple-testing correction for multiple miRNA candidates, mixed models for repeated measures or nested individuals, and reporting fold changes, p-values, effect sizes, and confidence intervals, because none of the related works provides quantitative benchmarks. Finally, the method should include rescue or epistasis tests linking miRNA manipulation to Bursicon signaling, such as co-treatment with CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, or CcBurs-R RNAi, heterodimer protein feeding, target-site mutant CcBurs-R rescue, and downstream measurement of CcTre1 and CcCHS1, so that a miRNA effect is interpreted as part of the seasonal polyphenism pathway rather than as an isolated post-transcriptional interaction.

## To demonstrate the structural basis of Bursicon signaling: We will express individual Bursicon subunits and co-express them in HEK293T cells, then use SDS-PAGE under reduced and non-reduced conditions to demonstrate homodimer and heterodimer formation.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4), which used Drosophila Burs a2a and Burs b2b recombinant subunits and compared reducing versus non-reducing SDS-PAGE, reporting bands consistent with Burs a2a/Burs a2a and Burs b2b/Burs b2b homodimers plus the Burs a2a/Burs b2b heterodimer; this is directly analogous to the proposed HEK293T co-expression of CcBurs-α and CcBurs-β and supports the feasibility of detecting disulfide-linked Bursicon dimers by SDS-PAGE. This newer protein-level result builds on the older transcript-level model of [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc), which used RT-PCR, FGENESH gene modeling, pCR2.1 TOPO cloning, and DNA sequencing of honeybee larval cDNA to show separate bursicon and bursicon subunit ORFs with signal sequences, supporting the research plan's premise that CcBurs-α and CcBurs-β should be treated as two secreted subunits rather than a single fusion protein, although it did not test protein dimerization. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460), which produced a recombinant crab CasBurs heterodimer and validated it by RP-HPLC, RIA, Western blotting, and a bioassay showing cuticle thickening/hardening and hemocyte recruitment, supporting the idea that a purified heterodimer can be functionally meaningful for the plan's rescue experiments. The weakest direct support is [(Zhang et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eedba163fc5a0de6b3b4a6cb49abb5f870ba4736), which used recombinant bursicon homodimers in Aedes aegypti to induce AMP gene expression via Relish2, supporting the biological relevance of homodimeric Bursicon but not providing structural SDS-PAGE evidence for the proposed assay.

### Contradictions

The strongest challenge is that the proposed method relies on reduced and non-reduced SDS-PAGE alone to demonstrate homodimer and heterodimer formation, but the closest precedent, [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4), reported only qualitative non-reducing bands and did not provide quantitative band intensities, exact molecular weights, subunit-specific detection, or statistical significance, so SDS-PAGE alone may not unambiguously distinguish CcBurs-α/CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β/CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-α/CcBurs-β if the subunits have similar sizes. The next strongest limitation is that none of the cited studies used HEK293T co-expression; [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4) used recombinant Drosophila subunits, [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) used recombinant crab CasBurs, and [(Zhang et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eedba163fc5a0de6b3b4a6cb49abb5f870ba4736) used recombinant Aedes bursicon, so the assumption that HEK293T cells will produce correctly disulfide-bonded, secreted, and detectable CcBurs dimers is an extrapolation that may be affected by host-specific folding, glycosylation, secretion, and tag placement. A further contradiction to the sufficiency of the method is that [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc) provides only transcript-level evidence from honeybee larvae, with RT-PCR and sequencing of two ORFs, and no protein-level dimerization data, so it cannot support the structural claim that the CcBurs subunits form homodimers or heterodimers. Finally, [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460) and [(Zhang et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eedba163fc5a0de6b3b4a6cb49abb5f870ba4736) provide functional evidence for heterodimeric or homodimeric Bursicon activity but do not report reduced/non-reduced SDS-PAGE dimer quantification, so they do not validate the proposed structural assay.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add subunit-specific detection to the SDS-PAGE plan, such as Western blotting with anti-CcBurs-α and anti-CcBurs-β antibodies or distinct epitope tags, under both reducing and non-reducing conditions, so that CcBurs-α/CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β/CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-α/CcBurs-β bands can be assigned rather than inferred from band position alone; this should include molecular-weight markers, gel image quantification, biological and technical replicates, and statistical comparison of dimer/monomer ratios. The next most important action is to optimize and control the HEK293T expression system by expressing each subunit alone and co-expressing them, collecting both cell lysate and conditioned medium, using disulfide-stabilizing or reducing/non-reducing sample preparation, and including negative controls such as empty vector, single subunit, scrambled co-expression, or unrelated secreted protein; if HEK293T yield or disulfide pairing is poor, the plan should include an alternative recombinant expression and refolding route analogous to [(An et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d13d2385da6e3e694e90b6909bb4e7bf070c18c4), [(Chung et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1de3b7fa921646bff68bf0f5d3fff66127d30460), or [(Zhang et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eedba163fc5a0de6b3b4a6cb49abb5f870ba4736). The plan should also validate the CcBurs-α and CcBurs-β ORFs, signal peptides, and predicted cystine knot domains before expression, following the transcript-level logic of [(Loy et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/588203316420b7498cd2fcff77e466689f9d95cc), and should complement SDS-PAGE with orthogonal dimerization assays such as co-immunoprecipitation, size-exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering, or mass spectrometry if available. Finally, because the research plan includes rescue experiments by feeding heterodimer proteins to RNAi-treated nymphs, the protein interaction study should be linked to purification and functional validation, using the purified CcBurs heterodimer to test cuticle pigmentation, chitin content, cuticle thickness, transition percentage, and downstream CcTre1/CcCHS1 expression, with quantitative outcomes, the planned n=3 replicates and at least 50 individuals per replicate, and appropriate statistical analysis.

## To connect Bursicon signaling to cuticle remodeling: We will examine the expression of chitin biosynthesis pathway genes (CcTre1 and CcCHS1) following Bursicon signaling manipulation to establish the downstream molecular cascade.

### Support

Although only one related work is provided, so it cannot be evaluated as a sequence of newer versus older studies, it functions as prior evidence for the proposed downstream pathway analysis. The strongest support is that [(An et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f24d785effb322183724deecf7469809923b6ddc) directly linked recombinant bursicon treatment to candidate chitin and trehalose biosynthesis genes in Drosophila, selecting CG10369 and CG10844 from the 3 h post-injection microarray for qPCR validation. This is conceptually analogous to the plan's measurement of CcTre1 and CcCHS1 after Bursicon signaling manipulation, because both studies use bursicon exposure as the perturbation and chitin/trehalose pathway genes as downstream readouts. A second, weaker but still relevant support is the use of a short time course, 1 h and 3 h after r-bursicon injection, which supports the general feasibility of detecting rapid transcriptional responses to bursicon and suggests that expression assays should include early time points in addition to the plan's 3, 6, and 10 day seasonal treatments. The weakest support is the validation workflow itself, DNA microarray followed by qPCR, which mirrors the plan's qRT-PCR approach for CcTre1 and CcCHS1 but does not by itself establish that the same genes are regulated in C. chinensis or that the response is tied to seasonal polyphenism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the provided excerpt does not report fold changes, p-values, qPCR validation outcomes, or statistical significance for CG10369, CG10844, or the other selected genes, so it cannot confirm that bursicon actually up- or down-regulates chitin/trehalose biosynthesis genes in a reproducible quantitative manner. A second contradiction is taxonomic and phenotypic mismatch: the study used Drosophila CG nomenclature and neck-ligated flies, not C. chinensis summer-form or winter-form nymphs, and did not test CcTre1, CcCHS1, CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, CcBurs-R, CcTRPM, low temperature, or seasonal transition percentage. A third contradiction is mechanistic: recombinant bursicon injection bypasses endogenous subunit production, receptor binding, and RNAi-sensitive signaling, whereas the proposed plan manipulates CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R by dsRNA feeding and then measures downstream genes; therefore the related work does not demonstrate that receptor-mediated bursicon signaling, rather than exogenous hormone exposure, controls CcTre1 and CcCHS1. A fourth limitation is reproducibility: the excerpt lacks species, sample sizes, control treatment, microarray platform, normalization method, statistical thresholds, qPCR efficiency, and biological replicate details, so it cannot be used to justify the plan's n=3 with 50 individuals per replicate, Student's t-test, or ANOVA with Tukey's HSD for this specific downstream assay.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the downstream pathway analysis explicitly C. chinensis-specific and quantitatively validated: first confirm that CcTre1 and CcCHS1 are orthologous or functionally analogous to the Drosophila chitin/trehalose-related genes CG10369 and CG10844 reported by [(An et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f24d785effb322183724deecf7469809923b6ddc), then measure them by qRT-PCR with validated primers, reference genes, qPCR efficiency, and the plan's biological replicates and statistical tests. The second priority is to align the time course with both the related work and the seasonal plan: include early acute time points, for example 1 h and 3 h after bursicon manipulation or rescue, alongside the planned 3, 6, and 10 day post-treatment samples, because the related work detected candidate genes at 3 h and the seasonal transition may require longer integration. The third priority is to test receptor-mediated signaling rather than only exogenous hormone effects: measure CcTre1 and CcCHS1 after RNAi of CcBurs-α, CcBurs-β, and CcBurs-R, after dsEGFP control, and after heterodimer rescue, so that expression changes can be attributed to Bursicon signaling rather than nonspecific feeding or stress. The fourth priority is to broaden the downstream screen, because the related work selected 14 genes at 1 h and 13 genes at 3 h, indicating that a two-gene assay may miss additional chitin, trehalose, cuticle remodeling, or melanization genes; RNA-seq or microarray of RNAi- and rescue-treated nymphs would strengthen the cascade. Finally, integrate the molecular readouts with the plan's phenotypic assays, chitin content, cuticle thickness, pigmentation, and transition percentage, and report effect sizes and significance for each gene and phenotype to avoid overinterpreting expression changes as proof of seasonal polyphenism.

## To support quantitative comparisons across treatments: Statistical analysis will be performed using Student's t-test for pairwise comparisons and ANOVA followed by Tukey's HSD for multiple comparisons.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Lupo et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70880cfac4fe9481b6f58c3e86bbce22754aa7d5) used the same inferential-statistics logic proposed for the Bursicon plan: Student's t-test for specific pairwise comparisons and ANOVA followed by Tukey HSD for multiple comparisons. In that study, one-way ANOVA tested leaf-age effects within each variety-treatment combination, two-way ANOVA tested leaf-age by treatment and variety-treatment effects, and Tukey HSD was used for post hoc pairwise comparisons in R 4.2.1. The experiment had two grapevine rootstocks, 1103P and SO4, 9 replicates per rootstock-treatment combination, 3 blocks, 20-L sand-filled pots, 33% leaching fraction, 16.7% Hoagland solution with 40 ppm nitrogen, and salinity treatments of 0.7 dS m-1 control and 4 dS m-1 with 30 mM NaCl. Using this framework, the authors detected meaningful quantitative differences, including photosynthesis significantly correlated with leaf age in both rootstocks (P < 0.01), a 16.7% photosynthesis decline and 13.85% PSII efficiency decline between 28 and 42 days, 28% higher daily intrinsic water-use efficiency in SO4 under control, 57% and 65% age-associated stomatal conductance declines in 1103P and SO4, and 32% lower stomatal conductance and 37% lower photosynthesis in old versus young 1103P leaves. This directly supports the proposed use of t-tests and ANOVA/Tukey for comparing treatment, control, and multi-factor groups in the Bursicon research plan. A second, slightly weaker support is that the related work's factorial structure—variety, treatment, leaf age, and diurnal time—maps onto the proposed insect design, which also has multiple fixed factors such as 25 C vs 10 C, 3/6/10 days post-treatment, RNAi targets CcBurs-alpha, CcBurs-beta, CcBurs-R, and dsEGFP, and summer-form versus winter-form comparisons. A third, weaker support is implementation detail: the related work specified R 4.2.1 and standard post hoc testing, which supports the proposed plan's need to specify software and statistical procedures, although the plant gas-exchange, ion, and osmolality measurements themselves are not directly transferable to insect molecular assays.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Lupo et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70880cfac4fe9481b6f58c3e86bbce22754aa7d5) contains an internally inconsistent statistical statement: two-way ANOVA for midday osmolality was reported as showing no significant variety differences while the text also states P < 0.05. This warns that the proposed method's generic phrase, Student's t-test for pairwise comparisons and ANOVA followed by Tukey's HSD for multiple comparisons, is insufficient unless the Bursicon plan pre-specifies hypotheses, comparison families, exact P values, and consistency checks; otherwise conclusions may contradict reported statistics. A second contradiction is replication and power: the related work used 9 replicates per rootstock-treatment combination and 3 blocks, whereas the proposed plan states n=3 with at least 50 individuals per replicate. For the proposed multi-factor insect experiments—temperature by time, RNAi target by form, rescue, miRNA agomir/antagomir, and downstream CcTre1/CcCHS1 expression—n=3 biological replicates is likely underpowered for interaction effects and multiple endpoints, especially if the 50 individuals are pooled or treated as technical replicates rather than independent biological units. A third contradiction is endpoint mismatch: the proposed plan implies t-test/ANOVA for all quantitative comparisons, but transition percentage from summer-form to winter-form is a proportion/binomial outcome, qRT-PCR expression often requires log2 fold-change and reference-gene validation, TEM cuticle thickness may involve repeated measurements per specimen, and dual-luciferase, RIP, FISH, agomir, and antagomir assays may require different models. The related work used t-tests only for specific pairwise comparisons within a defined variety-leaf age combination or between varieties, not as a default for all outcomes. A fourth, weaker contradiction is that the related work's plant physiological context—LI-6800 gas exchange at 400 ppm CO2, 1000 umol m-2 s-1 PFD, 35 C, and 50% RH, ICP-OES sodium after 0.5 M nitric acid extraction, elemental analysis, and vapor pressure osmometry—does not directly validate the insect molecular assay setup, so its support is limited to the statistical framework rather than the biological measurements.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace the generic statistical statement with endpoint-specific, pre-registered models: define the biological unit (individual nymph, replicate pool, TEM section, or assay well), randomization/blocking, fixed effects (temperature, time, RNAi target, form, treatment), interactions, and random/repeated effects; use two-way or mixed ANOVA/linear mixed models for continuous endpoints, binomial/logistic regression or chi-square/Fisher exact tests for transition percentage, and appropriate models for qRT-PCR (delta-delta Ct, log2 fold change, reference gene validation), TEM (nested/repeated measures), and miRNA assays. Second, increase or justify replication and conduct a power analysis: the related work's 9 replicates per treatment combination and 3 blocks is a useful benchmark, and the proposed n=3 should be expanded or the 50 individuals per replicate analyzed as independent biological units if justified; report expected effect sizes based on comparable biological differences, such as the 16.7% photosynthesis decline, 28% water-use efficiency difference, and 32-37% leaf-age differences reported in [(Lupo et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70880cfac4fe9481b6f58c3e86bbce22754aa7d5), and set alpha, power, and minimum detectable effect. Third, specify multiple-comparison control: use Tukey HSD only for planned all-pairwise comparisons within a factor, use Bonferroni/FDR for multiple endpoints or genes, and reserve Student's t-test for a small number of pre-specified pairwise control-versus-treatment comparisons with correction. Fourth, add assumption and reporting safeguards: test normality/homoscedasticity, apply transformations where needed, report exact P values, effect sizes, confidence intervals, software (e.g., R version and packages), and ensure conclusions match P values to avoid the inconsistency observed in [(Lupo et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70880cfac4fe9481b6f58c3e86bbce22754aa7d5). Fifth, include baseline and control details: compare against dsEGFP and untreated summer/winter forms, report RNAi efficiency before functional endpoints, and for rescue and miRNA studies include specificity and dose controls.
