## Context: Core generative model for architectural floor plans. Method: We will employ a customized adaptation of the U-Net architecture configured within a diffusion modeling framework. This adaptation will be geared towards capturing the nuanced requirements of architectural designs, including the layout of spaces and their functional relationships. We will utilize the UNet2DModel from the diffusers library with specific configurations including ResNet layers, progressive channel depths, and attention mechanisms.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed customized U-Net diffusion floor-plan method comes from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), which demonstrates that a diffusion-based HouseDiffusion model can generate vector floorplans from graph or bubble-diagram constraints and outperform House-GAN++ by 67% in diversity and 32% in compatibility, with a realism score of -0.19 versus -0.18 for the best House-GAN++ configuration, where the latter required expensive post-processing and filtered out incompatible samples that accounted for 90% of generated samples. This directly supports the plan's core assumption that a diffusion denoising objective is suitable for floor-plan generation and that architectural constraints can be learned, although it uses 60,000 RPLAN vector floorplans rendered at 256x256, room-count groups of 5, 6, 7, or 8 rooms with the target group excluded from training, AdamW for 250,000 steps with batch size 512 on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000, learning rate 1e-3 divided by 10 every 100,000 steps, and 1000 diffusion steps, all of which are larger or more explicit than the proposed 12,000-image, 128x128, batch-size-16, learning-rate-1e-4 setup. A further strong support is [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), whose Modified HouseDiffusion uses diffusion for floor-plan generation on the 5,372-plan MSD dataset containing 163.5K rooms and 18.5K units, with non-Manhattan room shapes, zoning labels, and door geometry, and incorporates wall-cross attention, room-category/door-type embeddings, and structural/zoning conditioning, aligning with the proposed UNet2DModel attention-enhanced blocks and architectural specialization. Although the excerpt does not report MHD's numerical MIoU, compatibility, topology, or proportion scores, its evaluation design using mean IoU, graph compatibility with a 5-pixel buffer for 512-pixel images, topology, proportions, and an architect user study supports the idea that a diffusion U-Net can be made architecturally aware. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a), which uses a Pix2Pix-style U-Net encoder-decoder with skip connections to predict floorplan images from boundary and activity-map inputs on 80,000 RPLAN pairs split into 75,000 training, 2,500 validation, and 2,500 test examples, achieving normalized mutual information of 0.565 with minimum 0.434 and maximum 0.669, the lowest MSE and MAE among Pix2Pix, CycleGAN, and Floor-GAN, and vectorization success of 83 out of 100 compared with 27 out of 100 for Floor-GAN; this supports the U-Net spatial architecture and image-based floor-plan generation, but it is GAN-based and uses a much larger dataset. Additional support for the plan's low-resolution training plus upscaling comes from [(Weber et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615c8aae55d860f77c284faf92f485c8603f24dc), which notes that prior image-based floor-plan methods were constrained by 256x256 raster resolution, fuzzy outputs, postprocessing burden, and dataset quality, motivating a lower-resolution diffusion training stage plus upscaling and careful preprocessing. Quantitative but less domain-specific support for low-resolution-to-high-resolution refinement comes from [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921), whose stacked GAN first generates 64x64 images and then refines them to 256x256, achieving inception scores of 3.70 +/- 0.04 on CUB, 3.20 +/- 0.01 on Oxford-102, and 8.45 +/- 0.03 on MS COCO, with average human ranks of 1.37 +/- 0.02, 1.13 +/- 0.03, and 1.11 +/- 0.03 respectively; however, it is a text-to-image GAN, not a diffusion floor-plan model. Denoising-style learning of spatial regularities is supported by [(Wei et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ab2fc3fd557c9a8fcc449bc68cda17f80beb065), whose LEGO-Net achieved 91.2% to 97.2% success on synthetic symmetry, spacing, and grouping tasks using Langevin dynamics, and by the GAN/VAE reviews in [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) and [(Baduge et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53a7d4e0f7ee028911bc294d86b90d17243d5102), which show that floor-plan generation can be conditioned on room graphs, bubble diagrams, land shapes, and architectural constraints, although they do not provide quantitative evidence for diffusion U-Net training at 128x128.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8): the successful diffusion floor-plan system was trained on 60,000 RPLAN vector floorplans rendered at 256x256, used batch size 512, 250,000 AdamW steps, learning rate 1e-3 with decay, 1000 diffusion steps, and evaluated with quantitative diversity, compatibility, and human realism, whereas the proposed plan uses only 12,000 512x512 images, trains at 128x128 with batch size 16, learning rate 1e-4, 500-step warm-up, and relies primarily on qualitative visual inspection. This suggests the proposed data scale, training budget, and evaluation may be insufficient to demonstrate architectural accuracy, coherence, and aesthetics. A second strong contradiction is [(Leng et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04a0f3f39c5a6aeddcd2841457d0b500adfcb534), where the Imagen diffusion text-to-image baseline achieved only micro IoU 14.74 and macro IoU 15.57 under artificial instructions, 9.29 and 6.64 under human instructions, and 12.17 and 14.96 under warm-up plus fine-tuning, far below the specialized Seq2Seq T2D model's 54.34 and 53.30; this warns that diffusion image generation without task-specific architectural conditioning and quantitative overlap evaluation may produce imprecise floor plans, and no statistical significance tests are reported for these comparisons. A further contradiction is [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), which shows that state-of-the-art diffusion floor-plan work uses explicit structural and zoning conditioning, wall-cross attention, room/door embeddings, and combined visual/topological metrics such as MIoU, graph compatibility, topology, and proportions; the proposed method's lack of explicit room-graph, boundary, zoning, or door/window conditioning and its qualitative-only evaluation may miss topological errors that visual inspection alone cannot reliably detect. The review by [(Weber et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615c8aae55d860f77c284faf92f485c8603f24dc) also contradicts the assumed sufficiency of low-resolution raster generation by reporting that image-based methods at 256x256 produced fuzzy outputs requiring significant postprocessing, and that dataset curation strongly affects quality; training at 128x128 and then upscaling may therefore inherit or amplify low-resolution artifacts unless validated against direct high-resolution generation. [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a) further contradicts the proposed evaluation by using 80,000 RPLAN pairs, normalized mutual information of 0.565, MSE/MAE comparisons, vectorization success of 83 out of 100, and an architect user study, indicating that a 12,000-image qualitative-only study may be underpowered and less reproducible, especially because no statistical significance tests are reported for its user-study comparisons. Weaker contradictions include the absence of quantitative results in [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) and [(Baduge et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53a7d4e0f7ee028911bc294d86b90d17243d5102), which limits their ability to validate the proposed diffusion U-Net, the fact that [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921) validates low-resolution-to-high-resolution refinement only for GAN text-to-image synthesis with inception scores and human ranks rather than architectural floor plans, and the lack of direct evidence in the related work for the proposed exact UNet2DModel configuration of 2 ResNet layers per block and progressive channel depths of 128, 128, 256, 256, 512, and 512.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should add quantitative architectural evaluation before or alongside qualitative inspection, including pixel or segmentation IoU/MIoU for walls, rooms, doors, and windows, graph compatibility/topology metrics using extracted room adjacency graphs, validity heuristics, and possibly environmental or functional checks, as evidenced by [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), [(Leng et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04a0f3f39c5a6aeddcd2841457d0b500adfcb534), and [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a). This would make accuracy, coherence, and aesthetics measurable and comparable to baselines. Second, the model should be conditioned on explicit architectural metadata such as room graphs, bubble diagrams, zoning labels, boundary/wall structure, room counts, and door/window locations, using attention or embedding mechanisms similar to HouseDiffusion and Modified HouseDiffusion, rather than relying only on image-to-image denoising; this is supported by [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0), and [(Baduge et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53a7d4e0f7ee028911bc294d86b90d17243d5102). Third, the 12,000-image, 128x128, batch-size-16 training setup should be validated with ablations against larger or higher-resolution configurations, such as 256x256 or 512x512 direct training, progressive resolution training, or a super-resolution stage, because [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) used 60,000 samples at 256x256 with batch size 512, [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a) used 80,000 pairs, and [(Weber et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615c8aae55d860f77c284faf92f485c8603f24dc) warns that low-resolution raster floor plans can be fuzzy and require postprocessing. Fourth, the training hyperparameters should be benchmarked rather than fixed: compare the proposed learning rate 1e-4, 500-step warm-up, fp16, and batch size 16 against schedules such as AdamW with learning rate 1e-3 and decay, larger batch sizes, more diffusion steps, and longer training, while reporting hardware, seeds, and convergence curves, as in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) and [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a). Fifth, preprocessing should be tested for its effect on orientation and geometry: rotation alignment may improve consistency but should be compared with orientation-preserving conditioning, as [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) retains original orientation, and dataset standardization should include validity checks and curation criteria suggested by [(Weber et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/615c8aae55d860f77c284faf92f485c8603f24dc) and [(Weber et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/503356d4dfb2e35ca4e015d820d386fc2827b944). Finally, the user study should be expanded beyond direct visual inspection to include architects, paired comparisons, Elo or preference scores, and statistical significance tests, following the evaluation designs in [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a), [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), and [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb).

## Context: Efficient training and high-resolution output pipeline. Method: We will train the model at lower resolutions (128x128 pixels) to balance detail and computational efficiency, then employ post-generation upscaling techniques to achieve high-resolution outputs without sacrificing computational resources during training. We will integrate advanced upscaling techniques post-generation, allowing the model to operate efficiently at lower resolutions while maintaining high-resolution outputs, thus addressing the dual challenges of detail fidelity and computational efficiency.

### Support

The strongest support for training at 128x128 and then enhancing outputs is [(Ho et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f183bcfe65781c06b1a48a6f56e0f3c63e8e4a4), which directly studies cascaded class-conditional diffusion where a low-resolution base model generates z0 and a high-resolution SR diffusion model refines it; on ImageNet it reports 128x128 FID 3.52/3.76 and IS 128.80, and 256x256 FID 4.88/4.63 and IS 158.71, with 50k FID samples, 100 inference steps, and SR3-like SR models, showing that most modeling capacity can remain at lower resolution while a learned refinement stage reaches high resolution. This is more directly relevant than older generic SR work because it uses diffusion-generated low-resolution conditioning and demonstrates that conditioning augmentation is essential: a 16x16 to 64x64 cascade without augmentation degraded to FID 6.02/5.84, while non-truncated augmentation at s=1001 reached FID 2.13/2.79. Newer work extends this idea: [(Hwang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea7d472879de137bbb763eb9fb0ce6de187a90c3) proposes upsample guidance for pixel and latent diffusion, decomposing target-resolution noise into a trained low-resolution component plus residual and using a time-dependent guidance scale, which is a plausible training-free route from a 128x128-trained floor-plan model to 512x512, although the excerpt lacks quantitative floor-plan evidence; [(Teng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48329152cc038da5e573834123c43d20654b1b02) further supports low-resolution pretraining plus inference-time upscaling by pretraining DiM-Huge at 256x256, fine-tuning at 512x512 with FID-50K 3.94 using 15M samples, and producing 1024x1024/1536x1536 with upsample guidance on a single H800, though it does not test 128x128 or floor plans. Learned cascade guidance from [(Ren et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc7ed870fb23e1319c10553e266b3df6f8243cf3) also supports using a low-resolution stage to guide high-resolution latent synthesis; it reports preference percentages such as 97.8% and 99.4% against baselines and uses 8 A100 GPUs, batch size 64, 15,000 iterations, and low-resolution guidance corrupted at timestep 0.05, but the excerpt does not clearly map percentages to baselines, so it is weaker than the direct FID evidence in Ho. Domain-specific support comes from [(Khare et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc2525ac600518d71d75b0a42014564b17025a72), which applied stacked super-resolution to 90 low-resolution CubiCasa5k floor plans before a multitask detector and reported higher room detection accuracy than the detector alone, indicating that upscaling can improve floor-plan structure, although it did not test diffusion-generated images. Generic SR evidence is weaker but still relevant: [(Lim et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ba5d3808e117e7a68dc40331ce1d483ceeedcb2) shows EDSR/MDSR trained on DIV2K with L1 loss, no batch normalization, and geometric self-ensemble can improve x4 PSNR/SSIM over SRResNet on Urban100 and DIV2K validation images, e.g. EDSR+ 23.48 dB/0.6048 SSIM versus SRResNet 23.14 dB/0.5891 on Urban100 img034, providing candidate post-generation upscalers; [(Nair et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bcf3caa5dd02783e5943bb2b6994e8cb048e1b) similarly evaluates WDSR and EDSR on DIV2K and T91 but provides no numerical values in the excerpt, so it only weakly supports the feasibility of such upscalers. Computational-efficiency support is provided by [(Liu, and Ji, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/937b84e9544db2b64919d88d9db6eb60bee3a569), which trained RED-Net on 768x384 crops from 5376x5376 aerial images and achieved at least 50% MAE improvement over R-MVSNet on WHU, showing that smaller training inputs can reduce memory while retaining strong performance, but it uses direct full-resolution inference rather than post-generation upscaling. Finally, [(Harichandana et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3287554b694fa6282fa64d007f1ec493b5bf39a6) supports the general principle that post-generation enhancement can improve no-reference quality, with local histogram equalization giving the lowest mean BRISQUE score of 23.77 on 18 true-color Landsat images, but it does not upscale resolution and is only weakly relevant to floor plans.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed plan's generic post-generation upscaling may suffer a train-test mismatch if the upscaler is trained on clean 512x512 floor plans but applied to 128x128 diffusion outputs. [(Ho et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f183bcfe65781c06b1a48a6f56e0f3c63e8e4a4) shows that cascaded diffusion without conditioning augmentation degraded from FID 2.13/2.79 with non-truncated augmentation to FID 6.02/5.84 without augmentation, and that conditioning on ground-truth low-resolution images degraded monotonically as augmentation timestep s increased from 0 to 1501, from FID 0.76/1.76 to FID 1.11/2.04, indicating that the low-resolution input distribution must be explicitly modeled. A second strong contradiction is domain-specific: [(Khare et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc2525ac600518d71d75b0a42014564b17025a72) found that super-resolution improved room detection on 90 CubiCasa5k floor plans but caused a slight drop in icon detection, increased post-processing time when many icon points were detected, and could produce polygon splitting where polygons fail to join into the wall skeleton, which directly threatens the plan's accuracy and coherence criteria for walls, doors, windows, and room flow. Newer diffusion-native work also contradicts the assumption that a standalone external upscaler is sufficient: [(Hwang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea7d472879de137bbb763eb9fb0ce6de187a90c3) requires a time-dependent guidance scale and, for latent diffusion, reduced guidance near t=0 to remove artifacts, while [(Teng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48329152cc038da5e573834123c43d20654b1b02) achieves high-resolution outputs by combining 512x512 fine-tuning with upsample guidance rather than relying solely on a separate post-generation module, and [(Ren et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc7ed870fb23e1319c10553e266b3df6f8243cf3) uses learned low-resolution guidance, scale-aware normalization, and cascade diffusion rather than a generic upscaler, reporting preference percentages such as 97.8% and 99.4% against baselines but without clear metric mapping in the excerpt. Older generic SR evidence is less directly contradictory but limits transferability: [(Lim et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ba5d3808e117e7a68dc40331ce1d483ceeedcb2) and [(Nair et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bcf3caa5dd02783e5943bb2b6994e8cb048e1b) evaluate natural-image SR on DIV2K, Set5, Set14, B100, Urban100, or T91 using PSNR/SSIM/MSE, and neither demonstrates preservation of architectural line fidelity, junction connectivity, or semantic room correctness. Weaker contradictions include [(Liu, and Ji, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/937b84e9544db2b64919d88d9db6eb60bee3a569), which reduces training size by using 768x384 sub-units but infers full-resolution depth directly rather than validating post-generation upscaling, and [(Harichandana et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3287554b694fa6282fa64d007f1ec493b5bf39a6), which shows post-processing can increase processing time and does not increase spatial resolution, so it cannot by itself justify the plan's high-resolution output claim.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace the vague plan to use advanced upscaling techniques with a learned diffusion-native enhancement stage that conditions on the generated 128x128 floor plan and is trained or regularized to expect diffusion-generated low-resolution inputs, following [(Ho et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f183bcfe65781c06b1a48a6f56e0f3c63e8e4a4) by adding conditioning augmentation such as forward-diffusion noise or blur to the low-resolution conditioning, and by testing truncated versus non-truncated augmentation and timestep s. Equally important is to evaluate floor-plan-specific semantic and geometric metrics rather than only visual inspection, including wall/room/door/window detection, junction-point accuracy, polygon connectivity, self-intersection rate, and downstream CubiCasa5k-style detection, because [(Khare et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc2525ac600518d71d75b0a42014564b17025a72) shows super-resolution can improve room detection but degrade icon detection and increase post-processing failures. The plan should benchmark multiple upscaling routes against direct 512x512 training: generic SR such as EDSR/MDSR or WDSR from [(Lim et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ba5d3808e117e7a68dc40331ce1d483ceeedcb2) and [(Nair et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bcf3caa5dd02783e5943bb2b6994e8cb048e1b), diffusion SR/cascade from [(Ho et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f183bcfe65781c06b1a48a6f56e0f3c63e8e4a4), training-free upsample guidance from [(Hwang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea7d472879de137bbb763eb9fb0ce6de187a90c3) and [(Teng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48329152cc038da5e573834123c43d20654b1b02), and learned low-resolution guidance from [(Ren et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc7ed870fb23e1319c10553e266b3df6f8243cf3), reporting FID or learned no-reference metrics, PSNR/SSIM against 512x512 ground truth where available, wall/room accuracy, inference time, and GPU memory for the planned batch size 16, fp16, 1e-4 learning rate, and 500-step warm-up U-Net. If resources allow, consider a small 512x512 fine-tuning or latent-space refinement stage, since [(Teng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48329152cc038da5e573834123c43d20654b1b02) used 512 fine-tuning with 15M samples to reach FID-50K 3.94 and [(Ren et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc7ed870fb23e1319c10553e266b3df6f8243cf3) used 8 A100 GPUs, batch size 64, 15,000 iterations, and low-resolution guidance corrupted at timestep 0.05, suggesting that pure 128x128 training may need additional high-resolution adaptation. Finally, account for computational and post-processing costs, as [(Liu, and Ji, 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/937b84e9544db2b64919d88d9db6eb60bee3a569) shows smaller training crops can reduce memory but full-resolution inference can still be expensive, and [(Harichandana et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3287554b694fa6282fa64d007f1ec493b5bf39a6) shows enhancement can increase processing time; the plan should report per-image latency for 128x128 generation, upscaling, and any geometric post-processing, and use statistical significance or confidence intervals across the 12,000-image dataset rather than only qualitative judgments.

## Context: Training data source. Method: We will use a comprehensive dataset containing 12,000 architectural floor plan images at 512x512 pixel resolution, including distinct images for walls, room segmentation, and overall floor plans with descriptive metadata. This dataset size provides sufficient diversity for reliable model training compared to smaller alternatives we explored.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), which trained a graph-constrained diffusion model on 60,000 vector floorplans rendered at 256x256, using a single NVIDIA RTX 6000, batch size 512, 250,000 steps, Adam with decoupled weight decay, initial learning rate 1e-3, learning-rate division by 10 every 100,000 steps, 1,000 diffusion steps, and uniform timestep sampling, and showed that diffusion can generate floorplans with correct room sets, cleaner walls, and better diversity and compatibility than House-GAN++ and House-GAN, with average diversity improvement of 67% and compatibility improvement of 32% over House-GAN++ and a realism score of -0.19 versus -0.18 for a heavily post-processed House-GAN++ variant that filtered about 90% of samples. This directly supports the plan's central choice of a diffusion U-Net for architectural floorplan generation and its emphasis on wall and room structure, although Shabani uses graph constraints and direct 256x256 generation rather than 128x128 training with upscaling. A newer and complementary support is [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), which curates MSD from Swiss Dwellings into image, vector, graph, binary structural-component, room-type, zoning, and door-geometry forms, showing that floor-plan datasets with structural metadata and controlled near-duplicates are suitable for generation-oriented tasks; after removing 2,305 non-residential plans, 4,395 near-duplicates, 1,541 small or insufficiently zoned plans, and 388 additional cases, MSD retains 5,372 plans with 163.5K rooms, 18.5K units, average 8.68 corners per room, 8.75 rooms per unit, 3.52 units per floor, graph entropy 8.02, and near-duplicate rate below 1%, supporting the proposed 512x512 wall, room, and overall modalities and metadata even though MSD is smaller than the proposed 12,000-image set. Earlier recognition work also supports the resolution and learning-rate choices: [(Zeng et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99ef26d08cb55564130b168785e07ab9e33d7256) trained a multi-task 512x512 floorplan recognition network on an NVIDIA TITAN Xp with 40,000 iterations, Adam at fixed 1e-4, batch size 1, no batch normalization, and evaluation every five epochs, demonstrating that 512x512 preserves thin wall lines and that shared multi-task room-boundary and room-type features with a spatial contextual module improve recognition, which aligns with the plan's 512x512 dataset, 1e-4 learning rate, and wall and room segmentation modalities. [(Kalervo et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da519b06b25de4d99d817c592486fe5eaac5fba3) provides a smaller but directly relevant floorplan benchmark, CubiCasa5K, with 5,000 annotated raster scans, SVG room, wall, icon, and geometric annotations, and a multi-task CNN trained for 400 epochs, batch size 20, Adam initial learning rate 1e-3, about 3 hours on an Nvidia GeForce GTX TitanX, with 90-degree rotation, color jitter, and random crop or scaling to 256x256, showing that rotation test-time augmentation and wall/room parsing are useful, although it is a parsing pipeline rather than diffusion generation. The foundational U-Net evidence from [(Ronneberger et al., 2015-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6364fdaa0a0eccd823a779fcdd489173f938e91a) supports the use of a U-Net backbone and augmentation or rotation invariance for structured 2D segmentation, with 512x512 electron microscopy images, warping error 0.0003529, Rand error 0.0382, and 92% average IoU on PhC-U373, but it does not validate diffusion generation or architectural semantics. Finally, [(Chen, and Pan, 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd8e681b5137fd1835b3f93c293d092b1bf90769) and [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7026ec2374b04a5c7f227389b679ffea7a4302) provide weaker but relevant support for 512x512 floor-plan preprocessing and U-Net-style backbones: Chen and Pan use 268,035 training plans resized to 512x512 for graph-based line-segment parsing with junction sAP2J 76.64±0.16, sAP4J 87.97±0.07, and sAP8J 89.38±0.02, while Huang et al. report improved wall category accuracy on CubiCasa5K with U-Net and MURA variants, although neither addresses diffusion generation, metadata conditioning, or upscaling.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8): its successful diffusion floorplan generation used 60,000 vector floorplans, graph constraints specifying required room types and adjacency relationships, 256x256 direct generation, 1,000 diffusion steps, batch size 512 on a single RTX 6000, and quantitative diversity, compatibility, and human realism evaluation, whereas the proposed plan relies on only 12,000 images, 128x128 training with post-generation upscaling, and qualitative visual inspection; this makes the plan's claim of reliable training and professional-grade output less certain, especially for room-count diversity, adjacency compatibility, and wall fidelity, and the paper reports no statistical significance tests. A newer contradiction is [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), which shows that dataset quality is not merely size: after removing 2,305 non-residential plans, 4,395 near-duplicates, 1,541 small or insufficiently zoned plans, and 388 additional cases, MSD retains 5,372 plans with graph entropy 8.02 and near-duplicate rate below 1%, compared with RPLAN's 4.56 entropy and 70% near-duplicate rate and LIFULL's 7.79 entropy and 50% near-duplicate rate; if the proposed 12,000-image set has not been deduplicated, orientation-normalized, or characterized by room-count and graph diversity, its apparent size may overstate usable diversity. The 128x128 training choice is contradicted by [(Zeng et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99ef26d08cb55564130b168785e07ab9e33d7256), which explicitly used 512x512 inputs to preserve thin wall lines and reported that shared multi-task recognition improves boundary and room-type performance; training at 128x128 risks losing thin walls, door and window details, and small room labels that upscaling may not faithfully recover, and its batch size 1 and no-normalization setting is not directly comparable to the plan's batch size 16 and mixed-precision U-Net. [(Chen, and Pan, 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd8e681b5137fd1835b3f93c293d092b1bf90769) further suggests that structural floor-plan understanding benefits from much larger annotated data, 268,035 training plans with endpoints, line categories, room contours, and mm-per-pixel scale, and that line-segment and junction detection requires precise 512x512 evaluation with structural average precision thresholds; the proposed 12,000-image dataset may be adequate for coarse generation but is less clearly sufficient for accurate wall, door, and window geometry. Weaker contradictions come from [(Kalervo et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da519b06b25de4d99d817c592486fe5eaac5fba3) and [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7026ec2374b04a5c7f227389b679ffea7a4302): CubiCasa5K is only 5,000 images and was used for parsing at 256x256 with 400 epochs, batch size 20, Adam initial learning rate 1e-3, and rotation, color, and crop augmentation, so it does not validate 12,000-image 512x512 diffusion training, and Huang et al. provide only relative accuracy improvements without numeric metrics, hardware, resolution, or generation evidence. The oldest U-Net paper [(Ronneberger et al., 2015-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6364fdaa0a0eccd823a779fcdd489173f938e91a) is the weakest contradiction because its small 30-image electron microscopy training set and segmentation-only metrics do not directly challenge floorplan generation, but it also does not support diffusion, metadata conditioning, or architectural coherence.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace or supplement qualitative visual inspection with quantitative architectural evaluation, following [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) by measuring room-count accuracy, room-type accuracy, adjacency compatibility, wall IoU or wall error, diversity, and human-rated realism, and by comparing the proposed 128x128-plus-upscaling pipeline against direct 256x256 or 512x512 diffusion generation and against House-GAN-style baselines. Second, make the metadata and wall and room segmentation modalities explicit conditioning signals rather than merely descriptive data, using graph or bubble-diagram constraints for room types and adjacencies as in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), and multi-task room-boundary and room-type supervision as in [(Zeng et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99ef26d08cb55564130b168785e07ab9e33d7256); this would help the diffusion U-Net produce viable walls, doors, windows, and room flow. Third, address the 128x128 detail risk by adding a resolution ablation: train or fine-tune at 256x256 and 512x512, or use progressive resolution and super-resolution constrained by wall and room masks, because [(Zeng et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99ef26d08cb55564130b168785e07ab9e33d7256) shows 512x512 is important for thin walls and [(Chen, and Pan, 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd8e681b5137fd1835b3f93c293d092b1bf90769) shows structural line and junction accuracy depends on precise 512x512 annotations. Fourth, curate and report the 12,000-image dataset using the MSD-style protocol from [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb): remove non-residential or non-floorplan images, deduplicate near-identical plans, report room counts, units, corners, graph entropy, near-duplicate rate, orientation, and metadata coverage, and ensure train, validation, and test splits prevent memorization. Fifth, adopt practical training and augmentation details from earlier floorplan work: use rotation alignment and 90-degree rotation test-time augmentation as in [(Kalervo et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da519b06b25de4d99d817c592486fe5eaac5fba3), consider U-Net and MURA-style attention or multi-task heads as in [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f7026ec2374b04a5c7f227389b679ffea7a4302), and retain U-Net augmentation and rotation invariance principles from [(Ronneberger et al., 2015-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6364fdaa0a0eccd823a779fcdd489173f938e91a), while documenting batch size, learning-rate schedule, normalization, mixed precision, and hardware to make the 12,000-image 512x512 diffusion experiment reproducible.

## Context: Standardized input preparation. Method: We will implement a rigorous preprocessing routine to standardize and optimize our dataset. This will include: (1) detection and isolation of floor plans from extraneous background elements, (2) rotation alignment to ensure consistent orientation across all floor plans, and (3) background standardization to create uniform image conditions for training.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), which directly implements the proposed detection/isolation and background standardization ideas by curating a Swiss residential corpus, removing non-floor-plan geometries, non-residential plans, near-duplicates, and plans with fewer than 15 areas or fewer than two Zone 2 areas, producing 5,372 floor plans, 163.5K rooms, 18.5K units, graph-diversity entropy 8.02, and a near-duplicate rate below 1%, and by creating diffusion conditioning inputs through morphological thinning to one-pixel skeletons, skeleton-network simplification, padded segmentation-based interior/exterior inference, and three background channels, in-wall-out, in-out, and raw-boundary, at 512-pixel scale with a 5-pixel adjacency buffer; read iteratively, this newer work extends earlier fixed-resolution input standardization into explicit structure isolation and background conditioning, making it the closest evidence that the proposed preprocessing routine is scientifically plausible for floor-plan diffusion. The next strongest support is [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), which shows that standardized training inputs for diffusion floor-plan generation can yield strong results: 60,000 RPLAN vector floorplans rendered at 256x256, room-count stratification into 5, 6, 7, and 8-room groups, leave-group-out evaluation, 250,000 steps with Adam with decoupled weight decay, batch size 512, initial learning rate 1e-3 with factor-of-10 decay every 100,000 steps, 1,000 diffusion steps, and a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 produced 67% higher diversity and 32% higher compatibility than House-GAN++, a -0.19 realism score interpreted as 81% as-realistic-as-ground-truth, and better non-Manhattan handling; although it does not explicitly report detection/isolation, rotation alignment, or background standardization, it supports the broader premise that consistent, fixed-resolution, stratified inputs improve diffusion training and evaluation. Weaker but still relevant support is [(Chen, and Pan, 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd8e681b5137fd1835b3f93c293d092b1bf90769), where 1080x720 JPG floor plans are only edge-padded and resized to 512x512 before training, and the full GLSP model still achieves junction detection sAP2 76.64 plus or minus 0.16, sAP4 87.97 plus or minus 0.07, and sAP8 89.38 plus or minus 0.02, outperforming HAWP-M* at 65.08 plus or minus 0.51, 81.86 plus or minus 0.25, and 84.60 plus or minus 0.08; this supports size and boundary standardization but not the full proposed routine. The weakest support is [(Kurupathi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c7015d8583c748fda47437b9bd16a57ca22ab5), which standardizes SVG CubiCasa5K vector elements through annotation, subdivision, normalization, optional splitting or merging, and graph conversion, achieving balanced accuracy 1.00 with ENNet-sc plus UniMP versus 0.75 for baseline ENNet on 4,200 training, 400 validation, and 400 test samples; this supports rigorous input normalization but is vector-entity classification, not raster diffusion preprocessing.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is to the rotation-alignment component: [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) explicitly preserves original floor-plan orientation rather than enforcing axis-aligned rotation, which suggests that forced rotation may discard orientation-related architectural or site semantics and that a diffusion model can be conditioned on standardized structure and background inputs without normalizing orientation; this directly challenges the proposed step of rotation alignment even though the same paper supports detection/isolation and background standardization. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) obtains strong diffusion floor-plan generation without explicitly reporting detection/isolation, rotation alignment, or background standardization, using only baseline-consistent 256x256 RPLAN floorplan images, room-count stratification, and leave-group-out evaluation; because RPLAN is already a clean vector floor-plan corpus, this is not a direct refutation, but it indicates that the proposed preprocessing may be less necessary when the source dataset is already isolated and uniform. Weaker contradiction is [(Chen, and Pan, 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd8e681b5137fd1835b3f93c293d092b1bf90769), which achieves high junction-detection performance with only edge padding and resizing to 512x512, without detection/isolation, rotation alignment, or background standardization, and without an ablation isolating the preprocessing effect; this suggests that elaborate image-level preprocessing may not be required for clean annotated floor-plan parsing, though the task is parsing rather than generative diffusion. The weakest contradiction is [(Kurupathi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c7015d8583c748fda47437b9bd16a57ca22ab5), which uses vector-CAD SVG preprocessing and graph conversion rather than raster background, rotation, or isolation preprocessing, achieving balanced accuracy 1.00 with ENNet-sc plus UniMP; this implies that if vector data are available, entity-level normalization may be more appropriate than image-level background standardization, but it does not directly test the proposed diffusion image pipeline.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a controlled ablation of the preprocessing routine because none of the related works isolates the effect of detection/isolation, rotation alignment, or background standardization; train identical U-Net diffusion models on raw 512x512 inputs, pad/resize-only inputs, detection plus background-standardized inputs, and rotation-aligned inputs, then report room-count accuracy, wall/door/window IoU or junction sAP, graph topology compatibility, diversity, realism, FID or IS if applicable, and pairwise user-study scores with confidence intervals and multiple seeds. Second, adopt the strongest Engelenburg-style implementation for detection and background standardization: remove non-floor-plan geometries and non-residential plans, filter near-duplicates and small plans, use segmentation to infer interior/exterior, create in-wall-out, in-out, and raw-boundary channels, and use morphological thinning or skeletonization if wall-line conditioning is needed; at 512-pixel scale, verify that a 5-pixel adjacency buffer or equivalent topology check remains valid after downscaling to the proposed 128x128 training resolution. Third, treat rotation alignment as a hypothesis rather than a default: compare axis-aligned rotation against preserving original orientation and against rotation augmentation, and if orientation matters, condition the diffusion model on orientation or site metadata instead of silently rotating all plans. Fourth, strengthen evaluation beyond qualitative inspection by using quantitative baselines and metrics from related work: Shabani's diversity, compatibility, and realism protocol with 1,000 generated samples, 150 pairwise evaluations per pair by 10 participants; Chen's sAP2, sAP4, and sAP8 thresholds for wall and junction accuracy; Kurupathi's balanced accuracy if vector entity classification is added; and Engelenburg's graph-based topology and proportion evaluation with architect user studies. Fifth, document reproducibility details that are missing or under-specified in the plan and related work: dataset provenance, exact detection model, rotation rule, background channel definitions, hardware, total training steps, batch size 16 versus Shabani's batch size 512, learning-rate schedule, diffusion steps, and upscaling model; consider training at 256x256 or 512x512 if compute allows, because Shabani used 256x256 and Engelenburg used 512-pixel conditioning, while the proposed 128x128 training may lose wall thickness and adjacency detail.

## Context: Optimization and precision settings. Method: Batch sizes of 16 for both training and evaluation; Initial learning rate of 1e-4 with 500-step warm-up phase; Automatic mixed precision (fp16) to accelerate training without compromising quality.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed batch size 16 and initial learning rate 1e-4 comes from [(Sun et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/115f8fd513c38214ea15ceb49add5b970831cd7e), which fine-tuned a GLIDE diffusion backbone with AdamW at learning rate 1e-4 for 235,000 iterations and batch size 8, then trained a skip cross-attention module with AdamW at learning rate 1e-5 for 50,000 iterations and batch size 16 on a single RTX 3090 using a 17,000-image SG-Fashion dataset plus Polyvore, 64x64 generation followed by super-resolution to 256x256, DDIM 100 sampling, and classifier-free guidance with 0.8 conditional probability; it reported best LPIPS on both datasets, lowest FID on SG-Fashion, Polyvore FID only 0.04% lower than FuseDream, and 256x256 synthesis within 10 seconds, supporting the feasibility of batch size 16 and 1e-4 for conditional diffusion training and the low-resolution-then-upscale strategy, although it did not test 500-step warm-up or fp16 and was not architectural. The next strongest support is domain-specific evidence that diffusion models can generate floor plans and should be evaluated quantitatively: [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) trained a graph-constrained diffusion model on 60,000 RPLAN vector floorplans rendered at 256x256, using 250,000 steps, batch size 512, initial learning rate 1e-3 with division by 10 every 100,000 steps, 1,000 diffusion steps, uniform diffusion-time sampling, and one NVIDIA RTX 6000, and outperformed House-GAN++ and House-GAN with average 67% diversity improvement, average 32% compatibility improvement, and human realism score -0.19 versus -0.18 for the strongest baseline after post-processing and filtering 90% of incompatible samples, supporting the diffusion-model direction and metric-based evaluation but not the proposed batch size 16, 1e-4, warm-up, or fp16. In the iterative sequence, this newer diffusion result also supports moving beyond the older GAN line represented by [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0), which trained a House-GAN WGAN-GP floor-plan generator for 200,000 iterations with batch size 32, learning rate 0.0001 for generator and discriminator, ADAM beta1=0.5 and beta2=0.999, leaky ReLU alpha=0.1, one critic, and dual NVIDIA Titan RTX GPUs, showing that 1e-4 is a plausible floor-plan generative-model learning rate, but it is a GAN, not a diffusion model, and reports no quantitative outcomes, warm-up, or mixed precision. The weakest support is from [(Kuhn, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea0bec0bd97b1dc6d02411e78de56e261f64188d), which trained a diffusion floor-plan model on the MSD dataset with 3,804 training and 100 validation samples, discarded about 1,000 high-room-count samples for GPU memory, used batch size 32 for about 340,000 steps, and supports memory-constrained diffusion training and data filtering, but provides no learning rate, warm-up, fp16, resolution, or generation-quality metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed optimization settings is also the most direct diffusion floor-plan evidence: [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) achieved strong results with batch size 512 and initial learning rate 1e-3, not batch size 16 and 1e-4, and used a stepwise decay factor of 10 every 100,000 steps rather than a reported 500-step warm-up or fp16; this suggests that the proposed smaller batch and lower initial learning rate may be underpowered for diffusion floor-plan generation, especially given its 60,000-image 256x256 setting, and its realism advantage over the strongest baseline was only -0.19 versus -0.18 with no confidence intervals or significance tests. The next strongest contradiction is that diffusion floor-plan training can plateau under memory-constrained settings: [(Kuhn, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea0bec0bd97b1dc6d02411e78de56e261f64188d) reported that during the last 100,000 of about 340,000 training steps with batch size 32 on MSD, loss did not improve significantly, contradicting the assumption that a 500-step warm-up and fp16 alone will guarantee useful convergence or quality gains, and highlighting the need for loss monitoring, early stopping, data filtering, and quantitative validation. Older GAN evidence provides a weaker contradiction: [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) used batch size 32 rather than 16 and did not use warm-up or fp16, and although it used the same 1e-4 learning rate, it reported no quantitative outcomes or statistical comparisons, so it does not validate the proposed precision or warm-up choices. The weakest contradiction is from [(Sun et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/115f8fd513c38214ea15ceb49add5b970831cd7e), which used batch size 8 for backbone fine-tuning due to GPU memory limits and only batch size 16 for a frozen-backbone conditional module, and did not report warm-up or fp16, so it supports batch size 16 only partially and does not establish that full-model floor-plan training at batch size 16 with fp16 will preserve quality.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a small, controlled ablation of the proposed optimization and precision settings before full training, because the related work gives mixed evidence for batch size 16 and learning rate 1e-4: compare batch sizes 16, 32, and possibly 64 or 128 if memory allows, initial learning rates 1e-4 and 1e-3, warm-up lengths 0, 500, and 1000 steps, fp16 on/off, and a learning-rate decay schedule such as division by 10 every 100,000 steps from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), using a 128x128 subset of the 12,000-image dataset for 20,000 to 50,000 steps on a single GPU and monitoring validation loss for the plateau behavior reported by [(Kuhn, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea0bec0bd97b1dc6d02411e78de56e261f64188d). The second most important action is to replace or supplement purely qualitative visual inspection with quantitative metrics and statistical reporting, including FID, LPIPS, diversity, compatibility, wall/room validity, IoU or segmentation accuracy, and human pairwise realism judgments with confidence intervals, following the metric set and human-evaluation protocol in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) and the FID/LPIPS/runtime/memory reporting in [(Sun et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/115f8fd513c38214ea15ceb49add5b970831cd7e). The third action is to specify and test the sampling and conditioning pipeline, because the plan omits diffusion sampling details: report DDIM or DDPM step counts, guidance strength, evaluation batch size 16, and compare a fast 100-step DDIM setting from [(Sun et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/115f8fd513c38214ea15ceb49add5b970831cd7e) against a higher-fidelity 1,000-step setting from [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8). The fourth action is to make the memory and data pipeline explicit: filter or downsample high-room-count samples as in [(Kuhn, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea0bec0bd97b1dc6d02411e78de56e261f64188d), use 128x128 training with post-generation upscaling, and report whether fp16 causes instability or quality loss relative to fp32. The least important but still useful action is to document reproducibility details, including GPU model, number of training steps, random seeds, dataset split, hyperparameters, and statistical significance tests, so that the proposed efficiency claims can be compared with the hardware and training-step details reported in [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0), [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), and [(Sun et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/115f8fd513c38214ea15ceb49add5b970831cd7e).

## Context: Qualitative assessment of generated floor plans. Method: We will conduct qualitative analysis through direct visual inspection of generated images, assessing three primary criteria: Accuracy: Verification that generated images represent viable architectural spaces with correct placement of walls, doors, and windows; Coherence: Assessment of logical architectural layout including room flow, functionality, and real-world applicability; Aesthetics: Evaluation of visual appeal and professional design quality.

### Support

First, the strongest direct support comes from [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ead4df17d14c53d4aef9af844c434b9c8c8de9f3), which generated single-story, single-family residential floor plans from large-language-model prompts using Stable Diffusion v1.5 in both official and floor-plan-fine-tuned configurations and qualitatively judged whether the outputs were usable layouts that designers could further modify. This directly supports the proposed plan's premise that generated floor plans can be assessed by visual inspection for architectural usability, although it did not define a formal rubric for accuracy, coherence, or aesthetics. Second, the strongest methodological support for making such inspection rigorous comes from [(Saharia et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9695824d7a01fad57ba9c01d7d76a519d78d65e7), which used structured human evaluation on COCO and DrawBench with explicit photorealism/image-quality and caption-similarity judgments, multiple raters, category-wise aggregation, and 95% confidence intervals; its 1024x1024 cascaded diffusion system with a frozen T5-XXL encoder, reported zero-shot COCO FID of 7.27, and photorealism preference rates of 39.2% on full COCO and 43.6% on the no-people subset show that qualitative assessment can be made more defensible when criteria, raters, and uncertainty are specified. Third, strong support for the proposed U-Net diffusion and low-resolution-to-upscaling pipeline comes from [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da), which used an f=4 perceptual autoencoder, a UNet latent diffusion model, and cross-attention conditioning for class-conditional, text-to-image, layout-to-image, super-resolution, and inpainting tasks; its inpainting evaluation on 30,000 512x512 Places crops reported FID 9.39 and LPIPS 0.246 on all samples and FID 1.50 and LPIPS 0.137 on hard 40-50% masked examples, supporting both visual comparison and quantitative validation of enhancement pipelines. Fourth, [(Nichol et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7002ae048e4b8c9133a55428441e8066070995cb) supports the proposed post-generation upscaling and visual assessment by generating 64x64 diffusion samples and upsampling them to 256x256 with a separate diffusion upsampler, using a 2.3B-parameter visual model, 1.2B-parameter text encoder, 1.5B-parameter upsampler, 2.5M training iterations at batch size 2048, and reporting zero-shot MS-COCO FID of 12.24 at 64x64 and 12.89 on a filtered validation subset; its pairwise human evaluation also supports direct visual judgment of realism and prompt adherence. Fifth, [(Zhao et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4704d8698b9e50aee53a5970172bd046af58ec6) supports visual inspection of layout-conditioned generation by checking whether 64x64 scene images respect bounding-box locations, preserve object identity, and remain consistent when boxes are added or moved; its Layout2Im model used SN-GAN, Adam with learning rate 0.0001, batch size 8, 300,000 iterations, about 3 days on a single Titan Xp GPU, and reported a diversity score of 0.15 versus 0.02 for sg2im and 0 for pix2pix. Sixth, [(Gu et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/414e554d281d529401c873cb9c97186365ec5dd8) supports visual side-by-side evaluation of diffusion outputs, using VQ-Diffusion-S with 34M parameters and VQ-Diffusion-B with 370M parameters, T=100 diffusion steps, a VQ-VAE codebook of 2886, and mask-and-replace inpainting, although its qualitative comparisons did not use a formal architectural rubric. Seventh, [(Ding et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75bb9eda70751c63fc54dbe63377c673b7dbdb15) supports large-scale human visual comparison, using 4,600 comparison groups on COCO captions and reporting that CogView2 performed best in all evaluated aspects, while also showing that COCO fine-tuning reduced FID from 24.0 to 19.2 at 2,500 iterations and 17.5 at 7,500 iterations. Eighth, older but relevant support comes from [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921), which used 10 human raters and 5 generated images per text description on CUB, Oxford-102, and COCO, reporting StackGAN average human ranks of 1.37 +/- 0.02 on CUB, 1.13 +/- 0.03 on Oxford-102, and 1.11 +/- 0.03 on COCO, demonstrating that human visual ranking can compare generated outputs. Ninth, [(Kawar et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23e261a20a315059b4de5492ed071c97a20c12e7) supports visual inspection of diffusion-generated edits across style, appearance, color, posture, and composition, using 8 random seeds per image-text pair, Imagen fine-tuning for 1500 steps, DDIM sampling, and about 8 minutes per image on two TPUv4 chips or about 7 minutes on a single Tesla A100 for Stable Diffusion. Tenth, [(Li et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd3595f65519e4af6bcd073790ac32acdafadf55) supports visual inspection for style fidelity, diversity, and artifact avoidance in few-shot adaptation settings such as FFHQ to Bitmoji, AFHQ animal faces with 10 cat and 10 dog images, Artistic-Faces with 10 images per artist, and CLP to 10 pencil drawings. Finally, [(Nichol, and Dhariwal, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de18baa4964804cf471d85a5a090498242d2e79f) supports the broader diffusion training context by showing that hybrid diffusion objectives with cosine schedules can improve log-likelihood while keeping FID competitive on CIFAR-10 and ImageNet 64x64, with best ImageNet 64x64 models trained for 1.5M iterations, indicating that sample-quality metrics should accompany visual inspection.

### Contradictions

First, the strongest contradiction is that the proposed method remains an unstructured visual inspection, whereas the most rigorous related evaluations operationalize criteria, use multiple raters, and report uncertainty. [(Saharia et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9695824d7a01fad57ba9c01d7d76a519d78d65e7) used explicit photorealism and caption-similarity judgments, multiple raters, category-wise aggregation, and 95% confidence intervals, while the proposed plan only names accuracy, coherence, and aesthetics without rubric items, rater qualifications, sample counts, prompt categories, or reliability statistics. The direct floor-plan study [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ead4df17d14c53d4aef9af844c434b9c8c8de9f3) also contradicts the sufficiency of the proposed method by reporting only a qualitative conclusion that Stable Diffusion v1.5 outputs could be modified by designers, without a formal protocol for wall, door, window, room-flow, or aesthetic scoring. Second, the proposed 128x128 training plus post-generation upscaling strategy is challenged by evidence that fine-grained detail can be lost in latent or upsampling pipelines. [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da) notes that autoencoder reconstruction fidelity can become a bottleneck when fine-grained pixel-space accuracy is required, and its inpainting ablations show that fine-tuning and attention materially affect FID and LPIPS, implying that 128x128 generation followed by upscaling may not preserve walls, doors, windows, or room boundaries without explicit detail-preserving evaluation. [(Nichol et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7002ae048e4b8c9133a55428441e8066070995cb) similarly shows that upsampled 256x256 diffusion outputs require careful human pairwise judgment and that CLIP score can be gamed by adversarial examples, so visual inspection alone may not reliably separate architectural accuracy from superficial realism. Third, human and quantitative evidence can diverge, which contradicts relying on either unstructured inspection or a single metric. [(Ding et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75bb9eda70751c63fc54dbe63377c673b7dbdb15) found that COCO-fine-tuned CogView2 improved FID from 24.0 to 19.2 at 2,500 iterations and 17.5 at 7,500 iterations but performed worse than the original CogView2 in human evaluation, indicating that neither FID nor unstructured visual inspection alone is sufficient. Fourth, older visual-inspection studies contradict the proposed method's current rigor by lacking reliability and statistical validation: [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921) used 10 raters and 5 images per text but reported no inter-rater reliability or significance tests; [(Zhao et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4704d8698b9e50aee53a5970172bd046af58ec6) inspected 64x64 layout-conditioned images without numerical scores or significance tests; [(Li et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd3595f65519e4af6bcd073790ac32acdafadf55) relied on visual inspection without quantitative metrics, hardware, hyperparameters, or a scoring rubric; [(Gu et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/414e554d281d529401c873cb9c97186365ec5dd8) provided qualitative side-by-side comparisons without a formal scoring rubric or inter-rater agreement; and [(Kawar et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23e261a20a315059b4de5492ed071c97a20c12e7) selected the best of 8 seeds for display, introducing selection bias and no statistical evidence. Finally, [(Nichol, and Dhariwal, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de18baa4964804cf471d85a5a090498242d2e79f) underscores that diffusion sample quality should be evaluated with FID and NLL, not only by inspection, although its natural-image setting does not directly validate architectural criteria.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to convert the proposed accuracy, coherence, and aesthetics criteria into an explicit, reproducible evaluation rubric and human-study protocol. Based on [(Saharia et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9695824d7a01fad57ba9c01d7d76a519d78d65e7), the plan should define binary or Likert items for wall connectivity, door/window placement, room adjacency, circulation, scale, and visual professionalism; recruit multiple raters including architects and non-experts; use pairwise comparisons or rankings; report inter-rater reliability such as Cohen's kappa or intraclass correlation; and provide 95% confidence intervals by prompt category. This should be informed by the direct floor-plan evidence in [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ead4df17d14c53d4aef9af844c434b9c8c8de9f3), which shows that designers can judge whether generated single-story plans are modifiable, but only if the study specifies what makes a plan usable. The second priority is to add quantitative architectural validation alongside visual inspection. Drawing on [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da) and [(Nichol, and Dhariwal, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de18baa4964804cf471d85a5a090498242d2e79f), the plan should report FID, LPIPS, and, where ground truth is available, wall/room IoU or Dice, door/window detection precision/recall, room-graph adjacency accuracy, circulation path length, and buildability constraint violations. The third priority is to explicitly evaluate the 128x128 training plus upscaling strategy, because [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da) warns that autoencoder reconstruction can bottleneck fine detail and [(Nichol et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7002ae048e4b8c9133a55428441e8066070995cb) shows upsampled diffusion outputs need careful human and metric comparison; the plan should compare direct 512x512 or 128x128 generation against upscaled 128x128 outputs using wall-edge sharpness, door/window presence, and human preference. The fourth priority is to standardize sampling and reporting to avoid selection bias: use fixed seeds, generate multiple samples per prompt, report the number of prompts, categories, raters, and significance tests, and avoid presenting only the best of multiple seeds unless best-of-N is itself the evaluated condition, as cautioned by [(Kawar et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23e261a20a315059b4de5492ed071c97a20c12e7). The fifth priority is to include domain-specific baselines and prompt categories, such as pretrained versus floor-plan-fine-tuned Stable Diffusion v1.5 or U-Net diffusion models, single-story residential prompts, and comparisons to direct high-resolution generation, following [(Li et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ead4df17d14c53d4aef9af844c434b9c8c8de9f3) and [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da). Finally, the training and conditioning choices should be aligned with evidence from [(Rombach et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f58acfeb022ba15282ec5fc047c98010311fa9da), [(Nichol, and Dhariwal, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de18baa4964804cf471d85a5a090498242d2e79f), and [(Nichol et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7002ae048e4b8c9133a55428441e8066070995cb) by using cross-attention layout conditioning, hybrid diffusion objectives or classifier-free guidance where appropriate, and ablations of guidance strength, noise schedule, and upscaling method.

## Context: Experimental validation and trade-off analysis. Method: 1. Initial Generation: Train the diffusion model on preprocessed data and generate baseline floor plans; 2. Enhancement Phase: Apply upscaling techniques to improve resolution and detail of generated plans; 3. Comparative Analysis: Evaluate the trade-offs between computational efficiency and output quality using our lower-resolution training with upscaling approach versus direct high-resolution generation.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed diffusion-based floor-plan pipeline comes from recent floor-plan-specific diffusion work. [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) trained a guided diffusion model on 60,000 RPLAN vector floorplans rendered as 256x256 images, conditioned on bubble diagrams or graphs, and reported that it outperformed GAN and scene-graph baselines, improving diversity by 67% and compatibility by 32% over the strongest GAN baseline, with a realism score of -0.19 versus -0.18 for the GAN while producing direct network outputs rather than filtering about 90% of samples; this supports the plan's initial generation step and the choice of diffusion over GANs for architectural layout, although it does not validate 128x128 training or upscaling. The next strongest domain support is [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), whose Modified HouseDiffusion generates 512x512 wall and area masks from building-structure inputs and evaluates mean IoU, graph compatibility, topology, and proportions, while also testing 128x128 baselines HouseGAN++ and FLNet; this supports the proposed emphasis on high-resolution architectural outputs and the need to compare lower-resolution generation against stronger baselines, but it does not test post-generation upscaling. A strong but non-floor-plan precedent for the enhancement phase is [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921), which used a stacked GAN to generate 64x64 images and then refine them to 256x256, achieving inception scores of 3.70 +/- 0.04 on CUB, 3.20 +/- 0.01 on Oxford-102, and 8.45 +/- 0.03 on COCO, with average human ranks of 1.37 +/- 0.02, 1.13 +/- 0.03, and 1.11 +/- 0.03, and showed that direct Stage-I generation was rough and blurry relative to the stacked model; this supports the hypothesis that lower-resolution generation followed by learned refinement can beat direct low-resolution output, but it uses text-conditioned GANs rather than diffusion floor plans. Additional support for multi-scale UNet-based spatial generation comes from [(Deng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5dfeefdbb220ac772fdc51923fc5a24d4eebfa9f), which trained 128x128, 256x256, and 512x512 block generation models for city layouts and used outpainting/inpainting refinement initialized from the corresponding UNets, supporting the idea that lower-resolution models can be part of a multi-scale refinement pipeline, although no quantitative trade-off against direct high-resolution training is reported. The foundational diffusion evidence is [(Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2dcef55a07f8607a819c21fe84131ea269cc2e3c), which demonstrated diffusion probabilistic models on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and bark-image inpainting, including conditioning on observed pixels for a 100x100 missing region; this supports the general diffusion framework and conditional image completion, but it provides no floor-plan, 128x128, or upscaling evidence. Finally, floor-plan GAN and graph-based work provides weaker but useful support for the evaluation context: [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) introduced House-GAN and realism/diversity/compatibility evaluation, [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a) benchmarked floor-plan generation with NMI, MSE/MAE, vectorization success, and architect Elo ratings, and [(Tang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bad068b5c83b3aa18fb401c0061e0ae289ca815b) used graph transformer GANs with graph cycle-consistency and connectivity-oriented evaluation; these support the need for architectural-specific metrics and structured layout conditioning, but none directly supports the proposed 128x128 diffusion training plus upscaling trade-off. As an iterative sequence, the foundational diffusion model of [(Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2dcef55a07f8607a819c21fe84131ea269cc2e3c) is extended by staged-resolution generation in [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921), then by floor-plan GAN evaluation in [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) and [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a), and finally by diffusion floor-plan models in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) and [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), which together make the proposed method plausible but still require direct validation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that diffusion image generation can be weak for precise floor-plan geometry. [(Leng et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04a0f3f39c5a6aeddcd2841457d0b500adfcb534) used Imagen as a diffusion baseline for language-guided floor-plan generation and reported micro IoU of 12.17 and macro IoU of 14.96 under warm-up plus fine-tuning, 14.74/15.57 with artificial instructions only, and 9.29/6.64 with human instructions only, far below a structured sequence-to-sequence model with micro IoU 54.34 and macro IoU 53.30; this contradicts the assumption that a diffusion model trained on preprocessed floor-plan images will automatically produce accurate walls, rooms, doors, and windows without strong structured conditioning and quantitative pixel-level evaluation. The next strongest domain-specific contradiction is that 128x128 floor-plan generation appears insufficient without additional high-resolution refinement. [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) evaluated 128x128 baselines HouseGAN++ and FLNet on the MSD benchmark and qualitatively reported that they do not perform well, while its diffusion model operates at 512x512 with graph compatibility, topology, and proportion evaluation; although it does not test upscaling, this suggests the proposed 128x128 baseline may be too coarse for architectural topology and that upscaling must be validated against 512x512 outputs. A further contradiction to the necessity of the proposed efficiency trade-off is that direct higher-resolution diffusion can already be competitive. [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) trained a 256x256 diffusion model for 250,000 steps with batch size 512 on a single NVIDIA RTX 6000 and reported direct outputs that outperformed GAN baselines in diversity and compatibility, implying that if 256x256 or 512x512 diffusion training is feasible, the proposed 128x128-plus-upscaling route must demonstrate a clear quality or compute advantage rather than being assumed. Weaker contradictions arise from the absence of direct evidence for the proposed upscaling mechanism: [(Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2dcef55a07f8607a819c21fe84131ea269cc2e3c) provides only qualitative inpainting and no lower-resolution training plus upscaling comparison; [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921) supports staged refinement but uses a learned Stage-II GAN, not a generic upscaler, and its 64x64-to-256x256 setting is not floor-plan-specific; [(Deng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5dfeefdbb220ac772fdc51923fc5a24d4eebfa9f) uses multi-scale city layouts but reports no quantitative outcomes or direct high-resolution baseline; and [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0), [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a), and [(Tang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bad068b5c83b3aa18fb401c0061e0ae289ca815b) are GAN or graph-based rather than diffusion and do not test post-generation upscaling, so they cannot validate the proposed enhancement phase. Read iteratively, the newer floor-plan-specific evidence from [(Leng et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04a0f3f39c5a6aeddcd2841457d0b500adfcb534) and [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) constrains the more optimistic general diffusion and staged-resolution evidence from [(Sohl-Dickstein et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2dcef55a07f8607a819c21fe84131ea269cc2e3c) and [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921), indicating that the proposed method is plausible but not yet validated.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to replace or supplement qualitative visual inspection with quantitative architectural evaluation. The plan should report micro and macro IoU for walls and room masks, graph compatibility using an adjacency buffer such as the 5-pixel buffer in [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), topology and proportion scores, FID/KID, vectorization success rate, and, where relevant, NMI, MSE, or MAE as in [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a) and [(Nauata et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a026d2dd10af43f8bc7fc9fd7a4f888c74efe0) realism/diversity/compatibility; human evaluation should use structured pairwise comparisons or Elo ratings with architects, following the 1,000-sample, 150-pair, 10-participant realism protocol in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) or the 5-architect, 1,500-rating Elo study in [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a). Second, the enhancement phase must specify a learned upscaling or refinement model rather than an unspecified upscaling technique. A strong design would train a 128x128-to-512x512 refinement UNet or super-resolution network conditioned on the low-resolution floor plan, wall/room masks, and optional graph or boundary channels, using paired 128x128 and 512x512 data, and compare it against nearest-neighbor/bicubic interpolation, direct 128x128 generation, and direct 256x256 or 512x512 diffusion training; this mirrors the staged refinement logic of [(Zhang et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea67d2d5f2a7d5760ec6b67ea93d11dd5affa921) and the scale-specific inpainting/outpainting refinement in [(Deng et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5dfeefdbb220ac772fdc51923fc5a24d4eebfa9f). Third, because 128x128 floor-plan baselines are reported to perform poorly in [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) and generic diffusion floor-plan IoU is low in [(Leng et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/04a0f3f39c5a6aeddcd2841457d0b500adfcb534), the plan should include a direct high-resolution baseline at 256x256 or 512x512 and measure compute time, memory, training steps, and sample quality to establish whether 128x128 training plus upscaling actually improves the efficiency-quality trade-off; the 256x256 RPLAN diffusion setup in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8) is a useful reference for resolution, room-count stratification, and 250,000-step training. Fourth, the model should be conditioned on architectural structure rather than relying only on preprocessed images: use wall, room segmentation, building boundary, bubble diagram, zoning graph, or connectivity inputs, as in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb), [(Tang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bad068b5c83b3aa18fb401c0061e0ae289ca815b), and [(Wang et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20d319c2abc6bbd6166c6399ea8d4629ef8e175a), and ensure doors/windows are represented in the training targets if they are part of the accuracy criterion. Fifth, the training configuration should be made reproducible and sufficiently powered: report hardware, diffusion steps, sampler, seeds, learning-rate schedule, and statistical significance; consider increasing batch size or using gradient accumulation because the proposed batch size of 16 is much smaller than the batch size 512 used in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8), and use room-count stratification or exclusion of target room-count groups to reduce memorization as in [(Shabani et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1889ae7c47137d7c8c39cc82022558c13a46f0b8). Finally, the dataset plan should state how the 12,000 512x512 floor plans are split, whether they are comparable to RPLAN or MSD, and how preprocessing handles rotation, background, and vector-to-raster artifacts; smaller curated datasets such as MSD's 5,372 plans in [(Engelenburg et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5f654f500bcbcf29e8f34c658423b5c0c065efb) can still support evaluation if the split and metrics are rigorous.
