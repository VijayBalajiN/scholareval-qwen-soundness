## We will develop a novel fairness framework called "principal counterfactual fairness" using the concept of principal stratification from causal inference literature. This is the central methodological contribution: it reframes counterfactual fairness as a criterion that applies only to individuals for whom the protected attribute has no individual causal effect on the outcome.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed principal counterfactual fairness method comes from [(Balke, and Pearl, 1997-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7abc07c41b2bade96bc52f10c892f206fbabbe63), which shows that unobserved counterfactual quantities can be bounded by linear programming over observed joint probabilities, enumerating dual vertices, deriving closed-form upper and lower bounds, and producing testable inequalities. In its Vitamin A trial example, with N=23,682, z0=14,007, z1=9,675, P(d1|z0)=2,419/14,007 about 0.1727 and P(d0|z1)=0/9,675=0, the monotonicity-based bound width is about 17.3 percentage points and overall noncompliance is 10.2%, demonstrating that finite-dimensional probability-constraint optimization can yield sharp, interpretable bounds for quantities that are not directly observed. This directly supports the plan's optimization-based evaluation of P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) and the use of empty feasible regions or bounds excluding zero as violation tests. As an iterative extension, [(Horowitz, and Manski, 2000-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/948ad7ff478d48500350278e93c6559c06f7cc2a) adds finite-sample inference for bounds using empirical plug-in estimates and bootstrap joint confidence intervals of the form [L_n - z, U_n + z]; its Monte Carlo using a DVA trial found nominal 95% bootstrap intervals with empirical coverage between 0.93 and 0.96, and its NLSY application with 1,578 respondents used complete-case, MCAR, and no-assumption missing-data scenarios with 0.05-level hypothesis tests. This supports the plan's need to estimate and test bounds for principal counterfactual fairness rather than relying only on point estimates. The next strongest support is [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850), which develops conditional principal fairness by conditioning on W such that A is independent of the principal stratum R given W, and shows that conditional principal fairness implies counterfactual equalized odds and three statistical fairness definitions, with equivalence under an additional Assumption 1. This supports the plan's variants, especially principal conditional counterfactual fairness and principal counterfactual equalized odds, and the use of covariates to address infra-marginality. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), which grounds counterfactual fairness in an explicit causal model M, a predictor Yhat = g_theta(U, X_A), and MCMC sampling over latent U, with NYC stop-and-frisk named as an application; this validates the individual-level counterfactual framing but does not implement principal stratification. The weakest support is [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), which uses ten propensity-score bins on Adult and Census Income to compare protected and unprotected individuals and shows causal discrimination scores RD-c can differ from non-causal RD, but it provides only graphical or qualitative results, no principal stratum, no Y(0)=Y(1), and no statistical tests.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or challenge to the proposed method is that the most directly relevant bounds literature, [(Balke, and Pearl, 1997-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7abc07c41b2bade96bc52f10c892f206fbabbe63), is developed for binary treatment and outcome under randomized assignment, no direct effect of assignment, and optional monotonicity, and it warns that multivalued treatments create exponentially many latent equivalence classes, making LP computationally expensive. The proposed plan instead uses observational OULAD with 32,593 students and 11 attributes, a PC-learned causal structure, random DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 edges, multiple base models, and a disability-sensitive attribute that may be multivalued or context-dependent; without strong restrictions, the probability-constraint space for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) may be intractable and the ignorability assumption A independent of (Y(1), Y(0), D(1), D(0)) given X may not be testable or plausible. The next strongest contradiction is from [(Horowitz, and Manski, 2000-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/948ad7ff478d48500350278e93c6559c06f7cc2a), which shows that bound-based inference can be conservative or inconclusive: bootstrap nominal 95% intervals had empirical coverage only 0.93 to 0.96, and under weak missing-data assumptions confidence intervals were too wide to reject hypotheses at the 0.05 level. This suggests the plan's evaluation may produce wide bounds for PCF that include zero even when violations exist, reducing power. The third strongest contradiction is from [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850), which is theoretical only and requires A independent of R given W, plus Assumption 1 for equivalence to statistical fairness definitions; if the plan's conditioning covariates X do not satisfy these conditions, the proposed variants may not imply the intended fairness properties, and no empirical validation is provided. Weaker contradictions come from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), which emphasizes that counterfactual fairness assumptions are not fully falsifiable and requires a justified causal model M and MCMC with m samples per data point, but the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, baselines, or significance; this warns that the proposed PCF criterion may inherit unfalsifiability unless the bounds and post-processing are empirically validated. The weakest contradiction is from [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), whose causal discrimination evaluation is qualitative, lacks confidence intervals, sample sizes, hyperparameters, and statistical significance, and does not test principal strata, indicating that causal fairness metrics can be underreported and unstable if not paired with rigorous inference.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adapt the linear-programming bound machinery of [(Balke, and Pearl, 1997-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7abc07c41b2bade96bc52f10c892f206fbabbe63) to the plan's conditional principal stratum: construct observed joint probabilities for A, X, D, and Y under the ignorability assumption, derive sharp bounds for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) and the weaker variants, and implement feasibility tests that flag violations when the feasible region is empty or when bounds exclude zero. Because the plan uses OULAD with 32,593 students and 11 attributes, PC-learned structures, and random DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 edges, the method should report computational complexity, solver settings, and how multivalued disability or outcome categories are handled, possibly by binarization, stratification, or monotonicity-like restrictions, and should use these bounds to constrain the post-processing optimization that minimizes individual decision changes while ensuring PCF. The second most important action is to adopt the finite-sample inference approach of [(Horowitz, and Manski, 2000-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/948ad7ff478d48500350278e93c6559c06f7cc2a): use empirical plug-in estimates from OR, IPS, and DR, compute bootstrap joint confidence intervals for the lower and upper bounds, and validate nominal 95% coverage empirically on synthetic DAGs and OULAD subsamples, reporting coverage, interval width, and power to detect zero or threshold violations. The third most important action is to incorporate the conditioning logic of [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850): identify W or X such that A is independent of the principal stratum R given W, use the PC algorithm and conditional independence tests to justify this, and prove or empirically test when principal conditional counterfactual fairness implies counterfactual equalized odds and statistical fairness definitions. The fourth action is to benchmark against the causal fairness methods in [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) and [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29): implement FAIRLEARNING-style causal-model training with MCMC and propensity-score binned RD-c as baselines, and report CF, PCF, accuracy, decision-change counts, and statistical significance on Adult, Census Income, and OULAD subgroups. The least important but still necessary action is to document reproducibility details absent from the related work, including hardware, software, solver hyperparameters, PC algorithm parameters, base-model hyperparameters for Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, and Naive Bayes, missing-data handling, and subgroup definitions, so that the proposed post-processing and bounds can be reproduced.

## We will formulate several variants of principal counterfactual fairness, ordered from weakest to strongest: Principal counterfactual parity; Principal conditional counterfactual fairness; Principal counterfactual equalized odds; Principal counterfactual fairness (individual-level). This creates a hierarchy of fairness criteria that lets the plan compare increasingly strong requirements while preserving the principal-stratification focus.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Nilforoshan et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/106e1064097a51e42a46f3f262d73a6720fadaa8), which explicitly defines conditional principal fairness by restricting counterfactual decision parity to individuals with the same joint potential outcomes Y(1) and Y(0) and similar reduced covariates W, directly validating the plan's core stratum Y(a)=Y(a') and its college-admissions motivation. This is reinforced by [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850), which proves that if A is conditionally independent of principal stratum R given W, principal fairness implies counterfactual equalized odds and related statistical fairness criteria, and under an additional assumption is equivalent to them; this gives theoretical grounding for the proposed ordering from principal parity to principal conditional fairness to principal equalized odds. The post-processing and estimation components are strongly supported by [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf), which uses a four-parameter post-processed predictor, linear programming, doubly robust estimators, cross-fitting, random forests, and asymptotic normality to enforce counterfactual equalized odds; on COMPAS it reduced cFPR difference from -0.24 to -0.05 and cFNR difference from 0.16 to -0.03 at tolerance 0.05, flipped 9% of scores, and increased loss by 0.03, showing that constrained post-processing can be practical. Similar support comes from [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which derives observational and counterfactual parity/equalized odds conditions and uses doubly robust evaluation; its child-welfare counterfactual model achieved AUROC 0.62 (95% CI 0.61-0.63) and AUPR 0.18 (0.16-0.19) versus observational AUROC 0.48 (0.46-0.49), and its 50% threshold recall of 73% for placed cases supports the value of counterfactual-aware evaluation. The bounding and optimization evaluation plan is supported by [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169), which derives response-function bounds for path-specific counterfactual effects, validates them against exact synthetic ground truth, and shows tighter bounds than Markovian-exogenous baselines, e.g. lower bound 0.1772 versus previous [-0.2605, 0.2656] for a path-specific effect and bounds [-0.0783, -0.0212] for a counterfactual effect, demonstrating that threshold-based feasibility tests are feasible. Additional support is provided by [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), which operationalizes counterfactual equalized odds and direct/indirect/spurious error rates in a COMPAS-style graph, reducing disparate FPR from -0.476 to -0.238 while maintaining 90.4% accuracy, and by [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897), which restricts counterfactual invariance to unfair pathways and reports accuracy tradeoffs such as 67.1% for latent inference-projection versus 71.6% for the unfair direct link on Berkeley and 82.80% fair accuracy at 5,000 steps on Adult. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a), [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339), [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd), [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), and [(Dwork et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adaa0523a5c9d5f92aa2009a51226391d8e62380), which collectively justify causal restriction, path-specific or procedural fairness, causal discrimination diagnostics, competing fairness criteria, latent counterfactual modeling, and optimization-based individual/group fairness connections.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not to the use of principal strata, but to the assumption that a simple weakest-to-strongest hierarchy will behave monotonically in practice. [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd) shows that tuning a Black random-forest model to conditional use accuracy equality with a White model can produce near equality in one accuracy measure (.93 vs .94) while leaving violent re-arrest base rates of .11 vs .06 and false-positive-to-false-negative count ratios of about 4.2 for Blacks versus 3.1 for Whites, indicating that satisfying one fairness criterion can coexist with severe treatment inequality. This warns that principal parity, principal conditional fairness, principal equalized odds, and individual-level principal fairness may not nest cleanly in empirical performance. A second strong contradiction concerns post-processing: [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b) finds that post-processing to equalize observational generalized false positive and false negative rates can induce imbalance in counterfactual generalized false positive and false negative rates, and [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) shows that counterfactual equalized-odds post-processing can become infeasible or degenerate under cost weighting, returning an always-0 classifier when false positives are weighted more than 1.5 times false negatives and an always-1 classifier when false negatives are weighted more than 2 times false positives on child welfare data. This suggests the proposed post-processing for PCF may also produce degenerate decisions if principal-stratum constraints are estimated noisily. Third, identifiability and bound tightness are a risk: [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169) shows that even for path-specific effects, bounds can be uncertain and threshold decisions can change, and [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123) shows that enforcing counterfactual equalized odds can increase direct counterfactual error from 0.381 to 0.620, while removing prior convictions to make the model counterfactually explainable leaves residual direct discrimination (0.015 versus -0.066) and a {X,Z} classifier that eliminates direct discrimination only at 72.1% accuracy. This contradicts the idea that targeting one fairness quantity automatically improves the broader causal fairness profile. Fourth, [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) reports that applying counterfactual correction to both sexes on Adult can reduce fair accuracy to dummy-classifier levels by 20,000 steps, and that a PSE baseline constrained to 0.95-1.05 achieved only 72% accuracy versus 82% unconstrained, indicating that causal fairness constraints can be costly and sensitive to implementation. Weaker contradictions include [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), which relies on latent background variables and MCMC rather than the plan's ignorability assumption, suggesting the plan may understate confounding; [(Dwork et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adaa0523a5c9d5f92aa2009a51226391d8e62380), which shows individual fairness implies group parity only when Earthmover distance is small, implying individual-level principal fairness may not imply group-level principal parity without additional metric conditions; and [(Nilforoshan et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/106e1064097a51e42a46f3f262d73a6720fadaa8), which defines conditional principal fairness as group-level parity within principal strata rather than the proposed individual-level hierarchy, leaving a definitional gap.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize the hierarchy against the closest existing theory: define principal counterfactual parity, principal conditional counterfactual fairness, principal counterfactual equalized odds, and individual-level principal counterfactual fairness as nested or non-nested implications, and prove when each implies the next under the plan's ignorability assumption A independent of (Y(1), Y(0), D(1), D(0)) given X and under the conditional independence of A and the principal stratum given W used by [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850). This should also reconcile the proposed individual-level metric P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) with the group-level conditional principal fairness in [(Nilforoshan et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/106e1064097a51e42a46f3f262d73a6720fadaa8), including whether the plan's PCF is a stronger, weaker, or orthogonal criterion. Second, the bounding and optimization evaluation should borrow the response-function and linear-programming techniques of [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169), deriving sharp bounds for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) and for the four hierarchy variants, then using feasibility tests or threshold rules with reported lower and upper bounds rather than point estimates alone. Third, the post-processing algorithm should be built on the doubly robust, cross-fitted, linear-programming template in [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) and [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), using OR, IPS, and DR nuisance estimates, random forests or other flexible learners, confidence intervals, loss and flip-rate reporting, and explicit safeguards against degenerate always-0 or always-1 solutions when principal-stratum constraints are noisy or infeasible. Fourth, the experiments should include strong causal-fairness baselines: path-specific fairness from [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a) and [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897), counterfactual equalized odds from [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), procedural causal fairness from [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339), causal discrimination diagnostics from [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), confusion-table fairness tradeoffs from [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd), latent counterfactual fairness from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), and metric-based individual fairness from [(Dwork et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adaa0523a5c9d5f92aa2009a51226391d8e62380). Fifth, the synthetic design should be made more reproducible and less dense than 10 nodes with 40 directed edges, which is near the maximum for a 10-node DAG; use known ground-truth DAGs with varied edge densities, report random seeds, PC algorithm hyperparameters, and the exact structural equations, and include sensitivity analyses for unobserved confounding and latent variables. Sixth, for OULAD, report the 32,593-student sample, 11 attributes, disability coding, binarized final-grade threshold, PC-algorithm graph, subgroup divisions, and the estimated prevalence of Y(0)=Y(1), because if the principal fairness stratum is very small the metric P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) may be unstable; also report accuracy, loss, decision flips, confidence intervals, and comparisons to standard CF P(D(0)=D(1)) to show whether the claimed larger PCF improvement is real.

## The methodology will utilize the potential outcomes framework, where we define principal strata based on joint potential outcome values (Yi(a), Yi(a')) and focus fairness requirements only on the "principal fairness" stratum where Yi(a) = Yi(a'). This operationalizes the core idea by identifying the subpopulation in which the protected attribute does not change the individual outcome.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from the 2020 theoretical work of [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b34850), which explicitly uses a principal stratum R_i and proves that conditional principal fairness, under A_i ⊥⊥ R_i | W_i, implies counterfactual equalized odds and statistical fairness criteria, and under an additional assumption is equivalent to them. This directly validates the plan's central move of restricting fairness to a potential-outcome stratum and motivates the plan's principal conditional counterfactual fairness variant, especially because the paper addresses infra-marginality, i.e., conditional independence not implying marginal independence, which is exactly the concern in the plan's conditional variants. The next strongest support is from the 2019 work of [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which builds on earlier counterfactual evaluation by using doubly robust estimators with outcome regression and inverse-probability-weighted residual corrections, sample splitting, and normal-approximation 95% confidence intervals in a nonexperimental child welfare hotline screening setting; it shows that observational evaluation can make a counterfactual model appear inequitable while the doubly robust evaluation favors the counterfactual model, and at a 50 percent high-risk threshold the counterfactual model had 73 percent recall for placed cases while the observational model would screen out nearly 50 percent. This supports the plan's OR/IPS/DR estimation strategy and the need to evaluate fairness under counterfactual rather than observational quantities. It also supports the plan's post-processing idea by showing that fairness corrections must be evaluated on the target counterfactual metric, not only observational metrics. A further line of support is from the work of [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), which decomposes classification errors into counterfactual direct, indirect, and spurious rates using adjustment sets in the COMPAS recidivism domain, and shows that reducing the observed false-positive rate disparity for non-recidivating defendants from -0.476 to -0.238 can increase indirect counterfactual error from 0.381 to 0.620, while a counterfactual classifier trained on {X,Z} reduced direct counterfactual error to -0.001 at the cost of accuracy falling from 90.4% to 72.1%; this supports the plan's premise that fairness should be assessed through counterfactual causal quantities and that post-processing or training constraints can reduce specific causal disparities. Support is also provided by the 2017 work of [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a), which frames discrimination as path-specific effects and uses constrained outcome models with box constraints and regularized outcome models on COMPAS and Adult, aligning with the plan's hypothesis that fairness should depend on the causal relationship between the protected attribute and outcome. Weaker but still relevant support comes from the 2017 work of [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), which operationalizes individual-level counterfactual fairness through a predictor Ŷ = gθ(U, X_A), a supplied causal model P_M(U | x, a), and MCMC sampling of latent U, and from the 2016 work of [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), which uses 10 propensity-score bins on Adult and Census Income, with income >50K as the positive decision and females as the protected group, to compare causal and non-causal discrimination/favoritism, reporting approximate RD-Causal discrimination ranges of 0.05 to 0.30 and favoritism ranges of -0.70 to -0.10. Finally, the 2019 work of [(Kusner et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6db941f49f2672ef5de2420f712d21482a68e25) supports the plan's optimization-based post-processing direction by using a mixed-integer linear program with binary intervention variables and fairness constraints, solved with Gurobi in under 5 minutes for 345 New York City high schools with a budget of 25 schools and fairness parameters tau = 0.034 and tau = 0.097, although it does not use principal strata.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or challenge comes from the 2019 work of [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which shows that post-processing equalizing observational generalized false positive and false negative rates can create imbalance in counterfactual generalized rates, even when the original counterfactual model satisfied counterfactual equalized odds; this directly warns that the plan's optimization-based post-processing, if it targets only observed or marginal fairness, may fail to achieve principal counterfactual fairness P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) and could worsen counterfactual disparities. It also imposes practical constraints: doubly robust evaluation requires propensity and outcome nuisance models, sample splitting, and n^{1/4} convergence for asymptotic normality, so the plan's OR/IPS/DR estimators on OULAD's 32,593 students and synthetic DAGs must be validated for finite-sample behavior and convergence. A second major contradiction is from the work of [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), which shows that direct discrimination can remain significant after observed-disparity constraints, with direct counterfactual error rates of 0.015 and -0.066 for f_er and f_opt after removing prior convictions W and using adjustment set C = {Z}, and that including W made the classifiers not counterfactually explainable under the paper's criterion; this suggests that the plan's focus on the Y(0)=Y(1) stratum and decision equality may not capture indirect or spurious discrimination, and that PCF alone may be insufficient unless supplemented by path-specific or adjustment-set analyses. A third challenge is from the 2017 work of [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a), which discusses non-identification of path-specific effects and uses finite-sample box constraints and regularized outcome models, implying that the plan's principal-stratum bounds may also be wide or non-identified under weak assumptions, especially when the PC algorithm learns causal structure from OULAD and the ignorability assumption A ⊥⊥ (Y(1),Y(0),D(1),D(0))|X is untestable. Weaker contradictions include the 2017 work of [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), which treats counterfactual assumptions as provisional and potentially unfalsifiable, requires a supplied causal model P_M(U | x, a) plus MCMC sampling with an unspecified sample size m, and provides no quantitative experimental outcomes, challenging the plan's reliance on identifiable bounds without such a latent-variable model; the 2016 work of [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), which does not use principal strata, fairness bounds, or post-processing, and reports only approximate RD-Causal ranges rather than confidence intervals or statistical tests, limiting its evidential value for the plan's estimators; and the 2019 work of [(Kusner et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6db941f49f2672ef5de2420f712d21482a68e25), which enforces fairness through intervention-allocation constraints rather than individual-level principal strata and does not report statistical significance, so it does not directly validate the plan's individual-level PCF objective.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the theoretical structure of the 2020 work of [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b34850): define the principal stratum R_i = {Y_i(0)=Y_i(1)} explicitly, impose or test the conditional independence A_i ⊥⊥ R_i | X_i (or W_i) for the plan's principal conditional counterfactual fairness, and prove implications/equivalences to counterfactual equalized odds and statistical fairness criteria; this will strengthen the plan's theoretical development and address infra-marginality. Second, implement the estimation and post-processing protocol with the safeguards shown by the 2019 work of [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b): use OR, IPS, and DR estimators with separate nuisance models, sample splitting, influence-function or bootstrap confidence intervals, and n^{1/4} convergence checks; evaluate post-processing on both observational and counterfactual/principal metrics, including generalized false positive/false negative rates, calibration, AUROC, AUPR, and recall, to ensure that minimizing decision changes does not create counterfactual disparity. Third, add path-specific or mediation diagnostics from the works of [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123) and [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a): report direct, indirect, and spurious or path-specific counterfactual effects, use adjustment sets where available, and test whether PCF improvements hide indirect discrimination, especially in OULAD where disability may legitimately affect some outcomes. Fourth, report accuracy/utility tradeoffs and baselines, following the quantitative pattern in the work of [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), where direct-discrimination removal reduced accuracy from 90.4% to 72.1%, and the evaluation metrics in the 2019 work of [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), including AUROC 0.62 with 95% confidence interval 0.61 to 0.63 versus AUROC 0.48 with 95% confidence interval 0.46 to 0.49, and AUPR 0.18 with 95% confidence interval 0.16 to 0.19 versus AUPR 0.13 with 95% confidence interval 0.11 to 0.14 for counterfactual versus observational models; the plan should compare unconstrained, observed-fairness, counterfactual-fairness, path-specific-fairness, and PCF post-processing baselines with statistical significance. Fifth, improve reproducibility and scalability by specifying synthetic DAG generation (10 nodes, 40 edges, Erdős-Rényi parameters), PC algorithm settings, base model hyperparameters for Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, and Naive Bayes, OR/IPS/DR nuisance model choices, random seeds, hardware, and runtime; for post-processing, consider the MILP/Gurobi implementation and runtime under 5 minutes demonstrated in the 2019 work of [(Kusner et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6db941f49f2672ef5de2420f712d21482a68e25) for 345 schools, and test scalability to OULAD's 32,593 students. Finally, include sensitivity analysis for unobserved confounding and latent variables, inspired by the 2017 work of [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) and the 2016 work of [(Qureshi et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c616ee41371634c2cf91dbedcd11b1c665d8b29), by comparing propensity-score stratification, latent-variable MCMC approximations, and bounds under weakened ignorability, so that the plan's claims are not overgeneralized from the strong assumption A ⊥⊥ (Y(1),Y(0),D(1),D(0))|X.

## We will derive necessary conditions for algorithms to satisfy principal counterfactual fairness based on statistical bounds. Under the ignorability assumption (A ⊥⊥ (Y(1), Y(0), D(1), D(0))|X), we will establish sharp upper and lower bounds and prove theoretical results about the consistency of our estimation approaches. This provides the inferential foundation for testing fairness when principal strata are unobserved.

### Support

The strongest support for the core principal-stratum idea comes from [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850), which directly formalizes principal fairness using a principal stratum R_i and proves that, under A_i independent of R_i conditional on W_i, principal fairness implies counterfactual equalized odds and three statistical fairness criteria conditional on W_i, and under Assumption 1 is equivalent to those criteria; this validates the research plan's central move of restricting counterfactual fairness to a principal stratum and conditioning on pre-treatment covariates, although it does not estimate D(0), D(1), or derive sharp bounds for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)). The strongest empirical and estimation-strategy support comes from [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which uses an ignorability-style setup, sample splitting, outcome regression plus inverse-probability residual correction, and asymptotic normality to form 95 percent confidence intervals for counterfactual calibration and risk metrics; on child welfare data it shows that the counterfactual risk model was well calibrated by race under control and doubly robust evaluations while the observational evaluation suggested inequities because Black cases were more likely to receive risk-mitigating treatment, and on the out-of-home placement task the counterfactual model achieved AUROC 0.62 with 95 percent confidence interval 0.61 to 0.63 and AUPR 0.18 with 0.16 to 0.19, whereas the observational model achieved AUROC 0.48 with 0.46 to 0.49 and AUPR 0.13 with 0.11 to 0.14, supporting the plan's OR, IPS, and DR estimation strategy and the need to evaluate decisions counterfactually rather than observationally. [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a) provides further support by developing fair inference for path-specific effects with finite-sample inference, box-constraint formulations, regularized outcome models, Bayesian computation, and a treatment of non-identification on COMPAS and Adult, showing that causal fairness criteria can be formulated under unobserved counterfactuals and constrained even when not point-identified, though it targets path-specific effects rather than the Y(0)=Y(1) stratum. [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) supports the counterfactual-intervention logic by intervening on A along unfair pathways and using variational latent inference with MMD penalties; on a modified Berkeley Admission dataset with 4,526 applicants, 3,500 training, 1,026 test, and 1,000 Monte Carlo samples, it achieved 67.1 percent test accuracy versus 67.9 percent for the fair pathway-only model and 71.6 percent for the model using the unfair direct pathway, and on German Credit with 1,000 applicants, 700 training, 300 test, and 1,000 Monte Carlo samples, it reported little accuracy loss from latent correction, indicating that counterfactual fairness constraints can be operationalized with measurable accuracy tradeoffs. The weakest support is [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd), which shows that post-processing reassignment probabilities can be chosen by linear programming to satisfy equalized odds or equal opportunity while minimizing classification errors, but it operates on observed confusion-table rows rather than unobserved principal strata and reports no dataset, sample size, hardware, or quantitative empirical result.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is from [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which demonstrates that post-processing to equalize observational generalized false positive and false negative rates can induce imbalance in counterfactual generalized false positive and false negative rates, harming the group less likely to receive treatment, even though the original counterfactual model achieved counterfactual equalized odds; this contradicts any assumption that minimal observational post-processing automatically improves counterfactual or principal fairness, and requires the plan's post-processing to be constrained by doubly robust estimates of P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) rather than observed parity alone. The next strongest concern is from [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897), where counterfactual correction applied to both sexes on UCI Adult degraded fair accuracy to dummy-classifier level by 20,000 training steps, while correcting only females produced fair accuracies of 82.80 percent, 80.05 percent, 80.87 percent, and 76.44 percent after 5,000, 8,000, 15,000, and 20,000 steps; this warns that broad counterfactual fairness constraints can be unstable or overly aggressive, supporting the plan's principal-stratum restriction but contradicting a blanket counterfactual fairness objective if the method does not carefully limit changes to the Y(0)=Y(1) stratum. [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd) provides a weaker contradiction in scope: it treats fairness as equality of observed false positive and false negative rates across protected groups and uses linear programming over confusion-table reassignment probabilities, which does not address unobserved principal strata, ignorability, outcome regression, inverse propensity scoring, doubly robust estimation, or sharp statistical bounds, so it cannot validate the plan's central inferential claim. [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a) is less contradictory but still limits the evidence because it targets path-specific effects rather than principal strata and the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance values, so it does not confirm that sharp bounds for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) are attainable. [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850) is not a direct contradiction, but its assumption A_i independent of R_i conditional on W_i and equivalence results under Assumption 1 suggest that, under stronger conditional-independence assumptions, principal fairness may be linked to observable conditional fairness criteria, which could reduce the necessity of the proposed sharp bounds if the plan's ignorability assumption A independent of Y(1), Y(0), D(1), D(0) conditional on X is not needed or is too strong.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the doubly robust counterfactual evaluation design of [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b): use sample splitting, estimate treatment propensity and outcome regression nuisance models, form DR estimates of the conditional probabilities needed for P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)), and report asymptotic normality, confidence intervals, calibration, AUROC, AUPR, and counterfactual false positive and false negative rates; explicitly test that post-processing does not create counterfactual disparity, as their synthetic experiment showed observational equalization can harm the less-treated group. Second, align the theory with [(Imai, and Jiang, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5a271960396489b1f8708910e4e7b3488991850) by defining the principal stratum R_i, stating pre-treatment conditioning variables W_i or X, proving A_i independent of R_i conditional on W_i where possible, and clarifying how the proposed ignorability assumption A independent of Y(1), Y(0), D(1), D(0) conditional on X relates to, strengthens, or weakens the assumptions needed for principal fairness; if the equivalence results apply, show when bounds are unnecessary and when they remain sharp. Third, borrow the non-identification and constraint machinery of [(Nabi, and Shpitser, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87ce79fb2c5ef284dad9ec0d65add385ddaf040a) to justify sharp upper and lower bounds, box-constraint formulations, regularized outcome models, and Bayesian or frequentist finite-sample inference, while explicitly contrasting path-specific effects with the Y(0)=Y(1) principal stratum. Fourth, use the empirical tradeoff evidence from [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) to design validation experiments that report accuracy, fairness, and stability under increasing correction strength, including Monte Carlo sample sizes, training steps, MMD or distributional diagnostics, and failure modes such as degradation to dummy-classifier performance; include datasets comparable to COMPAS, Adult, Berkeley, German Credit, and child welfare before applying OULAD. Fifth, extend the post-processing idea of [(Berk et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9eacd7d43c95be4c4771bf1a324e200918e6c0cd) from observed confusion-table equalized odds to principal counterfactual fairness by solving an optimization that minimizes individual decision changes subject to DR-estimated PCF bounds, and report confusion-table changes, accuracy loss, and counterfactual fairness metrics. Finally, in the synthetic and OULAD experiments, report dataset sizes, DAG specifications, PC-algorithm outputs, base models, hyperparameters, hardware, statistical significance, and coverage of bounds so that the claimed consistency and sharpness are reproducible.

## We will develop an optimization-based evaluation method to test whether algorithms satisfy principal counterfactual fairness. The approach will involve solving constrained optimization problems where violations occur if the feasible region is empty or if certain probability bounds exclude zero. This turns the theoretical bounds into a practical diagnostic for whether a given decision rule meets the proposed fairness criterion.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed optimization-based evaluation comes from [(Nabi, and Benkeser, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39c98a671b47f9167cb44b7779775c7a285774b7), which solves a Lagrangian constrained optimization problem for a causal fairness functional and uses plug-in, inverse probability weighting, and augmented inverse probability weighting estimators that closely parallel the plan's OR, IPS, and DR estimators; in 1,000 replications with n=100, 200, 400, 800, and 1,600, correctly specified nuisance models kept the pathway-specific constraint near zero and approached optimal constrained risk, showing that a zero-constraint feasibility diagnostic is empirically meaningful when nuisance estimation is adequate. A somewhat weaker but directly relevant support is [(He et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8554e8e105c652214d4d23c53c410dd570b0750c), which uses constrained optimization over causal treatment-effect estimates and explicitly treats an infeasible region as a fairness violation, with EdGap experiments on about 2,000 counties, 19 features, 40 boosted causal trees, and parameters m_y=0.25, r_max=0.40, and m_r=0 or 1.0 showing that infeasibility shrinks as fairness constraints relax; this supports the plan's empty-feasible-region signal, although it is group-level rather than principal-stratum-level. Weaker still is [(Lara et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b846287f84a4c3b3cc0e25149a87db601c4fa92), which operationalizes counterfactual decision invariance by estimating counterfactual counterparts through non-regularized Kantorovich optimal transport and reports a counterfactual fairness rate P(D(0)=D(1)) with epsilon=0, delta=0.1, a lambda grid from 10^-4 to 10^1, and 10 train-test splits across Adult, COMPAS, Law School, and Communities and crimes; this supports measuring decision invariance and accuracy-fairness tradeoffs, but it does not use principal strata or probability-bound feasibility. Older work provides indirect support: [(Nabi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbe3df90d3ea2864fc983c40e3c83d901b2459b7) shows constrained maximum likelihood can impose box constraints on path-specific causal effects, with 5,000 observations, 20 percent missing outcomes, 100 repetitions, and KL/MSE diagnostics, while [(Zafar et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c64ee852f082f8f4e0113cfc1302b34e31539a2) demonstrates constrained optimization for group fairness on Adult with 45,222 subjects and Bank with 41,188 subjects using five 70/30 splits, decision boundary covariance, p%-rule, and accuracy-loss tradeoffs. Taken as an iterative sequence, the field has moved from group-boundary constraints to causal path-specific constraints, then to counterfactual decision invariance and constrained causal estimators, which collectively make the proposed constrained diagnostic plausible, but none directly validates principal counterfactual fairness.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to treating empty feasible regions or bound exclusion as a reliable PCF diagnostic comes from [(Nabi, and Benkeser, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39c98a671b47f9167cb44b7779775c7a285774b7), where misspecification of nuisance models, especially omitting cross-product terms, caused the inverse probability weighting and augmented inverse probability weighting constraint estimators to fail to satisfy the equality constraint and no estimator achieved optimal constrained risk; this implies that an infeasible or zero-excluding bound may reflect misspecified OR/IPS/DR components rather than a true principal-stratum violation. This newer result also tempers the older constrained MLE optimism in [(Nabi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbe3df90d3ea2864fc983c40e3c83d901b2459b7), which imposed a box constraint of -0.05 to 0.05 on path-specific effects but evaluated approximate fair-world distributions rather than fixed decision rules and reported higher prediction MSE for constrained methods. A second important contradiction is that the closest infeasibility-based method, [(He et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8554e8e105c652214d4d23c53c410dd570b0750c), interprets infeasible regions at the group level for treatment outcome and opportunity fairness, not individual principal counterfactual fairness; its EdGap results show infeasibility when m_y and r_max are too low because baseline county test-score differences cannot be closed, and it reports no numerical effect sizes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance, so the proposed method cannot assume that empty feasible regions automatically identify individual-level PCF violations. Weaker contradictions include [(Lara et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b846287f84a4c3b3cc0e25149a87db601c4fa92), whose counterfactual fairness rate is based on an empirical optimal-transport coupling rather than principal strata, uses deterministic learners, reports qualitative outcomes without p-values or confidence intervals, and has computational complexity O((n + m)nm log(n + m)) that may limit scalability to the 32,593-student OULAD setting; this suggests that decision-invariance metrics can be sensitive to the chosen counterfactual construction. Older work also contradicts the proposed causal individual-level focus: [(Zafar et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c64ee852f082f8f4e0113cfc1302b34e31539a2) targets group fairness via decision boundary covariance and p%-rule, not counterfactual invariance, and its regularized logistic regression baseline uses the sensitive attribute at test time, which the paper flags as failing disparate treatment and allowing reverse discrimination. None of these works evaluates the proposed PCF metric P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)) on OULAD, so the evidence is indirect and the feasibility diagnostic remains unvalidated for principal strata.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to validate the optimization-based evaluation against ground-truth principal strata in synthetic structural equation models before applying it to OULAD: generate random DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 directed edges as planned, but also include known Y(0), Y(1), D(0), D(1), and the principal stratum Y(0)=Y(1), then report false-positive and false-negative rates for empty feasible regions and bound-exclusion violations, confidence intervals, power, and sensitivity to sample size across Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, and Naive Bayes, because [(Nabi, and Benkeser, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39c98a671b47f9167cb44b7779775c7a285774b7) shows constraint satisfaction can fail under nuisance misspecification and [(He et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8554e8e105c652214d4d23c53c410dd570b0750c) shows infeasibility can be driven by baseline group differences rather than individual counterfactual violations. Second, make the feasibility diagnostic uncertainty-aware by estimating the probability bounds with OR, IPS, and DR, using bootstrap or influence-function confidence intervals, and declaring a violation only when the confidence interval excludes zero or the feasible region is empty under a pre-specified tolerance; report constraint values, dual variables, and Lagrangian diagnostics as in [(Nabi, and Benkeser, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39c98a671b47f9167cb44b7779775c7a285774b7) and [(Nabi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbe3df90d3ea2864fc983c40e3c83d901b2459b7), and include misspecification experiments that omit cross-product terms to mirror the failure modes in [(Nabi, and Benkeser, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39c98a671b47f9167cb44b7779775c7a285774b7). Third, align the OULAD evaluation with the plan's 32,593 students and 11 attributes by documenting PC-algorithm causal discovery, missingness, subgroup sizes, multiple-testing correction, and computational resources, since related works report limited hardware/software details and smaller or group-level datasets such as EdGap's 2,000 counties and Law's 163 schools. Fourth, add baselines and secondary metrics: report standard counterfactual fairness P(D(0)=D(1)) using the Lara-style counterfactual fairness rate with epsilon=0 and delta=0.1, group fairness metrics such as p%-rule and statistical parity from [(Zafar et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c64ee852f082f8f4e0113cfc1302b34e31539a2), path-specific effect constraints from [(Nabi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbe3df90d3ea2864fc983c40e3c83d901b2459b7), and infeasibility heatmaps over constraint parameters as in [(He et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8554e8e105c652214d4d23c53c410dd570b0750c). Finally, for post-processing, solve the minimal-change constrained optimization with explicit constraints against reverse discrimination and report the number of individual decision changes, accuracy loss, CF, PCF, and subgroup-level effects, ensuring that the method improves PCF more than CF as hypothesized while avoiding the test-time sensitive-attribute misuse identified in [(Zafar et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4c64ee852f082f8f4e0113cfc1302b34e31539a2).

## We will design an optimization-based post-processing method that adjusts unfair decisions with minimal individual changes. The approach will solve for optimal adjustment parameters ε(x) that ensure principal counterfactual fairness while minimizing the number of decision changes. This is the intervention method that makes existing algorithms satisfy the proposed fairness criterion with minimal decision changes.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) implements an optimization-based post-processing method for a binary predictor, solving a linear program over flip probabilities theta_{a,s} in [0,1]^4 to minimize expected weighted loss subject to counterfactual equalized odds, using doubly robust estimators with random forests and 5-fold cross-fitting; on COMPAS with 5,278 individuals, epsilon=0.05 flipped 9% of predictions, increased loss by 0.03, and reduced counterfactual false-positive and false-negative gaps to -0.05 and -0.03, while epsilon=0 flipped slightly less than 20% of predictions and raised loss to approximately 0.40, demonstrating that post-processing can enforce counterfactual constraints with quantified decision-change cost. Next, [(Ganguly et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/faf261c4dc30f71ab3c147e5fee070f780572cd6) directly supports the post-processing plus bounds architecture by proposing constrained optimization after training to reconstruct or adjust decisions when counterfactual quantities are unidentifiable, using c-component factorization and graphical criteria to bound counterfactual fairness, although it does not report quantitative outcomes or principal counterfactual fairness. [(Biega et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/630a718bead9ca7cee03bf9f1c7bd1db655d0ff7) supports constrained post-processing optimization with utility constraints: it uses an ILP solved with Gurobi, a feasibility threshold of 1e-7, a candidate pre-filter of 100, and NDCG constraints at rank 1 under the singular attention model or rank 5 under the geometric model with p=0.5, showing that fairness constraints can be embedded in reranking while monitoring quality, though it targets attention equity rather than principal counterfactual fairness. [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) supports post-prediction correction by subtracting a controlled fraction beta of a path-specific effect from predictions under a known linear structural equation model; on UCI Adult with 48,842 individuals, removing the sex path-specific effect of 3.716 gave fair accuracy of 78.87%, and full multi-level correction with beta=1 gave 77.92%, indicating that correction can preserve accuracy. [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) supports optimization under causal and counterfactual assumptions, using FAIRLEARNING to minimize an empirical loss over a causal posterior with MCMC latent samples when the posterior is intractable, but it is training-time and lacks quantitative outcomes. [(Chen et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/131640ac457b5d4253e1e488336d44ae58268a09) supports the plan's CF and CF-bound evaluation and baselines, with FLAP preprocessing achieving zero CF-metric in synthetic Example 1 and lower CF-bound on loan application data with 203,656 valid samples and COMPAS with 5,090 training and 1,697 test samples, though it is not post-processing. [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) supports counterfactual intervention and path-specific fairness, with variational latent inference and MMD penalties; on modified Berkeley with 4,526 applicants it achieved 67.1% accuracy close to the desired fair accuracy of 67.9%, and on Adult with 48,842 individuals it reported fair accuracies of 82.80, 80.05, 80.87, and 76.44% after 5,000, 8,000, 15,000, and 20,000 training steps for female-only correction. [(Bothmann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1718ca4d011265289a7b67514bcfa16ae15b57) supports constructing a counterfactual FiND world and evaluating individual prediction differences; RPID recovered FiND distributions with 0%, 0.4%, and 0% null rejections and rank correlation of 0.893, with German Credit warped-world accuracy of 70% for males and 75% for females, but it is training-time warping rather than post-processing.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that post-processing fairness constraints can force large or degenerate decision changes: [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) found that exact counterfactual equalized odds with epsilon=0 on COMPAS flipped slightly less than 20% of predictions and raised loss to approximately 0.40, and on child welfare data with over 30,000 calls and over 1,000 features, cost-sensitive post-processing returned the all-zero classifier when false positives were weighted more than 1.5 times false negatives and the all-one classifier when false negatives were weighted more than 2 times false positives, showing that optimizing weighted loss rather than explicit flip minimization can produce extreme solutions. A closely related contradiction is that counterfactual correction can destroy utility when applied broadly: [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) showed that on UCI Adult with 48,842 individuals, correcting both males and females degraded fair accuracy to dummy-classifier level after 20,000 steps, while female-only correction retained 76.44% to 82.80% accuracy, and the MMD value for the marital-status latent variable was reduced substantially only after 8,000 and 15,000 steps, indicating overcorrection risk. [(Biega et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/630a718bead9ca7cee03bf9f1c7bd1db655d0ff7) shows that constrained fairness optimization can become infeasible or revert to baseline: under exponential relevance with 100 subjects, the ILP could not satisfy the NDCG quality constraint for theta greater than or equal to 0.5 and rerankings became equivalent to the relevance heuristic, and under linear relevance fairness-adjusted methods with theta greater than 0 had higher unfairness than the objective baseline, so fairness constraints are not automatically satisfiable without utility loss. [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) contradicts the assumption that correction is cheap or assumption-light: it requires a known acyclic graph, known unfair paths, and a linear structural equation model; on Adult, full multi-level correction with beta=1 reduced accuracy to 77.92% from 78.87% for single-attribute correction, and no individual decision-change minimization or statistical significance is reported. [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) warns that the causal model M must be supplied or learned under explicit structural-equation assumptions and that counterfactual assumptions are unfalsifiable even with interventional data, which undermines direct verification of principal counterfactual fairness unless the plan's bounds are sharp and validated. [(Chen et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/131640ac457b5d4253e1e488336d44ae58268a09) shows that standard counterfactual fairness can fail when the non-sensitive condition does not hold: in synthetic Example 3 no algorithm achieved counterfactual fairness, and on adult income the CF-bound favored FLAP-2 with orthogonalization but was high for all methods, suggesting that fairness metrics can be unstable when assumptions are violated. [(Bothmann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1718ca4d011265289a7b67514bcfa16ae15b57) indicates that counterfactual-world construction is sensitive to direction and utility: reversed warping still recovered distributions but reduced non-rejection rates to 64% overall and 34% in the female subgroup for RPID, and warped German Credit accuracy shifted to 70% for males and 75% for females versus 71% for both groups in the real world, so counterfactual adjustments can alter group-specific performance. [(Ganguly et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/faf261c4dc30f71ab3c147e5fee070f780572cd6) is a weaker contradiction because it lacks quantitative outcomes, but its reliance on bounds rather than observed counterfactuals confirms that principal counterfactual fairness cannot be enforced by point estimates alone.

### Suggested Action

Most important: convert the proposed epsilon(x) post-processor into an explicit flip-minimization constrained program, using [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) as the template: define binary flip variables or flip probabilities, minimize the number or weighted number of flips, and enforce principal counterfactual fairness constraints using doubly robust, outcome regression, or inverse propensity scoring estimators with sample splitting or cross-fitting; report flip rate, loss, CF, PCF, and confidence intervals. Second, because PCF is unidentifiable, adopt the bounds-based constrained optimization logic of [(Ganguly et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/faf261c4dc30f71ab3c147e5fee070f780572cd6) and the CF-bound machinery of [(Chen et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/131640ac457b5d4253e1e488336d44ae58268a09): derive sharp PCF bounds from the principal stratum Y(0)=Y(1), enforce constraints only when bounds certify violation, use feasibility checks rather than assuming point identification, and include delta sensitivity analyses analogous to Chen's delta=0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, and 1. Third, add utility and feasibility safeguards from [(Biega et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/630a718bead9ca7cee03bf9f1c7bd1db655d0ff7): impose accuracy, loss, or NDCG-like utility constraints, use a solver with explicit feasibility tolerance such as 1e-7, pre-filter candidates if needed, and define a fallback when the PCF-feasible region is empty, such as relaxed epsilon(x) or amortized fairness over repeated decisions. Fourth, prevent overcorrection and degenerate solutions by borrowing diagnostics from [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) and [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf): monitor MMD or distributional gaps, accuracy, group-specific accuracy, and flip rates; restrict correction to the principal stratum or to individuals where Y(0)=Y(1) is plausible; use partial correction parameters analogous to beta in [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) rather than always full correction; and penalize all-zero or all-one solutions. Fifth, use causal-model and counterfactual-world diagnostics from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897), and [(Bothmann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1718ca4d011265289a7b67514bcfa16ae15b57): specify the DAG, path-specific unfair pathways, and FiND or warped-world baselines; report distribution tests, rank correlations, and individual prediction differences; and validate that the PC algorithm on OULAD recovers plausible disability-related causal structure before applying PCF. Finally, strengthen experimental validation by reporting computational resources, hyperparameters, solver runtimes, dataset splits, and statistical significance, because most related work omits these details; include FLAP-style preprocessing baselines from [(Chen et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/131640ac457b5d4253e1e488336d44ae58268a09), FAIRLEARNING-style causal training baselines from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774), and RPID or fairadapt warping baselines from [(Bothmann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad1718ca4d011265289a7b67514bcfa16ae15b57) to show that post-processing with minimal flips is preferable when decision-change cost is the objective. In the synthetic random-DAG experiments with 10 nodes and 40 edges and the OULAD experiment with 32,593 students and 11 attributes, report PCF bounds, flip rates, group-specific accuracy, and solver runtimes for each base model and estimator.

## We will implement three estimation approaches for the required conditional probabilities: Outcome regression (OR) estimator, Inverse propensity scoring (IPS) estimator, Doubly robust (DR) estimator. We will prove the asymptotic normality and consistency properties of these estimators. This supplies the statistical machinery needed to estimate the probabilities that support the bounds and evaluation tests.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Baba, and Ninomiya, 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16c9a96a3c99a9e45e04368b1b4d5a832cd8e82c), which directly develops inverse-probability-weighted and doubly robust estimators for marginal means under missingness or marginal structural models, estimates unknown propensity scores by maximum likelihood, augments IPS with a conditional outcome model, and shows the DR estimator is semiparametrically efficient when both the propensity score and outcome model are correctly specified and consistent when either is correctly specified. Its simulations with H=6, polynomial orders 0 through 5, true second-order structure, b=0.5, 0.3, or 0.1, N=100 or 200, 5000 repetitions, true propensity-score parameter vector (0.8, 1.0, 0.9, 0.7, 0.6), QICw penalty 12, and wCp penalty show finite-sample squared-error and model-selection behavior for known propensity scores, unknown propensity scores, and DR estimation, which is directly analogous to estimating the conditional probabilities needed for principal counterfactual fairness bounds. The next strongest support is [(Ninomiya, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/584ae03c1ba2f805c7ad490113475ffbc7d4ca12), which extends DR estimation to sparse causal-effect settings with IPCp, IPIC, and DRIC, using continuous linear/logit models with sigma^2=1 and target effect (theta2-theta1)/sqrt2, and discrete multinomial/logit models with H=4,6,8, N=200,400, m=5,10; its Monte Carlo results with 1,000 continuous iterations and 200 discrete iterations show DRIC and IPIC often outperform QICw in mean squared error and variable selection, including small samples p=8,16,32 and N=40,120, and LaLonde N=445 with subgroups N=74 and 39, supporting DR use under model uncertainty. Newer but less direct support is [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22), which reports quantitative variance reductions for direct-method, IPW, and DR/control-variate estimators using reduced-rank denoising on the Sahel RCT with 4,139 households, 36 baseline features, 7 outcomes, train/test 3,311/828, and dispersion reductions such as IPW 10.120 to 1.227 and DR/CV 10.244 to 1.231, suggesting that OR/IPW/DR-style estimators can be stabilized for probability-like quantities. The line is iterative: the 2016 DR/penalty framework is extended by the 2019 ATE/ATT operationalization, the 2020 AIPW interference application, the 2022 sparse DR criteria, and the 2024 variance-reduction extension, with newer work reinforcing but not replacing the older estimator logic. Weaker support is from [(Goetghebeur et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/baa94e38c7952f7d567e6ba0e80d9677e42d5580), which operationalizes OR, IPS, and AIPW/DR for ATE and ATT under no-unmeasured-confounding with balance and positivity checks, and [(Nabi et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6872ef595552b151af483ff10369d4a3356b163), which applies AIPW with random forests to counterfactual mean click probability under within-pageview interference on Bing PC traffic, showing the estimator family can be used in complex observational settings, although neither excerpt reports numerical performance for the fairness target.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the related work shows OR, IPS, and DR performance is sensitive to model selection, misspecification, positivity, and target definition, so the proposed method cannot assume that implementing the three estimators automatically yields valid principal counterfactual fairness bounds. [(Baba, and Ninomiya, 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16c9a96a3c99a9e45e04368b1b4d5a832cd8e82c) found that QICw often selected polynomial orders greater than three, which the paper judged unreasonable, and that uCp produced larger squared errors than QICw when the true structure was close to first order, indicating that naive model selection for the outcome or propensity components can degrade the OR/IPS/DR estimators the plan relies on. [(Ninomiya, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/584ae03c1ba2f805c7ad490113475ffbc7d4ca12) shows that DR-based criteria improve MSE but misspecification benefits are limited, active-set differences can be small, and binary LaLonde analyses lack ground truth, so DR consistency does not guarantee accurate estimation of principal-stratum probabilities in small subgroups or high-dimensional sparse settings. [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22) contradicts the plan's emphasis on asymptotic normality by providing only finite-sample generalization bounds under bounded outcomes and overlap, without proving asymptotic normality, and by applying DM/IPW/DR to scalarized policy values rather than calibrated probabilities, so its variance-reduction evidence does not directly validate normality-based fairness bounds. The iterative progression also shows that later work narrows the conditions under which earlier DR results apply. Weaker contradictions or limitations include [(Goetghebeur et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/baa94e38c7952f7d567e6ba0e80d9677e42d5580), which requires no-unmeasured-confounding, balance, and positivity but provides no numerical estimates, and [(Nabi et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6872ef595552b151af483ff10369d4a3356b163), which assumes partial interference within pageviews and excludes temporal interference, a setting not addressed by the plan's ignorability assumption; if OULAD or synthetic DAGs contain interference or unmeasured confounding, the OR/IPS/DR estimators may be biased.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should first specify the exact conditional probabilities to be estimated, such as P(Y(0)=Y(1), D(0), D(1) | X), P(D(a) | X), P(Y(a)=Y(a') | X), and principal-stratum indicators, and then derive influence functions for the OR, IPS, and DR estimators under cross-fitting, overlap, bounded propensity scores, and smoothness, so asymptotic normality is proved for the fairness estimands rather than generic causal means. Second, adopt the model-selection and misspecification safeguards evidenced by [(Baba, and Ninomiya, 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16c9a96a3c99a9e45e04368b1b4d5a832cd8e82c) and [(Ninomiya, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/584ae03c1ba2f805c7ad490113475ffbc7d4ca12): use wCp, DRIC, or IPIC-type penalties, compare OR/IPS/DR under misspecified propensity and outcome models, and report selection frequencies, mean squared error, coverage, and sensitivity to polynomial or sparse model order. Third, implement positivity, balance, and weight-stabilization diagnostics, including trimming or stabilized weights, especially for OULAD disability subgroups and small synthetic strata, because [(Goetghebeur et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/baa94e38c7952f7d567e6ba0e80d9677e42d5580) and [(Nabi et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6872ef595552b151af483ff10369d4a3356b163) show IPS/DR depend on overlap and flexible propensity/outcome modeling. Fourth, use cross-fitted random forests or gradient boosting for propensity and outcome models with probability calibration, as suggested by [(Nabi et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6872ef595552b151af483ff10369d4a3356b163), and consider reduced-rank or control-variate denoising from [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22) only if multiple outcome dimensions are present, while preserving the probability interpretation needed for fairness bounds. Finally, report reproducibility details, including sample sizes, random seeds, hyperparameters, hardware, train/test splits, confidence intervals, CF and PCF before and after post-processing, and decision-change counts, and validate on synthetic Erdős-Rényi DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 edges plus OULAD with 32,593 students, 11 attributes, binarized final grades, and PC-learned causal structure.

## We will generate data from structural equation models based on random DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 directed edges using the Erdős-Rényi model. We will test our approach using four different base models (Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, Naive Bayes) combined with the three estimation methods (OR, IPS, DR). This synthetic validation checks whether the framework and estimators recover expected fairness behavior across diverse causal structures and classifiers.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed synthetic validation is [(Kallus, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9361f57fc6a7d869ff67cfac5b5d963bdce82eb), because it uses a synthetic causal benchmark with known ground truth (true policy value 0.852, n=100 fixed covariate and treatment draws, 1000 conditional replications, five-treatment Gaussian mixture, sklearn gradient-boosted trees for outcome regression, Gurobi 7.0 for weight optimization) and directly compares inverse propensity weighting, doubly robust estimation, clipping/normalization, and outcome-regression variants. It reports that standard IPW with true propensities has zero bias but large standard deviation and RMSE, DR reduces bias relative to estimated-propensity IPW, and balanced DR reduces bias nearly to zero while increasing standard deviation; although no formal significance tests or exact RMSE values are reported, this supports the plan's core idea of using synthetic data to expose bias-variance tradeoffs among OR, IPS, and DR, and suggests the proposed 10-node/40-edge random-DAG experiment should report bias, SD, RMSE, and weight support rather than only CF/PCF point estimates. The next strongest support is [(Dudík et al., 2011-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ccf7658018981bf492d0c8d66277d22ebaac815), which evaluates the same estimator family in a bandit-feedback classification setting with UCI data, known uniform propensity 1/k, linear ridge direct loss model with one weight vector per action, roughly equal training/test splits, and 500 repeated transformations; it reports IPS and DR are unbiased when propensity is exact, direct method has larger bias under a misspecified linear model, and DR has lower variance/RMSE than IPS. This supports using known propensities in the SEM synthetic data and comparing OR, IPS, and DR, although it does not validate fairness metrics or the four base classifiers. A third strong support is [(Bottou et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b006aefa9c8be8c33138c4c5352f4d2d44378be9), which uses a randomized structural-equation-model experiment on 22 million Bing search result pages collected over five consecutive weeks, log-normal mainline-reserve randomization with rho=1 and sigma=0.3, 95% multiplier range [0.52, 1.74], page-level randomization, and reweighting to estimate counterfactual expectations; its 18% validation bucket matched reweighted estimates, supporting the use of SEM-based counterfactual reweighting and validation against known interventions. Fourth, [(Imbens, 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edd9b85508043176559c4e0ab8dfe3dbb77ff785) supports propensity-score adjustment, trimming, blocking, bias-adjusted matching, regression adjustment, and pseudo-outcome checks; in the lottery data with 496 individuals, trimming thresholds 0.0891 and 0.9109 left 323 units, pseudo-outcome estimates were not significant, and the preferred blocking estimate was about -5400 with SE about 1400, indicating that overlap and sensitivity diagnostics are essential for the proposed ignorability-based bounds. Fifth, [(Guo et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd68577e1b6fa63d06c6236b189fc1ce2843be13) supports DR estimation under hidden confounding in semi-synthetic BlogCatalog and Flickr networks, using two partial latent-confounder representations, fully connected neural networks with ELU activations except the last layer, logistic regression propensity models, self-normalized IPS weights, 60/20/20 splits, 10 runs, grid search, and RMSE/MAE; it reports consistent outperformance over kernel, weighted, direct, and DR baselines under kappa1=1 and kappa2=1 or 2, with one-tailed T-tests at 0.05 and less degradation as kappa2 increases from 1 to 2. Sixth, [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22) supports the OR/IPW/DR/CV estimator family in synthetic and real off-policy evaluation, with simulated n=100, p=8, k=5, r=2 and Sahel n=4139, 36 features, 7 outcomes, train/test 3311/828, and reports lower uncertainty for reduced-rank DM/IPW/CV than noisy DM/IPW/DR, e.g. noisy DM 1.836, 1.037, 11.959, 11.908 versus reduced-rank DM 0.909, 0.909, 11.461, 11.407. Seventh, [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) supports SEM-based counterfactual fairness validation using a known linear SEM, counterfactual sampling, and UCI Adult with 48,842 individuals, where removing a path-specific sex effect of 3.716 gave fair accuracy 78.87 percent and full multi-level correction of 10.65 with beta=1 gave 77.92 percent. Eighth, [(Helwegen et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/01175930928a46b256396a633187224329609149) supports causal counterfactual fairness evaluation on 94,274 Dutch social-welfare profiles with 85,274 training and 9,000 test points per split over 20 random splits, CEVAE mechanism estimation, and baselines MLP 0.691 +/- 0.007, random forest 0.683 +/- 0.003, logistic regression 0.690 +/- 0.004, showing that causal fairness can be tested with real data and multiple classifiers. Ninth, [(Islam et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0134a8e9224c0c22e0dd4751031cae494259973) supports the post-processing component by showing on COMPAS that post-processing approaches were more robust than pre- and in-processing under T1-T3 data errors, with errors affecting 50 percent of African-Americans and 10 percent of other races, and with only 2-5 percent accuracy/F1 drops and 5-10 percent fairness drops under T3, although it uses logistic regression and 18 variants of 13 approaches rather than the proposed random DAGs. Finally, [(Glymour et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b068f111b4b7f436bb99b945e3191d8061fe531b) supports the real-world OULAD step of learning causal structure with PC, but only under i.i.d., no unmeasured confounders, causal Markov, and faithfulness, and it provides asymptotic consistency rather than finite-sample guarantees for the 32,593-student, 11-attribute OULAD setting.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method's expectation that OR, IPS, and DR will stably recover expected fairness behavior across random DAGs and classifiers is [(Kallus, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9361f57fc6a7d869ff67cfac5b5d963bdce82eb), which shows that standard IPW with true propensities can have zero bias but large standard deviation and RMSE, that standard IPW-type weights used only about 10 to 16 of 100 observations while balanced weights used 88 to 94, and that balanced DR reduced bias nearly to zero while increasing standard deviation; this contradicts a simple assumption that the proposed IPS and DR estimators will be uniformly reliable without clipping, normalization, or balance-based weighting. A second strong contradiction is [(Bottou et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b006aefa9c8be8c33138c4c5352f4d2d44378be9), whose inner reweighted counterfactual confidence intervals widened sharply when the target reserve multiplier moved outside the randomized range explored by the 22 million-page Bing experiment, even though the 18% validation bucket matched estimates; this contradicts the proposed method if random 10-node/40-edge DAGs or OULAD propensity scores generate counterfactual targets outside observed support. Third, [(Imbens, 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edd9b85508043176559c4e0ab8dfe3dbb77ff785) contradicts the ignorability assumption in non-experimental settings: in the non-experimental training data with 185 treated men and 15,992 comparison controls, all four pseudo-outcome estimates were substantively large and rejected the null of zero effect, and initial normalized differences included values up to 2, casting doubt on unconfoundedness that the proposed principal-stratification bounds and PC-based OULAD analysis require. Fourth, [(Glymour et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b068f111b4b7f436bb99b945e3191d8061fe531b) contradicts relying on PC for OULAD unless its assumptions hold, because PC is asymptotically consistent only under i.i.d. sampling, no latent confounders, causal Markov, and faithfulness, and the provided sections do not report finite-sample error probabilities or confidence intervals for the estimated structure. Fifth, [(Guo et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd68577e1b6fa63d06c6236b189fc1ce2843be13) shows that hidden confounding degrades doubly robust and baseline estimators on BlogCatalog and Flickr when kappa2 increases from 1 to 2, even though the proposed network-based DR method degraded less; this contradicts the proposed method if the random DAGs or OULAD contain unmodeled confounding not captured by the SEM. Sixth, [(Islam et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0134a8e9224c0c22e0dd4751031cae494259973) contradicts the expectation that post-processing will always yield minimal decision changes with stable fairness gains, because under T3 missing-value imputation post-processing approaches still showed 2-5 percent drops in accuracy and F1 and 5-10 percent decreases in target fairness metrics, and causal computations incurred sharper runtime penalties than non-causal approaches. Seventh, [(Helwegen et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/01175930928a46b256396a633187224329609149) suggests that causal fairness interventions can be sensitive to model inputs: adding the sensitive attribute A to the auxiliary model produced accuracy 0.668 +/- 0.006 but statistical parity 0.949 +/- 0.012, lower than 0.964 +/- 0.011 without A, which contradicts a blanket assumption that including more causal information will monotonically improve fairness. Eighth, [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) contradicts the proposed method's novelty and generality by showing that path-specific counterfactual fairness can be evaluated with a fixed known linear SEM and plug-in correction, with UCI Adult accuracy dropping from 78.87 percent to 77.92 percent after full multi-level correction, indicating that fairness corrections can trade off accuracy and that fixed-graph validation may not capture the proposed random-DAG diversity. Finally, [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22) suggests that the proposed OR/IPW/DR pipeline may be less efficient than reduced-rank or denoised variants, because reduced-rank DM, IPW, and CV estimators reported lower uncertainty than noisy DM, IPW, and DR estimators in the Sahel test set, although it does not evaluate fairness.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add propensity-score overlap, support, and stabilization diagnostics to the OR, IPS, and DR estimators before claiming that the synthetic random-DAG validation recovers expected fairness behavior. Specifically, for each 10-node/40-edge Erdős-Rényi DAG and each base classifier, estimate or use the known propensity for the sensitive attribute, report its distribution, trimming thresholds, clipping/normalization, self-normalized IPS weights, and weight support, and report bias, standard deviation, RMSE, confidence intervals, and p-values for CF and PCF; this is directly motivated by [(Kallus, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9361f57fc6a7d869ff67cfac5b5d963bdce82eb), [(Dudík et al., 2011-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ccf7658018981bf492d0c8d66277d22ebaac815), [(Imbens, 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edd9b85508043176559c4e0ab8dfe3dbb77ff785), and [(Bottou et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b006aefa9c8be8c33138c4c5352f4d2d44378be9). The second most important action is to fully specify the synthetic data-generation and evaluation protocol: how the 10-node, 40-edge directed Erdős-Rényi graph is made acyclic, the topological ordering, edge-density 40/90, SEM coefficient distributions, noise variances, sample sizes, number of replications (at least 500-1000 as in [(Dudík et al., 2011-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ccf7658018981bf492d0c8d66277d22ebaac815) and [(Kallus, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9361f57fc6a7d869ff67cfac5b5d963bdce82eb)), random seeds, train/test splits, and how ground-truth CF and PCF are computed from the SEM. The third action is to compare not only OR, IPS, and DR but also clipped/normalized IPS, self-normalized IPS, balanced or variance-regularized weights, reduced-rank/denoised outcome models, and path-specific fairness baselines, with logistic regression, SVM, random forest, and naive Bayes tuned by grid search or default settings and reported hyperparameters, as supported by [(Kallus, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9361f57fc6a7d869ff67cfac5b5d963bdce82eb), [(Guo et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd68577e1b6fa63d06c6236b189fc1ce2843be13), [(Nwankwo et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bc457f24487b2035cf8a583174963dcfb563a22), and [(Helwegen et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/01175930928a46b256396a633187224329609149). The fourth action is to validate counterfactual estimates against known interventions or pseudo-outcomes: in the synthetic setting, create validation buckets with known counterfactual targets and compare reweighted estimates, as in [(Bottou et al., 2012-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b006aefa9c8be8c33138c4c5352f4d2d44378be9); in OULAD, use pseudo-outcome or negative-control checks and sensitivity analysis for unconfoundedness, as in [(Imbens, 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edd9b85508043176559c4e0ab8dfe3dbb77ff785). The fifth action is to test robustness to hidden confounding, model misspecification, and data errors by adding latent confounders to the random DAGs, corrupting features in the style of [(Islam et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0134a8e9224c0c22e0dd4751031cae494259973), and varying hidden-confounding strength as in [(Guo et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fd68577e1b6fa63d06c6236b189fc1ce2843be13), while reporting accuracy, F1, CF, PCF, statistical parity, and runtime. The sixth action is to treat the OULAD PC step as assumption-sensitive rather than definitive: report PC's i.i.d., no-latent-confounder, causal-Markov, and faithfulness assumptions, compare learned graphs to expert or alternative specifications, and provide finite-sample uncertainty or sensitivity bounds for the 32,593-student, 11-attribute dataset, as cautioned by [(Glymour et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b068f111b4b7f436bb99b945e3191d8061fe531b). The seventh action is to report the fairness-accuracy tradeoff and post-processing cost explicitly, including number of decision changes, accuracy/F1 drops, and causal computation runtime, because [(Islam et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0134a8e9224c0c22e0dd4751031cae494259973) and [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) show that fairness corrections can reduce accuracy and causal methods can be slower. The least important but still useful action is to include a small real-data or semi-synthetic sanity check with a known sensitive attribute and outcome, such as the UCI Adult or social-welfare style settings in [(Mhasawade, and Chunara, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b4a906539790c911bdadb941e192667155eeb44) and [(Helwegen et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/01175930928a46b256396a633187224329609149), to confirm that the synthetic CF/PCF behavior transfers to more realistic feature scales.

## We will use the Open University Learning Analytics Dataset (OULAD) containing 32,593 students and 11 attributes. We will treat disability as the sensitive attribute and binarized final grades as the outcome, learning causal structures using the PC algorithm and testing our framework across different subgroup divisions. This real-world experiment grounds the method in a high-stakes educational setting and tests causal-structure learning with a protected attribute.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed OULAD disability experiment comes from [(Verger et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc85ccd4fb46894b38c654ae4505f481aa11fa0d), which uses the same OULAD population of 32,593 samples and 28,785 students, binarized Pass/Fail with Distinction coded as Pass, disability as a binary sensitive attribute, and course-specific BBB and FFF models; it reports disability imbalance of 91.2%-8.8% in BBB and 91.7%-8.3% in FFF, evaluates MADD with epsilon=0.01 and threshold 0.5, and shows model-dependent MADD values, e.g., decision tree average 0.73 versus logistic regression 1.71 in BBB, with ABROCA comparisons showing that performance-oriented fairness metrics can miss distributional behavior, supporting the need for course and subgroup-specific fairness evaluation and showing that data imbalance alone does not determine unfairness. The next strongest support for the post-processing component is [(Verger et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6332492f49736fdd695ff7ea1a11b3ffbcd2dd01), which directly applies a post-processing fairness intervention to OULAD course BBB with 4,740 unique students, a logistic classifier, a 70-15-15 split, a lambda grid of 1,000 values in [0,1], and theta=0.5 weighting accuracy and MADD, reducing fairness loss from 0.431 to 0.158 while accuracy loss changed only from 0.332 to 0.336; this supports the plan's post-processing idea that fairness can be improved with small decision changes, although it targets gender and MADD rather than disability and PCF. The next strongest support is subgroup and intersectional auditing: [(Gardner et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/464506737f81a39ff28ef95f311ebd37c3f2ecf0) uses four institutions, L2-regularized logistic regression, LightGBM, and multilayer perceptrons, evaluates AUC and AUC gap across sex and URM intersections, reports that z-tests for AUC differences between local and voting or stacked models were not significant at alpha=0.05 with p-values greater than 0.1, finds that 20 of 24 transfer schemes have significantly nonzero AUC gaps, and finds no evidence of a fairness-accuracy tradeoff, supporting the plan's subgroup divisions and statistical testing. [(Yu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b91625df3785aaeafd41e778f3fa8fd616152827) uses 2,093 students in 10 fully online introductory STEM courses, compares institutional, LMS clickstream, and survey feature sets with multivariate imputation, computes FPR and FNR disparity ratios, applies one-sided two-proportion z-tests, and shows that removing individual institutional features does not eliminate subgroup bias, supporting subgroup fairness metrics and caution against simple feature exclusion. Counterfactual educational fairness is supported by [(Hu, and Rangwala, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d16e6c9b98c8ed2240988379d847d10f61a9ce16), whose MCCM uses a counterfactual attribute flip for gender and race in course-specific at-risk prediction on 10 years of George Mason data, top five majors, courses with at least 300 students, 75-15-15 splits, two hidden ReLU layers, dropout, and a KL-divergence penalty, and reports the best discrimination mitigation among compared methods, with logistic regression average discrimination of 7.3%, showing that counterfactual fairness can be operationalized in education and that fairness often trades off with accuracy. [(Deho et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3bfb1667a5a767cd161565d5aa428562f7dac628) is conceptually adjacent because its EqualRelevant balancing makes demographic groups comparable on relevant features while varying the protected attribute, uses grid search with 5-fold cross-validation, a 2019 test set, and SHAP values, although it reports no quantitative outcomes. [(Mohammed et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f949434b417f4211c3d6908eb23eac355ab3403) shows that flipping a binary treatment feature and using active learning with a linear SVM, C=1, alpha in [0, 0.5], and K=10 can improve accuracy and AUC, with reported 3-9% AUC gains over a counterfactual baseline, supporting the feasibility of counterfactual manipulation but not fairness. Read iteratively, the older representation-parity method in [(Zemel et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c3303d173c055592ef923235837e1cbc6bd986), which used German, Adult, and Health datasets, L-BFGS, A_x fixed at 0.01, and discrimination and consistency metrics, is superseded by later educational counterfactual and subgroup audits, which motivate the proposed causal PCF approach, while [(Chinta et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d77ec84060e768d443d3967ded168dfd73598d) provides weaker but relevant motivation by positioning OULAD as a benchmark for fine-grained individual and adaptive-system bias analysis.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the only OULAD disability-specific evidence does not validate the proposed causal PCF pipeline and instead shows course-specific, preprocessing-sensitive fairness. [(Verger et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc85ccd4fb46894b38c654ae4505f481aa11fa0d) removed 4% of samples with missing poverty information and 24% course withdrawals, leaving 19,964 distinct student samples rather than the proposed 32,593, and found course-specific distributions and model-dependent MADD values with epsilon=0.01 and threshold 0.5, so a single PC graph over all 32,593 students and 11 attributes may not capture course-specific causal structure. [(Verger et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6332492f49736fdd695ff7ea1a11b3ffbcd2dd01) selected lambda on only about 700 validation samples and noted that fairness loss continued decreasing, suggesting that post-processing for PCF may be unstable, especially with disability minority rates of 8.3%-8.8% and course-specific subgroups. A second contradiction is metric mismatch: related OULAD and educational fairness work evaluates observed subgroup disparities or distributional MADD, not potential-outcome PCF; [(Gardner et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/464506737f81a39ff28ef95f311ebd37c3f2ecf0) AUC gaps and [(Yu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b91625df3785aaeafd41e778f3fa8fd616152827) FPR and FNR disparity ratios can be significantly nonzero even when counterfactual fairness might hold, and vice versa, so the plan's CF and PCF metrics are not directly benchmarked by these studies. [(Hu, and Rangwala, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d16e6c9b98c8ed2240988379d847d10f61a9ce16) uses non-causal attribute-flip counterfactuals and reports no statistical significance tests, so it supports counterfactual fairness but not principal strata or PC algorithm identifiability. A third contradiction is that the causal assumptions are unsupported: [(Yu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b91625df3785aaeafd41e778f3fa8fd616152827) shows that removing institutional features such as gender does not eliminate subgroup bias, implying unobserved or structural confounding may persist, while the plan's ignorability assumption that A is independent of the potential outcomes given X may be too strong for OULAD observational data; no related work validates the PC algorithm with disability, missingness, withdrawals, or course heterogeneity. [(Mohammed et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f949434b417f4211c3d6908eb23eac355ab3403) uses counterfactual flipping for accuracy without a learned causal graph, showing that binary attribute manipulation alone is insufficient for causal fairness. Older [(Zemel et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c3303d173c055592ef923235837e1cbc6bd986) statistical parity can reduce discrimination but does not address causal effect, and later subgroup audits show disparities persist, contradicting the idea that simple fairness constraints are enough. [(Deho et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3bfb1667a5a767cd161565d5aa428562f7dac628) provides no quantitative outcomes, so it cannot support the expected PCF improvements, and [(Chinta et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d77ec84060e768d443d3967ded168dfd73598d) only motivates OULAD without methodological evidence. In addition, none of the related works reports hardware, runtime, random seeds, or PC-algorithm settings, so the proposed method's reproducibility and computational feasibility remain untested.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify OULAD preprocessing and course or subgroup design before applying the PC algorithm: follow [(Verger et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc85ccd4fb46894b38c654ae4505f481aa11fa0d) by reporting how missing poverty, withdrawals, and course-specific samples are handled; if using the full 32,593 students, justify imputation or sensitivity analysis, and if using a reduced sample such as 19,964, state it explicitly; use course-specific or subgroup-stratified 70-15-15 or 75-15-15 splits and report disability imbalance per course. The second most important action is to validate the PC causal structure rather than assume it: run PC with stability selection, compare graphs across courses and subgroup divisions, test faithfulness, use FCI or sensitivity analysis for unobserved confounders, report edge confidence, and show how PCF bounds change when edges involving disability are absent or present; use the synthetic random DAG experiments to benchmark recovery. The third most important action is to make PCF estimation robust and auditable: implement OR, IPS, and DR estimators with positivity diagnostics, bootstrap confidence intervals, and multiple-testing correction for subgroup divisions; report CF and PCF before and after post-processing, decision changes, accuracy loss, AUC, FPR, FNR, MADD, and ABROCA; report hardware, software versions, random seeds, and PC runtime because related works omit these details; and compare against baselines from related work, including logistic regression, k-nearest neighbors, decision tree, and naive Bayes from [(Verger et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc85ccd4fb46894b38c654ae4505f481aa11fa0d), LFR from [(Zemel et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c3303d173c055592ef923235837e1cbc6bd986), MCCM from [(Hu, and Rangwala, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d16e6c9b98c8ed2240988379d847d10f61a9ce16), CCRAL from [(Mohammed et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2f949434b417f4211c3d6908eb23eac355ab3403), MADD post-processing from [(Verger et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6332492f49736fdd695ff7ea1a11b3ffbcd2dd01), and EqualRelevant balancing from [(Deho et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3bfb1667a5a767cd161565d5aa428562f7dac628). The fourth most important action is to redesign post-processing selection to avoid the small-validation instability seen in [(Verger et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6332492f49736fdd695ff7ea1a11b3ffbcd2dd01): use larger validation sets, cross-validation, or constrained optimization with explicit accuracy-loss and decision-change penalties, and report lambda or epsilon selection, subgroup-specific PCF improvements, and whether improvements are larger for PCF than CF as hypothesized. The fifth most important action is to include subgroup fairness audits using AUC gap and disparity ratios with z-tests, as in [(Gardner et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/464506737f81a39ff28ef95f311ebd37c3f2ecf0) and [(Yu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b91625df3785aaeafd41e778f3fa8fd616152827), across intersectional subgroups such as disability by gender, poverty, course, and major if available, and to use [(Chinta et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d77ec84060e768d443d3967ded168dfd73598d) only as motivation for OULAD's suitability rather than as evidence for the PCF method.

## We will evaluate performance using two key metrics: Counterfactual fairness (CF): P(D(0) = D(1)) and Principal counterfactual fairness (PCF): P(D(0) = D(1)|Y(0) = Y(1)). We will measure percentage improvements in both metrics before and after applying our post-processing approach, expecting larger improvements in PCF since our method specifically targets the population where Y(0) = Y(1). This defines the empirical success criteria and links the post-processing algorithm to the intended fairness target.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed CF/PCF post-processing evaluation comes from [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf), which implements an optimal fair derived predictor by four flip probabilities theta over protected attribute A and input prediction S, solves a linear program minimizing weighted loss subject to counterfactual false positive and false negative rate constraints, and estimates coefficients with doubly robust cross-fitting and random forests. On COMPAS with 5,278 individuals, 3,175 African-American and 2,103 Caucasian, two-year recidivism, and treatment defined as leaving jail within three days, the input predictor had loss 0.36, cFPR difference -0.24, and cFNR difference 0.16; epsilon=0.05 reduced the differences to -0.05 and -0.03, flipped 9% of scores, increased risk by 0.03, and used theta=(0, 0.91, 0.23, 1), while epsilon=0 produced loss about 0.40 and slightly less than 20% flips. This directly supports the proposed plan's post-processing, minimal decision changes, and use of doubly robust estimation, although it targets counterfactual equalized odds rather than PCF. Iteratively, [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b) also uses post-processing and doubly robust estimators for counterfactual generalized error rates, and its warning that observational post-processing can create counterfactual imbalance supports the proposed method's explicit CF/PCF target. Next, [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169) supports the proposed bounds-based evaluation by deriving tighter path-specific counterfactual fairness bounds using response-function variables and linear expressions over response-function probabilities; on synthetic data true values fall within bounds, and on a second synthetic dataset previous Markovian bounds -0.2605 to 0.2656 around tau=0.1 are replaced by a lower bound of 0.1772, changing the discrimination judgment, while another group's bounds -0.0783 to -0.0212 indicate fairness; the Adult experiment with 65,123 records and O={age, edu, marital-status} also shows tighter bounds. This supports deriving sharp bounds for PCF and using optimization feasibility. [(Wu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fda1a576e706718bc6b72b8c68c6c65525763d) earlier validates bounds for ordinary counterfactual fairness on a synthetic discrete SCM with five binary endogenous variables and 100,000 examples, and on Adult with 65,123 records, a PC-learned graph using a 0.01 conditional-independence threshold and three-tier temporal ordering, showing A1 and CF satisfy tau=0.05 while BL and A3 fail and CF has higher accuracy than A1; this supports the proposed use of bounds and base classifiers such as logistic regression and SVM, but not PCF. [(Makhlouf et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5752891be570cdfddad5f2649c6619f14b70d309) supports the proposed OR/IPS/DR estimation strategy by applying inverse propensity weighting, propensity-score matching, double-robust estimation, stratification, and mediation imputation on Communities and crime with 1,994 communities, COMPAS with 5,915 individuals, German credit with 1,000 applicants, and Berkeley admission with 4,526 applicants, showing causal estimators often reduce discrimination relative to total variation and IPW improves standardized mean differences, for example reducing the unemployment-rate covariate absolute standardized mean difference from 0.68 to 0.078, though it does not estimate CF or PCF. [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) supports the CF metric P(D(0)=D(1)) by training g_theta(U,X_A) under an SCM and using MCMC latent sampling when P_M(U|x,a) is not analytic, making individual counterfactual invariance an explicit objective, but it is in-processing and does not evaluate PCF or post-processing. [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123) supports causal adjustment and counterfactual error-rate targeting: in a COMPAS-style simulation with X, W, Z, and D, removing W and using C={Z} makes the counterfactual direct error identifiable, and f_ctf- trained on {X, Z} achieves 72.1% accuracy and a counterfactual direct error rate of -0.001, showing counterfactual constraints can eliminate direct discrimination, though at an accuracy cost relative to 90.4% unconstrained and constrained baselines. [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339) supports post-hoc counterfactual adjustment of fitted local causal modules by optimizing a ReferencePoint mapping over objectionable edges; on UCI Adult it improves least-advantaged approval without flipping sex, but it uses approval rates rather than CF or PCF. [(Barker et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c3bb8cc846948a729eeeec2819677779dd3ffa7) provides newer evidence that counterfactual individual consistency can be improved, reducing mean absolute regular/counterfactual metric differences from 0.0277 to 0.0108 on German Credit, 0.3303 to 0.0503 on CivilComments-WILDS, 0.2563 to 0.1207 on BiasBios, 0.4618 to 0.1001 on CelebA, and 0.1468 to 0.0125 on UTKFace, but via in-processing FairVIC with lambda_acc=0.1 and lambda_inv=0.9 rather than post-processing and without P(D(0)=D(1)). [(Li et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da90b4db07987f908b7fdf5ba32ff9e87dacfc68) weakly supports sensitive-attribute invariance in recommendation by driving attacker AUC near 0.5 with BPR and adversarial training on MovieLens-1M and an Insurance dataset, but it does not estimate CF or PCF. [(Pearl, 2001-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8574a62874312b81347438b1566cdb1c6d5abe5) provides the weakest but foundational support for potential-outcome direct-effect identification, justifying counterfactual comparisons under protected-attribute interventions, though it gives no fairness metric, PCF, or post-processing.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed expectation that post-processing will improve counterfactual fairness is [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), which shows that post-processing to equalize observational generalized false positive and false negative rates can create imbalance in counterfactual generalized false positive and false negative rates; the original counterfactual model is reported to satisfy counterfactual equalized odds, while the post-processed model creates disparity that harms the group less likely to receive treatment. This directly contradicts any post-processing design that optimizes observational parity rather than the proposed CF and PCF targets. The next strongest contradiction is [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), where the constrained classifier f_er reduced the observed disparate error rate from -0.476 to -0.238 but had a larger counterfactual direct error rate of 0.620 than f_opt at 0.381; after removing W, direct discrimination remained significant for f_er- at 0.015 and f_opt- at -0.066, and the counterfactually constrained f_ctf- achieved only 72.1% accuracy compared with 90.4% for f_opt and f_er. This contradicts the assumption that standard disparate-error constraints or non-counterfactual post-processing will automatically improve counterfactual fairness, and it highlights a substantial accuracy cost. [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) provides a further contradiction to large fairness gains without utility collapse: on UCI Adult, a PSE-constrained baseline achieved 72% test accuracy versus 82% unconstrained; on Berkeley Admission, fair accuracy was 67.1% versus 71.6% for a model using the unfair direct sex pathway; and after 20,000 training steps, fair accuracy fell to dummy-classifier level when correction was applied to both sexes, while female-only fair accuracy declined from 82.80% at 5,000 steps to 76.44% at 20,000 steps. This contradicts the proposed success criteria if percentage PCF improvements are reported without accuracy, risk, or decision-change tradeoffs. [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339) contradicts the assumption that causal post-hoc fairness automatically benefits the protected group: on UCI Adult, a previous causal fairness baseline further decreases the already low approval rate for the least-advantaged female group in the low-approval regime, although the reference-point method improves approval rates, it does not report CF, PCF, percentage improvements, or statistical significance. [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) also contains a cautionary contradiction: in child welfare data with over 30,000 hotline calls and over 1,000 features, cost-sensitive post-processing returned near constant-zero classifiers when false positives were weighted more than 1.5 times false negatives and near constant-one classifiers when false negatives were weighted more than 2 times false positives, and in simulations exact counterfactual equalized odds increased loss by 0.10, showing that post-processing can be unstable or utility-costly even when it targets counterfactual error rates. Weaker contradictions include [(Wu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fda1a576e706718bc6b72b8c68c6c65525763d), where baseline logistic regression and SVM classifiers fail the tau=0.05 counterfactual fairness condition on Adult, and A1 discards more information than CF, indicating that ordinary CF is not guaranteed and that bounds are needed, but the work does not address PCF. The weakest contradiction is the absence of direct evidence for the proposed PCF metric: none of the cited works estimates P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)), reports percentage PCF improvements, or validates a post-processing algorithm specifically for the principal stratum, so the proposed success criteria are not directly supported by the related work.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the post-processing algorithm explicitly optimize and evaluate CF and PCF rather than observational parity, because [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b) shows observational post-processing can worsen counterfactual fairness. The proposed LP or constrained optimization should include constraints or objectives on estimated P(D(0)=D(1)) and P(D(0)=D(1)|Y(0)=Y(1)), use OR, IPS, and DR estimators with cross-fitting as in [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) and [(Makhlouf et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5752891be570cdfddad5f2649c6619f14b70d309), and report before-and-after percentage improvements with confidence intervals. The second most important action is to derive and report sharp statistical bounds for PCF and use them in an optimization-based feasibility test, following the response-function and linear-programming bounding approach of [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169) and the earlier CF bounds of [(Wu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fda1a576e706718bc6b72b8c68c6c65525763d); for OULAD, where the causal graph is learned with the PC algorithm, the plan should report sensitivity to graph uncertainty, multiple plausible DAGs, and the 0.01 conditional-independence threshold used in comparable Adult experiments. The third most important action is to quantify utility, decision changes, and stability, because [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf) reports flips, loss, and risk, [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) shows accuracy can collapse to dummy level, and [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339) shows a causal fairness baseline can worsen least-advantaged approval; the proposed method should report flip rates, loss or accuracy, risk, approval rates, group-level changes, and impose caps or regularization to avoid near-constant classifiers and large utility losses. The fourth most important action is to validate the OR, IPS, and DR estimators on synthetic random DAGs with 10 nodes and 40 directed edges using ground-truth abduction-action-prediction, as in [(Wu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fda1a576e706718bc6b72b8c68c6c65525763d), while checking positivity, bounded propensity scores, covariate balance, and cross-fitting diagnostics as in [(Makhlouf et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5752891be570cdfddad5f2649c6619f14b70d309); this is essential because PCF conditions on an unobserved stratum Y(0)=Y(1). The fifth most important action is to compare against strong baselines, including unconstrained models, CF-only models, counterfactual equalized odds post-processing from [(Mishler et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d8acbdd2820def27be114091b85ec39dfdc9baf), path-specific fairness methods from [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897) and [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169), causal fairness baselines from [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339), and in-processing counterfactual fairness from [(Kusner et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/043f084e379a44608c470059c2aa174a323e9774) and [(Barker et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c3bb8cc846948a729eeeec2819677779dd3ffa7), using comparable datasets such as COMPAS, Adult, German Credit, Berkeley Admission, and OULAD with reported sizes, splits, hyperparameters, seeds, and hardware. The sixth most important action is to incorporate path-specific and direct-effect reasoning, motivated by [(Pearl, 2001-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8574a62874312b81347438b1566cdb1c6d5abe5), [(Zhang, and Bareinboim, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6bb8e5c50eb219ce6344523bf9ebf0afc8b53123), and [(Wu et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1512a1039978bbefc9135b969f81bb5fc6ee169), to ensure that the principal stratum Y(0)=Y(1) is not conflated with legitimate causal pathways such as disability affecting athletic performance versus academic performance. The least important but still necessary action is to improve reproducibility and statistical rigor by reporting solver details, random seeds, confidence intervals, significance tests, and multiple-testing corrections, because many related works, including [(Wu et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fda1a576e706718bc6b72b8c68c6c65525763d), [(Chiappa, and Gillam, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8993bfbe06c0480bcb42233f058ef419278b8897), [(Coston et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5feed60ea2c0aaaca1e93283a726390f3b7ed9b), [(Makhlouf et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5752891be570cdfddad5f2649c6619f14b70d309), [(Tang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/677d5dd770481f82723c20eccad3b9fc3a987339), and [(Barker et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c3bb8cc846948a729eeeec2819677779dd3ffa7), do not provide full significance tests or complete implementation details.
