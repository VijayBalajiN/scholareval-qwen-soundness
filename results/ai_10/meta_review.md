## To prepare and structure the collected scientific content: We will use pylatexenc to convert LaTeX mathematical expressions into plain text and develop regular expression matching to identify and extract sub-figure captions. We will categorize figures into seven major types and extract sub-captions using automated regular expression matching.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method is that subfigure-level structure is common enough to justify dedicated extraction: [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) manually audited 2,000 arXiv figures, retained 1,926 valid figures, and found that 35.72 percent contained subfigures, with subfigure types including graph plots, bar charts, and scatter plots. This directly supports the research plan's need for Subfigure-to-Subcaption and Subcaption-to-Subfigure matching in MMS CIQA, because a Nature Communications dataset that treats each figure as a single caption would miss a substantial fraction of structured content. A second, more indirect support is that regex-based extraction of figure-associated text can be useful for scientific captioning: [(Cao, and Liu, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27d16a7f2ce8f2b787b34ff1f9b4fece079700c3) used a regular expression to extract 100-character windows around figure mentions, combined them with title and abstract, and achieved BLEU 4.92 and ROUGE-L F1 0.36 for image-plus-text captioning on 416,000 arXiv graph plots, outperforming a CNN+LSTM baseline. A third support is that automated figure-type structuring is feasible: [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) built an arXiv figure-caption pipeline that classified figure types and reported high precision for identifying graph plots without subfigures, although its taxonomy was limited to graph plots, bar charts, and scatter plots rather than the proposed seven major types. The weakest support is that [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) showed 76.68 percent of caption information could be recovered from figure-mentioning paragraphs plus OCR text, suggesting that structured text extraction around figures is valuable, but it did not test pylatexenc, sub-caption regexes, or a seven-type taxonomy.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the related works directly validates the proposed combination of pylatexenc LaTeX-to-plain-text conversion and regular-expression extraction of sub-figure captions. [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) detected and removed subfigures rather than extracting sub-captions, and its FigureSeparator was described as having high precision for graph plots that do not contain subfigures, suggesting that subfigure content may be error-prone and that a removal or fallback strategy may be safer than regex extraction. A second contradiction is that newer work moved away from subfigure-level extraction: [(Cao, and Liu, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27d16a7f2ce8f2b787b34ff1f9b4fece079700c3) used the no-subfigure subset of 416,000 arXiv graph plots and extracted in-text reference windows rather than sub-captions, implying that subfigure handling remains unresolved for captioning pipelines. A third contradiction is that [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) relied on OCR text, contextual word embeddings, and a high softmax threshold of 0.99 to align extracted text with captions, rather than regex matching, indicating that strict alignment and validation are needed to avoid false extractions. A fourth contradiction is that the proposed seven major figure types are not supported by the related work: [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) used only graph plots, bar charts, and scatter plots; [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) focused on line charts; and [(Cao, and Liu, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27d16a7f2ce8f2b787b34ff1f9b4fece079700c3) focused on graph plots, so the broader Nature Communications taxonomy requires independent validation. The weakest contradiction is that the related captioning results are not directly comparable to the proposed benchmark: [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) reported a low CNN+LSTM BLEU-4 of .0291, while [(Cao, and Liu, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27d16a7f2ce8f2b787b34ff1f9b4fece079700c3) found that a text-only model achieved BLEU 6.71 and ROUGE-L F1 0.30, outperforming its image-plus-text model on BLEU, which suggests that text extraction quality, model capacity, and compute constraints can dominate captioning outcomes.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a validation protocol for pylatexenc and regex-based sub-caption extraction before full-scale Nature Communications collection: sample articles across all 72 disciplines, manually label subfigure boundaries and sub-captions, and report precision, recall, F1, conversion failure rate, and inter-rater reliability, using the audit style of [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) but with multiple annotators and statistical significance tests. The second most important action is to combine regex candidates with OCR and embedding-based alignment, inspired by [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80), using a high-confidence threshold analogous to its 0.99 softmax threshold to reduce false sub-caption matches. The third most important action is to validate the seven major figure types with a human-audited classification sample, reporting the distribution and confusion matrix, because the related work only supports narrower taxonomies such as graph plots, bar charts, scatter plots, and line charts. The fourth action is to include article-level textual grounding as a fallback or auxiliary input, extracting title, abstract, and figure-mention windows as in [(Cao, and Liu, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27d16a7f2ce8f2b787b34ff1f9b4fece079700c3), while explicitly tracking whether a figure is single-caption or subcaptioned rather than silently using a no-subfigure subset. The least important but still useful action is to document preprocessing reproducibility, including pylatexenc version, regex patterns, OCR settings, hardware, runtime, and a pilot dataset size, so that the extraction pipeline can be compared against the arXiv-based baselines and adapted to Nature Communications.

## To test caption generation for scientific figures: Scientific Figure Captioning (MMS CICAP): Models will generate captions for scientific figures in both ungrounded and abstract-grounded settings.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed MMS CICAP caption-generation method comes from [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), which directly implements the same task on Nature Communications data, using 131,393 articles, 742,273 figures, 72 subjects, 514,054 sub-captions, pylatexenc LaTeX conversion, regular-expression sub-caption extraction, ungrounded and abstract-grounded captioning, and the planned metric suite of ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, G-EVAL, plus PhD expert evaluation. This is the most direct evidence that the proposed benchmark is feasible and aligned with the research plan, although the excerpt does not provide numeric captioning outcomes. The next strongest support is [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), which evaluates MLLM scientific figure caption generation from about 94k SciCap single-sentence pairs on 100 computer-science test samples using LLM judges and reports GPT-4 Turbo mean rank 2.16 ± 1.12, Gemini 1.5 Flash 3.00 ± 1.28, true caption 5.87 ± 1.45, and weak open-source models near 8.00 ± 1.18 to 8.82 ± 1.15, with deterministic decoding, A100 inference, and GPT-4V cost around $30, supporting MLLM captioning evaluation but only in a narrow CS subset. Further support for LLM-based caption assessment comes from [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177), which rates 3,600 scientific line-chart captions from 600 figures and finds GPT-4 few-shot correlations with PhD rankings of Pearson 0.531, Kendall 0.429, and Spearman 0.523, indicating that LLM judges can be useful but only moderately aligned. Support for the planned overlap metrics is provided by [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5), where METEOR achieved system-level Pearson 0.964 with human MT scores versus BLEU 0.817 and NIST 0.892 on 920 Chinese and 664 Arabic sentences across 7 Chinese and 6 Arabic systems with two human judges, and by [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008), which validates ROUGE against human content-coverage judgments on DUC 2001-2003 summaries, although the excerpt lacks numeric correlations. Additional domain support comes from [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c), which builds SCICAP from arXiv and shows scientific figure captioning is difficult for a ResNet-101 plus LSTM baseline, with BLEU-4 values around 0.0165 to 0.0291, motivating the need for a broader MLLM benchmark. Finally, [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) supports the general evaluation practice of correlating automatic scores with human judgments and estimating variance, reporting BLEU-human correlations of 0.99 and 0.96 on a 500-sentence Chinese-to-English corpus with 10 judges per group and 250 rated pairs, but its translation setting is not direct evidence for scientific figure captioning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that LLM-based caption evaluation and graduate-student human evaluation may be less reliable than assumed. [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) shows only moderate alignment between LLM caption ratings and PhD rankings, with GPT-4 few-shot Pearson 0.531 and Spearman 0.523, chain-of-thought underperforming zero-shot and few-shot, Falcon near zero, SciBERT lower, and undergraduate ratings correlating with PhD ratings at only Pearson 0.221, while the study used 3,159 valid caption samples from 600 single-panel line charts, 15 PhD raters, cost over $3,300, and was limited to NLP, computer vision, and HCI. This challenges the plan to rely on G-EVAL, modified FACTSCORE, and computer science graduate students as a baseline across 72 Nature Communications disciplines. The next strongest contradiction is that reference-based caption quality may not be straightforward and MLLM performance may be unstable. [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) reports that the true caption ranked 5.87 ± 1.45 on a 100-sample SciCap computer-science test, below several proprietary MLLMs, while open-source models ranked near 8.00 to 8.82, and it does not report formal significance tests or the proposed ungrounded and abstract-grounded conditions. This suggests that caption generation rankings can be sensitive to judge, prompt, and domain, and that a 100-sample CS-only evaluation is insufficient for the proposed 72-discipline benchmark. Further caution comes from [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c), where low BLEU-4 scores of 0.0165 to 0.0291, 35.72% of 1,926 error-free sampled figures containing subfigures, and no clear benefit from text normalization indicate that scientific figure captioning is hard, that subfigure handling is nontrivial, and that simple overlap metrics may be unstable. The older metric-validation papers provide the weakest contradiction because they are domain-mismatched: [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008), and [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) validate BLEU, ROUGE, and METEOR in machine translation or summarization, not in scientific figure captioning, ungrounded or abstract-grounded generation, or with FACTSCORE and G-EVAL.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt and report the direct MMS CICAP implementation from [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), including Nature Communications collection up to 2024-04-15, 72 subjects, 1% test and validation per subject, single-article sample derivation, pylatexenc conversion, regular-expression sub-caption extraction, ungrounded and abstract-grounded prompts, and the full metric suite, while adding the missing quantitative results, model versions, prompts, and statistical tests. Second, validate the automatic metrics against human judgments on a stratified subset using the protocol suggested by [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5), and [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008): compute system-level and sample-level Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlations for ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL, use multiple references where available, and report paired t-tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, or significance tests. Third, improve LLM-based evaluation based on [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) by using few-shot GPT-4o, Claude, or Gemini judges rather than chain-of-thought as the primary setting, aggregating multiple judges, calibrating against discipline-matched PhD experts rather than only computer science graduate students, and reporting judge-human correlations, inter-annotator agreement, and API cost. Fourth, strengthen dataset construction and subfigure handling using [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c), by explicitly annotating or filtering subfigures, reporting figure-type distributions, testing caption normalization ablations, and preventing article-level leakage. Fifth, add a ranking-based evaluation and deterministic decoding protocol inspired by [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), reporting mean rank with standard deviation, judge columns, temperature 0 or top-k 1, API versions, GPU specifications, and cost, while expanding beyond 100 CS samples to the full 72-discipline test set.

## To test figure-caption alignment: Figure Caption Matching (MMS CIQA): Multiple-choice questions with three settings - Figure-to-Caption matching, Subfigure-to-Subcaption matching, and Subcaption-to-Subfigure matching.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Figure Caption Matching (MMS CIQA) method is [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), which builds SciFIBench as a five-way multiple-choice scientific figure-caption alignment benchmark with Figure-to-Caption and Caption-to-Figure directions, adversarial negatives, 0-shot chain-of-thought prompting, deterministic decoding, and an LLM answer parser. Its results directly validate the proposed use of multiple-choice accuracy for figure-caption alignment: GPT-4o reaches 73.8% on Figure-to-Caption and 65.4% on Caption-to-Figure, Gemini 1.5 Flash reaches 70.1% and 66.1%, closed-source models outperform open-source models by 34.6 points on Figure-to-Caption, adversarial negatives are harder than random negatives, and CLIP, OCR, and human baselines provide useful reference points. This supports the research plan's evaluation of GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude, Qwen-VL, LLaVA, InternVL2, IDEFICS, and other MLLMs, as well as the plan's use of accuracy across multiple-choice settings. A second, weaker but still relevant support is [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f), which evaluates figure-caption alignment for TikZ drawings using human best-worst scaling on a 100-item subset with four expert annotators and automatic CLIPScore, showing that caption augmentation can raise CLIPScore from 24.76 to 29.12 for short captions and that image-reference comparison can be more robust than caption-only similarity. This supports adding human comparative judgments and automatic similarity diagnostics to the proposed MMS CIQA evaluation. The weakest but foundational support is [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c), which established a SciCap figure-caption pipeline with manual quality checking of 2,000 figures, 1,926 valid extractions, 35.72% subfigure prevalence, figure-type distribution, subfigure filtering, and 80/10/10 splits; although it evaluates caption generation with low BLEU-4 scores rather than matching, it supports the feasibility of extracting and quality-controlling scientific figure-caption data and motivates the proposed subfigure-to-subcaption and subcaption-to-subfigure settings.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that the closest multiple-choice benchmark, [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), explicitly removes figures containing subfigures and is restricted to computer-science arXiv figures, so it does not directly validate the proposed Subfigure-to-Subcaption or Subcaption-to-Subfigure settings on Nature Communications across 72 disciplines. It also shows that multiple-choice figure-caption matching can be sensitive to evaluation details: answer-order shuffling can change performance by about 5%, few-shot examples can change Gemini 1.5 Flash accuracy from 56.8 to 61.2, 59.4, or 58.0, bootstrapping 500-question samples yields variance around 0.05, and CLIP or OCR baselines can be competitive, suggesting that the proposed subfigure-level multiple-choice task may be confounded by visual similarity, text overlap, or small-sample instability if distractors and decoding are not carefully controlled. A second contradiction is from [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f), which finds that caption-based similarity can be inflated by typographic attacks, with GPT-4 and Claude 2 copying more than 10% of 1-grams and continuing to copy longer n-grams, implying that multiple-choice matching may be gamed by OCR or caption-text shortcuts rather than true scientific figure understanding. The weakest contradiction is from [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c), which shows that scientific figure-caption extraction is not trivial: 74 of 2,000 sampled figures had extraction errors, 35.72% contained subfigures, text normalization did not show a clear benefit, and captioning baselines achieved very low BLEU-4 scores, with the best Vision-only model at 0.0291 on Single-Sentence Caption. This suggests that the proposed regular-expression subcaption extraction and normalization may introduce noise, and that subfigure-level matching may be substantially harder than whole-figure captioning or matching.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the evaluation protocol from [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) for MMS CIQA: use five-way multiple-choice items, generate adversarial negatives rather than only random negatives, use deterministic decoding such as greedy search or temperature 0, use an LLM parser for answer letters, shuffle answer order, report per-setting accuracy, and include CLIP, OCR, and human baselines. For the proposed subfigure settings, adversarial subcaption and subfigure distractors should be drawn from the same article, figure type, or discipline to test scientific understanding rather than superficial visual or lexical similarity, and the plan should add confidence intervals or bootstrap estimates because Roberts et al. report small-sample variance around 0.05 for 500-question samples, which is relevant to the proposed 1% per-subject test and validation split. If feasible, the plan should also add a whole Caption-to-Figure diagnostic because Roberts et al. show it is generally harder than Figure-to-Caption. The second most important action is to validate the subcaption extraction pipeline using the quality-control approach in [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c): manually audit a sample of Nature Communications figures, label subfigure presence and subcaption boundaries, report extraction error rates and precision/recall for the regular-expression matcher, filter ambiguous subfigures, and ablate text normalization. The third action is to incorporate the human and automatic alignment diagnostics from [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f): run a best-worst scaling or pairwise human evaluation on a stratified subset, ideally with domain experts rather than only computer-science graduate students, and compute CLIPScore or similar image-caption similarity to detect whether models are relying on typographic or OCR shortcuts. This would make the proposed Figure-to-Caption, Subfigure-to-Subcaption, and Subcaption-to-Subfigure matching tasks more robust, reproducible, and aligned with the research plan's goal of measuring graduate-level multi-discipline scientific understanding.

## To assess benchmark performance across tasks: We will employ multiple evaluation metrics including overlap-based metrics (ROUGE, METEOR), similarity-based metrics (BERTScore), and LLM-based evaluation metrics (modified FACTSCORE, G-EVAL) for comprehensive assessment. For captioning tasks, we will use ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL. For multiple-choice questions, we will measure accuracy across different settings. For the materials case study, we will evaluate structural/compositional validity, coverage metrics, property distribution alignment, and metastability/stability percentages.

### Support

The strongest support is direct scientific-figure MCQ evidence from [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) which built SciFIBench from approximately 94k single-sentence SciCap pairs, created 5-option Figure-to-Caption and Caption-to-Figure questions with adversarial and random negatives, used deterministic decoding and Gemini-Pro parsing, and reported GPT-4o 73.8% and 65.4%, Gemini 1.5 Flash 70.1% and 66.1%, a 34.6-point closed-source over open-source gap on Figure-to-Caption, human outperformance, option-shuffling variation up to about 5%, and a 100-sample caption-generation ranking; this directly supports the plan's MMS CIQA accuracy and LLM-assisted evaluation, although it does not validate ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, or G-EVAL. Next, [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f) supports human-correlated captioning evaluation and detail sensitivity: SPICE achieved Pearson 0.88 with human overall quality on COCO C5, compared with 0.43 for CIDEr and 0.53 for METEOR, ranked human captions first, and used 255,000 MTurk judgments, C40 5,000-image data, Flickr 8K, and held-out test captions, indicating that MMS CICAP should validate metrics on held-out human judgments and consider semantic or structural signals. [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) supports consensus-based captioning validation on PASCAL-50S 1,000 images and ABSTRACT-50S 500 images with 50 references per image, 48 retained, 24 used for consensus, CIDEr 82% human-consensus accuracy versus 90% and 83% human performance, Pearson 0.98 for five-method ranking, and CIDEr-D Spearman 0.94, although exact ROUGE and METEOR accuracies are absent. [(Zhang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/295065d942abca0711300b2b4c39829551060578) supports BERTScore as a paraphrase-robust similarity metric, recommending F1 BERTScore and showing robustness on QQP and PAWS-QQP with 351 systems and metrics, 5,000 QQP training sentences, and 667 PAWS-QQP development sentences, though exact AUCs are absent and scientific figures are not tested. [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) supports METEOR's system-level human correlation on TIDES 2003, with full METEOR 0.964, recall 0.941, Fmean 0.952, F1 0.948, precision 0.752, BLEU 0.817, and NIST 0.892 on 920 Chinese and 664 Arabic sentences with four references and two judges, and [(Denkowski, and Lavie, 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5e4377d2149a8167d89383d785793967cf74602) supports parameterized METEOR from 101,099 WMT12 binary judgments, with held-out Russian correlation nearly double BLEU, although in-training languages are biased. Older overlap-metric validation protocols from [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) and [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008) support checking metric-human correlation and reference sensitivity: BLEU showed 0.99 and 0.96 regression with monolingual and bilingual judges on 500 Chinese-English sentences, block t-tests, and reference-count dependence from 0.3468 with four references to 0.2571 with two references, while ROUGE used Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlations on DUC 2001-2003 summaries with 12-18 systems and 29-624 judged summaries per system. For MCQ, [(Li et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4309d572a37d655779f9dce6a2c98c66334132de) supports likelihood-based multiple-choice accuracy on SEED-Bench, with InstructBLIP 60.29%, InstructBLIP Vicuna 60.20%, BLIP2 59.12%, and many dimensions below 50%, showing that accuracy can expose visual weaknesses. [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) supports benchmark-style accuracy with numeric tolerance, reporting human 80.47% on 5,860 PlotQA questions, best model 22.52%, image-only 4.84%, question-only 5.35%, and OCR 97.06% oracle versus 93.10% pipeline, though its chart QA task differs from scientific figure matching. Additional support comes from [(Shen et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3103678dc155dc2494c5456f4d8cc9e4b011eb86) for combining overlap and embedding metrics, with Sen2Pro Spearman and Pearson 87.3 and 81.4 on Daily(H), 100 and 85.9 on ConvAI2, and 80.9 and 81.6 on Empathetic; [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1be5041f6a16e58c8a59df2d0a764d2cfa477adf) for task-specific accuracy and Rouge-L protocols; [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) for multimodal benchmark evaluation on ScienceQA, although the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes; [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/205ada2927972cb3156e94247e29d454bf620399) for task-specific benchmark metric design; and [(Suhr et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf336d272a30d6ad6141db67faa64deb8791cd61) for controlled multimodal benchmarking with human baselines, Krippendorff alpha 0.912 and Fleiss kappa 0.889, although its binary NLVR2 accuracy is not captioning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed reliance on ROUGE and METEOR is top-system metric instability in [(Ma et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c035a8e6ad1da2a8b22d5f2951f01f1f309632): on WMT19 newstest2019 with 233 systems and 18 language pairs, sacreBLEU-BLEU had 0.969 correlation over all systems but fell below 0.5 for top 10 and became negative for top 6 or top 4, while baseline correlations approached zero or negative for top systems and quality estimation systems ranged from 0.028 to 0.947 system-level and -0.153 to 0.351 segment-level; this warns that overlap metrics may fail to discriminate the strongest MLLMs in MMS CI. A second strong contradiction is utterance-level weakness of overlap metrics in [(Lowe et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/339c6e6d46836c173fb6a23b493c724896d4cc70): on 4,104 Twitter dialogues, ROUGE reached Spearman 0.062 and 0.096 and Pearson 0.114 and 0.147, METEOR reached Spearman 0.021 and 0.013 and Pearson 0.022 and 0.021, BLEU-4 reached Spearman 0.051 and 0.063, whereas ADEM reached Spearman 0.410 and 0.428 and Pearson 0.418 and 0.436 with p-values below 0.001 and system-level Pearson 0.954, indicating that ROUGE and METEOR are poor item-level caption quality signals. [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f) also contradicts METEOR and ROUGE as sufficient for detailed captions: SPICE Pearson 0.88 versus METEOR 0.53 and CIDEr 0.43, with METEOR ranking human captions fourth and CIDEr seventh, suggesting that n-gram and alignment metrics miss semantic detail. [(Novikova et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d441ab58a1027cb64084ad065cfea5e15b8e74c) further contradicts item-level automatic evaluation: on BAGEL, SFHOTEL, and SFREST with three raters, 6-point scales, and ICC 0.45, no automatic metric reached moderate utterance-level correlation, with ROUGE 0.20, METEOR 0.19, BLEU-1 0.30, and a random score 0.09. Surface-overlap gaming is shown by [(Dinan et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ae17b09c59f06f02ef824b856a440de663471d0), where a fixed frequent-word F1 baseline scored 19.6 and 20.5 versus Hugging Face 19.5 and 19.1, and parrot distractors reduced Hits@1, implying ROUGE and METEOR can be inflated by generic scientific terms. For MCQ, [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) contradicts naive accuracy reporting: option shuffling caused up to about 5% variation, Gemini-Pro parsing could flag invalid outputs, open-source Caption-to-Figure remained near chance, and humans outperformed all models, so the plan must specify parsing, shuffling, and confidence intervals. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) contradicts stable reference-based scoring by showing BLEU changed from 0.3468 with four references to 0.2571 with two references on 500 sentences, and used only 250 human-rated pairs from non-professional judges, cautioning against overinterpreting ROUGE and METEOR when scientific captions vary in length, subcaption structure, and reference availability. [(Denkowski, and Lavie, 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5e4377d2149a8167d89383d785793967cf74602) limits METEOR transferability because five WMT13 directions were in-training and only Russian was held out, with no scientific-figure test. A weaker contradiction is [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534), where CIDEr outperformed earlier metrics on PASCAL-50S and ABSTRACT-50S, with 82% human-consensus accuracy versus 90% and 83% human performance, although exact ROUGE and METEOR accuracies are not provided. The plan's human baseline is also weakened by domain mismatch: [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) used undergraduate and postgraduate humans and [(Suhr et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf336d272a30d6ad6141db67faa64deb8791cd61) used pruned crowdworker labels with Krippendorff alpha 0.912 and Fleiss kappa 0.889, while the proposed CS graduate students may lack expertise across 72 disciplines; [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) shows human accuracy 80.47% on PlotQA with numeric tolerance, but best model 22.52%, indicating that domain-specific ground truth and tolerance matter. Finally, the materials case study has little direct support: [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) provides no quantitative ScienceQA or captioning outcomes, [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/205ada2927972cb3156e94247e29d454bf620399) is a survey without results, and [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1be5041f6a16e58c8a59df2d0a764d2cfa477adf) reports no quantitative outcomes, leaving structural/compositional validity, coverage, property distribution, and metastability/stability metrics under-evidenced.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace the current underspecified evaluation with a metric-validation protocol tied to MMS CICAP and MMS CIQA: recruit domain-matched human raters, not only CS graduate students, for a stratified sample across 72 disciplines and seven figure types, use at least three raters per caption, a rubric for accuracy, completeness, scientific correctness, and fluency, report ICC, Krippendorff alpha, and Fleiss kappa, and compute item-level Spearman, Pearson, and Kendall correlations plus system-level correlations for ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL, with Williams tests, block-level confidence intervals, and top-n stability analyses inspired by [(Ma et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c035a8e6ad1da2a8b22d5f2951f01f1f309632) and [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9); ensure test and validation splits are article-level and subfigure-level held out, with no article, figure, subcaption, or near-duplicate leakage, following controlled splitting in [(Suhr et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf336d272a30d6ad6141db67faa64deb8791cd61) and [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8). Second, reduce reliance on ROUGE and METEOR by reporting them as diagnostics and adding semantic or structural metrics: use BERTScore F1 with a fixed encoder and no idf weighting as recommended by [(Zhang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/295065d942abca0711300b2b4c39829551060578), add CIDEr or CIDEr-D and SPICE-like attribute, scene-graph, or structured scientific-caption metrics motivated by [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) and [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f), and calibrate G-EVAL and modified FACTSCORE with multiple LLM judges, prompt ablations, temperature and seed controls, and agreement with human domain experts, using deterministic decoding and LLM parsing as in [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc). Third, make MMS CIQA accuracy robust: specify the number of options, adversarial and random negative construction, option shuffling, invalid-answer handling, likelihood-based scoring or constrained decoding as in [(Li et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4309d572a37d655779f9dce6a2c98c66334132de), report per-setting accuracy with bootstrap confidence intervals, and include human, CLIP, and OCR baselines as in [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc). Fourth, define the materials case study quantitatively: use MP-20 ground truth to compute exact and tolerance-based structural/compositional validity, coverage, property-distribution distance, and metastability/stability percentages, with numeric tolerances analogous to PlotQA's 5% rule in [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c), and compare LLaMA2-7B pretraining against baselines with statistical tests. Fifth, document reproducibility and resources: fix METEOR version and parameters, ROUGE variants, BERTScore model, G-EVAL prompt, FACTSCORE fact extractor, LLM judge model and API version, random seeds, decoding temperature, hardware, API cost, and human annotation budget, using resource reports such as 8 A100 GPUs in [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8), A100 inference and about $30 GPT-4V cost in [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), and 255,000 MTurk judgments in [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f). Finally, validate data construction: report pylatexenc conversion accuracy, regular-expression subcaption extraction precision and recall, figure-type taxonomy agreement, deduplication, and low-agreement pruning, using agreement metrics from [(Suhr et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf336d272a30d6ad6141db67faa64deb8791cd61) and human probes from [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c).

## To measure current MLLM capabilities: We will evaluate a diverse range of models including proprietary MLLMs (GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Flash/Pro, Claude-3-Opus, Claude-3.5-Sonnet) and open-source models (Kosmos-2, Qwen-VL series, LLaVA series, InternVL2 series, IDEFICS series, MiniCPM-V-2.6, Llama3.2-11B-Vision).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), which is the closest prior benchmark to MMS CI because it evaluates a broad set of proprietary and open-source MLLMs on scientific figure-caption tasks, including Figure-to-Caption and Caption-to-Figure multiple-choice questions with adversarial and random negatives, a human baseline, CLIP/Google embedding baselines, an OCR-only baseline, and a caption-generation ranking on 100 samples. Its model list overlaps substantially with the proposed list, including GPT-4V, GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4o, Gemini-Pro Vision, Gemini 1.5 Flash and Pro, Claude 3 Opus, Sonnet and Haiku, IDEFICS, Qwen-VL, LLaVA-1.5, InternLM-Composer, CogVLM, OmniLMM, Yi, InstructBLIP, Monkey, TransCore-M, and Emu2, and it uses A100 GPUs for open-source inference, deterministic decoding, temperature 0/top-k 1 for Gemini/Claude/GPT-4, and a Gemini-Pro parser for answer letters. Quantitatively, GPT-4o achieved 73.8% on Figure-to-Caption and 65.4% on Caption-to-Figure, Gemini 1.5 Flash achieved 70.1% and 66.1%, closed-source models outperformed open-source models by 34.6% on Figure-to-Caption, the best open-source model TransCore-M was below the worst closed-source model, the human baseline outperformed all models, bootstrapping 100,000 resamples gave low variance for Gemini-Pro Vision with mean accuracies 56.00 and 52.40 and variance 0.05, and shuffling answer order caused about 5% performance variation. This directly supports the proposed plan to evaluate GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Flash/Pro, Claude-3-Opus, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Qwen-VL, LLaVA, IDEFICS, and related open-source models on scientific figure captioning and matching, to include human baselines, to report category-level breakdowns, and to use controlled inference. The next strongest support is [(Li et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56994972adca9319577617345128e46803a4043f), which evaluates LVLMs on ArXivCap scientific figure captioning, multiple-figure captioning, contextualized captioning, and title generation, with a 9:1 training-to-test split and a test set containing 161.3K single-figure, 12.8K multiple-figure, 57.2K contextualized, and 57.2K title-generation samples. Its manual inspection of 100 generated single-figure captions found only 16% acceptable, with contextual misinterpretation as the dominant failure mode, 23% oversimplified, and 19% recognition errors, supporting the proposed use of both automatic metrics and human evaluation for MMS CICAP and MMS CIQA. Iteratively, [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) supports the proposed LLM-based G-EVAL component by showing that GPT-4 few-shot prompting had the strongest agreement with Ph.D. student caption rankings on 3,159 valid caption samples from 600 SCICAP line charts, with Pearson 0.531, Kendall 0.429, and Spearman 0.523 for reversed rank, while GPT-3.5, LLaMA-2-70B, and Falcon were weaker and chain-of-thought generally lowered performance. This supports using strong LLM judges but also motivates prompt ablations. Older but still relevant work shows the iterative progression from synthetic chart QA to scientific figure understanding: [(Kahou et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cb6be69c67b0b15ebbda89a126f4dd62a4d32958) introduced a synthetic figure-question benchmark with 100 X11 colors, alternated color splits, CNN+LSTM and relation-network baselines, and a human baseline on 16,876 questions from 1,275 figures, supporting held-out visual attributes and human baselines; [(Kafle et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7289a240c9425bc7cad87b3b835e5f0cac22f488) extended chart VQA to DVQA with 3,487,194 question-answer pairs over 300,000 images, Test-Familiar and Test-Novel label splits, ResNet-152 448x448 inputs, 1024-unit LSTM question encoders, and an OCR branch using 128x128 patches, N=8 spatial attention, a bidirectional GRU, and CTC, supporting controlled novel-label evaluation and OCR-aware chart understanding; [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) showed that PlotQA chart QA is hard, with image-only 4.84%, question-only 5.35%, proposed model 22.52%, and human accuracy 80.47% on 5,860 questions from 160 test images, supporting human baselines and numeric tolerance. [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) then moved to real arXiv scientific figure captioning with SCICAP, using a ResNet-101 and LSTM baseline, 80/10/10 splits, dropout 0.5, batch size 16 or 32, learning rate 4e-4 with decay 0.8 after 8 epochs, early stopping after 20 epochs, and low BLEU-4 scores such as 0.0291 for the best Vision-only Single-Sentence Caption model, supporting the difficulty of scientific captioning and the need for richer metrics. [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) supports broad MLLM-style comparison on SCIENCEQA, with 21,208 examples, 48.7% image context, 48.2% text context, 30.8% both, 83.9% lectures, and 91.3% explanations, showing that GPT-3 chain-of-thought reached 75.17% average accuracy, fine-tuned UnifiedQA reached 70.12%, VisualBERT reached 61.87%, and human answers were collected from three workers per question, supporting multiple-choice evaluation, chain-of-thought prompting, and human baselines. MLLM training work also supports evaluating the proposed open-source families: [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) reports LLaVA training on 8 A100 GPUs, pre-training on a filtered CC-595K subset for 1 epoch with learning rate 2e-3 and batch size 128, fine-tuning on 158K LLaVA-Instruct samples for 3 epochs with learning rate 2e-5 and batch size 32, and finding that GPT-4 generated higher-quality instruction data than ChatGPT, which is relevant to evaluating LLaVA series models and reporting compute. [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a2a756c60dbc99f666ae6e32b0dd1a58e1e2de8) provides weaker but relevant support for instruction-tuning evaluation, using BLIP2-OPT-2.7B with Ziya-13B, M3IT with 2,429,264 instances, stage-I AdamW batch size 256, 300,000 steps, peak learning rate 5e-5, 2,000-step warmup, cosine decay, weight decay 0.05, and stage-II 3 epochs with learning rate 1e-5 and 1,000-step warmup. Finally, [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f) supports robust scientific-figure evaluation by showing that caption augmentation improved CLIPScore from 24.76 to 29.12 for short captions, that GPT-4 and Claude 2 copied more than 10% of 1-grams from captions while CLiMA 13b and LLaMA 13b copied about 6.5%, and that reference-image similarity can be more robust than caption similarity, supporting the proposed need for leakage-aware and visually grounded metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that the planned human baseline of computer science graduate students may not be representative for a 72-discipline Nature Communications benchmark. [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) shows that domain-specific Ph.D. student rankings were needed to obtain meaningful caption-quality judgments on 600 SCICAP line charts from computer vision, natural language processing, and human-computer interaction, while 20 STEM undergraduates correlated only weakly with Ph.D. rankings, with Pearson 0.221, Kendall 0.195, and Spearman 0.221 for reversed rank. If MMS CI uses only CS graduate students, the human baseline and any G-EVAL calibration may be biased toward CS/ML figures and may not reflect graduate-level expertise in biology, materials science, astronomy, chemistry, or other disciplines. The next strongest contradiction is that multiple-choice accuracy can be unstable unless negative selection, answer order, and output parsing are tightly controlled. [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) found that adversarial negatives produced much harder results than random negatives, that closed-source models outperformed open-source models by 34.6% on Figure-to-Caption, that the best open-source model TransCore-M was below the worst closed-source model, that shuffling answer order caused about 5% performance variation, and that many models required a Gemini-Pro parser to extract or flag answer letters. If the proposed MMS CIQA does not report adversarial versus random negatives, answer-order sensitivity, parser failure rates, and category-level variance, its accuracy scores may overstate or understate model capabilities. A further contradiction is that automatic captioning metrics alone may miss the dominant failure modes in scientific figure captioning. [(Li et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56994972adca9319577617345128e46803a4043f) found that only 16% of 100 manually inspected ArXivCap single-figure captions were acceptable, with contextual misinterpretation dominant, 23% oversimplified, and 19% recognition errors, suggesting that ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL may still undercount context-dependent scientific errors unless paired with a human error taxonomy. [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) also contradicts an overreliance on chain-of-thought LLM judges, because chain-of-thought prompting generally lowered agreement with Ph.D. rankings, with GPT-4 CoT QA reaching Pearson 0.283, Kendall 0.270, and Spearman 0.315, below GPT-4 few-shot. Older chart benchmarks contradict the assumption that simple exact-match or overlap metrics are sufficient for scientific figures: [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) showed that small visual-element detection errors materially affected downstream QA, with OCR dropping from 97.06% in oracle mode to 93.10% when using detected boxes and dotline detection below 20% at IOU 0.9, and it required a 5% relative tolerance for numeric answers; [(Kafle et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7289a240c9425bc7cad87b3b835e5f0cac22f488) showed that Test-Novel labels absent from training dictionaries and answer sets create a harder open-vocabulary regime, implying that ROUGE and exact-match-style metrics may be harsh for rare scientific terminology in Nature Communications. [(Kahou et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cb6be69c67b0b15ebbda89a126f4dd62a4d32958) similarly shows that even synthetic chart questions required relational reasoning and that humans still outperformed CNN+LSTM and relation-network baselines, but its simple functional shapes and 100 X11 color scheme do not validate the proposed 72-discipline, graduate-level scope. [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) also cautions against overinterpreting model rankings because it did not report statistical significance tests, and its grade-level SCIENCEQA setting is less aligned with graduate-level Nature Communications figures. [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) suggests that LLaVA-style models trained on general COCO-derived instruction data may not automatically transfer to scientific figures, so including LLaVA series models is reasonable but should not be assumed to be competitive without scientific-domain evaluation. [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a2a756c60dbc99f666ae6e32b0dd1a58e1e2de8) provides limited contradiction because it evaluates a single general instruction-tuned model rather than the proposed scientific benchmark, but it shows that general vision-language instruction tuning does not by itself establish scientific-figure competence. Finally, [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f) contradicts the assumption that caption-based evaluation is immune to typographic attacks, because GPT-4 and Claude 2 copied more than 10% of 1-grams from captions and continued copying for n > 5, indicating that MMS CI should include leakage controls, n-gram copying analysis, and reference-image similarity.

### Suggested Action

Most important, revise the human evaluation protocol so that it is discipline-stratified rather than limited to computer science graduate students. Based on [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177), recruit graduate students or domain experts across the 72 Nature Communications disciplines, use multiple annotators per sample, report inter-annotator agreement such as Kendall or Spearman, and use human rankings to calibrate G-EVAL and modified FACTSCORE. If full 72-discipline expert coverage is infeasible, at minimum stratify the test set by discipline, report discipline-level human baselines, and flag CS-only evaluations as a limitation. Second, make MMS CIQA robust to multiple-choice confounds by following [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc): generate both adversarial and random negative captions/subcaptions for Figure-to-Caption, Subfigure-to-Subcaption, and Subcaption-to-Subfigure, report accuracy for each, randomize answer order and report answer-order sensitivity, use deterministic decoding where possible, specify temperature 0/top-k 1 for API models, use a robust parser for answer letters, report parser failure rates, and use bootstrap resampling to estimate variance and confidence intervals. Third, expand and document the model list to include strong open-source MLLMs that were competitive in prior scientific-figure benchmarks, such as TransCore-M, CogVLM, OmniLMM, Yi, InstructBLIP, Monkey, Emu2, and InternLM-Composer from [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), while retaining the proposed GPT-4V, GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5-Flash/Pro, Claude-3-Opus, Claude-3.5-Sonnet, Kosmos-2, Qwen-VL, LLaVA, InternVL2, IDEFICS, MiniCPM-V-2.6, and Llama3.2-11B-Vision; also include baselines such as CLIP/MetaCLIP/Google embedding nearest-neighbor, OCR-only, text-only, random, and human. Fourth, strengthen the captioning evaluation by combining ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL with a human error taxonomy inspired by [(Li et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56994972adca9319577617345128e46803a4043f), categorizing captions as acceptable, oversimplified, recognition error, or contextual misinterpretation, reporting ungrounded and abstract-grounded captioning separately, and applying numeric tolerance for quantitative values as in [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c). Fifth, control for caption copying and training-data leakage by reporting n-gram copying rates, using held-out articles and the April 15, 2024 cutoff, validating subfigure/subcaption extraction, and adding reference-image similarity metrics such as CLIPScore_img as in [(Belouadi et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9486179c5ab6e0cff0ef919c34080a882bb9fe9f). Sixth, report statistical significance and variance for all major comparisons, including paired bootstrap tests, confidence intervals, and discipline-level breakdowns, addressing the lack of significance testing in [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) and the answer-order variance in [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc). Seventh, ablate prompt formats, including zero-shot, few-shot, answer-only, and chain-of-thought, because chain-of-thought improved SCIENCEQA accuracy in [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) but lowered caption-rating agreement in [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177). Eighth, for the training experiments, document compute, hyperparameters, and data leakage controls when fine-tuning Qwen2-VL-2B and pre-training LLaMA2-7B, using the detailed training reports in [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) and [(Li et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a2a756c60dbc99f666ae6e32b0dd1a58e1e2de8) as templates, and evaluate whether task-specific scientific data improves performance beyond general instruction tuning. Finally, validate the dataset construction pipeline, including pylatexenc conversion, regular-expression subcaption extraction, figure-type categorization, 1% per-subject test/validation allocation, and the single-article sample constraint, by reporting extraction error rates, subfigure prevalence, caption length distributions, and discipline balance, following the data-quality checks in [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) and the controlled synthetic variation in [(Kahou et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cb6be69c67b0b15ebbda89a126f4dd62a4d32958) and [(Kafle et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7289a240c9425bc7cad87b3b835e5f0cac22f488).

## To establish a human reference: We will also include human evaluation using computer science graduate students as a baseline.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177), which directly uses 15 Ph.D. students in computer vision, NLP, and HCI as a human reference, with paid annotation at $20 per hour, IRB approval, batches of 10 figures, six candidate captions per figure, and 3,159 valid caption samples after exclusions. This is the closest precedent to the proposed MMS CI plan because it uses computer-science graduate-level annotators to create a reference for scientific figure captioning, and it shows that such a baseline can be used to compute model-human alignment (GPT-4 few-shot Pearson 0.531, Kendall 0.429, Spearman 0.523; GPT-3.5 Pearson 0.462; LLaMA-2-70B Pearson 0.424; undergraduate ratings Pearson 0.221). The next strongest support is [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc), which uses undergraduate and postgraduate students on the same multiple-choice Figure-to-Caption and Caption-to-Figure tasks planned for MMS CIQA, with adversarially selected negatives; human mean outperformed all evaluated MLLMs, whose best scores were 73.8% and 70.1% on Figure-to-Caption and 65.4% and 66.1% on Caption-to-Figure, showing that a human baseline is both feasible and necessary for calibrating MLLM performance. This newer work extends the older chart and captioning human-evaluation literature by moving from chart QA and summary quality to MLLM multiple-choice scientific figure understanding. [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) supports the use of human judgment for scientific figure captions, using 90 cs.CL SCICAP figures, 20 MTurk workers per figure, and worst-caption selection, with TrOCR selected as worst in 41 of 90 cases; although the main domain-expert evaluation is not quantified in the excerpt, the pilot demonstrates that human evaluation can identify weak captioning baselines before formal evaluation. [(Kantharaj et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca2f63950685a97e5ab6b8e6b2db78a8995e94a2) supports human pairwise caption evaluation, using 150 Statista charts, four native English annotators, and 74.3% agreement excluding ties, and shows that human judgments can distinguish factual correctness, coherence, and fluency among gold, TAB-T5, and OCR-T5 outputs. [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5d936b142eb81826002888230649c805532630e) supports adding human qualitative statement-level analysis to caption evaluation, analyzing 512 L2/L3 statements from 176 generated captions and finding 47.1% factual accuracy, with identity errors accounting for 86 statements or 22.93% of analyzed statements. [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) supports human labeling of scientific figure attributes, using one author to label 2,000 arXiv figures, retaining 1,926 error-free figures, and finding subfigures in 35.72% of retained figures, which is relevant to the proposed subfigure-to-subcaption and subcaption-to-subfigure tasks. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c), which established a human reference on 5,860 PlotQA questions over 160 plots with 80.47% human accuracy and a 5% numeric tolerance, demonstrating that human baselines can contextualize model performance, although the task is chart QA rather than multidisciplinary scientific figure understanding.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed method uses computer science graduate students as the human baseline for a benchmark spanning 72 Nature Communications disciplines, while the related human baselines are concentrated in computer science, arXiv, or chart domains. [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) used Ph.D. students in computer vision, NLP, and HCI on single-panel SCICAP line charts, not microscopy, spectroscopy, astronomy, molecular structures, or phylogenetic trees. [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) used undergraduate and postgraduate students on arXiv computer science figures and explicitly cautions that human curation may bias the benchmark toward easier questions. [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) used MTurk workers and domain experts on cs.CL arXiv line charts, and [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) used a single author on arXiv figures. Therefore, a CS-graduate baseline may not represent a valid human ceiling for non-CS scientific figures and could systematically underestimate or misjudge domain-specific content. The next strongest contradiction is that human performance is not a perfect or uniform reference, and the newer Roberts 2024 work complicates the older Hsu 2023 graduate-student precedent by showing that even on arXiv CS figures, human performance is variable and task-dependent. [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) reports that humans did not achieve a perfect score, showed variability with some participants scoring equal to or below the best models, and performed worse on Caption-to-Figure than Figure-to-Caption. [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) reports only moderate model-human correlations, with GPT-4 few-shot Pearson 0.531 and inter-annotator Kendall 0.427, indicating that graduate-level rankings are informative but noisy. [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) also found human errors due to numerical precision even with a 5% tolerance, suggesting that human baselines can be task-limited. A third contradiction is that the proposed method is under-specified relative to reproducible related work. The plan does not state the number of CS graduate students, compensation, training, IRB status, batch size, number of figures per student, annotation interface, scoring rules for captioning, multiple-choice response format, inter-annotator agreement, or statistical tests, whereas [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) reports 15 Ph.D. students, $20 per hour, 30 to 60 minutes per batch, 10 figures per batch, six captions per figure, and IRB approval; [(Kantharaj et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca2f63950685a97e5ab6b8e6b2db78a8995e94a2) reports four annotators, 150 charts, and 74.3% agreement; and [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) reports 20 workers per figure. A fourth contradiction is that the proposed subfigure and subcaption tasks require careful human validation, but the related work shows subfigures are common and error-prone: [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) found subfigures in 35.72% of 1,926 retained arXiv figures, and [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc) warns that human verification during curation can bias benchmark difficulty. The weakest contradiction is that older chart-based human baselines may overstate transferability to scientific figures: [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c) reports 80.47% human accuracy on PlotQA, a value that should not be treated as a comparable ceiling for Nature Communications multidisciplinary figures.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to align the human reference population with the multidisciplinary scope of MMS CI. Instead of using only computer science graduate students as a universal baseline, the plan should recruit domain-appropriate graduate students or experts for the 72 Nature Communications disciplines, or stratify CS graduate students by relevant coursework and report discipline-specific human performance. If the study is constrained to CS graduate students, it should explicitly frame them as a CS-graduate reference rather than a scientific human ceiling and add a smaller domain-expert validation subset for non-CS figure types such as microscopy, spectroscopy, astronomy, molecular structures, and phylogenetic trees. The next most important action is to specify a reproducible annotation protocol modeled on [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) and [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc): recruit at least 15 graduate students, report compensation such as $20 per hour, obtain IRB approval, define qualification criteria, training, batch size such as 10 figures per batch, number of items per student, randomization, and annotation interface. For MMS CICAP captioning, use ranking or pairwise comparisons among model captions and gold captions, following [(Hsu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22019445f51088b064141d0934324ed73346177) and [(Kantharaj et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca2f63950685a97e5ab6b8e6b2db78a8995e94a2); for MMS CIQA, present the same five-option Figure-to-Caption, Subfigure-to-Subcaption, and Subcaption-to-Subfigure prompts used for MLLMs, following [(Roberts et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e852785ce2acc2b79aad22f7b2585585a9c88bfc). The third action is to add reliability and statistical analysis: double-annotate a subset, report inter-annotator agreement such as Krippendorff alpha, Cohen kappa, or Kendall/Spearman for rankings, report mean and standard deviation by discipline, task, and figure type, and use paired bootstrap or Wilcoxon tests to compare human and model performance, rather than reporting only a single human mean. The fourth action is to run a pilot calibration on 90 to 150 figures across multiple disciplines, using worst-caption selection or pairwise comparisons to remove ambiguous items and identify underperforming models, as in [(Chieh-Yang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f6c67f3111ca652e6709f5ae3b6821c13838a80) and [(Kantharaj et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca2f63950685a97e5ab6b8e6b2db78a8995e94a2), and to audit subfigure/subcaption extraction because [(Hsu et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/08b3c84da0da5bdc0e49a1c2f440743d829a5e1c) found subfigures in 35.72% of retained figures. The least important but still useful action is to complement LLM-based metrics with human statement-level error analysis, decomposing captions into factual statements and coding error types as in [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5d936b142eb81826002888230649c805532630e), and to use numeric tolerance only where appropriate, as in [(Methani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c496f00f2b85b496abdc36e4dc4105541248fd3c).

## To ensure fair and discipline-balanced evaluation: We will allocate 1% of articles from each subject to test and validation sets respectively, ensuring balanced coverage across disciplines while managing evaluation costs. Each sample will be derived from a single article to prevent content reuse.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 1%-per-subject article allocation comes from [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5), which used a paper-level, domain-balanced split for a scientific figure and table QA benchmark: 25,859 CS papers were divided into 200 evaluation papers, 200 validation papers, and 25,459 training papers, with the explicit goal that each split contain papers from all domains; after manual filtering, test-A had 666 questions, and evaluation used METEOR, CIDEr, ROUGE-L, BERTScore F1, and L3Score, with GPT-4o reaching 65.18 overall L3Score and fine-tuning InstructBLIP/LLaVA 1.5 improving average L3Score by 28 and 26 points. This directly supports the research plan's need for article-level, discipline-balanced test/validation partitions for MMS CI figure captioning and matching, although it uses fixed 200-paper splits rather than 1% and covers CS conferences rather than 72 Nature Communications subjects. The next strongest support is [(Dasigi et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e3935ef7da6bcbb202ec7f8b285c313cadcd044), whose QASPER protocol partitions 1,585 NLP papers into 1,200 training, 150 development, and 235 test papers, yielding 3,848, 475, and 726 questions, and evaluates document-level LED-base/LED-large models with span-level F1; the paper-level split prevents overlap and supports the proposed single-article provenance control, but it is single-domain and does not implement 1% per subject. Earlier but still relevant, [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed) used an article-level disjoint split for MELINDA, assigning all records from 1,497 articles to train/validation/test with empty article-ID intersections and balancing coarse participant-method labels, which supports the proposed use of article provenance and balanced coverage, though it used 10% validation/test rather than 1%. The Nature Communications corpus evidence in [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51) supports feasibility: 131,393 articles and 742,273 figures across 72 subjects, with article-level extraction of titles, abstracts, main text, references, figures, and sub-captions, make a 1% per-subject allocation computable, although the excerpt does not report split sizes or model outcomes. Older article-derived benchmarks provide weaker but iterative support: [(Jin et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c3c4c88c7b07596221ac640c7b7102686e3eae3) used 500 randomly sampled PubMedQA instances as test and 500 for 10-fold cross-validation, showing small article-derived held-out sets can be used, but without subject stratification; [(Suster, and Daelemans, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd36768795c696c990ff5c89be8d8b3b205858bd), [(Pappas et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37cdc28d45c9e10af44d887bdc4160336c465683), and [(Pampari et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cbdeb96380e028fb204f9802694e77bb772ed60) established held-out train/dev/test evaluation for medical/biomedical/clinical QA with metrics such as exact match, F1, BLEU, embedding similarity, and accuracy, but their query-, instance-, or template-level splits do not enforce article-level disjointness or subject balance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that allocating exactly 1% of articles from each subject may not ensure balanced coverage or sufficient statistical power, especially across 72 Nature Communications subjects with likely unequal article counts. [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5) used fixed 200-paper evaluation and validation splits to ensure all domains were represented, not a proportional 1% rule, and its final test-A contained only 666 questions after filtering, suggesting that small per-domain evaluation sets require explicit quotas and quality filtering. [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed) also balanced coarse labels evenly across splits rather than using a fixed percentage, and [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) reported per-textbook scientific problem-solving results without a validation set or 1% allocation, indicating that discipline comparability can be achieved through per-domain reporting rather than a uniform percentage. If 1% of the 131,393-article corpus in [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51) is applied per subject, rare subjects may contribute very few articles, and if each article yields only a limited number of figure-caption samples, per-subject test/validation counts may be too small for stable ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, FACTSCORE, G-EVAL, or accuracy estimates. The second major contradiction is that the phrase each sample will be derived from a single article does not by itself prevent content reuse across splits; the related work that actually prevents leakage assigns whole articles or papers to one split and enforces disjoint article/paper identifiers. [(Dasigi et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e3935ef7da6bcbb202ec7f8b285c313cadcd044) uses paper-level splits with 1,200/150/235 papers, [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed) requires empty intersection of article identifiers, and [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5) uses paper-level splits, all of which are stronger controls than merely deriving each sample from one article. A weaker but relevant contradiction is that several influential benchmarks used random example-, query-, instance-, or template-level splits and still produced model comparisons, which challenges the necessity of the proposed article-level 1% rule as a universal requirement. [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) used a random 60:20:20 example-level split for ScienceQA, with 12,726 training, 4,241 validation, and 4,241 test examples, and reported GPT-3 chain-of-thought accuracy of 75.17% and fine-tuned UnifiedQA BASE accuracy of 70.12% without subject-stratified article allocation; [(Suster, and Daelemans, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd36768795c696c990ff5c89be8d8b3b205858bd) split queries rather than reports, [(Pappas et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37cdc28d45c9e10af44d887bdc4160336c465683) split approximately 16.4M generated instances without article-level guarantees, and [(Pampari et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cbdeb96380e028fb204f9802694e77bb772ed60) used 80-20 template/instance splits. These older protocols are less controlled, but they show that the proposed method is not the only viable evaluation design and may reduce usable evaluation data if applied too restrictively.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify an article-level disjoint split across train, validation, and test, not merely a single-article sample rule: assign each Nature Communications article ID to exactly one split, ensure no figure, sub-figure, caption, sub-caption, or text passage from the same article appears in more than one split, and report the number of articles and samples per subject. This is directly supported by [(Dasigi et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e3935ef7da6bcbb202ec7f8b285c313cadcd044), [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed), and [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5). Second, replace or supplement the 1% per-subject rule with a stratified quota design that guarantees a minimum number of articles or samples per subject, or conduct a power analysis for the target metrics; if 1% is retained, report per-subject counts, confidence intervals, and sensitivity analyses, and consider fixed per-subject quotas similar to the 200-paper domain-balanced splits in [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5) or label-balanced splits in [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed). Third, define how many samples are generated per article and how multiple samples from the same article are handled: limit the maximum number of figure-caption or matching samples per article, cluster standard errors by article, or use article-level bootstrap resampling to avoid inflated significance, especially because [(Pappas et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37cdc28d45c9e10af44d887bdc4160336c465683) showed non-monotonic test accuracy across training sizes and no standard deviations, and [(Jin et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c3c4c88c7b07596221ac640c7b7102686e3eae3) used only 500 test instances. Fourth, add a quality-control and filtering stage for evaluation articles and figures, including checks for figure interpretability, caption/sub-caption extraction correctness, LaTeX conversion errors, duplicate or near-duplicate figures, and non-triviality, analogous to the manual filtering that discarded about 11% of evaluation questions in [(Pramanick et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8c31cdb4b8d2cd27a2cf2b1e59ff0b3457d51e5) and the 100-sample quality check in [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed). Fifth, use the validation split strictly for model selection and hyperparameter tuning, report repeated runs or seeds where feasible, and evaluate per-subject as well as aggregate performance, following the per-textbook reporting in [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) and the subject/grade diversity reporting in [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d). Finally, ensure that all training data for supervised fine-tuning or continuous pre-training excludes the validation and test articles, and include a human baseline with multiple graduate-student annotators and inter-annotator agreement, consistent with the human-evaluation practices in [(Dasigi et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e3935ef7da6bcbb202ec7f8b285c313cadcd044), [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), and [(Wu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9074d9719c5ce0dd3a7369dd0749cd08d7f67ed).

## To turn the collected scientific articles into training resources: We will create task-specific multimodal training data formatted as single-turn and multi-turn conversations, and develop interleaved text-image data suitable for continuous pre-training of MLLMs.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the most directly analogous and newest work, [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), which operationalizes the proposed training-resource method by crawling open-access Nature Communications articles up to 2024/04/15, extracting titles, abstracts, main text, references, figures, and captions, converting LaTeX with pylatexenc, and using regular-expression matching to extract 514,054 sub-captions from 742,273 figures in 131,393 articles across 72 subjects. This matches the research plan's data-collection strategy and its materials-science case study, and it demonstrates that the proposed interleaved article-figure corpus can be built at scale and used for continuous pre-training of LLaMA2, directly supporting the plan's LLaMA2-7B continuous pre-training and MP-20 evaluation. The next strongest support is [(Huang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbfef4723d8c8467d7bd523e1d0b703cce0e0f9c), which shows that interleaved image-text pretraining is a viable MLLM training signal: a 1.6B-parameter MLLM with a 1.3B 24-layer transformer, CLIP ViT-L/14 at 224x224, and a 1.2M-token batch containing 0.5M text, 0.5M image-caption, and 0.2M interleaved tokens for 300k steps, about 360B tokens, improves zero-shot Flickr30k CIDEr to 67.1 versus 60.6 and 61.5 for 3B and 9B Flamingo, improves Rendered SST-2 from 67.1 to 72.9 with multimodal chain-of-thought, and shows WebSRC performance drops by 12.0 EM and 20.7 F1 when extracted text is removed, indicating that image layout and style carry information beyond text. This supports the proposed interleaved text-image data for continuous pre-training, although it is web-scale rather than scientific. The earlier and less direct support is [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), which shows that task-specific multimodal science data can improve reasoning: on SCIENCEQA's 21,208 examples, fine-tuned UnifiedQA BASE reached 70.12% accuracy, chain-of-thought generation added 3.21 points for QCM->AE and 3.99 points for QCM->ALE, and GPT-3 chain-of-thought reached 75.17%, while VisualBERT reached 61.87%. This supports creating task-specific scientific training data and using explanations or chain-of-thought, which can be adapted to the plan's single-turn and multi-turn conversations for figure captioning and subfigure matching, but it does not support full-article interleaved pre-training.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly analogous work, [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), does not report quantitative outcomes, baseline comparisons, statistical significance, compute resources, training steps, batch size, learning rate, or evaluation metrics for the LLaMA2 continuous pre-training experiment; it only states that the interleaved scientific article and figure corpus was used to target stable material generation. Therefore, although the curation pipeline is highly relevant, the evidence does not yet prove that the proposed interleaved scientific pre-training improves MMS CICAP, MMS CIQA, or MP-20 validity, coverage, property distribution, or stability. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Huang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbfef4723d8c8467d7bd523e1d0b703cce0e0f9c) validates interleaved pre-training on general web data rather than graduate-level scientific articles, and its interleaved stream is only 0.2M of each 1.2M-token batch, so the benefit may be diluted by 1.0M text and image-caption tokens; its Raven IQ evidence is limited to 50 examples with 5.3% and 9.3% improvements over random, and its language-only instruction-tuning variant is relevant to conversational data but lacks conversation format, dataset size, and multi-turn details. This means the paper supports feasibility but does not directly contradict or confirm the proposed scientific single-turn/multi-turn conversational training data. The weakest contradiction is from the older [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), which is a multiple-choice science QA benchmark rather than full-article figure captioning or subfigure matching, uses captions generated by a ViT-GPT-2 captioning model for text-only language models, and reports that GPT-3 zero-shot was already near the best performance while answer-only few-shot prompting produced a negligible change. This suggests that task-specific conversational training may have diminishing returns for strong proprietary MLLMs and that the proposed method must be validated against zero-shot and few-shot baselines, but it does not invalidate the method because chain-of-thought and fine-tuning still improved smaller models.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative validation protocol for the proposed training resources, because the strongest related evidence is curation-level rather than outcome-level: report compute, steps, batch size, learning rate, token budget, and ablations for continuous pre-training of LLaMA2-7B and supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2-VL-2B with and without interleaved article-figure data, and evaluate on MMS CICAP, MMS CIQA, and MP-20 with ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, G-EVAL, accuracy, validity, coverage, property distribution, and stability, including confidence intervals or significance tests. The second most important action is to specify how single-turn and multi-turn conversations are constructed from the Nature Communications corpus, using article-level 1% test/validation splits per subject to prevent leakage, and include tasks such as figure-to-caption, subfigure-to-subcaption, subcaption-to-subfigure, caption generation, and chain-of-thought explanations, with explicit prompt templates, turn counts, and negative examples for matching. The third action is to calibrate the interleaved pre-training mixture using [(Huang et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbfef4723d8c8467d7bd523e1d0b703cce0e0f9c) as a baseline: start with a documented token ratio, for example 0.5M text, 0.5M image-caption, and 0.2M interleaved tokens per 1.2M-token batch, but test higher interleaved fractions because scientific figures are central to the plan, and use full-sentence packing, deduplication, and removal of downstream task data. The fourth action is to incorporate the chain-of-thought and explanation evidence from [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), training models to generate captions together with rationale or lecture-style explanations and evaluating both automatic metrics and human relevance, correctness, completeness, and gold quality, while also reporting zero-shot and few-shot GPT-4V/GPT-4o/Gemini/Claude baselines to show whether task-specific conversational data improves over strong proprietary models. The least important but still useful action is to document reproducibility details from [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), including figure-page crawling, pylatexenc conversion, regular-expression subcaption extraction, subject distribution, and image quality controls, so that the 131,393-article, 742,273-figure, and 514,054-subcaption corpus can be audited and reused for the 72-discipline benchmark.

## To test whether scientific data improves MLLMs: We will conduct supervised fine-tuning of Qwen2-VL-2B using our task-specific training data and explore continuous pre-training using interleaved article and figure data.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), which shows that task-specific scientific training data and reasoning supervision can improve science question answering on ScienceQA, a 21,208-example benchmark with 12,726 training, 4,241 validation, and 4,241 test examples, 9,122 unique questions, 48.7% image context, 48.2% text context, 30.8% both, 83.9% lectures, and 91.3% explanations. Fine-tuned VQA baselines reached up to 61.87% average accuracy with VisualBERT, while fine-tuned UnifiedQA BASE reached 70.12%, and chain-of-thought generation added 3.21 points for answer plus explanation and 3.99 points for answer plus lecture plus explanation; GPT-3 chain-of-thought reached 75.17%, with training details including up to 50 epochs at learning rate 5e-5 for VQA and 50,000 iterations with evaluation every 1,000 iterations and patience three for UnifiedQA. This directly supports the research plan's hypothesis that scientific data can improve model performance, although it tests multiple-choice ScienceQA rather than the plan's graduate-level MMS CICAP captioning and MMS CIQA matching tasks on Nature Communications figures. A weaker but highly relevant support is [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), which uses continuous pre-training of LLaMA2-7B on interleaved Nature Communications article and figure data from 131,393 articles and 742,273 figures across 72 subjects, crawled up to 2024-04-15, with webpage figure extraction, LaTeX-to-plain-text conversion, and regular-expression sub-caption extraction; this matches the proposed data source and interleaved pre-training idea, but the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes, so it supports feasibility more than effectiveness. Further methodological support comes from [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8), which constructs 158K visual instruction samples from COCO using text-only GPT-4 or ChatGPT with captions and bounding boxes, then fine-tunes with 8 A100 GPUs, Vicuna hyperparameters, filtered CC-595K pre-training for 1 epoch at learning rate 2e-3 and batch size 128, and instruction fine-tuning for 3 epochs at learning rate 2e-5 and batch size 32; this supports the plan's SFT data construction and training recipe, but it is not scientific and the excerpt reports no numerical outcomes. The broadest and weakest support is [(Huang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1230ef1de1ba9c4f6cb4789184a295133afac0), which formalizes visual instruction tuning with instruction-input-output data, vision encoder adapters, and evaluation via human, GPT-4, and task metrics, but provides no measured gains for scientific data or Qwen2-VL-2B. Finally, [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) weakly supports the motivation by showing that untrained or prompt-only models perform poorly on SCIBENCH multimodal scientific problems, with GPT-4 Program-of-Thoughts at 13.8% and the best open-source model LLaVA-LLaMA-2-13B at 7.4%, but it does not test training and therefore only justifies the need for a trained scientific MLLM baseline.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the related work directly validates the proposed method of supervised fine-tuning Qwen2-VL-2B on task-specific scientific data and continuous pre-training on interleaved Nature Communications article-figure data for graduate-level figure captioning and figure-caption matching. The closest empirical evidence, [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d), is limited to ScienceQA multiple-choice accuracy and explanation generation, not the plan's open-ended MMS CICAP captioning, subfigure/subcaption matching, 72-discipline Nature Communications coverage, or 1% per-subject test/validation split; its gains from fine-tuning and chain-of-thought may not transfer to specialized microscopy, spectroscopy, astronomy, molecular, or phylogenetic figures. A second contradiction is that [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51) uses the same Nature Communications article-figure data but only describes continuous pre-training of LLaMA2-7B for text-based materials generation, with no reported accuracy, validity, coverage, property-distribution, or stability results, no hardware, hyperparameters, or training duration, and no multimodal Qwen2-VL-2B evaluation; therefore it does not contradict the idea but also fails to provide evidence that interleaved scientific pre-training improves the proposed multimodal tasks. A third contradiction is that [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) and [(Huang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1230ef1de1ba9c4f6cb4789184a295133afac0) support instruction-tuning methodology but do not report quantitative outcomes for scientific-domain data, Qwen2-VL-2B, or continuous pre-training, so their evidence is methodological rather than empirical. The weakest contradiction is that [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) reports low SCIBENCH multimodal accuracy, 13.8% for GPT-4 with Program-of-Thoughts and 7.4% for LLaVA-LLaMA-2-13B, without confidence intervals, p-values, subset size, or training details; this suggests that current models struggle, but it does not show that supervised fine-tuning or continuous pre-training will close the gap, and the proprietary model advantage may reflect captioning and OCR augmentation rather than native scientific figure understanding.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a controlled training ablation that directly tests the proposed method: evaluate Qwen2-VL-2B zero-shot, after supervised fine-tuning on the task-specific MMS CI data, after continuous pre-training on interleaved Nature Communications article-figure data, and after continuous pre-training followed by supervised fine-tuning, using the plan's 1% per-subject test and validation split and reporting accuracy for MMS CIQA, ROUGE, METEOR, BERTScore, modified FACTSCORE, and G-EVAL for MMS CICAP, with paired bootstrap confidence intervals or significance tests to avoid overgeneralizing from single-run gains. The second most important action is to make the continuous pre-training experiment reproducible by adopting and reporting the Nature Communications pipeline details from [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), including the 131,393 articles, 742,273 figures, 72 subjects, 2024-04-15 cutoff, webpage figure extraction, pylatexenc LaTeX conversion, regular-expression sub-caption extraction, and, for the materials case study, LLaMA2-7B continuous pre-training and MP-20 evaluation of structural/compositional validity, coverage, property distribution, and stability, while adding hardware, learning rate, batch size, sequence length, number of steps, and catastrophic-forgetting checks. The third action is to incorporate the scientific reasoning evidence from [(Lu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3135733aa39dec20ce72aa138589dda27c8406d) by including lecture/explanation-style chain-of-thought or rationale fields in the SFT conversations, but ensuring the final evaluation remains open-ended captioning and matching rather than only multiple-choice ScienceQA, and by reporting explanation quality with BLEU-1, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, sentence similarity, and human relevance, correctness, and completeness. The fourth action is to use the training and data-generation setup from [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) and [(Huang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1230ef1de1ba9c4f6cb4789184a295133afac0) as a baseline recipe, for example 8 A100 GPUs, Vicuna-style hyperparameters, filtered pre-training alignment, and SFT around 3 epochs at learning rate 2e-5 and batch size 32, while generating single-turn and multi-turn scientific instructions from Nature Communications captions and sub-captions with GPT-4 or ChatGPT and then filtering for scientific accuracy. The least important but still useful action is to include external scientific visual baselines inspired by [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4), such as SCIBENCH multimodal problems and GPT-4 caption/OCR-augmented prompting, to show whether the trained Qwen2-VL-2B improves beyond untrained open-source MLLMs and to report confidence intervals for all comparisons.

## To demonstrate domain-specific downstream utility: We will conduct a focused case study in materials science, continuously pre-training LLaMA2-7B on interleaved scientific content and evaluating performance on material generation tasks using the MP-20 dataset.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), which directly aligns with the proposed materials-science case study by continuously pre-training LLaMA2 on an interleaved Nature Communications article-and-figure corpus and using materials science as a downstream utility test. That work uses a corpus of 131,393 articles, 742,273 figures, and 514,054 extracted sub-captions/sub-figures across 72 subjects, with materials science identified as the most represented subject, closely matching the research plan's Nature Communications collection through 2024-04-15 and its focus on interleaved scientific content. Although the excerpt does not report numeric MP-20 outcomes, it provides the closest methodological precedent for the proposed LLaMA2-7B continuous pre-training and material-generation evaluation. A second, methodologically important support is [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4), which supplies a contamination-control protocol for large-scale pretraining: duplicate detection against a 400M image-text corpus, manual thresholding, Overlap/Clean/All subsets, one-tailed binomial tests, Clopper-Pearson intervals, and Bonferroni correction. Their finding that median overlap was 2.2%, average overlap was 3.2%, and most accuracy shifts were below 0.1%, with a maximum 0.6% shift on Birdsnap at 12.1% overlap, supports the need to test whether MP-20 evaluation is inflated by overlap with pretraining scientific articles. A third support is [(Chen et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d2434d6290d8800e4af08352fc88fac40a5e04), which shows that LLaMA2-7B can be adapted to materials-science synthesis question answering using 13,878 instruction examples derived from 35,675 synthesis processes, trained with LoRA on one NVIDIA A100 at learning rate 1e-4, batch size 8, and one epoch, and qualitatively outperformed general-purpose LLMs on two literature-based synthesis questions. This supports the feasibility of domain-specific LLaMA2-7B materials training, although it is text-only supervised fine-tuning rather than continuous pre-training on interleaved content. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), which demonstrates that large decoder-only pre-training can improve downstream generation and reasoning, with 175B GPT-3 trained on 300B tokens achieving 20.5 PTB perplexity versus 35.8 for reported zero-shot state-of-the-art and improved few-shot performance on several tasks. However, that work did not continuously pre-train on scientific content, did not use multimodal interleaved data, and did not evaluate MP-20 material generation, so its support for the proposed case study is indirect.

### Contradictions

The most serious limitation is that the closest direct precedent, [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51), does not provide quantitative outcomes for the materials-generation case study, including validity, coverage, property distribution, stability, metastability, or comparisons against baselines, and the excerpt does not specify model size, MP-20 use, prompt templates, hardware, optimizer, learning rate, pre-training steps, or evaluation metrics. This means the proposed expectation that continuous pre-training on interleaved scientific content will improve MP-20 material generation is not yet empirically substantiated by the most relevant work. A second important contradiction is a potential modality mismatch: the proposed method says LLaMA2-7B will be continuously pre-trained on interleaved scientific content, but LLaMA2-7B is a text-only language model, and the related work does not clarify whether figures are represented as images, captions, sub-captions, or text-encoded structures. If the case study relies on actual figure pixels, a vision encoder or multimodal backbone is required; if it relies only on text, the claim of interleaved multimodal pre-training should be narrowed. Third, [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4) shows that pretraining/test overlap can inflate downstream accuracy and that Overlap and Clean subsets can differ in distribution or difficulty, with examples such as Country211 having 21.5% overlap but only a 0.2% accuracy increase and Kinetics-700 showing a 20% apparent drop on the Overlap subset due to distribution shift. Although their overall shifts were small, MP-20 material generation may be more sensitive if target materials, formulas, crystal structures, or synthesis descriptions appear in the pretraining corpus, so uncontrolled overlap could make validity and stability metrics reflect memorization rather than generalization. Fourth, [(Chen et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d2434d6290d8800e4af08352fc88fac40a5e04) provides only qualitative evaluation on two literature-based synthesis questions, with no quantitative metrics, repeated runs, confidence intervals, statistical significance, or MP-20 evaluation, so it cannot robustly support the proposed material-generation claims. Finally, [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) warns that large pre-training alone does not establish domain-specific utility: GPT-3 did not perform continuous scientific pre-training, had a filtering bug that left benchmark overlap, and some few-shot results remained below fine-tuned baselines, such as Winogrande 77.7% versus 79% for fine-tuned RoBERTA and 84.6% for fine-tuned T5, while ARC Challenge was near chance at 51.5%.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a rigorous MP-20 contamination and evaluation protocol before claiming that continuous pre-training improves material generation. Following [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4), the team should run duplicate or near-duplicate detection between MP-20 prompts, target materials, formulas, structures, and any reference answers against the interleaved Nature Communications pretraining corpus, then report Overlap, Clean, and All subsets with one-tailed binomial tests, Clopper-Pearson intervals, and Bonferroni correction, and should present clean performance as the primary result. The second priority is to resolve the LLaMA2-7B modality issue by specifying exactly what interleaved scientific content means: if using LLaMA2-7B, pre-train on text-only article text plus figure captions, sub-captions, and text-encoded material structures; if using actual images, replace or augment LLaMA2-7B with a multimodal architecture and report the vision encoder, projection layer, and training details. The third priority is to define a reproducible MP-20 evaluation setup with explicit metrics for structural/compositional validity, coverage, property distribution alignment, stability and metastability percentages, and baselines including base LLaMA2-7B, a text-only caption pre-training control, a supervised fine-tuning control, and prior LLaMA2-based material-generation systems. The fourth priority is to report full training and inference details, including hardware, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, sequence length, number of pre-training steps, data mixture, sampling strategy, deduplication, filtering, prompt templates, decoding parameters, and random seeds, because [(Li et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e073a2fda0c4d6198ba231c8e9df10f4f2ba1c51) omits these details and [(Chen et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9d2434d6290d8800e4af08352fc88fac40a5e04) shows that materials-science adaptation can be resource-light but also that qualitative evaluation is insufficient. The least urgent but still useful action is to borrow evaluation discipline from [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) by using multiple decoding settings, likelihood scoring where applicable, exact-match or F1 for formula/structure fields, and ablations comparing generic pre-training, scientific text pre-training, and interleaved caption pre-training to isolate the contribution of multimodal scientific content.
