## To improve interpretability of transformer-based text classification, the plan proposes: 'Contrast-CAT, a novel activation contrast-based attribution method that improves upon existing activation-based approaches by filtering out class-irrelevant features through an activation contrasting framework.'

### Support

The strongest support for Contrast-CAT comes from work that already evaluates transformer token attributions with deletion-style faithfulness on the same or closely related datasets. [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764) shows ALTI outperforms gradient baselines such as Grad_l2, GxI_l2, GxI_mu, IG_l2, and IG_mu on BERT, RoBERTa, and DistilBERT for SST-2, IMDB, Yelp, and SVA, using erasure-based comprehensiveness and sufficiency, and reports that ALTI is more robust across 10 MultiBERTs initializations; this directly supports the plan focus on BERT text classification, token deletion, and attention-based attribution, although it does not implement activation contrast. [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef) provides the closest methodological support for class-specific, perturbation-validated token attribution, because DecompX propagates decomposed token vectors through feed-forward networks, biases, and the classification head, and outperforms vector and gradient baselines on AOPC and accuracy when masking most or least important tokens on SST2, QNLI, HATEXPLAIN, and MNLI; its ablations show that removing feed-forward networks, biases, or the classification head harms faithfulness, supporting Contrast-CAT need for full transformer propagation and class-specific attribution. [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9) supports combining gradient-weighted attention relevancy with MoRF/LeRF perturbation AUC in transformers, reporting AUC gains over raw attention, rollout, partial LRP, and Grad-CAM in LXMERT and ViT settings, which aligns with the plan AOPC/LOdds deletion evaluation. [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) supports class-specific transformer attribution by combining relevance and gradient diffusion over attention graphs, with BERT-base sentiment experiments using top-k token-F1 and baselines including raw attention, rollout, partial/full LRP, and Grad-CAM, reinforcing the plan baseline set. [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) supports the plan evaluation design by introducing GAE, which combines MoRF/LeRF local consistency with contrastiveness, and shows absLRP outperforming GuidedGradCAM and contrastive-LRP on ImageNet and PascalVOC with Wilcoxon significance, although its quantitative results are image-based and only qualitative for BERT sentiment. [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad) supports the motivation for moving beyond attention by showing that top-attended items often cause only small JS divergence changes and that decision flips can require removing many tokens, which justifies deletion-based refinement and class-irrelevant filtering. [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) supports the need to suppress class-irrelevant tokens because raw attention is weakly aligned with blank-out importance and input gradients, while rollout and flow improve alignment but still assign high weights to attractors in subject-verb agreement. [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) supports Taylor-style treatment of normalization-like components because first-order Taylor propagation in local renormalization layers lowered AUC under highest-pixel-first perturbation on CIFAR-10, ImageNet, and MIT Places relative to identity propagation, analogous to using Taylor-based activation contrast. [(Schulz et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57d697214af89d8b8f77eeaaf040a43d1a0c651b) supports filtering uninformative feature components by fitting a per-sample bottleneck that suppresses uninformative regions and by using sensitivity-n masking, conceptually aligned with Contrast-CAT goal of removing class-irrelevant activation features. [(Hao et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1686203adc5f2dbc18627ce64f66d33eb81432a5) supports attention-gradient attribution in BERT-base-cased, where pruning high self-attention attribution heads caused larger accuracy changes than average-attention pruning, but its evidence is head-level rather than token-level. [(Brunner et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1fe62a928bf5cfac0f373728f3a4de3cefe0951d) supports first-order Taylor attribution of BERT hidden embeddings to input tokens on MRPC, although it does not evaluate class-prediction faithfulness. [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762) supports layer-wise gradient attribution in BERT probing tasks, where token gradients and Spearman correlations localize inverted bigrams, but it does not use deletion-based faithfulness or activation contrast. [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) supports the use of a reference baseline and gradient integration, which is the conceptual ancestor of Contrast-CAT reference activations, although it does not use low-confidence references or transformer attention. [(Kindermans et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62e39c6dbcead1fe4d29017914591d929aa6ac4c) supports reference-based attribution when the reference is chosen carefully, because Integrated Gradients with a black-image reference and PatternAttribution-based DTD satisfied input invariance, while zero-vector references failed, motivating explicit validation of low-confidence reference selection. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) for efficient backpropagation-based attribution, [(Baehrens et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e6238c8613b5b81f81552939bce33296aedfbfe) for local gradient attribution validated against known toxicophores and detoxicophores, [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) for activation-gradient class-discriminative attribution and bias visualization, [(Du et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3df952d4a724655f7520ff95d4b2cef90fff0cae) for using attribution heatmaps to detect reliance on irrelevant words, [(Feng et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74e9053d6f44f4507bd40bbea999ee65f0cbefb2) for reduced low-confidence inputs and cross-architecture generalizability, [(Wiegreffe, and Pinter, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce177672b00ddf46e4906157a7e997ca9338b8b9) for testing whether attention is necessary in text classification, [(Kovaleva et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d78aed1dac6656affa4a04cbf225ced11a83d103) for BERT self-attention extraction and head importance, [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6) for comparative token attribution with random saliency baselines, [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) for survey-level support of gradient, LRP, and occlusion methods, [(Lopardo et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77ea6f87e4733747ea6c39363c2777e782f4c410) for signed GxI token weights, [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6f7dab88659053c2be5fc9cd885627c58b9ee6) for the efficiency of one forward-backward gradient attribution, and [(Kokalj et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7823b7c83ce4b7d739fbd3436ba2c911dfec4d82) for qualitative visualization preferences, although the last is a user study rather than faithfulness evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that layer-wise activation or gradient attributions may not be the most faithful token explanations on the plan sentiment datasets. [(Kroeger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70017c9096cca317942156e3f91f8141da42eef1) reports that GPT-4 with 16 in-context word-removal examples outperforms Layer Integrated Gradients, Layer Gradient SHAP, Layer Deep Lift, Layer Gradient x Activation, Layer Activation, and Layer Conductance on Amazon, IMDb, and Yelp under modified PGI and PGU, with P-ICL giving a 35 percent increase in PGI-text over random on Amazon versus 30 percent for LIME-16; although the model is a single-layer transformer and the metrics differ from AOPC/LOdds, this cautions that Contrast-CAT must demonstrate faithfulness gains over strong perturbation-informed baselines. A second major contradiction is that attention weights are not reliably causal. [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad) finds that top-attended items often produce only small JS divergence changes, many positive divergences are near zero even with attention gaps around 0.4, and decision flips often require removing many tokens, with random orderings sometimes competitive; this contradicts any design that treats averaged attention as sufficient evidence of token relevance. [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) further contradicts the assumption that attention-based maps fully remove class-irrelevant tokens, because raw attention is weakly aligned with blank-out importance and input gradients, and rollout/flow, while better, still assign relatively high weights to attractors in verb-number examples. [(Kindermans et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62e39c6dbcead1fe4d29017914591d929aa6ac4c) contradicts naive reference construction by showing that reference-based attribution can be highly sensitive to the reference and input transformations: Integrated Gradients with a zero-vector reference failed input invariance while a black-image reference succeeded, and a checkered-box transformation caused most Integrated Gradients references except PatternAttribution to fail; this implies Contrast-CAT low-confidence reference activations must be tested for stability, not merely selected by a confidence threshold. [(Wiegreffe, and Pinter, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce177672b00ddf46e4906157a7e997ca9338b8b9) provides a caution that learned attention may not be necessary, because uniform-attention LSTM classifiers often achieved comparable one-class F1 to learned attention on SST, IMDB, 20News, AgNews, diabetes, and anemia, although TVD/JSD values are not fully reported. [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) reports that random attention weights affected text classification performance minimally, while uniform attention substantially degraded machine translation, suggesting attention may be less load-bearing in text classification than in sequence-to-sequence tasks. [(Lopardo et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77ea6f87e4733747ea6c39363c2777e782f4c410) warns that nonnegative aggregation rules such as G-l1 and G-l2 cannot distinguish positive from negative token contributions, so Contrast-CAT should avoid absolute-value aggregation if class-specific signed attribution is a goal. Weaker contradictions include [(Kokalj et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7823b7c83ce4b7d739fbd3436ba2c911dfec4d82), whose user study shows SHAP received the lowest positioning score and willingness-to-use score while LIME received the highest overall ranking, indicating that attribution quality or visualization usability may not transfer automatically to user preference; [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6), which provides no quantitative attribution-quality comparison and therefore cannot validate the plan faithfulness claims; and [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6f7dab88659053c2be5fc9cd885627c58b9ee6), which notes that gradient-based attribution can be expensive for very large models, a minor but relevant computational constraint for multi-layer, multi-reference Contrast-CAT.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run the planned BERT-base experiments on Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB with a direct faithfulness benchmark against the strongest transformer token-attribution baselines, including ALTI, DecompX, CAT, AttCAT, TIS, raw attention, rollout, flow, LRP, IG, GxI, and random saliency, reporting AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF with confidence intervals and Wilcoxon or bootstrap significance tests, following the evaluation protocols in [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764), [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef), and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8). Second, the reference-activation mechanism must be validated as a causal and stable component: ablate low-confidence references against random references, same-class references, and no-reference baselines; vary threshold gamma and number of references; test reference sensitivity and invariance inspired by [(Kindermans et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62e39c6dbcead1fe4d29017914591d929aa6ac4c); use reduced or low-confidence input constructions inspired by [(Feng et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74e9053d6f44f4507bd40bbea999ee65f0cbefb2); and check robustness across random seeds or MultiBERTs-style initializations as in [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764). Third, preserve signed, class-specific attribution by using signed gradients and classification-head propagation rather than nonnegative norm aggregation, and evaluate class-distinctness with Kendall-tau and a contrastive metric analogous to GAE, motivated by [(Lopardo et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77ea6f87e4733747ea6c39363c2777e782f4c410), [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef), and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8). Fourth, propagate attribution through the full transformer computation, including residual connections, layer normalizations, feed-forward networks, biases, and attention, and ablate single-layer versus multi-layer aggregation, because [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef) and [(Modarressi et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5470f044662ada6ca1ce9b23e7e4bba0ffc87b87) show that incomplete propagation or norm-only aggregation can lose faithfulness. Fifth, specify the deletion protocol precisely: use [MASK] or zeroing consistently, define token versus subword units, report top-k or top-percent curves, control for sequence length and special tokens, and include rationale sufficiency at top 20 or 40 percent as in [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef) and [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4). Sixth, treat attention as an auxiliary signal rather than a faithfulness guarantee, include raw/rollout/flow baselines, and test attractor or class-irrelevant token cases as in [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) and [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad). Seventh, report full reproducibility details, including hardware, seeds, learning rates, fine-tuning epochs, integration steps, threshold gamma, reference count, runtime, and dataset splits, and consider extending to RoBERTa and DistilBERT to match [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764) and [(Modarressi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3327bdc202fb4b3673c2fa1ac18ab436adc72fef). Finally, if the paper claims practical interpretability, add a small qualitative or user evaluation, but keep faithfulness metrics primary, since [(Kokalj et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7823b7c83ce4b7d739fbd3436ba2c911dfec4d82) shows user preference is a separate construct.

## To approximate class-relevant activation contributions, the plan states that the approach: 'builds on the first-order Taylor expansion approximation where we contrast target activations with reference activations from sequences that produce low confidence scores for the target class.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed first-order Taylor activation-contrast method is the direct contrastive token-level attribution evidence in [(Yin, and Neubig, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ec16018e9152c50bbaed1d49c3077b9fb6d2838), which compared non-contrastive and contrastive Gradient Norm, Gradient x Input, and Input Erasure on GPT-2 1.5B and GPT-Neo 2.7B next-token prediction; in correct-prediction examples, contrastive Gradient x Input improved mean reciprocal rank from 0.59 to 0.64, contrastive Erasure from 0.64 to 0.68, and contrastive Gradient Norm from 0.58 to 0.61, while a user study with 10 participants, 20 confusable word pairs, 40 sentences per participant, and 4000 data points found statistically significant simulatability gains, including 65.62 percent for contrastive Gradient x Input versus 64.00 percent non-contrastive and 64.88 percent versus 46.50 percent usefulness for contrastive versus non-contrastive Erasure. This supports the plan's central idea that contrasting a target state with an alternative reference can isolate class- or token-relevant evidence, although it uses foil tokens rather than low-confidence class references and autoregressive models rather than BERT classifiers. The next strongest support is [(Eberle et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b940088cc1fb62459ef1a848aa6658a67502b757), which evaluates contrastive and non-contrastive LRP, Gradient x Input, and Gradient Norm on RoBERTa-base for SST2 and DynaSent, showing that contrastive class-logit rationales are viable for transformer text classification and that human annotations agree more with model rationales than gaze does (SST2 human-model Cohen's Kappa 0.46, gaze-human 0.31, gaze-model 0.26; DynaSent human-model 0.41), but its near-identity of contrastive and non-contrastive rationales in binary tasks (0.99 agreement and correlation above 0.99) makes the support for low-confidence activation references weaker. A third strong support is the evaluation design in [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), which uses Most Relevant First and Least Relevant First zero-masking, T=10 steps, gradient-based impact scores, a contrastiveness mosaic with one positive and three negative images, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests at p=0.05; on VGG16, absLRP scored 0.272 on ImageNet and 0.238 on PascalVOC versus GuidedGradCAM 0.207 and 0.167 and contrastive-LRP 0.12 and 0.112, and on ResNet50 absLRP and contrastive-LRP were more than double the next-best method, directly supporting the planned AOPC/LOdds MoRF/LeRF and class-distinctness evaluation, albeit at pixel level. Fourth, [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9) supports gradient-weighted attention relevancy propagation in transformers, with LXMERT VQA perturbation AUCs of 63.24 negative and 51.10 positive on 10,000 validation samples, beating Transformer attribution 61.46/52.75, raw attention 61.34/54.17, partial LRP 60.90/52.82, Grad-CAM 60.08/59.21, and rollout 58.64/57.23, which supports the plan's use of gradients, attention, and perturbation AUC, but it does not use low-confidence reference activations. Fifth, [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6) supports LRP baselines and perturbation faithfulness on transformer text classification datasets including SST-2 11,844 reviews, IMDB 50,000 reviews, TweetEval 59,899 examples, hate detection 12,970 examples, and emotion recognition 5,052 examples, reporting that combined attention-head and layer-normalization LRP outperformed gradient-times-input, raw attention, rollout, attention-flow, generic attention, and LRP variants without the rules, with higher activation-curve area and lower pruning MSE, though exact values and significance were not provided. Sixth, [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) supports transformer LRP and class-specificity testing, using BERT-base 512-token limit, ImageNet 50,000 validation images, ImageNet-Segmentation 4,276 images, and Movie Reviews 1,600/200/200 splits, but only qualitative outcomes are reported, so it is weaker support. Seventh, [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) supports attention rollout and flow over raw attention and blank-out deletion as reference importance, using a 6-layer 8-head transformer with hidden size 128 and 0.96 subject-verb agreement accuracy, and Spearman correlations with blank-out and input gradients, but it also shows low correlations on DistillBERT SST-2, making it mixed support for the plan's averaged attention component. Eighth, [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e257b5f0a0148076e9161202880b022e521ac59) supports activation-based faithfulness by using activation-derived soft masks and target-class confidence increases on VGG16 ILSVRC2012 validation, with 31.5 percent average drop and 30.6 percent average increase when 50 percent of pixels were muted, deletion/insertion gains over 2000 images, and over 60 percent saliency energy inside ground-truth boxes on 500 localization images, but it uses a single baseline input rather than low-confidence reference sequences. Ninth, [(Maladry et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b45e5b84fe14a0fffef7f6254a57b5f0116ab7e7) supports token deletion/replacement faithfulness and bias-word analysis in a fine-tuned Dutch RobBERT irony classifier on 4453 training and 1113 test tweets, with 10-fold cross-validation, 2 epochs, AdamW 5e-5, batch size 8, and an NVIDIA Tesla V100; removing intensifiers from non-ironic tweets improved non-ironic recall by 7 percent, decreased ironic recall by 8 percent, and reduced average predicted irony probability by 8 percent, supporting the plan's token-wise deletion refinement, although adjective replacement effects were only 1 percent and 0.5 percent and no significance tests were reported. Tenth, [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) provides conceptual support for the plan's faithfulness-oriented occlusion evaluation and integrated-gradients baseline, noting that occlusion requires n plus one forward passes for n positions and that saliency should be judged by whether identified words change predictions, but it supplies no quantitative transformer text-classification results. Eleventh, [(Achtibat et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2539713a31ee08cc8116c1355da9f96896df145) supports LRP-based transformer attribution and efficiency, using a linearized decomposition adjacent to first-order approximation on ViT-B-16 ImageNet, LLaMA-2-7B IMDB and Wikipedia, and SQuAD v2 with Mixtral 8x7B and Flan-T5-XL, but the excerpt lacks numeric faithfulness scores, so it is weak support. The older iterative lineage also supports the general first-order and activation-attribution premise: [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) showed class-specific activation maps in CNNs with only 1-2 percent classification drop from global average pooling and GoogLeNet-GAP top-5 localization error of 43 percent on ILSVRC validation and 37.1 percent on test; [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) showed Grad-CAM can expose class-relevant and biased regions in VGG-16, including a doctor/nurse model improving from 82 percent to 90 percent after gender-balanced retraining; [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) established Integrated Gradients as a reference-based path integral with completeness and sensitivity; [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) established DeepLIFT as a single-pass reference-based backpropagation method; [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) established additive local reference-based attribution and user-study support for SHAP; [(Baehrens et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e6238c8613b5b81f81552939bce33296aedfbfe) showed local gradients in a Gaussian process classifier on 6512 compounds with 142 features, AUC 0.84, and 9 of 10 toxicophores near the top with mean rank 19; and [(Li et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fafa541419b3756968fe5b3156c6f0257cb29c23) showed first-derivative saliency qualitatively highlights sentiment-bearing words, but these older methods do not implement low-confidence activation contrast for BERT token attribution.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed low-confidence activation-contrast method is also the most directly relevant result in [(Eberle et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b940088cc1fb62459ef1a848aa6658a67502b757): on RoBERTa-base binary SST2 and DynaSent, contrastive and non-contrastive LRP rationales were nearly identical, with 0.99 agreement on binarized scores and continuous-score correlation above 0.99, because a binary target class already implicitly contrasts with the only alternative class. Since the plan evaluates Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, all binary, this evidence suggests that the added low-confidence reference activations may yield limited gains over simpler non-contrastive or foil-class contrastive attributions unless the reference selection changes the decision boundary in a way not captured by logit contrast. The next strongest contradiction is that contrastive attribution is not uniformly beneficial: [(Yin, and Neubig, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ec16018e9152c50bbaed1d49c3077b9fb6d2838) found mixed gains on incorrect predictions, with contrastive Gradient x Input improving mean reciprocal rank from 0.55 to 0.64 but contrastive Erasure remaining at 0.55, and weaker GPT-Neo correlations for evidence distance (Gradient x Input 0.56, Gradient Norm 0.41, Erasure -0.51), plus computational expense that limited clustering to GPT-2; this cautions that low-confidence references may help correct predictions but may not improve error explanations or scale efficiently. A third important contradiction concerns the plan's averaged attention scores: [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) found that raw attention had low Spearman correlation with blank-out and input gradients, and although rollout and flow were better, correlations on DistillBERT SST-2 for 100 test samples were very low, suggesting that attention-based weighting may not be a faithful proxy for token importance in the exact sentiment setting targeted by the plan. Fourth, [(Maladry et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b45e5b84fe14a0fffef7f6254a57b5f0116ab7e7) shows that token-level correlated features can have small causal effects and that perturbation-based refinement may be noisy: replacing a highly correlated adjective with the least correlated positive synonym reduced average irony probability by only 1 percent, a random synonym by 0.5 percent, and augmenting training with modified samples did not improve held-out performance, with base precision/recall/F1/accuracy 0.7242/0.7210/0.7210/0.7227 versus augmented 0.7245/0.7221/0.7222/0.7237 and no statistical significance reported. Fifth, [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9) reports only modest margins over strong baselines and no significance tests, for example LXMERT VQA negative-perturbation AUC 63.24 versus Transformer attribution 61.46 and partial LRP 60.90, and positive-perturbation AUC 21.61 versus Transformer attribution 21.68 for text, implying that gains from more elaborate transformer attribution may be small and must be tested rigorously. Sixth, [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) shows a tradeoff between local faithfulness and contrastiveness: on ViT-Base, absLRP had the highest total score and more than double HiResCAM local consistency, but gradient-based methods scored higher on Contrastiveness and the overall score was lower than in convolutional settings, warning that optimizing MoRF/LeRF AOPC/LOdds may not simultaneously maximize the planned Kendall-τ class-distinctness metric. Seventh, several related methods provide only qualitative or incomplete quantitative evidence, limiting their ability to validate the proposed mechanism: [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) reports qualitative class-specificity and perturbation protocols but no numeric token-F1 or AUC in the excerpt; [(Achtibat et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2539713a31ee08cc8116c1355da9f96896df145) claims faithfulness and efficiency but provides no numeric outcomes in the excerpt; and [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) summarizes occlusion and gradient methods without dataset-level quantitative results, while noting n plus one forward passes for n positions. Finally, the older activation and gradient lineage does not directly establish the need for low-confidence reference activation contrast: [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) and [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) show class-specific activation maps can be useful without reference activation subtraction, and [(Baehrens et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e6238c8613b5b81f81552939bce33296aedfbfe) and [(Li et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fafa541419b3756968fe5b3156c6f0257cb29c23) show first-order gradients can already identify domain-relevant features, so the plan's claim that class-irrelevant activation features are a fundamental limitation must be demonstrated against CAT, AttCAT, TIS, LRP, and contrastive logit baselines rather than assumed.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to test whether low-confidence reference activations provide a measurable advantage over foil-class contrast and non-contrastive baselines on the plan's binary datasets, because [(Eberle et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b940088cc1fb62459ef1a848aa6658a67502b757) found near-identity of contrastive and non-contrastive LRP on SST2 and DynaSent; run Contrast-CAT against CAT, AttCAT, TIS, Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att x Att-grads, Grad-SAM, Gradient x Input, Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT, and contrastive LRP on Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, and report AOPC, LOdds, MoRF/LeRF, Kendall-τ, confidence intervals, paired Wilcoxon or bootstrap tests, and effect sizes, while adding at least one multi-class dataset to determine whether contrast benefits emerge when the target class is not implicitly opposed by a single alternative. Second, re-evaluate the averaged attention component because [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) shows attention can be weakly aligned with blank-out and gradients on SST-2; ablate no-attention, raw averaged attention, attention rollout, attention flow, and gradient-weighted attention as in [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9), and report Spearman or Kendall correlations with blank-out, input gradients, and deletion-based importance before retaining attention as a multiplicative or additive term. Third, strengthen statistical and reproducibility reporting in line with [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9), and [(Maladry et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b45e5b84fe14a0fffef7f6254a57b5f0116ab7e7): specify BERT-base checkpoints, GPU, random seeds, batch size, threshold gamma, number of reference activations, layer selection, aggregation rule, and runtime/memory; use multiple seeds, paired tests, multiple-comparison correction, and sanity checks with random, uniform, and shuffled attributions. Fourth, refine the token-wise deletion quality filter using evidence from [(Maladry et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b45e5b84fe14a0fffef7f6254a57b5f0116ab7e7) and [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451): use multiple deletion protocols such as UNK, MASK, zero embedding, and random synonym replacement, evaluate on a held-out deletion set, report effect sizes and significance, and avoid selecting references or layers using the same deletion metric that is later reported as the final faithfulness score. Fifth, address computational cost and reference availability, motivated by [(Yin, and Neubig, 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ec16018e9152c50bbaed1d49c3077b9fb6d2838) and [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c): report forward/backward pass counts for reference selection, multi-layer aggregation, and deletion refinement; test gamma values such as 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, and 0.7, reference counts such as 1, 3, 5, and 10, and fallback strategies when few low-confidence target-class sequences exist, including foil-class references, zero-activation references, or Integrated-Gradients-style baselines from [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) and DeepLIFT from [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6). Sixth, validate generalization and bias robustness using [(Maladry et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b45e5b84fe14a0fffef7f6254a57b5f0116ab7e7), [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2), and [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6): include bias-word or spurious-feature probes, low-confidence and misclassified samples, cross-architecture tests such as RoBERTa, DistilBERT, and ALBERT, and multi-layer ablations comparing last-layer, middle-layer, and all-layer aggregation, so that the claimed filtering of class-irrelevant activation features is supported by faithfulness, class-distinctness, efficiency, and bias-robustness evidence rather than by qualitative plausibility alone.

## To filter class-irrelevant features, the plan uses: 'constructing contrastive references by selecting activation sequences from inputs where the model's confidence for the target class falls below a predefined threshold γ.'

### Support

The strongest support for constructing low-confidence contrastive references comes from newer activation-contrast evidence in CNNs: Score-CAM uses a baseline input and an Increase of Confidence signal to weight upsampled normalized activation maps, and on 2,000 ILSVRC2012 validation images with VGG16 it produced a 31.5% average target-class score drop and a 30.6% average increase under masking, plus more than 60% saliency energy inside ground-truth boxes on 500 single-object images, showing that baseline-contrast activation attribution can improve faithfulness and localization [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e257b5f0a0148076e9161202880b022e521ac59). Although it uses a fixed baseline rather than a gamma-thresholded low-confidence pool, it directly supports the plan's core assumption that contrasting target activations with a reference and weighting by class-confidence change can isolate class-relevant features. This newer activation-contrast result extends earlier reference-based attribution ideas from DeepLIFT's reference and positive/negative contribution rules [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) and SHAP's background/reference distribution for additive attribution on MNIST and user studies [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2). The next strongest support is newer transformer-specific evidence that filtering irrelevant information improves attribution quality: Song et al. filter attention heads with syntactic and position masks using xi_synt=0.1 and xi_pos=0.8, and report the highest AOPC and lowest LOdds on SST-2, IMDB, Yelp Polarity, QQP, and MNLI, with random-mask and 10-100% corruption ablations showing that arbitrary or corrupted filters degrade performance toward GAE [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e); this supports the plan's random-reference and same-class-reference ablations and the use of AOPC and LOdds, though it filters heads rather than selecting low-confidence activation sequences. Gu et al. provide strong problem-level support by showing LRP maps for AlexNet, VGG16, and GoogLeNet are instance-specific but not class-discriminative, with top-3 and random top-100 class maps almost identical, motivating the need to remove class-irrelevant activation information and evaluate class-distinctness with Kendall-τ [(Gu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbdb103a298c20644553c201c9dfb0399b552b98). Selvaraju et al. offer weaker but directly relevant support for low-confidence target activations: Grad-CAM on ImageNet-pretrained VGG16 localized present categories even when adversarial inputs made absent categories exceed 0.9999 confidence, suggesting low-confidence target-class activations can still contain class-relevant signal [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2), but the evidence is qualitative and does not test reference subtraction. Transformer-specific support for gradient and attention attribution and layer aggregation comes from Mohebbi et al., who compute layer-wise gradients of BERT token representations to correct-class probabilities on SentEval tasks with 100k train and 10k test examples, find swapped-bigram attribution increases and Spearman correlation with an inversion mask rises over layers, and show 10%-data classifiers produce correlated attributions [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762). Finally, Bastings and Filippova and Wu et al. support the plan's deletion, occlusion, and gradient baselines, noting occlusion requires n+1 forward passes and gradient methods require one forward and backward pass but can be expensive [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6f7dab88659053c2be5fc9cd885627c58b9ee6), while Du et al. qualitatively support the motivation that models may rely on irrelevant words [(Du et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3df952d4a724655f7520ff95d4b2cef90fff0cae).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that reference selection is not automatically stable or meaningful: Kindermans et al. show that identical-weight MNIST MLPs with 1024 ReLU units per layer, trained for 10 epochs to 98.3% accuracy, produce different saliency heatmaps under a constant input shift from [0,1] to [-1,0] for some methods; Integrated Gradients with a zero-vector reference fails input invariance while a black-image reference passes, Deep Taylor Decomposition with LRP fails while PatternAttribution passes, and a checkered-box transformation breaks the black-image reference [(Kindermans et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62e39c6dbcead1fe4d29017914591d929aa6ac4c). This directly challenges the assumption that selecting references by a low target-class confidence threshold gamma will yield stable, class-relevant contrasts unless the reference pool is invariant to input encoding, paraphrase, and threshold perturbations. A second important contradiction is newer evidence that deletion-based refinement can be unstable: Nam and Lee report that deletion-based perturbation was unstable in their image experiments and therefore use MoRF insertion into a blank image with a Gaussian filter, with 1% increments up to 20% distortion, and show their method and RAP keep predictions stable on top 4,000 pixels while LRP and random noise change predictions [(Nam, and Lee, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bda9c1dc81c301104bdfbe145cf2c426a6df631d); this cautions the plan's token-wise deletion tests and deletion-based filtering may be noisy for BERT text classification. Third, Bastings and Filippova report that random attention weights had minimal effect on text classification while uniform attention weights substantially degraded machine translation, and that LRP was reported to work better than gradient-based methods on text classification [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), which contradicts the assumption that attention scores and gradient-based activation contrast will automatically improve classification attribution. Fourth, Gu et al. show that standard LRP maps are not class-discriminative, with maps for different target classes almost identical [(Gu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbdb103a298c20644553c201c9dfb0399b552b98), suggesting that merely contrasting target activations with low-confidence references may not be sufficient to produce class-distinct maps without explicit class-specific weighting or validation. Fifth, computational and tuning constraints are a weaker contradiction: Wu et al. note perturbation-based scoring requires N inferences for N tokens and gradient scoring can be expensive for large models [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6f7dab88659053c2be5fc9cd885627c58b9ee6), while constructing and tuning a low-confidence reference pool adds threshold gamma, reference count, class balance, and collection costs that are not addressed by the cited works. Finally, the cited works do not provide direct evidence that low-confidence target-class activation sequences are better references than random, zero, same-class, or high-confidence references in transformer text classification; the closest newer transformer filtering work uses syntactic and position head masks rather than confidence-thresholded activation references [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e), leaving the older reference-based assumption unvalidated.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should validate the low-confidence reference construction before claiming Contrast-CAT improves attribution: sweep gamma, compare low-confidence references against random references, same-class references, zero or blank references, and high-confidence references, and report AOPC, LOdds, Kendall-τ, and confidence distributions on Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB; include invariance tests to paraphrase, token order, or input encoding inspired by Kindermans et al. [(Kindermans et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62e39c6dbcead1fe4d29017914591d929aa6ac4c) and random-mask or corruption ablations inspired by Song et al. [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). Second, adopt robust faithfulness evaluation beyond deletion: use MoRF and LeRF AOPC and LOdds, deletion and insertion curves in 1% token increments, and a robust insertion variant that reconstructs or inserts high-attribution tokens into a neutral template with smoothing or masking to mitigate deletion instability, following Nam and Lee [(Nam, and Lee, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bda9c1dc81c301104bdfbe145cf2c426a6df631d) and Score-CAM's confidence-increase and deletion/insertion protocol [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e257b5f0a0148076e9161202880b022e521ac59). Third, incorporate confidence-increase or positive/negative contribution balancing into the activation contrast term, so references are not simply subtracted but weighted by how much they change target-class confidence, as in Score-CAM's Increase of Confidence [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e257b5f0a0148076e9161202880b022e521ac59) and DeepLIFT's positive/negative multiplier rules [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6); this should be ablated against plain activation difference. Fourth, add class-distinctness and known-perturbation controls: compute Kendall-τ between target-class and non-target-class maps, compare maps for top-3 and random classes as in Gu et al. [(Gu et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cbdb103a298c20644553c201c9dfb0399b552b98), and validate attributions on swapped bigrams or token inversions using Spearman correlation with an inversion mask and 10%-data classifier controls as in Mohebbi et al. [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762). Fifth, refine layer and attention aggregation by testing single-layer versus multi-layer aggregation, filtering irrelevant attention heads or features with syntactic, position, or learned masks, and reporting the effect of mask corruption, following Song et al. [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e) and Mohebbi et al. [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762). Sixth, manage computational cost by precomputing and caching reference activations, limiting the number of references, using integrated-gradient interpolation steps m where appropriate, and reporting inference counts, hardware, and runtime, because occlusion and deletion require n+1 passes and gradient methods can be expensive [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a6f7dab88659053c2be5fc9cd885627c58b9ee6). Finally, include qualitative visualizations and, if possible, a localization or pointing-game analog for token spans, while keeping the primary quantitative claims tied to AOPC, LOdds, and Kendall-τ.

## To generate token-level attribution maps, the plan uses: 'computing attribution maps using the difference between target and reference activations combined with gradient information and attention scores.'

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Barkan et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac10b39e4d7b75332e0b437a5426cfa81686f29), which is closest to the proposed target-reference activation/attention contrast because Iterated Integrated Attributions integrates from a zero reference attention tensor to the target attention tensor, computes a Gradient Rollout integrand using attention weights multiplied by gradients of the target class score, aggregates [CLS]-based attention across heads and layers, and reports consistent outperformance over baselines across ViT-Base, ViT-Small, ResNet101, DenseNet201, and ConvNext-Base, with IIA3 improving over IIA2 by adding penultimate-layer integration using 10 interpolation steps. This supports the plan's use of reference-to-target contrast, gradient-weighted attention, and multi-layer aggregation, although it uses visual ViTs and a zero reference rather than low-confidence text references. The next strongest support is quantitative perturbation evidence for gradient-plus-attention token attribution in [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9), where attention-gradient relevancy propagation on LXMERT achieved negative image-perturbation AUC 63.24 versus 61.46 Transformer attribution, 61.34 raw attention, 60.90 partial LRP, 60.08 Grad-CAM, and 58.64 rollout, and positive image-perturbation AUC 51.1 versus 52.75, 54.17, 52.82, 59.21, and 57.23; on 10,000 VQA validation samples, text perturbation AUCs were 48.70 negative and 21.61 positive, again beating raw attention, partial LRP, Grad-CAM, and rollout, and using the target class rather than predicted class improved performance, supporting class-specific target activations. This is strong for the plan's MoRF/LeRF deletion evaluation and gradient/attention combination, but it is multimodal VQA rather than BERT sentiment. Direct evaluation support for the plan's perturbation and contrastiveness metrics appears in [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), which uses MoRF and LeRF zero-masking with T=10 steps, L1 attribution-similarity curves, output-change curves, and a four-image contrastiveness mosaic, and reports absLRP total scores of 0.272 on ImageNet and 0.238 on PascalVOC with VGG16 versus GuidedGradCAM 0.207/0.167 and contrastive-LRP 0.12/0.112 with Wilcoxon signed-rank p=0.05, validating the plan's MoRF/LeRF, AOPC-like perturbation, and class-distinctness evaluation. Direct transformer-text support for attention plus gradients appears in [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451), where attention rollout and attention flow had higher Spearman correlations with blank-out and input-gradient scores than raw CLS attention on a 6-layer, 8-head, hidden-128 subject-verb agreement transformer with 0.96 accuracy, and on DistillBERT SST-2 with 100 test samples rollout/flow were slightly better than raw attention, supporting the plan's use of attention scores and gradient signals and its token deletion/blank-out evaluation. Class-specific token attribution in BERT is supported qualitatively by [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), which applies LRP to BERT-base with 512-token sequences and [CLS], outperforming raw attention, rollout, partial LRP, and Grad-CAM in visual comparisons and producing distinct maps for two objects, and evaluates ERASER Movie Reviews with 1,600 train, 200 validation, and 200 test reviews using token-F1 for top-k tokens k=10 to 80. Newer IR evidence supports reference selection and deletion-based fidelity in [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969), which reviews DeepSHAP baseline sensitivity, Integrated Gradients with padding-token baselines, and token-removal fidelity, supporting the plan's reference threshold gamma and deletion refinement. Additional support for reference-like inputs and gradient smoothing comes from [(Zhang et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11fda0a04d50904153867ab68a0527373e9606a2), which uses neighbor texts and aggregated gradients for sentiment attribution, and from [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), which supports gradient saliency and integrated gradients with baseline interpolation, although it reports no quantitative outcomes. Older reference-based gradient attribution is supported conceptually by [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) and [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2), which position DeepLIFT as reference/backpropagation attribution and compare it with LIME/SHAP on MNIST and user studies, but the excerpts lack quantitative faithfulness values. Activation-based class maps are historically supported by [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2), where GAP-modified GoogLeNet achieved top-5 localization error 43% on ILSVRC validation and 37.1% on test with a 20% max-value threshold and largest connected component, showing class-specific activation maps can localize despite 1-2% classification drops, but this is CNN localization, not transformer text. Finally, [(Achtibat et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2539713a31ee08cc8116c1355da9f96896df145) supports attention-aware LRP as a strong transformer baseline, with faithfulness planned on ViT-B-16 ImageNet and LLaMA 2-7b IMDB/Wikipedia, though the excerpt lacks numeric results.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that contrastive or attention-based attribution does not automatically dominate strong gradient/LRP baselines: in [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), contrastive-LRP scored 0.12 on ImageNet and 0.112 on PascalVOC with VGG16, far below absLRP 0.272/0.238 and GuidedGradCAM 0.207/0.167, and although absLRP and contrastive-LRP were comparable on ResNet50, gradient-based methods scored higher on contrastiveness than Rollout and Last Layer Attention on ViT-Base, suggesting that the plan's attention-score component and contrastive term must be validated rather than assumed to improve AOPC, LOdds, or Kendall-τ. A second major contradiction is that attention weights can be misleading in transformer text: [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969) reports that special marker tokens receive high attention and their removal hurts performance, stopwords can appear as important as regular words under attention but their removal does not affect ranking, attention becomes more uniform in deeper layers, and Integrated Gradients attributions can be negatively correlated with attention-weight attributions, undermining the assumption that averaged attention alone identifies class-relevant tokens. This is reinforced by [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451), where raw CLS attention had low Spearman correlation with blank-out importance except in the first layer and, on DistillBERT SST-2 with 100 test samples, all attention methods had very low correlations with input gradients, which is directly relevant because SST2 is one of the plan's datasets. Third, gradient-based attribution may not be sufficient or may be outperformed by propagation methods in text: [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) argues saliency methods are preferable to attention and reports that a propagation-based LRP method outperforms gradient-based methods on text classification, although no numeric values are provided, so the plan must include Full LRP, Partial LRP, and TransAtt baselines to show that activation contrast adds value beyond LRP. Fourth, simple last-layer attention can already perform well on span-level tasks, as in [(Tillmann et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9acdc4ef59125a27cfb14676feebc1d834ac17ff), where a 4-layer 153M XLM-RoBERTa Piccolo-HAP model using last-layer head-averaged CLS attention, thresholding, and word-level max outperformed random, Hate-BERT, and multilingual toxicity baselines on English Toxic Spans Detection and most German TBO settings, with single-core CPU/V100 latency of 0.65 s CPU and 0.64 s GPU for full prediction and 0.22 s for attention-map computation; this does not refute faithfulness-based attribution, but it contradicts the assumption that multi-layer activation contrast is always necessary for useful token maps. Fifth, older activation-based methods can alter model behavior or be architecture-sensitive: [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) reports 1-2% classification drops when replacing fully connected layers with GAP, with AlexNet most affected, and localization quality depends on mapping resolution and thresholding, reminding the plan that activation maps are not automatically faithful. Finally, the lack of quantitative results in several key papers, including [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6), [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2), [(Zhang et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11fda0a04d50904153867ab68a0527373e9606a2), and [(Achtibat et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2539713a31ee08cc8116c1355da9f96896df145), limits the strength of their support and means the proposed method cannot rely on them for quantitative validation.

### Suggested Action

Most important, run the full planned quantitative evaluation on BERT-base for Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, reporting AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, Kendall-τ class-distinctness, and paired statistical tests such as Wilcoxon signed-rank or bootstrap confidence intervals, following the MoRF/LeRF and contrastiveness protocol in [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) and the token-removal AUC protocol in [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9); include baselines RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att×Att-grads, Grad-SAM, Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, CAT, AttCAT, and TIS, and report effect sizes rather than only means. Second, validate the activation-contrast term with explicit ablations over reference selection: low-confidence target-class references below threshold gamma, random references, same-class references, and multiple references, while sweeping gamma and reference count, because [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969) shows baseline/reference choice materially changes attribution and [(Barkan et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac10b39e4d7b75332e0b437a5426cfa81686f29) shows reference-to-target integration over attention can improve maps; report whether low-confidence references beat random/same-class references on AOPC, LOdds, and Kendall-τ. Third, test whether the attention-score component helps or hurts by comparing last-layer attention, multi-layer head-averaged attention, attention rollout/flow, attention×gradient, and gradient-only variants, using the evidence that raw attention can be poorly correlated with blank-out and gradients in [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) and that attention can over-weight special tokens or stopwords in [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969); if attention reduces faithfulness or contrastiveness, down-weight it, filter it with token deletion, or replace it with gradient-weighted attention. Fourth, strengthen the LRP comparison by implementing conservation-aware LRP baselines such as Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, and the attention-aware LRP described in [(Achtibat et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2539713a31ee08cc8116c1355da9f96896df145) and [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), because [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) reports LRP can outperform gradient-based methods on text classification; this will show whether activation contrast improves on the strongest propagation baselines. Fifth, add complementary evaluation instruments: blank-out/UNK ablation as in [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451), ERASER-style token-F1 for top-k tokens as in [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), and, if span-level outputs are considered, character-level F1 and latency reporting as in [(Tillmann et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9acdc4ef59125a27cfb14676feebc1d834ac17ff). Sixth, document reproducibility constraints: dataset sizes and splits, BERT-base parameter count, max sequence length, gamma threshold, number of reference activations, layer aggregation weights, random seeds, hardware, and runtime, and test generalization across architectures such as DistillBERT, RoBERTa, or XLM-RoBERTa, while noting that [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) and [(Barkan et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dac10b39e4d7b75332e0b437a5426cfa81686f29) show architecture and reference choice affect attribution quality.

## To incorporate depth-specific signals, the plan uses: 'aggregating information across multiple transformer layers to capture layer-specific semantic meanings.'

### Support

The strongest support for aggregating information across multiple transformer layers comes from [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9), which aggregates gradient-weighted attention across all self-attention, co-attention, and encoder-decoder layers in LXMERT, VisualBERT, DETR, and ViT. On 10,000 VQA validation samples, its multi-layer relevancy propagation achieved AUC 63.24 and 51.1 for LXMERT image perturbation, beating Transformer attribution 61.46 and 52.75, raw attention 61.34 and 54.17, partial LRP 60.90 and 52.82, Grad-CAM 60.08 and 59.21, and rollout 58.64 and 57.23; for text perturbation it achieved AUC 48.70 and 21.61, closely matching Transformer attribution. This directly supports the Contrast-CAT plan's assumption that layer-specific transformer signals can be combined to improve token-level attribution, although the method uses attention matrices rather than activation contrasts and does not test low-confidence references, AOPC, or LOdds.

The next strongest support is [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764), which shows that attention-derived attribution can be faithful and robust. ALTI achieved the best comprehensiveness and sufficiency across BERT, RoBERTa, and DistilBERT on SST-2, IMDB, Yelp, and subject-verb agreement, outperforming the strongest gradient baseline IG_l2 by 58 percent in comprehensiveness and 38 percent in sufficiency on average, with larger gains on longer IMDB and Yelp reviews. It also produced more homogeneous top-25 percent token rankings and higher Spearman rank correlations across 10 BERT models with different random initializations. This supports the plan's use of layer/attention signals and deletion-based faithfulness evaluation, but the excerpt does not isolate multi-layer aggregation or activation contrast.

[(Clark et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95a251513853c6032bdecebd4b74e15795662986) provides direct evidence that individual attention heads and layers specialize, which motivates multi-layer aggregation. The best head reached 34.5 UAS versus 26.3 for the right-branching baseline, and relation-specific heads often outperformed fixed offsets, e.g. pobj head 9-6 reached 76.3 versus 34.6, det head 8-11 reached 94.3 versus 51.7, and dobj head 8-10 reached 86.8 versus 40.0. Because no single head captured syntax overall, combining depth-specific signals is plausible for capturing layer-specific semantic meanings. However, this is syntax probing on the WSJ portion of Penn Treebank, not text classification attribution, and it does not evaluate token deletion faithfulness.

[(Zheng et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d29430adccb805ab57b349afa8553954347b3197) supports the architectural feasibility of preserving and combining layer-specific transformer features. SETR-MLA selects M features uniformly across transformer depth, keeps separate streams with 1x1, 3x3, and 3x3 convolutions, combines them by top-down addition and channel-wise concatenation, and upsamples for Cityscapes semantic segmentation. Although the excerpt does not provide numeric mean IoU, it demonstrates that multi-level transformer aggregation can be used for dense prediction, analogous to token attribution.

[(Pires et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16b1a5f10b0d221a6455de13db3f568f11a4d64f) supports the premise that transformer layers carry distinct semantic spaces. In Transformer Big machine translation models, shared-FFN models show lower normalized CKA/LNS similarity to a baseline, ShareEncDec is both least similar and least accurate in BLEU, and One Wide FFN is more accurate but diverges more in LNS than CKA, suggesting semantic-space shift. This supports layer-specific semantic aggregation, but it is a representation-similarity diagnostic rather than an attribution method.

[(Jiang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27b7fc608e66298a0a92776d986bca61b521efa0) supports depth-specific attribution quality. IBA applied to all 24 BERT-large layers on MSMARCO passage reranking produced the largest normalized average probability drop, 61.3 percent, compared with 29.0 percent for Integrated Gradients, and outperformed LIME and random removal over the 6980-query dev set. It also highlights surface and semantic token matches. This supports using layer-specific signals, but the method does not aggregate layers into a single attribution map and does not report statistical significance.

[(Kovaleva et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d78aed1dac6656affa4a04cbf225ced11a83d103) provides weaker support by analyzing layer- and head-level self-attention in BERT-base-uncased with 12 layers, 768 hidden units, and 12 heads on GLUE tasks such as MRPC, STS-B, SST-2, QQP, RTE, QNLI, and MNLI matched. It shows that attention maps can be inspected for linguistic and semantic signals, but the excerpt contains no quantitative outcomes, baselines, or faithfulness metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is that a well-chosen single layer may be sufficient, reducing the necessity of aggregating many layers. [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cab8726de462497bbbdcac5e10736b9f5a5c2d8b) uses a single-layer variational information bottleneck in CLIP, trained per sample with 10 Adam steps at learning rate 1, and finds that layer choice matters: ROAR+ is best at layer 9 for Conceptual Captions text and image, with values 18.16 and 12.72, at layer 7 for MS-CXR text with 15.77, and at layer 9 for MS-CXR image with 37.17. This suggests that multi-layer aggregation in Contrast-CAT must be justified by ablations, because a single tuned layer can already produce useful attributions. The evidence is indirect because it concerns CLIP rather than BERT text classification and uses binarized ROAR+ rather than AOPC or LOdds.

[(Jiang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27b7fc608e66298a0a92776d986bca61b521efa0) and [(Hou, and Castañón, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94abb4a364c7572173f01b9657a1f5f11aa13608) also create a caution against assuming that aggregation automatically improves attribution. Jiang applies IBA separately to all 24 BERT-large layers and reports layer-wise degradation, but does not show that combining those layers improves token removal faithfulness. Hou's Decoded Grad-CAM maps a selected hidden layer of a 12-layer RoBERTa base model back to input tokens on SST-2 and AG News, but the excerpt provides no quantitative results. If per-layer signals already identify relevant tokens, aggregating all layers may dilute class-relevant activation contrasts or introduce class-irrelevant features.

[(Clark et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95a251513853c6032bdecebd4b74e15795662986) contains a related caution: although relation-specific heads can outperform fixed offsets, many relations only slightly improve over simple baselines, and no single head performs well on syntax overall. This supports aggregation but also shows that layer/head signals are noisy and task-specific; indiscriminate aggregation across all layers may not improve attribution unless layers are selected, weighted, or filtered by class relevance.

[(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9) and [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764) do not directly contradict multi-layer aggregation, but they weaken the transferability of their evidence to Contrast-CAT because they aggregate attention-based signals rather than activation contrasts. Their gains may arise from gradient-weighted attention, cross-modal normalization, or attention rollout, not from removing class-irrelevant activation features with low-confidence references.

[(Kovaleva et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d78aed1dac6656affa4a04cbf225ced11a83d103) and [(Pires et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16b1a5f10b0d221a6455de13db3f568f11a4d64f) provide only qualitative or representation-level evidence that layers differ. They do not demonstrate that aggregating those layer-specific differences improves token deletion faithfulness, AOPC, LOdds, or class-distinct attribution in text classification.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add an explicit single-layer versus multi-layer ablation to the Contrast-CAT plan. On BERT-base for Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, compute AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF for each individual layer, for all-layer aggregation, for top-k layers, and for layer-weighted aggregation. Report mean and standard deviation over multiple seeds, statistical significance, and per-dataset results. This directly tests whether aggregating multiple transformer layers improves faithfulness and addresses the tension from [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cab8726de462497bbbdcac5e10736b9f5a5c2d8b), [(Jiang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27b7fc608e66298a0a92776d986bca61b521efa0), and [(Hou, and Castañón, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94abb4a364c7572173f01b9657a1f5f11aa13608).

The second priority is to define and compare the aggregation operator. The plan should specify whether layer-specific activation-contrast maps are combined by unweighted mean, sum, max, learned linear fusion, attention-weighted fusion, or recursive propagation similar to [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9). Because [(Zheng et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d29430adccb805ab57b349afa8553954347b3197) preserves layer-specific streams before fusion, Contrast-CAT should consider normalizing each layer's contrast scores, e.g. by rank or z-score, before aggregation to prevent one layer from dominating. Include attention-based baselines such as ALTI from [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764) to isolate the benefit of activation contrast over multi-layer attention.

The third priority is to validate that the aggregated layers actually capture layer-specific semantic meanings rather than merely improving a faithfulness score. Use layer-wise probes, CKA/LNS representation similarity inspired by [(Pires et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16b1a5f10b0d221a6455de13db3f568f11a4d64f), attention-head analysis inspired by [(Clark et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95a251513853c6032bdecebd4b74e15795662986) and [(Kovaleva et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d78aed1dac6656affa4a04cbf225ced11a83d103), and degradation tests similar to [(Jiang et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27b7fc608e66298a0a92776d986bca61b521efa0). Report which layers contribute most to AOPC/LOdds and whether lower layers capture lexical signals while higher layers capture semantic or class-relevant signals.

The fourth priority is to align the evaluation protocol with the strongest related work. In addition to AOPC and LOdds, report comprehensiveness and sufficiency as in [(Ferrando et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb1c9cb431e771660cffdda1d80a7f15ff40c764), perturbation AUC as in [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9), and optionally ROAR+ if binarized saliency is appropriate as in [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cab8726de462497bbbdcac5e10736b9f5a5c2d8b). Include the planned baselines RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att×Att-grads, Grad-SAM, Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, CAT, AttCAT, and TIS, and add single-layer and multi-layer variants of each where feasible. Report Kendall-tau rank correlation for class-distinct attributions and robustness across random initializations or checkpoints if resources allow.

The final priority is to report reproducibility and computational constraints. Multi-layer aggregation increases memory and runtime, so the plan should specify hardware, batch size, number of reference activations, threshold gamma, layer indices, aggregation weights, optimization steps, and inference time. If multi-layer aggregation yields only small gains over a well-chosen single layer, the method should prefer selective layer aggregation or learned layer weighting to preserve faithfulness while reducing cost.

## To broaden class coverage in the contrastive signal, the plan uses: 'extend this to use multiple reference activations from various classes.'

### Support

The strongest support for extending the contrastive signal to multiple reference activations from various classes comes from [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), which directly uses multiple non-target classes as a contrastive signal by sampling one positive image and three negative images, constructing a scoring map from predicted class probabilities, and evaluating class-specific attribution on ImageNet and PascalVOC2012 with VGG16, ResNet50, and ViT-Base; its absLRP method achieved total scores of 0.272 on ImageNet and 0.238 on PascalVOC with VGG16, outperforming GuidedGradCAM at 0.207 and 0.167 and contrastive LRP at 0.12 and 0.112, with differences verified by the Wilcoxon signed-rank test at p = 0.05, supporting the plan's hypothesis that multiple class contrasts can improve class-specificity, although it evaluates contrast rather than using multiple reference activations inside the attribution formula. A second strong support comes from [(Bexte et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb611ef82584892e76abe4cfef4df01885322366), which uses a class-diverse pool of reference answers with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 S-BERT on ASAP-SAS, with about 2,000 answers per prompt across 10 prompts and QWK averaged over five runs; fine-tuned S-BERT with AVG over references was on par with BERT in the full-data setting, and using only 60 of more than 1000 reference answers caused only a 0.01 QWK drop, supporting the plan's ablation over the number of reference activations and the use of multiple class-diverse references, while also showing that aggregation choice and prompt-specific tuning matter. [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a33311f58b24ff14dbbb92bfcd57b3bd452b745e) supports the broader activation-reference premise on BERT-base text classifiers: DeepLift with a single reference vector produced strong AOPC sufficiency and comprehensiveness, with normalized values of .71/.52 on SST, .46/.61 on IMDB, and .45/.59 on Yelp, and FRESH macro F1 of 83.0 versus 81.1 on SST with 20% rationales, 79.2 versus 80.4 on IMDB with 2% rationales, and 97.1 versus 93.6 on Yelp with 10% rationales, indicating that reference-based activation attribution can be faithful in the exact model family targeted by the plan, though it does not test multiple class-specific references. [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) supports the goal of class-specific transformer attribution and suppression of negative or irrelevant contributions in BERT-base with 512-token inputs and ViT-base, using relevance propagation, gradient information, and evaluation via perturbation AUC, mIoU, and token-F1, but it does not implement multiple reference activations from various classes. [(Conia, and Navigli, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f38831567e60dab286c8327c4027865fa31f3ee) supports the plan's multi-layer aggregation: in frozen BERT-base-cased, RoBERTa-base, multilingual BERT-base, and XLM-RoBERTa-base probing, W-Avg of all hidden layers consistently outperformed Top-4/Last-4 and static representations, with random representations already strong at 84.8% sense accuracy and 72.8% roleset micro-averaged F1 for BERT-base-cased, and Top-4 improving over static by about 6% on sense probing, supporting the use of multiple activations and the need for random-reference baselines. [(Tutek, and Šnajder, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10f38aff84cf736706bd018696ddd0d3baed081d) supports the motivation to reduce class-irrelevant hidden-state support: regularized attention-LSTM variants lowered average per-token prediction probability on correctly classified examples, for example AG News base 0.887/0.862 versus tying 0.615/0.646 and 20 Newsgroups base 0.811/0.687 versus tying 0.577/0.516, aligning with the plan's aim to filter class-irrelevant features, though it does not use activation contrast. Weaker but foundational support comes from single-reference gradient and reference-based attribution methods: [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) establishes Integrated Gradients with a single baseline and completeness, [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) establishes reference-based DeepLIFT/Deep SHAP attribution, and [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) applies integrated gradients to text classification with a single generic baseline, all supporting the reference-plus-gradient mechanism but not the multi-class extension. [(Möller et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de69a963e97b0d00f35a142301c08ccfa7c91598) provides additional support for reference-based attribution with a black-image and padding-token reference, 50 integration steps, and CLIP ViT-B-16, but it uses a single neutral reference rather than multiple class-specific references. Finally, [(Danilevsky et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/829e36b23f7b42e109f84b5b761052498b291962) supports the plan's evaluation emphasis by showing that 32 of 50 surveyed explainability papers lacked standardized evaluation and that saliency-based visualization is dominant, justifying the plan's use of AOPC, LOdds, Kendall-tau, and class-specificity metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that a single-reference activation attribution method can already be highly faithful on BERT-base text classification, which weakens the necessity of multiple class-specific reference activations: [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a33311f58b24ff14dbbb92bfcd57b3bd452b745e) reports DeepLift with one reference vector achieving normalized AOPC sufficiency/comprehensiveness of .71/.52 on SST, .46/.61 on IMDB, and .45/.59 on Yelp, with FRESH macro F1 of 83.0 versus 81.1 on SST with 20% rationales, 79.2 versus 80.4 on IMDB with 2% rationales, and 97.1 versus 93.6 on Yelp with 10% rationales, so the proposed multi-reference extension must be shown to improve over a strong single-reference baseline rather than only over weaker baselines. A second major contradiction is that multi-class contrast is not uniformly superior: [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) shows that contrastive LRP scored only 0.12 on ImageNet and 0.112 on PascalVOC with VGG16, below absLRP at 0.272 and 0.238 and GuidedGradCAM at 0.207 and 0.167, and that gradient-based methods obtained higher Contrastiveness scores than the proposed method on ViT-Base, implying that adding multiple non-target classes may improve class-specificity but can hurt total score or local consistency depending on architecture and attribution method. Third, multiple references may not transfer well or may be less useful under limited data: [(Bexte et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb611ef82584892e76abe4cfef4df01885322366) finds cross-prompt transfer weak, with prompt-specific fine-tuned Biology average QWK over 0.70 while cross-prompt performance was about half of that, and in the limited-data setting logistic regression outperformed S-BERT-finetune by QWK 0.06, suggesting that class-diverse reference pools may require prompt- or domain-specific tuning and may not automatically improve transformer text classification. Fourth, random or averaged activations can be surprisingly strong, so class-specific references may have diminishing returns: [(Conia, and Navigli, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f38831567e60dab286c8327c4027865fa31f3ee) reports random representations achieving 84.8% sense accuracy and 72.8% roleset micro-averaged F1 with BERT-base-cased, with W-Avg consistently best, indicating that the plan's random-reference and same-class-reference ablations are essential and that multiple class-specific references may not outperform simple averaged or random activation baselines. Fifth, class-specific transformer attribution can be achieved without activation contrast: [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) obtains class-specific BERT and ViT visualizations through LRP-based relevance propagation and suppression of negative contributions, with evaluation via perturbation AUC, mIoU, and token-F1, suggesting that the proposed activation-contrast extension is one viable mechanism among several and must be compared against relevance-propagation baselines. Weaker but relevant contradictions come from the prevalence of single-baseline methods: [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be), [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2), and [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) establish single-reference gradient or reference-based attribution as standard, and [(Möller et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de69a963e97b0d00f35a142301c08ccfa7c91598) uses only a black-image and padding-token reference with 50 integration steps on CLIP ViT-B-16, showing that reference-based attribution is often evaluated with one neutral reference rather than multiple class-specific references. Finally, [(Tutek, and Šnajder, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10f38aff84cf736706bd018696ddd0d3baed081d) shows that reducing per-token confidence can be achieved through regularization rather than contrastive references, and [(Danilevsky et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/829e36b23f7b42e109f84b5b761052498b291962) highlights that many saliency-based explanations lack standardized evaluation, so any claimed benefit of multiple references must be demonstrated with rigorous faithfulness, class-specificity, and reproducibility metrics.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a controlled ablation that directly tests the proposed extension against single-reference, same-class, random, and no-contrast baselines on BERT-base over Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, reporting AOPC, LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, Kendall-tau rank correlation, and a Vukadin-style contrastiveness score using multiple non-target classes, with multiple seeds and paired statistical tests such as the Wilcoxon signed-rank test or bootstrap confidence intervals, because [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a33311f58b24ff14dbbb92bfcd57b3bd452b745e) shows single-reference DeepLift is already strong and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) shows multi-class contrast is not uniformly best. Second, define reference selection and aggregation explicitly: choose low-confidence target-class references below threshold gamma, sample from various classes, and test the number of references, for example 1, 4, 10, and 60, along with aggregation strategies such as mean, max, and similarity-weighted AVG/MAX, following [(Bexte et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb611ef82584892e76abe4cfef4df01885322366), which found that 60 references caused only a 0.01 QWK drop and that AVG/MAX behavior depended on fine-tuning; also test whether references should be fine-tuned, aligned, or used as raw activations. Third, incorporate layer aggregation and layer ablations using W-Avg and Top-4/Last-4 as in [(Conia, and Navigli, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f38831567e60dab286c8327c4027865fa31f3ee), because W-Avg outperformed Top-4 and static representations in probing, and include random-layer and single-layer baselines to avoid attributing gains to layer averaging rather than class contrast. Fourth, add class-specificity and negative-contribution controls: compare against relevance-propagation and LRP baselines such as [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), clip or suppress negative attributions, and report per-token confidence diagnostics inspired by [(Tutek, and Šnajder, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10f38aff84cf736706bd018696ddd0d3baed081d) to show that class-irrelevant hidden states are reduced without harming classification accuracy. Fifth, standardize and report implementation details to address the evaluation gap highlighted by [(Danilevsky et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/829e36b23f7b42e109f84b5b761052498b291962): dataset splits, BERT-base checkpoint, tokenization, maximum sequence length, threshold gamma, number of reference activations, integration or gradient steps, attention averaging, deletion protocol, hardware, seeds, and runtime, because many prior saliency works lack reproducible evaluation. Sixth, evaluate generalization cautiously across architectures and domains, including out-of-domain or cross-prompt tests, because [(Bexte et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb611ef82584892e76abe4cfef4df01885322366) found cross-prompt transfer weak and prompt-specific fine-tuning much stronger, so the plan should not assume multiple references transfer without domain-specific validation. Finally, report computational cost and efficiency, since multiple reference activations multiply forward and gradient passes; consider approximations or caching reference activations, and compare cost against single-reference methods such as [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be), [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2), [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), and [(Möller et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de69a963e97b0d00f35a142301c08ccfa7c91598), which use single references.

## To improve attribution quality, the plan uses: 'employ a refinement process that selectively filters attribution maps based on quality assessment using token-wise deletion tests.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Arras et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22ac2e3e45debb13d635fddaccfff7e997e7db9) because it directly evaluates token-level attribution in a text sentiment setting using zero-embedding deletion of up to five words on 1849 Stanford Sentiment Treebank sentences with at least 10 words, and finds that LRP-guided deletion has the most pertinent effect for both correctly and incorrectly classified sentences while random deletion is a necessary baseline. This directly supports the plan's use of token-wise deletion tests to judge whether Contrast-CAT maps identify decision-relevant tokens, and its MoRF and LeRF design mirrors the paper's decreasing and increasing relevance deletion. The next strongest support is [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) because it shows deletion-guided feature removal can distinguish useful from useless attribution maps: LRP-guided removal of relevant features caused a significant accuracy decline, saliency-guided removal behaved similarly to random removal, and class-difference LRP removal increased misclassifications toward contrasted classes on 415 correctly classified class 0 US financial complaint documents, with post-removal accuracies ranging from 92.77 percent to 100 percent at 50 to 100 removals. This supports filtering maps whose deletion effect is indistinguishable from random and using class-specific deletion signals, which aligns with Contrast-CAT's target and reference activation contrast. Further support is [(Ancona et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2447a25012c71ad316487b4b9e7378a4fcccc0) because Occlusion-1 computes the target-class score change S_c(x) minus S_c(x[x_i=0]) as a direct marginal-effect deletion benchmark, validating the remove-and-rescore logic used in the proposed refinement, although it also warns that deletion is slow and sensitive to how many features are removed. Weaker but still relevant support is [(Li et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3900d81bdf06de36bf149c369f8eff2087ec2aa3) because it uses an incremental deletion score as a deletion-based interpretability metric and reports MAProtoNet IDS 6.2 ± 1.8 versus MProtoNet 6.2 ± 3.7 with activation precision 85.8 ± 2.0 versus 81.2 ± 3.7 on BraTS 2020, showing that deletion metrics can coexist with improved localization. The weakest direct support is [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) because it uses pixel perturbations rather than token deletion, but its controlled perturbation framework supports using input perturbations to test whether an attribution map is stable and meaningful.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52) because on 512 correctly classified ImageNet images, random sign perturbations at L∞ = 8 reduced top-1000 pixel overlap below 30 percent for simple gradient, integrated gradients, and DeepLIFT, and influence rankings became essentially uncorrelated after a gradient-sign attack, showing that attribution maps can be unstable under small controlled perturbations. This contradicts the assumption that a single token-deletion test reliably certifies a map, and suggests the plan's selective filter could reject or accept maps based on noisy deletion responses unless stability is measured. The next strongest contradiction is [(Ancona et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2447a25012c71ad316487b4b9e7378a4fcccc0) because deletion-based Occlusion-1 is very slow as the number of features grows and is strongly influenced by how many features are removed at each iteration, so token-wise deletion tests in BERT may be computationally expensive and sensitive to deletion budget, potentially making a refinement loop unreliable if applied exhaustively. The next is [(Arras et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22ac2e3e45debb13d635fddaccfff7e997e7db9) because SA performed worse than random deletion when deleting words in increasing relevance order from initially misclassified sentences, showing that low-relevance tokens are not always inhibiting and that deletion direction and initial correctness matter; the plan must therefore not treat LeRF deletion as a universal quality signal. The next is [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) because saliency-guided removal behaved similarly to random removal, indicating that some attribution methods fail deletion-based quality tests, and because the paper's deletion targets embedding columns and convolutional filters rather than transformer tokens, so transfer to BERT token masking is not guaranteed. The weakest contradiction is [(Li et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3900d81bdf06de36bf149c369f8eff2087ec2aa3) because IDS is used only as an evaluation metric, not to filter maps, and its 3D MRI segmentation setting with 4 x 128 x 128 x 96 inputs, ResNet152, 30 prototypes, and NVIDIA RTX A6000 training does not directly validate token-wise deletion filtering for text classification.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement deletion-test filtering as a secondary quality gate rather than the primary attribution method: compute Contrast-CAT maps, then apply token-wise deletion only to a subset or all maps with both MoRF and LeRF, compare AOPC and LOdds against random deletion and baselines, and filter only maps whose deletion curve significantly improves over random and whose target-class score decreases under MoRF or increases under LeRF in the expected direction. This directly addresses evidence from [(Arras et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f22ac2e3e45debb13d635fddaccfff7e997e7db9) and [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) that deletion can identify relevant tokens but some maps fail. The second most important action is to control deletion budget and cost: use small budgets such as 1, 3, 5, or 10 percent of tokens, zero or mask token embeddings or attention, subsample examples, and report computational cost; this follows [(Ancona et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de2447a25012c71ad316487b4b9e7378a4fcccc0) warning that deletion is slow and granularity-sensitive. The third action is to add stability checks inspired by [(Ghorbani et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96cf326c91adc96aa59382bf71fbead29ea34c52): repeat deletion with multiple random orders or perturbation magnitudes, measure rank correlation or overlap of attribution maps before and after deletion, and require stable top-k token sets before filtering. The fourth action is to make deletion class-contrastive: remove tokens to reduce the target-class logit and increase reference or other-class logits, and evaluate class-distinctiveness with Kendall-tau and misclassification direction, aligning with the class-difference removal evidence in [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) and the plan's activation contrast. The least important but still necessary action is to report reproducibility details and statistical significance: dataset sizes, BERT base hyperparameters, threshold gamma, number of reference activations, deletion budgets, random seeds, confidence intervals, and significance tests, because related works often omit hardware, hyperparameters, or significance.

## To test the proposed method on standard text classification tasks, the plan will: 'conduct comprehensive experiments using pre-trained BERT base models on four popular text classification datasets: Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed BERT-base, four-dataset attribution experiment comes from [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e), which evaluates a post-hoc explanation method on pretrained BERT base with a 512-token limit and [CLS] classifier input on SST-2, IMDB, and Yelp Polarity, uses syntactic and relative-position masks with xi_synt=0.1 and xi_pos=0.8, and uses the same faithfulness metrics as the plan, AOPC and LOdds, by masking top-k% tokens ranked by attribution score. That work compares RawAtt, Rollout, LRP, PartialLRP, GAE, CAM, GradCAM, AttCAT, and a random mask baseline, and reports that important-head LRP generally achieved the highest mean AOPC and lowest mean LOdds, with a random-mask ablation performing worse and a 100% corrupted mask on SST-2 degrading to GAE, directly supporting the plan's baseline set, masking-based faithfulness evaluation, and motivation to suppress irrelevant information. A complementary and older line of evidence is [(Liusie et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/909f63e1ddbc72c97f310a8a6cc2d12e5bcb84c1), which fine-tunes BERT and a randomly initialized transformer using ensembles of three models on Yelp, IMDB, and SST, shows that stop words alone can yield 77% accuracy on Yelp and 69% on IMDB, that full models evaluated on shuffled stop words retain at least 57% in-domain accuracy, and that pretrained BERT is more robust than the random transformer under domain shift, dropping 12.1% and 23.0% versus 14.5% and 29.1%, thereby supporting the plan's concern that class-irrelevant features can contaminate BERT-based text classifiers. [(Sabir et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20a49e4ebda59a5f9d2a2edb79ed7a329a90daf7) further supports the feasibility of using explainability or attribution information from BERT and ROBERTA classifiers on IMDB, YELP, and SST2, using Bayesian hyperparameter optimization with hyperopt, 10-fold stratified cross-validation, early stopping, and MCC selection, and reporting a LightGBM detector with average MCC 0.87, F1 above 93.5%, balanced accuracy 93.0%, best MCC 0.929 for ROBERTA on YELP, and feature extraction time 0.085 seconds per example on a T4 GPU with 16 GB RAM, although it does not evaluate AOPC, LOdds, or Amazon Polarity. [(Zhang et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96fd89de07a69dd2dc94d71f884e64174c5974e2) supports the use of pretrained BERT base-cased models, together with TextRNN, RoBERTa, and XLNet, on balanced 50,000-example IMDB and YELP subsets, averaging three seeds and using perturbation probabilities from 0.001 to 1.00, with log AUC learnability values such as 1.492 for whitespace and 1.898 for visual attack letters on IMDB and significant Spearman correlations between learnability and robustness, which is relevant to the plan's perturbation-based AOPC and LOdds evaluation but does not measure attribution faithfulness. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec46aecae2e3f2c785e9423cf9dfe6f7832b3845) supports the dataset selection by using Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, and IMDB, but only 500 examples per dataset with LLM paraphrasing, entropy filtering, semantic diversity computed as mean cosine distance to the dataset centroid, and linguistic diversity from paraphrase embeddings, so it is weaker evidence for BERT-base attribution. [(Maas et al., 2011-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c61f9ef06fe74505775a833ff849185757199e7) provides the older IMDB polarity benchmark context, with a balanced 50,000-review subset, 25,000 train and 25,000 test examples, polarized rating thresholds, at most 30 reviews per movie, and low-variance estimates where 0.1% accuracy equals 25 reviews, but it uses a linear support vector machine with LIBLINEAR rather than BERT. [(Yang et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0c6abdbdecf04ffac65c440da77fb9d66bb474c) supports BERT finetuning as a standard baseline and provides pretraining context such as BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia, sequence length 512, and XLNet-Large training on 512 TPU v3 chips for 500K steps with batch size 8192, about 5.5 days, Giga5, ClueWeb 2012-B, and Common Crawl producing 32.89B SentencePiece pieces, partial prediction K=6, and span length sampled from 1 to 5, but it does not report BERT-base-specific results for the four target datasets. Finally, [(Sundararaman et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf7159ff13aa4f1811b75febb84044e56ada0ee) supports the use of gradient-based token saliency in BERT, where normalized gradients of classification loss with respect to input token embeddings improved aspect-polarity transfer, with full-model automatic classifier score 0.5918 and BLEU 39.3838 versus BERT baseline 0.5158 and 36.0683, and implementation details including MLM masking probability 0.25, denoising word-drop probability 0.25, shuffle window size 3, and denoising loss scheduled from 1 to 0.1 over 30,000 steps and then to 0 over 70,000 steps, but it is an aspect-based sentiment transfer setting rather than the proposed standard classification attribution benchmark.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that the closest related work, [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e), does not validate the full proposed protocol: it omits Amazon Polarity, does not explicitly report Least Relevant First, does not use Kendall tau rank correlation, evaluates on validation sets rather than test sets, provides no numeric table values in the excerpt, reports no statistical significance, and does not specify hardware or exact dataset sizes, so it supports only three of the four datasets and only part of the planned MoRF/LeRF/Kendall evaluation, even though it specifies mask hyperparameters xi_synt=0.1 and xi_pos=0.8. A second major caveat is that [(Liusie et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/909f63e1ddbc72c97f310a8a6cc2d12e5bcb84c1) shows strong class-irrelevant stop-word bias, with BERT fine-tuned on shuffled stop words reaching 77% on Yelp and 69% on IMDB and full models retaining at least 57% accuracy on shuffled stop words, which contradicts the assumption that standard BERT-base attributions will automatically isolate class-relevant tokens unless the contrastive references explicitly capture such spurious features; it also lacks attribution metrics, Amazon Polarity, and explicit BERT-base specifications, and its results are reported with ensembles of three models. [(Sabir et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20a49e4ebda59a5f9d2a2edb79ed7a329a90daf7) is less directly supportive because it uses explainability maps for adversarial detection and transformation rather than faithfulness, trains detectors on only 1000 clean and 1000 adversarial examples with an 80:20 stratified split, 10-fold cross-validation, hyperopt Bayesian optimization, and MCC selection, reports MCC, F1, balanced accuracy, and false positive/negative rates but not AOPC, LOdds, or Kendall tau, omits Amazon Polarity, and does not explicitly confirm BERT-base, so its high detector performance, such as average MCC 0.87 and best MCC 0.929 for ROBERTA on YELP, cannot be taken as evidence that attribution maps are faithful explanations. [(Zhang et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96fd89de07a69dd2dc94d71f884e64174c5974e2) also limits direct comparability because it uses balanced 50,000-example subsamples, BERT base-cased rather than an explicitly stated BERT-base variant, compares TextRNN, RoBERTa, and XLNet, excludes duplicate punctuation from YELP because it occurs naturally, and studies perturbation learnability and robustness rather than activation attribution, so its significant correlations, such as Spearman -0.643 on IMDB and -0.821 on YELP with p below 0.001, do not confirm the planned AOPC/LOdds attribution evaluation. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec46aecae2e3f2c785e9423cf9dfe6f7832b3845) is weaker still because it uses only 500 examples per dataset due to API cost, does not use BERT, omits SST2, reports no numeric correlation values or faithfulness metrics, and relies on entropy filtering plus semantic and linguistic diversity measures, so it cannot support comprehensive attribution experiments. [(Maas et al., 2011-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c61f9ef06fe74505775a833ff849185757199e7) introduces dataset-construction caveats, such as keeping only highly polarized reviews, excluding neutral labels, limiting to 30 reviews per movie, and using a linear support vector machine with LIBLINEAR and cross-validated parameters, while providing no exact accuracy values or significance tests, so it is not direct evidence for BERT-base attribution. [(Yang et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0c6abdbdecf04ffac65c440da77fb9d66bb474c) does not provide BERT-base-specific quantitative results for Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST-2, or IMDB, and reports that XLNet outperforms BERT by a sizable margin under a matched recipe, with XLNet-Large trained on 512 TPU v3 chips for 500K steps, batch size 8192, and about 5.5 days, which suggests that BERT may be a weaker baseline but does not contradict the plan. Finally, [(Sundararaman et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf7159ff13aa4f1811b75febb84044e56ada0ee) is task-mismatched, using SemEval 2014, 2015, and 2016 restaurant-domain ABSA data, Yelp style transfer, 513 queries, a 100-query manual subset, PyTorch with learning rate 1e-4, batch size 32, and 8 epochs, and its table is captioned as manual evaluation but contains automatic classifier and BLEU scores, so it does not evaluate the proposed standard classification datasets or faithfulness metrics.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should explicitly operationalize the BERT-base model and the four datasets by specifying whether BERT-base-uncased or BERT-base-cased is used, the tokenizer, 512-token limit, [CLS] pooling, dataset splits, class balance, preprocessing, maximum sequence length, number of seeds, hardware, and training hyperparameters, because related work shows that model variant, dataset construction, and split size materially affect results, as in [(Maas et al., 2011-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c61f9ef06fe74505775a833ff849185757199e7), [(Zhang et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96fd89de07a69dd2dc94d71f884e64174c5974e2), and [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). Next, the evaluation should be completed to match the plan's stated metrics by reporting AOPC and LOdds under both Most Relevant First and Least Relevant First, Kendall tau rank correlation, top-k% masking curves, confidence intervals, multiple random seeds, and statistical significance tests on test sets rather than validation sets, following the metric setup in [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e) and the multi-seed averaging in [(Zhang et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96fd89de07a69dd2dc94d71f884e64174c5974e2). The plan should also add explicit controls for class-irrelevant features, including stop-word-only or shuffled-stop-word inputs, likelihood-ratio bias measurements, retention curves, and perturbation-learnability probes, because [(Liusie et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/909f63e1ddbc72c97f310a8a6cc2d12e5bcb84c1) shows that stop words can carry substantial predictive signal and [(Zhang et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96fd89de07a69dd2dc94d71f884e64174c5974e2) shows that perturbation sensitivity is strongly correlated with robustness. Baselines should be expanded to include the comparison set used in [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e), namely RawAtt, Rollout, LRP, PartialLRP, GAE, CAM, GradCAM, AttCAT, and a random mask or random reference baseline, in addition to CAT and TIS, so that the contribution of activation contrast is isolated from generic relevance propagation. Ablations should test random references, same-class references, the confidence threshold gamma, the number of reference activations, single-layer versus multi-layer aggregation, and a corruption or random-mask ablation similar to the 10% to 100% mask corruption in [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). Computational reproducibility should be reported with hardware and runtime, for example noting that BERT/ROBERTA explainability feature extraction can be feasible on a T4 GPU with 16 GB RAM and that feature extraction times can differ substantially across methods, as in [(Sabir et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20a49e4ebda59a5f9d2a2edb79ed7a329a90daf7). Finally, the plan should include Amazon Polarity with enough examples to avoid the small-subset limitation seen in [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec46aecae2e3f2c785e9423cf9dfe6f7832b3845), and it should use confidence or entropy filtering only as a sensitivity analysis rather than as the main evaluation, because removing high-entropy examples may bias faithfulness estimates.

## To compare against existing attribution approaches, the plan will: 'evaluate our method against various baseline attribution methods categorized into attention-based (RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att×Att-grads, Grad-SAM), LRP-based (Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt), and activation-based methods (CAT, AttCAT, TIS).'

### Support

Strongest support is that the closest recent transformer text-classification work already uses BERT-base, AOPC, LOdds, and overlapping baselines, and finds that a better attribution method can achieve highest AOPC and lowest LOdds on SST-2, IMDB, and Yelp Polarity, while attention-based methods can over-attribute non-influential tokens [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). This directly validates the plan's BERT-base, AOPC/LOdds, MoRF/LeRF-style masking, and baseline categories, although it omits Amazon Polarity and several planned baselines such as Att-grads, Att x Att-grads, Grad-SAM, TransAtt, CAT, and TIS. Next, Barkan et al. provide direct evidence for the attention-gradient baselines in the plan, using BERT-base-uncased on SST, AGNews, IMDB, and MultiRC, and showing that Grad-SAM outperforms Gradient and [CLS] Att in masked mean Macro F1 and AOPC, with SST top-20% masking producing a 19.5-point accuracy drop for Grad-SAM versus 16.5 for [CLS] Att and IMDB using top-30% preservation [(Barkan et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90f7b61762e4454b9cba3afbb10c5f07465cff85). This supports including Grad-SAM, Att-grads, and Att x Att-grads and using AOPC, but it does not test LRP or activation-contrast methods. Liu et al. support the planned comparison of RawAtt, Rollout, AttGrad, PLRP, and TransAtt across six tasks, nine datasets, and eight architectures, and show that all tested methods have positive polarity violation ratios, with RawAtt and AttIN around 40% violation on BUTD and LXMERT while still obtaining competitive AUC-TP and Sufficiency [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fdb05e17fd503b8dbdbadc338e0a00829929dcc). This supports the baseline set and the need for token-level faithfulness, but also implies that AOPC/LOdds alone may be insufficient. Ali et al. support the LRP-based branch by showing that LRP(AH+LN) consistently improves activation and pruning over Gradient x Input, Attention-last, Rollout, attention flow, and GAE on SST-2 with 11,844 reviews and IMDB with 50,000 reviews, using AUAC and AU-MSE [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6). Chefer et al. support TransAtt and Partial LRP as baselines: the 2020 paper compares LRP/gradient attention propagation with raw attention, rollout, full/partial LRP, and Grad-CAM on BERT-base Movie Reviews with 1600/200/200 splits, while the 2021 paper reports perturbation AUCs for TransAtt, raw attention, partial LRP, Grad-CAM, and rollout on 10,000 VQA validation samples, e.g. negative image AUC 63.24 versus TransAtt 61.46 and positive image AUC 51.1 versus TransAtt 52.75 [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9). Chrysostomou and Aletras support token-deletion faithfulness by ranking tokens with word omission, InputXGrad, Integrated Gradients, and alpha-grad-alpha, then removing tokens until decision flip, with BERT averages such as SST 0.22 with Lin-TaSc versus 0.55 without, IMDB 0.07 versus 0.20, and AG News 0.50 versus 0.60 [(Chrysostomou, and Aletras, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32a6daa76efd00f657e65842771971decf104efc). Abnar and Zuidema support RawAtt and Rollout as attention baselines by comparing raw attention, rollout, and attention flow against blank-out and input-gradient references on a 6-layer, 8-head, 128-hidden GPT-2-style subject-verb model with 0.96 accuracy and on 100 DistillBERT SST-2 samples, finding rollout and flow more reliable than raw attention [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451). Serrano and Smith provide older but important evidence that attention rankings are not always faithful, using JS divergence and decision-flip tests on Yahoo, IMDB, Yelp 2017, and Amazon, where Amazon FLANnoenc required more than 0.50 item removals in 12.4%, 2.8%, 0.9%, and 0.5% of cases for four ranking schemes [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad). Weaker but still relevant support comes from Jayaram and Allaway, whose BERT stance detector on VAST uses crowdsourced oracle attributions and rationale diagnostics, with full, reduced25, and reduced10 settings retaining 519 oracle examples, and reduced25 prior-tfidf:gold macro-F1 0.661 versus priorbin:tfidf 0.655, showing that attribution quality can be tied to rationale quality [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444). Read iteratively, the literature moves from early vision LRP, CAM, Grad-CAM, and IG, through BERT attention and hidden-token attribution, to transformer-specific LRP/TransAtt, AOPC/LOdds, and polarity-violation tests, which collectively justify the plan's baseline categories and faithfulness metrics while exposing gaps in reference-activation ablation and sign consistency [(Bach et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17a273bbd4448083b01b5a9389b3c37f5425aac0) [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) [(Mudrakarta et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a9831e5fec549edee454709048a51997ef60fb7) [(Kovaleva et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d78aed1dac6656affa4a04cbf225ced11a83d103) [(Brunner et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1fe62a928bf5cfac0f373728f3a4de3cefe0951d) [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6) [(Voita et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9bc8169326d3cca0860ec76247715bc7beaf9c9) [(Ferrando, and Costa-jussà, 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fbd8505973851271d6e4f72a2068a855e21b2df) [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77b7786b93a2a1c6ab682512926e18429d3fbde7).

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the plan's primary metrics, AOPC and LOdds, may not be sufficient to establish faithfulness because Liu et al. show that every tested attribution method has a positive polarity violation ratio, and that RawAtt and AttIN can have about 40% violation on BUTD and LXMERT while still obtaining competitive AUC-TP and Sufficiency, meaning magnitude-based perturbation metrics can miss sign errors [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fdb05e17fd503b8dbdbadc338e0a00829929dcc). This directly challenges the plan's reliance on AOPC, LOdds, and Kendall-tau without an explicit polarity or violation test. A second important contradiction is that LRP-based methods can be very strong, potentially undermining the assumption that an activation-contrast method will automatically outperform all baselines; Ali et al. report that LRP(AH+LN) consistently improves activation and pruning over Gradient x Input, Attention-last, Rollout, attention flow, and GAE on SST-2 and IMDB [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6), and Song et al. find that a head-filtered LRP method achieves the highest AOPC and lowest LOdds across five classification benchmarks, outperforming baselines including AttCAT, RawAtt, Rollout, LRP, and PartialLRP [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). Third, Chefer et al. show that TransAtt and gradient-based methods can be competitive with or close to stronger methods, e.g. on LXMERT text perturbation the proposed method's negative AUC 48.70 is only slightly above TransAtt 48.24, and positive AUC 21.61 is close to TransAtt 21.68, while raw attention, partial LRP, Grad-CAM, and rollout produce different rankings depending on perturbation direction [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9). This suggests that the planned baseline set may contain strong competitors and that results can be metric- and direction-sensitive. Fourth, Serrano and Smith show that perturbation-based decision-flip metrics can be influenced by model brittleness and that random or non-attention rankings can sometimes flip decisions faster than attention, especially on encoderless Amazon models, so AOPC/LOdds may partly measure model robustness rather than attribution quality [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad). Fifth, Jayaram and Allaway indicate that reference or oracle quality matters: pseudo-oracle tf-idf attributions produced worse macro-F1 than gold oracle attributions in reduced25, with 0.655 versus 0.661, and base/prior-bin:gold were comparable, so low-confidence reference activations may not automatically improve attribution unless carefully selected [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444). Weaker contradictions include LRP implementation sensitivity: Binder et al. show that identity versus Taylor treatment of local renormalization layers changes AUC, e.g. epsilon=0.01 identity 37.10 versus Taylor 35.47 and beta=1 identity 56.13 versus Taylor 53.82, so Full LRP, Partial LRP, and TransAtt must be specified carefully to avoid unfair comparisons [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d). Also, Abnar and Zuidema find that even rollout and attention flow have very low Spearman correlation with input gradients on 100 DistillBERT SST-2 samples, which complicates using rank correlation as a universal ground truth [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451). Finally, several related works provide only qualitative or limited quantitative evidence, such as Chefer et al. 2020 and Wu et al., so they cannot by themselves validate the planned quantitative claims [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77b7786b93a2a1c6ab682512926e18429d3fbde7).

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a polarity or sign-consistency violation test in addition to AOPC, LOdds, and Kendall-tau, because Liu et al. show that standard perturbation metrics can miss polarity violations across RawAtt, AttGrad, PLRP, Rollout, TransAtt, and other methods [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fdb05e17fd503b8dbdbadc338e0a00829929dcc); report violation ratio, AUC-TP, Sufficiency, Comprehensiveness, and rank correlation. Second, make the baseline comparison fully reproducible by specifying exact BERT-base checkpoints, tokenizer, sequence length, masking strategy, top-k percentages, random seeds, and statistical tests, and by aligning AOPC/LOdds with the protocol in Song et al. and AOPC top-20% masking with Barkan et al. [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e) [(Barkan et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90f7b61762e4454b9cba3afbb10c5f07465cff85). Third, expand and standardize the baseline set beyond the listed methods to include GAE, GenAtt, InputGrad, Integrated Gradients, CAM/Grad-CAM, Conservative LRP, and possibly TokenTM, because these appear in recent transformer attribution comparisons and may be strong competitors [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fdb05e17fd503b8dbdbadc338e0a00829929dcc) [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6) [(Chefer et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ec5f207263100ea2d45db595712f611a74bafd9) [(Wu et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77b7786b93a2a1c6ab682512926e18429d3fbde7). Fourth, ablate the activation-contrast reference mechanism rigorously by varying threshold gamma, number of references, same-class versus random versus low-confidence references, and multiple classes, because Jayaram and Allaway show pseudo-oracle references can underperform gold references and Song et al. show corruption of irrelevant information degrades relevance propagation [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444) [(Song et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c80ec8ad4bde6553e54a8bd6d67bd80ab5212e). Fifth, implement LRP baselines with explicit propagation rules and report sensitivity to epsilon, beta, LayerNorm handling, attention-head handling, and negative suppression, following Binder et al., Ali et al., Voita et al., and Chefer et al. [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6) [(Voita et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9bc8169326d3cca0860ec76247715bc7beaf9c9) [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4). Sixth, complement Kendall-tau with Spearman or other rank correlations against gradient, blank-out, or rationale references, and include human or rationale-based evaluation where available, following Abnar and Zuidema, Serrano and Smith, and Jayaram and Allaway [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad) [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444). Seventh, report computational cost, runtime, FLOPs, and hardware, because attribution methods differ in cost and prior work reports FLOPs or runtime, e.g. Hao et al. reports about one second per 128-token sequence on an Nvidia V100 for integrated-gradient attention attribution and Atanasova et al. uses FLOPs as a cost measure [(Hao et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1686203adc5f2dbc18627ce64f66d33eb81432a5) [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6). Finally, use qualitative visualizations and token-F1 or human ratings only as secondary evidence, not as the main quantitative claim, following Chefer et al. and Jayaram and Allaway [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444).

## To measure faithfulness of attributions, the plan will: 'use faithfulness metrics including Area Over the Perturbation Curve (AOPC) and log-odds (LOdds) under both Most Relevant First (MoRF) and Least Relevant First (LeRF) settings.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Samek et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6df11b0bb0244d4d36e8955436067cc5d19734fa), which directly establishes the MoRF and LeRF perturbation logic used in the plan: rank input features by attribution, perturb highest or lowest scores first, record prediction changes, and derive AOPC and LOdds-style curve metrics. Although the excerpt lacks numeric tables, it is the closest conceptual precedent for transferring pixel-level MoRF/LeRF evaluation to token-level BERT attribution. The next strongest support is [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), which uses MoRF and LeRF zero-masking with T=10 steps, records normalized output curves, tests differences with Wilcoxon signed-rank at p=0.05, and shows absLRP achieving total GAE 0.272 on ImageNet and 0.238 on PascalVOC versus GuidedGradCAM 0.207 and 0.167, supporting MoRF/LeRF as a statistically testable way to separate attribution quality, even though it targets images rather than transformer text tokens. Strong text-specific support comes from [(Nguyen, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3785f9083dcb46d2bea7ee771c2a513bb43917a6), which evaluates word-level explanations on 20newsgroups and movie reviews using deletion, switching point, and AOPC with top k=10, and reports that most method differences are p < 0.001, LIME improves from 500 to 5000 samples, and AOPC correlates with crowd accuracy in an OLS model with coefficient 0.543, SE 0.042, R-squared 0.133, F 49.572, n=1620, validating AOPC for text classification although it does not use LOdds or explicit LeRF. Direct BERT text-classification support comes from [(Zhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a662c84c32bf19f2d3c33519ba874a8200abb59), which reports AOPC-derived ADiff improvements for BERT on AG News, MR, and IMDB, with AOPC Diff increasing from 0.02 to 0.17, 0.03 to 0.18, and 0.02 to 0.14, and RDiff increasing from 6.98 to 10.68, 3.60 to 4.77, and 12.42 to 13.85, using batch size 64, Adam, learning rates 5e-4 for TextCNN/LSTM and 3e-5 for BERT, and five runs, showing AOPC can detect faithfulness changes in BERT text classification. Additional support comes from [(DeYoung et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/087dd95e13efd47aef2a6582e6801b39fc0f83d8), which uses ordered token removal, comprehensiveness and sufficiency, and random baselines averaged over 10 runs on ERASER, supporting the plan's ordered-removal logic and the need for random references, although it does not use AOPC or LOdds. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Kroeger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70017c9096cca317942156e3f91f8141da42eef1), which uses token masking and AUC over k=1 to 3 on Amazon, IMDb, and Yelp with a single-layer transformer, and from [(Du et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26f8d83c5dd370e5f291798e896a9b82e6cf83ce), which uses single most-relevant sentence deletion on SST2 and Yelp Polarity with GRU, LSTM, and BiGRU models, both showing that attribution-ordered perturbation changes predictions. The weakest support is from [(Bastings et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1818e24137383ece9b2f379a63674b2632e16d04) and [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444), which evaluate token or rationale faithfulness for BERT text classification using Precision@k, Mean Rank, MAW/GI, Likert ratings, and Omega, but not AOPC or LOdds, so they support the broader faithfulness goal rather than the specific metric protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is a methodological caveat from [(Ju et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5969598152974c6bc35671b6b631aefe9c89dcbc), which argues that meaningful-perturbation metrics such as AOPC can be interpreted as using another attribution procedure as ground truth and that outcomes depend on the modification strategy, such as deleting tokens versus replacing them, the top-k fraction, and the ordering. This directly challenges the plan's reliance on AOPC and LOdds under MoRF/LeRF unless the token removal strategy, k, confidence filtering, and reference selection are fully specified. The next strongest contradiction is empirical noise and limited human alignment from [(Nguyen, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3785f9083dcb46d2bea7ee771c2a513bb43917a6), where AOPC differences for negative evidence were not significant in many cases, switching point differences had exceptions, and AOPC only moderately predicted crowd accuracy with R-squared 0.133, adjusted R-squared 0.130, F 49.572, and n=1620, suggesting AOPC alone may be insufficient for the plan's BERT token attribution claims. A further contradiction comes from [(DeYoung et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/087dd95e13efd47aef2a6582e6801b39fc0f83d8), which shows that perturbation-based comprehensiveness and sufficiency can be sensitive to rationale length, that random baselines can be marginal for poorly performing models, and that attention weights can achieve better AUPRC with human rationales while being less faithful than gradient or LIME scores, implying that AOPC/LOdds may not capture all dimensions of attribution quality. Weaker contradictions include [(Bastings et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1818e24137383ece9b2f379a63674b2632e16d04), which uses ground-truth shortcut tokens and Precision@k/Mean Rank rather than AOPC/LOdds, and reports strong model dependence such as GRAD l2 precision 0.99 or higher on seven of nine BERT dataset-shortcut combinations but only around 0.5 on six LSTM combinations, suggesting that perturbation-curve metrics may need ground-truth sanity checks to avoid missing known important tokens. The weakest contradiction is from [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444), which evaluates BERT rationale quality with MAW/GI, five-point Likert ratings, and Omega rather than AOPC/LOdds, and reports that prior-bin:gold and base achieve comparable macro-F1 while reduced25 prior-tfidf:gold and prior-bin:tfidf achieve 0.661 and 0.655 macro-F1, indicating that rationale faithfulness may require human or rationale-specific metrics beyond the plan's perturbation metrics.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define the AOPC and LOdds protocol exactly as a reproducible token-perturbation experiment: specify whether tokens are deleted, masked, or replaced, the top-k or percentage of tokens removed at each step, the MoRF and LeRF ordering, the confidence threshold gamma used for reference selection, the number of reference activations, and the random baseline, because [(Ju et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5969598152974c6bc35671b6b631aefe9c89dcbc) warns that modification strategy materially affects meaningful-perturbation results and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) shows MoRF/LeRF protocols should include explicit step counts, normalization, and statistical testing. The second most important action is to add complementary faithfulness metrics, including comprehensiveness/sufficiency, PGI/PGU or AUC over k, switching point, Precision@k and Mean Rank on synthetic shortcut data, and AUPRC or human rationale evaluation, so that AOPC/LOdds are not the sole evidence, following [(DeYoung et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/087dd95e13efd47aef2a6582e6801b39fc0f83d8), [(Kroeger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70017c9096cca317942156e3f91f8141da42eef1), [(Bastings et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1818e24137383ece9b2f379a63674b2632e16d04), and [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444). The third action is to report statistical rigor: at least five independent runs, confidence intervals, paired or Wilcoxon tests, p-values, and effect sizes, as done in [(Zhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a662c84c32bf19f2d3c33519ba874a8200abb59) with five runs, [(Nguyen, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3785f9083dcb46d2bea7ee771c2a513bb43917a6) with p < 0.001 comparisons and OLS diagnostics, and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) with Wilcoxon signed-rank at p=0.05. The fourth action is to validate the metrics on the planned BERT base Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB experiments with token deletion, while reporting in-distribution accuracy changes and ablations for gamma, reference count, and layer aggregation, consistent with [(Zhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a662c84c32bf19f2d3c33519ba874a8200abb59), [(Du et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26f8d83c5dd370e5f291798e896a9b82e6cf83ce), and [(Bastings et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1818e24137383ece9b2f379a63674b2632e16d04). The fifth action is to include a small human or rationale-quality evaluation, especially for challenging phenomena such as implication, multi-label, quotes, and sarcasm, because [(Jayaram, and Allaway, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf20c1ba36ce063c85c797eff38508ef3d542444) shows rationale quality is not fully captured by task performance. The least important but still useful action is to document implementation details such as hardware, random seeds, model sizes, tokenization, masking or deletion implementation, top-k values, number of perturbation steps, and LIME or reference hyperparameters, following the reporting standards in [(Zhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a662c84c32bf19f2d3c33519ba874a8200abb59), [(Du et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26f8d83c5dd370e5f291798e896a9b82e6cf83ce), and [(Kroeger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70017c9096cca317942156e3f91f8141da42eef1).

## To ensure class-distinct attributions, the plan will: 'conduct confidence evaluation using Kendall-τ rank correlation to ensure our method generates class-distinct attributions.'

### Support

The strongest support for using Kendall-tau in the proposed confidence evaluation comes from [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65), which directly applies Kendall-tau to token-level importance rankings for BiLSTM and DistilBERT on SST-2, IMDb, SNLI, MultiNLI XNLI English, and Quora, a setting close to the plan's BERT-base experiments on SST2 and IMDB. It reports mean Kendall-tau 0.2684 among LIME, Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT, Grad-SHAP, and Deep-SHAP, and 0.1736 against attention, with model-dependent values of 0.4281 for BiLSTM and 0.1088 for DistilBERT among non-attention methods, and task-dependent values of 0.273 for single-sequence data versus 0.1883 for pair-sequence data. This supports the feasibility of Kendall-tau as a token-ranking metric for transformer text classification, although it evaluates agreement rather than class-distinctness or confidence. A second supporting line is [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04), which extends the same BiLSTM and DistilBERT setup to top-k comparisons on SST-2, IMDb, MNLI, SNLI, and Quora, noting that dynamic sparse top-k can be more effective than fixed k=5 or k=10; this supports the plan's token-wise deletion and top-k perturbation ideas, but the excerpt lacks numerical Kendall values and significance tests. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Adebayo et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dc8f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8), which uses Spearman rank correlation, SSIM, and HOG similarity to compare gradient, SmoothGrad, GradCAM, Guided BackProp, Integrated Gradients, and Gradient Input under random-label and random-weight sanity checks on Inception v3/ImageNet and CNNs/MNIST/FashionMNIST, demonstrating that rank-based quantitative comparison is appropriate and visual inspection is insufficient; however, it does not use Kendall-tau, confidence, or class-distinctness. Further support for the class-distinctness goal, rather than the Kendall metric itself, appears in [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8), which tests whether token attributions rank agreement cues above attractors on PTB, Syneval, CoNLL-2012, and a 1000-example Winobias subset, with SmoothGrad N=30 and sigma^2=0.15 times the L2 norm of the word embedding matrix, Integrated Gradients N=100, and pass rates such as Transformer SmoothGrad 0.842 on PTB, 0.895 on Syneval, 0.956 on CoNLL, and 0.994 on Winobias; this supports ranking-based class-relevance evaluation but not confidence-based Kendall. Finally, [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) supports the broader objective of class-distinct attributions by using LRP and saliency attribution differences on TextCNN over 415 correctly classified class-0 financial complaints, showing that removing features by true-minus-contrast attribution differences shifts misclassifications toward contrast classes, but it does not use Kendall-tau or confidence scores.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that the newer [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65) directly tests Kendall-tau in the same transformer text-classification setting and shows agreement is often low and model-dependent, with DistilBERT non-attention XAI agreement at 0.1088 and XAI-to-attention agreement at 0.1658, while BiLSTM is higher at 0.4281 and 0.1814; it also reports widespread disagreement among LIME, Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT, Grad-SHAP, and Deep-SHAP, and it does not evaluate class-distinctness or confidence, so low or high Kendall-tau should not be interpreted as evidence that attributions are class-specific. A second contradiction is [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04), which qualitatively reports weak DistilBERT correlations across explanation methods, mixed fixed top-k results, and persistent disagreement even when dynamic sparse top-k is used; this is weaker than the 2021 paper because it lacks numerical Kendall values, but it reinforces that Kendall-tau alone may be an unreliable off-the-shelf confidence signal for BERT-base. A third contradiction comes from [(Adebayo et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dc8f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8), whose random-label models trained to greater than 95% training accuracy and random-weight sanity checks show that rank correlation and visual similarity can be misleading because some methods, especially Gradient Input and Integrated Gradients on sparse MNIST and Fashion MNIST, retain input structure even when labels are random; this contradicts the assumption that rank correlation by itself ensures class-distinctness, although it uses Spearman rather than Kendall-tau and provides no numerical correlation values. A fourth contradiction is methodological rather than metric-specific: [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) establishes class-distinctness through perturbation and per-class misclassification on 415 correctly classified TextCNN financial-complaint documents, where LRP attribution-difference removal shifts predictions toward contrast classes while saliency removal behaves like random removal; this suggests that the plan's Kendall-tau confidence evaluation would be insufficient unless paired with deletion-based class-shift tests, and the excerpt provides no numerical accuracy values or Kendall setup. A weaker contradiction is [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8), which uses a cue/attractor pass-rate criterion rather than Kendall-tau or confidence, reports architecture- and task-dependent plausibility without significance tests, and shows that Transformer SmoothGrad is not uniformly best, for example 0.895 on Syneval versus LSTM SmoothGrad 0.950 and a large CoNLL gap; this cautions that a single rank-based confidence threshold may not transfer across architectures, tasks, or class-relevance definitions.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to redefine the Kendall-tau confidence evaluation as an explicit class-distinctness test rather than generic method agreement. For each correctly classified example in Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, compute target-class attribution vectors from Contrast-CAT and contrast-class or low-confidence reference attribution vectors, then report Kendall-tau between target and contrast rankings, with lower tau indicating class-distinctness, and also report Kendall-tau between attribution rankings and class-specific deletion outcomes; bin examples by model confidence relative to the planned threshold gamma, include bootstrap confidence intervals or permutation tests, and compare against RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att×Att-grads, Grad-SAM, Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, CAT, AttCAT, and TIS. This directly addresses the main limitation in [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65) and [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04), where Kendall-tau measured agreement and was weak for DistilBERT. The second action is to add perturbation-based class-distinctness metrics inspired by [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418): remove top-k tokens using target-class, contrast-class, and target-minus-contrast attribution differences, then measure accuracy drop, AOPC, LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, and per-class misclassification toward contrast classes; use a fixed subset of correctly classified examples per class, report effect sizes and significance, and connect this to the plan's existing token-wise deletion tests. The third action is to add cue/attractor plausibility tests inspired by [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8): define class-relevant tokens and class-irrelevant attractors for sentiment or polarity, report pass rates and Kendall-tau rank of relevant tokens above irrelevant tokens, and test across BERT-base and other transformer architectures to check whether class-distinctness transfers. The fourth action is to add randomization sanity checks inspired by [(Adebayo et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dc8f3e0127adc6985d4695e9b69d04717b2fde8): use random-label or random-reference controls, compute Spearman and Kendall correlations between true and random attributions, and verify that Contrast-CAT changes more than baselines under randomization; this should be integrated with the planned ablations comparing random references, same-class references, multiple layers, and varying numbers of reference activations. The fifth action is to report full reproducibility details, including dataset sizes, sequence-length filters, top-k values, number of reference activations, confidence threshold gamma, layers, random seeds, hardware, and statistical tests, because several related works omit hardware, hyperparameters, or significance, and because the Neely results show that Kendall-tau outcomes are model- and task-dependent.

## To isolate the contribution of activation contrasting, the plan will: 'analyze the effect of our activation contrasting approach by comparing with random references and same-class references.'

### Support

First and strongest, the planned random-versus-same-class activation-reference ablation is supported by quantitative evidence that random-order baselines and first-order Taylor approximations can be evaluated with perturbation-curve AUC, which is directly analogous to the planned AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF: [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) used CIFAR-10 with 50,000 32x32 images and 5,000 test images, plus 5,000 ImageNet and MIT Places images, tracked real-valued classification scores under highest, random, and lowest pixel replacement, and found that first-order Taylor propagation improved AUC for epsilon=0.01 and beta=1, supporting both the use of a random null baseline and the Taylor-based contrastive formulation in Contrast-CAT. Second, activation-level ablation is supported by [(Jung, and Oh, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/552b456fab9eebcb80374e6937bb8e6ee98c74ce), which zeroed convolutional activation maps and used the resulting target-class score change as importance, reporting Increase in Confidence of 24.1, 34.4, and 35.0 percent, Average Drop of 29.41, 25.49, and 23.99 percent, and Average Drop in Deletion of 32.52, 19.42, and 26.75 percent on ImageNet, PASCAL VOC, and MS COCO with VGG16 and 1,000 images per dataset, showing that activation contrasts can quantify class-relevant contributions, although it uses a fixed zero reference rather than random or same-class references. Third, controlled activation interventions are supported by [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/996445d847f06e99b0bd259345408a0cf1bce87e), which corrupted inputs, inserted clean activations, and measured average indirect effects in GPT-2 XL with 1.5B parameters on 1,000 factual statements, finding an 18.6 percent total effect, an 8.7 percent indirect effect at layer 15, and a 6.6 percent MLP contribution, demonstrating that contrasting activation conditions can isolate component contributions. Fourth, random-reference nulls are supported by [(Cunningham et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edb548fe7574d99454b352ffdb61bca93c3072ba), which compared dictionary features against random directions, the default basis, PCA, and ICA, reported layer-1 interpretability scores of 0.33, -0.11, 0.55, 0.57, and 0.19, and showed that ablating an apostrophe feature changed next-token logits, supporting random-direction comparisons while also showing depth-dependent effects. Fifth, controlled activation-processing comparisons are supported by [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2), which compared average and max pooling in GoogLeNet on 1.3 million ILSVRC images, used a 14x14 activation map, a 20 percent threshold, and largest connected component selection, and reported top-5 localization error of 43 percent on validation and 37.1 percent on test, supporting the value of isolating activation-processing choices. Sixth, contrastive explanation is supported by [(Dhurandhar et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/748dc54a89a276639f9d571cdd8e7d1ae3f9a57a), which used pertinent positives and negatives with FISTA, elastic-net regularization, and optional autoencoder constraints, and reported 99.4 percent MNIST accuracy, 91.6 percent procurement-fraud accuracy, about 61.17 percent ABIDE accuracy, and 100 percent class preservation or switching, supporting contrast as a way to isolate class-relevant evidence, although in input space rather than activation space. Seventh, transformer-specific attribution context is supported by [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), which evaluated BERT-base with maximum sequence length 512 and ViT-base with 16x16 patches, and proposed AUC erasure, segmentation, and token-F1 protocols on ImageNet validation, ImageNet-Segmentation, and Movie Reviews, although the supplied excerpt is qualitative. Eighth, reference-choice sensitivity is supported by [(Chang et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1173ca43481c1b33f4e7891ce77200e51eecba2), which compared FIDO saliency references including per-channel mean, Gaussian blur with sigma=10, random perturbation with sigma=0.2, local averaging over a 15x15 window, VAE predictive mean, and Contextual Attention GAN, showing that reference construction affects saliency. Ninth, reference-based attribution is conceptually supported by [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6), which motivates DeepLIFT-style reference inputs and multiplier rules, although the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes. Tenth, control-based interpretability is weakly supported by [(Aljaafari et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fcc1d0887672d6d4f6280e92ac813050402d9a), which uses ROME-style causal tracing and frequency-matched expectations in a reverse dictionary task, but the supplied text is qualitative. Eleventh and weakest, contrastive representation steering is supported qualitatively by [(Shu et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be97409a865f72edc4828407496e9d31bbbc4ec7), which contrasts feature-present and feature-absent token representations to steer SAE-based LLM behavior, but it does not evaluate token-level attribution faithfulness.

### Contradictions

First and strongest, the first-order Taylor assumption in Contrast-CAT is not uniformly supported because [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) found that Taylor propagation improved AUC for epsilon=0.01 and beta=1 but was worse or on par for other settings, with Taylor-minus-identity AUC differences of +8.47 on ImageNet and +5.34 on MIT Places for epsilon=100, and mixed beta results, implying that Taylor-based activation contrasting may be sensitive to reference distance, confidence threshold, and propagation hyperparameters. Second, simple activation ablation is not automatically superior because [(Jung, and Oh, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/552b456fab9eebcb80374e6937bb8e6ee98c74ce) reported that Ablation-CAM was below LIFT-CAM on Increase in Confidence, with 24.1 versus 25.2 on ImageNet, 34.4 versus 38.7 on PASCAL VOC, and 35.0 versus 39.3 on MS COCO, and below LIFT-CAM on pointing-game proportion, 51.87 versus 52.43 percent, while also being slower due to repeated ablation, suggesting that random or same-class activation references must be compared against stronger single-pass baselines and efficiency constraints. Third, random references may be too off-manifold to serve as a clean null because [(Chang et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1173ca43481c1b33f4e7891ce77200e51eecba2) found that heuristic in-fills, including random perturbations, appear far from the natural-image distribution, while generative in-fills are more texture-consistent, so random activation or token references may introduce distributional artifacts that confound the attribution signal. Fourth, contrastive isolation may require more than selecting low-confidence references because [(Dhurandhar et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/748dc54a89a276639f9d571cdd8e7d1ae3f9a57a) used hinge-like class-change or class-preservation losses, elastic-net regularization, and optional autoencoder reconstruction to obtain pertinent positives and negatives, implying that merely choosing same-class low-confidence sequences below gamma may not fully remove class-irrelevant activation content. Fifth, random references may capture non-class-specific structure because [(Cunningham et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edb548fe7574d99454b352ffdb61bca93c3072ba) found that dictionary-feature interpretability advantages over random directions declined with depth and became comparable to ICA in layer 4, so random references may not be a pure null in deeper transformer layers and same-class references may not eliminate depth-specific variance. Sixth, activation maps can remain suboptimal even after controlled activation-processing changes because [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) reported that average-pooling GoogLeNet achieved 37.1 percent top-5 localization error on ILSVRC test, close to a fully supervised AlexNet baseline of 34.2 percent but still below fully supervised GoogLeNet, suggesting that activation-based attribution may require additional constraints. Seventh, transformer-specific evidence is weaker than desired because [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4) provides only qualitative outcomes in the supplied excerpt and no numeric AUC, mIoU, mAP, or token-F1 values, so it cannot directly validate the proposed reference ablation. Eighth, reference-based attribution remains under-tested in the supplied evidence because [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) reports no quantitative experimental outcomes, baseline comparisons, or reference-choice ablation. Ninth, causal tracing evidence is limited by missing implementation details because [(Aljaafari et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fcc1d0887672d6d4f6280e92ac813050402d9a) reports qualitative patterns without model names, parameter counts, hardware, or hyperparameters, limiting reproducibility. Tenth and weakest, contrastive steering evidence is not directly contradictory but is insufficient for the proposed attribution claim because [(Shu et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be97409a865f72edc4828407496e9d31bbbc4ec7) reports only qualitative behavior changes and no faithfulness metrics such as AOPC, LOdds, or Kendall-tau.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should operationalize the random-versus-same-class ablation with explicit reference-construction controls: define random references as random token sequences, shuffled activations, or random activation directions, and define same-class references as low-confidence target-class sequences below gamma, then report gamma values, number of references, confidence distributions, sequence lengths, token overlap, activation norms, PCA distances, and class balance, because reference quality is central and random references can be off-manifold as in [(Chang et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1173ca43481c1b33f4e7891ce77200e51eecba2) while random directions can be depth-dependent as in [(Cunningham et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edb548fe7574d99454b352ffdb61bca93c3072ba). Next, evaluate the ablation with the planned AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, but also report full perturbation curves, AUC, confidence intervals, and statistical significance across Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, following the random-order perturbation protocol of [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) and the transformer evaluation protocols of [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4). Third, include strong baselines and efficiency reporting: zero-reference activation ablation, Grad-CAM or TransAtt, CAT, AttCAT, TIS, LIFT-CAM-like single-pass methods, and DeepLIFT-style reference baselines, with forward and backward pass counts, because [(Jung, and Oh, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/552b456fab9eebcb80374e6937bb8e6ee98c74ce) shows activation ablation can be slower and not always best, and [(Shrikumar et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a2118bed729579528deb51e745d58dd3629baf6) motivates reference-based attribution baselines. Fourth, test the first-order Taylor assumption by varying reference distance, gamma, number of references, and layer aggregation, and compare against finite-difference or direct ablation estimates, since [(Binder et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7192b47b259a249f055cd448f6381088601354d) shows Taylor can be worse for some settings and [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) shows activation-processing choices materially affect class-specific maps. Fifth, add causal-intervention controls, such as corrupting inputs and inserting reference or clean activations to measure indirect effects on target-class logits, inspired by [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/996445d847f06e99b0bd259345408a0cf1bce87e), and report depth-wise MLP and attention contributions, with depth-wise random-direction analysis as in [(Cunningham et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edb548fe7574d99454b352ffdb61bca93c3072ba). Sixth, strengthen reference quality by optionally optimizing references to be low-confidence but natural, using autoencoder or generative constraints, as suggested by [(Dhurandhar et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/748dc54a89a276639f9d571cdd8e7d1ae3f9a57a) and [(Chang et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1173ca43481c1b33f4e7891ce77200e51eecba2). Finally, add semantic and qualitative validation with token-F1, pointing-game or deletion-insertion metrics, expert or semantic-role checks, and frequency-matched controls, drawing on [(Chefer et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0acd7ff5817d29839b40197f7a4b600b7fba24e4), [(Aljaafari et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fcc1d0887672d6d4f6280e92ac813050402d9a), and [(Shu et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be97409a865f72edc4828407496e9d31bbbc4ec7), while documenting BERT-base hyperparameters, hardware, random seeds, and dataset splits for reproducibility.

## To determine the role of depth, the plan will: 'analyze the impact of using multiple layers versus single layers.'

### Support

The strongest support for the planned depth ablation comes from the newer benchmark of [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6), which operationalizes the earlier layer-wise concern raised by [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) by contrasting a last-layer-only attention baseline that averages final-layer head vectors with layerwise attention propagation methods on SST-2, IMDB, TweetEval, SILICONE, MNIST, and BACE, using activation and pruning AUC-style faithfulness metrics; its reported finding that LRP-based layerwise variants, especially attention-head plus layer-normalization rules, outperform last-layer attention and that gradient-based explanations beat raw attention supports the plan's need to test whether Contrast-CAT should aggregate multiple layers rather than rely on one layer. The earlier [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) provides direct layer-wise evidence in a 6-layer, 8-head Transformer with hidden size 128 that achieved 0.96 accuracy on subject-verb agreement, and in DistillBERT on SST-2 with 100 test samples, showing that raw attention becomes nearly uniform above layer three and correlates poorly with blank-out and input-gradient importance except in the first layer, while rollout and attention flow are more distinctive and better correlated; this supports multi-layer propagation and warns that a single final-layer attention map may be insufficient for the plan's AOPC and LOdds evaluation. Next, [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca4ecf116a9b97ce525a01f3f51117877688ddf5) provides BERT/XLNet layer-wise evidence using ckasim, Jensen-Shannon attention similarity, and localization scores on SST-2, MNLI, and QQI, showing top layers change more during fine-tuning and become less localized, which motivates the plan's single-layer versus multi-layer ablation on BERT base because layer choice may materially affect token attribution. More recent quantitative layer diagnostics in [(Sengupta et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/78a08ff468e1f69bcc6463dad95f9476f3177f48) extend this concern by computing per-layer activation bounds and differential entropy on IMDb and CharIMDb across six encoder layers, eight heads, and four experts, reporting that vanilla Transformer entropy and interquartile ranges increase with depth while TransJect stabilizes, with average entropy 5.80 versus 1.3 and a Spearman correlation of 1.0 with p=0.001 between activation bound and entropy; this suggests later single layers may be more stochastic and that aggregation or layer selection should be tested. Weaker but relevant support is provided by [(Hou, and Castañón, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94abb4a364c7572173f01b9657a1f5f11aa13608), which uses Decoded Grad-CAM on a 12-layer RoBERTa base model for SST-2 and AG News to produce layer-specific token attributions, and by [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c), whose LRP custom backward pass propagates relevance across layers, although neither provides quantitative single-layer versus multi-layer attribution outcomes. The weakest direct support is from [(Kitaev et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/055fd6a9f7293269f1b22c1470e63bd02d8d9500) and [(Lawson et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2edde8b5c985c0c7ad57e49fc2eae8c4e58d163): Kitaev shows a 1-layer Transformer can solve a 1024-token copy task and that 12- to 20-layer Reformers improve long-sequence modeling, while Lawson shows multi-layer sparse autoencoders over residual-stream activations from Pythia can reduce FVU and delta cross-entropy with tuned-lens transformations, but both evaluate model performance or representation reconstruction rather than token-level attribution faithfulness, so none of these works directly validates the Contrast-CAT activation-contrast depth ablation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the implicit assumption that multi-layer aggregation will uniformly improve Contrast-CAT is the later architectural study of [(Brown et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86fb2ee92569d35a8b471d814fa4c7653728536f), which directly ablates depth versus width with fixed attention heads on IMDb, Listops, and 4k matching, testing 6/3/2/1 layers with 8/16/24/48 heads and 4/2/1 layers with 4/8/16 heads across ten attention mechanisms over three runs; it finds single-layer wide models average +0.3% accuracy over the deepest models, Listops improves +1.5% from deepest to widest, Sinkhorn gains +1.0% from widening, while Longformer worsens by -2.0%, and widest models are 71% the size of deepest models, showing that depth is not automatically necessary for classification performance and therefore the plan must empirically test whether multi-layer attribution improves AOPC and LOdds rather than assume it. A weaker contradiction is [(Kitaev et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/055fd6a9f7293269f1b22c1470e63bd02d8d9500), where a 1-layer full-attention Transformer with d_model=d_ff=256 and 4 heads solves a 1024-token copy task perfectly and a 1-hash LSH model trained from scratch reaches almost perfect accuracy when evaluated with 8 hashes, suggesting that for some tasks a single layer may already contain sufficient information, although deeper 12- and 20-layer Reformers improve long-sequence modeling and the paper does not evaluate attribution. Another tension comes from the earlier [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451), which reports that raw attention becomes nearly uniform in higher layers and has low Spearman correlation with blank-out and input-gradient importance except in the first layer; this contradicts a naive design that averages raw attention across many layers or relies on the final layer, because unpropagated multi-layer attention may dilute rather than improve token-level faithfulness. [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca4ecf116a9b97ce525a01f3f51117877688ddf5) adds a further caution: top BERT/XLNet layers are less similar after fine-tuning and have lower localization scores, so aggregating top layers may introduce task-specific, less localized signals, while lower layers are more stable; this does not refute multi-layer aggregation but contradicts a uniform all-layer average. Finally, the more recent [(Sengupta et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/78a08ff468e1f69bcc6463dad95f9476f3177f48) reports that vanilla Transformer entropy and interquartile ranges increase with depth on IMDb and CharIMDb, with average entropy 5.80 versus 1.3 for TransJect and a negative Pearson correlation of -0.81 with p=0.09 between activation bound and final performance on IMDb, suggesting that later single layers may be more stochastic and that indiscriminate multi-layer aggregation could inherit instability.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the depth ablation as a controlled faithfulness experiment on the planned BERT base models for Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, comparing single-layer Contrast-CAT variants (e.g., layers 0, 1, 3, 6, 9, and 11) against multi-layer aggregation variants (all 12 layers, bottom half, top half, and layer-weighted aggregation), while holding the activation-contrast threshold gamma, reference-activation count, gradient, attention averaging, and token-deletion refinement fixed; report AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, Kendall-tau class distinctness, mean and standard deviation over at least three seeds, and paired bootstrap or Wilcoxon significance tests, because [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6) and [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) show that layerwise propagation and gradient-based signals can outperform last-layer raw attention but only quantitative faithfulness metrics can isolate the effect. Second, include attention- and LRP-based baselines that explicitly separate depth from method: RawAtt, Rollout, Attention Flow, TransAtt, Full LRP, and Partial LRP, with conservation checks for LRP, so the plan can determine whether any gain from multi-layer Contrast-CAT is due to layer aggregation rather than the use of propagated relevance, as motivated by [(Bastings, and Filippova, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/508884a136a461869be128027950d2aa1778518c) and [(Ali et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/577a350ade92578913245e2d474aeabcb576e6d6). Third, add a layer-selection diagnostic before aggregation: compute per-layer deletion curves, Spearman or Kendall correlations between layer-specific attributions and blank-out or gradient importance, and optionally per-layer activation entropy or spread, then use these diagnostics to justify uniform averaging, weighted averaging, or pruning unstable top layers, following the layer-specific instability evidence in [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca4ecf116a9b97ce525a01f3f51117877688ddf5) and [(Sengupta et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/78a08ff468e1f69bcc6463dad95f9476f3177f48). Fourth, to address the possibility that single-layer models are already competitive, include a width-matched or single-layer transformer control trained on the same datasets, or at least report model accuracy, confidence calibration, and attribution runtime/memory, because [(Brown et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86fb2ee92569d35a8b471d814fa4c7653728536f) shows single-layer wide models can match or exceed deeper models on classification accuracy. Fifth, document implementation details needed for reproducibility: exact BERT base checkpoint, fine-tuning hyperparameters, gamma values, number of reference activations, layer indices, aggregation formula, random seeds, hardware, and batch size, and compare against the plan's baselines CAT, AttCAT, TIS, Grad-SAM, and attention methods, using the same AOPC/LOdds protocol; this is especially important because several related works, including [(Hou, and Castañón, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94abb4a364c7572173f01b9657a1f5f11aa13608) and [(Lawson et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2edde8b5c985c0c7ad57e49fc2eae8c4e58d163), provide only indirect or qualitative layer-wise evidence and therefore cannot substitute for a direct Contrast-CAT depth ablation.

## To determine the role of reference count, the plan will: 'analyze the effect of varying the number of reference activations.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed reference-count analysis is that [(Shrikumar et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e23c63c09e61992839179dbdd9de5f6c3a71db2) explicitly uses a reference activation to define both attribution and ablation: Neuron Integrated Gradients integrates the gradient of the output with respect to a neuron activation along a linear path from the input to a single reference, and the ablation evaluation clamps neurons to their reference activation. This is directly analogous to Contrast-CAT's activation contrast term, where target activations are contrasted with reference activations, and it validates that reference-activation differences can be used to identify units whose removal changes the output. A second, more specific support is the paper's ablation protocol: it selects the top 10% of neurons in the first and second convolutional layers by difference from the reference activation, ablates them, and measures error as the absolute difference between the actual pre-nonlinearity top-softmax-minus-mean output change and the sum of contribution scores for the ablated neurons. This provides a concrete, reproducible way to test whether a reference-based attribution map predicts the effect of replacing activations with reference values, which can be adapted to token-level transformer ablations when varying the number of references. A weaker but still relevant support is the paper's use of pre-nonlinearity outputs and top-class-minus-mean-class scoring to reduce saturation and class interdependence; this supports the plan's need to avoid saturated softmax outputs and to evaluate class-specific attribution quality, although the related work does not use transformer layers, attention scores, or text classification. A final, weaker support is the reported implementation detail that Neuron Integrated Gradients has runtime linear in the number of path steps and neuron-gradient computation; this supports the plan's need to measure computational cost when increasing reference count, although it does not show that multiple references improve attribution.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no evidence for the central proposed ablation, namely the effect of varying the number of reference activations; [(Shrikumar et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e23c63c09e61992839179dbdd9de5f6c3a71db2) uses only a single all-black MNIST reference and does not compare one reference against multiple references, low-confidence references, same-class references, or random references. Therefore it cannot support the hypothesis that multiple reference activations improve Contrast-CAT's filtering of class-irrelevant features. A second contradiction is the domain and model mismatch: the method was evaluated on an MNIST CNN, not BERT-base or transformer text classification, and it does not use attention scores, token deletion, AOPC, LOdds, Kendall-τ, or confidence threshold γ, so its findings about reference-based attribution cannot be directly transferred to the proposed setting. A third, weaker contradiction is the lack of quantitative and statistical detail: the excerpt reports only that mean absolute errors and runtimes are shown in figures, without numeric values, integration steps, hardware, model architecture, dataset size, or significance tests, and it cautions that gradient times difference-from-reference should not be presumed to generalize because of saturation effects. This limits confidence that a simple activation-difference contrast, especially with multiple references, will be stable or faithful in transformer activations.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the reference-count ablation explicitly comparative and statistically rigorous: test a controlled grid of reference counts, for example 1, 2, 5, 10, and 20, against single-reference CAT or AttCAT, random references, same-class references, and low-confidence target-class references, and report AOPC, LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, Kendall-τ, runtime, and variance across seeds with paired bootstrap or Wilcoxon tests. The second most important action is to adopt the ablation-error protocol from [(Shrikumar et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e23c63c09e61992839179dbdd9de5f6c3a71db2): replace or clamp selected token activations with the corresponding reference activation(s), compute the actual change in a pre-nonlinearity target-class score such as top logit minus mean logit, and compare that change to the summed attribution scores for the removed tokens, thereby directly validating whether the reference contrast predicts the effect of activation replacement. Third, use pre-nonlinearity activations and avoid saturated softmax outputs; if the method uses logits, report sensitivity to the confidence threshold γ and to reference sampling strategy, including whether references are averaged, weighted by inverse confidence, or filtered by quality. Finally, document reproducibility details that the related work omitted: number of integration steps if path-based attribution is used, BERT-base layer selection, dataset sizes, hardware, hyperparameters, random seeds, and computational cost as a function of reference count.

## To provide qualitative evidence, the plan will: 'qualitative evaluation through visualization of attribution maps.'

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8), which directly visualizes class-specific attribution maps for a BERT-based text sentiment classifier and shows attributions concentrated on sentiment-bearing phrases, with visual sparsity, object focus, and contrastiveness consistent with quantitative local consistency and contrastiveness scores; this supports the plan's use of visualization for BERT sentiment datasets such as Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, and its pairing of visual inspection with faithfulness metrics. The next strongest support is [(Nguyen, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3785f9083dcb46d2bea7ee771c2a513bb43917a6), which uses highlighted token explanations in a human forward-prediction task and finds that AOPC has a positive OLS coefficient of 0.543 with standard error 0.042 and p < 0.01 for crowd accuracy, showing that visually presented token attributions and AOPC are relevant to human understanding, although the model is an MLP rather than BERT. [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) supports visualization of token-level maps in BERT and DistillBERT settings, but also shows raw attention is weakly correlated with blank-out and gradient importance, so the plan should visualize Contrast-CAT alongside attention baselines and rank-correlation checks. [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762) provides a BERT-specific precedent for layer-wise gradient attribution maps and inversion-centered difference maps, supporting the plan's layer aggregation and qualitative inspection, though it does not report AOPC, LOdds, or Kendall-τ. [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418) and [(Arras et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/78201113a852abba42a94b6d7b6098fb261f8759) support token-level visual attribution plus deletion or perturbation checks, with LRP-guided removal causing larger accuracy drops than saliency or random removal in TextCNN and word-level LRP maps supporting deletion-based intrinsic validation in 20Newsgroups. [(Dhurandhar et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/748dc54a89a276639f9d571cdd8e7d1ae3f9a57a) supports contrastive qualitative evaluation by combining visual pertinent positive and pertinent negative maps with expert feedback and 100 percent class-preservation or class-switching checks, which is conceptually aligned with Contrast-CAT's reference-activation contrast. Weaker but still supportive precedents include vision-based visualizations that are tied to quantitative localization or part metrics, such as [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2), [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2), and [(Zhang et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/773ca5c76da50cf6f21553b0f8eee391ac65f9c8), as well as qualitative attribution-map demonstrations in [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be), [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2), [(Li et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fafa541419b3756968fe5b3156c6f0257cb29c23), [(Komorowski et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bba254ecfc356a3e383db7af48794ece8bea3f0), and [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04); these are less directly relevant because they are older, domain-specific, case-based, or lack the plan's faithfulness and rank-correlation evaluation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction to treating visualization as sufficient evidence is [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad), which shows that high-attended tokens do not always have causal impact, attention-order removal is not optimal, and random or alternative removal orders can flip decisions faster, especially in encoderless models; this directly motivates the plan's AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF rather than relying on visually salient attention or activation maps. The next strongest contradiction is [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65), which reports low mutual Kendall-τ agreement among LIME, Integrated Gradients, DeepLIFT, Grad-SHAP, and Deep-SHAP with a mean of 0.2684 and low agreement with attention with a mean of 0.1736, with DistilBERT non-attention agreement only 0.1088, indicating that visually inspecting one attribution map cannot establish faithfulness without rank-correlation and perturbation validation. [(Kim et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/682b9d2212258fd5edbfca589c86390c31a956b0) contradicts the assumption that saliency maps communicate the correct concept: workers identified the more important concept only 52 percent of the time, and high confidence did not align with correctness, so the plan should add human or ground-truth validation and avoid interpreting visually plausible maps as faithful. [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969) shows that BERT-based visual attributions can be shaped by baseline choice, special marker tokens, and layer depth, with attention negatively correlated with Integrated Gradients, warning that Contrast-CAT visualizations must be treated as diagnostic ablations and tested for reference or baseline sensitivity. [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451) also contradicts raw attention visualization, finding raw attention nearly uniform in higher layers and low Spearman correlation with blank-out and input gradients, while rollout and flow were better; this supports including attention baselines but not using them as primary evidence. [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1) provides qualitative cases where plausibility and faithfulness diverge, rankings are unstable across architectures or saliency methods, and interpretations can be hard for humans to understand, so the plan should explicitly visualize failure cases and instability. Weaker contradictions or limitations are that many earlier visual-attribution studies lack the quantitative faithfulness protocol planned here: [(Li et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fafa541419b3756968fe5b3156c6f0257cb29c23) reports no AOPC, LOdds, Kendall-τ, or significance tests; [(Zhou et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31f9eb39d840821979e5df9f34a6e92dd9c879f2) and [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) rely on example figures without systematic human or faithfulness metrics; [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be) and [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) provide qualitative or under-specified user-study evidence; [(Zhang et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/773ca5c76da50cf6f21553b0f8eee391ac65f9c8) does not report numeric visualization outcomes; and [(Komorowski et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bba254ecfc356a3e383db7af48794ece8bea3f0) describes metrics but does not provide visualization results in the excerpt. These do not refute visualization, but they contradict the sufficiency of visual inspection alone.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should keep visualization only as a qualitative diagnostic ablation and report AOPC, LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, and Kendall-τ for Contrast-CAT against RawAtt, Rollout, Att-grads, Att x Att-grads, Grad-SAM, Full LRP, Partial LRP, TransAtt, CAT, AttCAT, and TIS on Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB, with confidence intervals and paired significance tests, because [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad), [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65), and [(Kim et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/682b9d2212258fd5edbfca589c86390c31a956b0) show visual maps can be unfaithful, method-dependent, or misleading. Second, pre-register a stratified example-selection protocol for visualizations: sample correct and incorrect predictions, high and low confidence, both classes, short and long sequences, and include failure cases, rather than cherry-picking sentiment-bearing examples, following the case-based limitations in [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1) and [(Li et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fafa541419b3756968fe5b3156c6f0257cb29c23). Third, add a small human or ground-truth validation component, such as forward-prediction with highlighted tokens or token-relevance ratings with confidence, inspired by [(Nguyen, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3785f9083dcb46d2bea7ee771c2a513bb43917a6) and [(Kim et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/682b9d2212258fd5edbfca589c86390c31a956b0), and report whether Contrast-CAT improves perceived faithfulness or label-guessing accuracy over baselines. Fourth, explicitly test sensitivity to the activation-contrast design by visualizing and quantitatively scoring maps under different γ thresholds, numbers of reference activations, random references, same-class references, and baseline choices, because [(Anand et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf528fe74378b774aab485d650a830adb948969), [(Sundararajan et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f302e136c41db5de1d624412f68c9174cf7ae8be), and [(Dhurandhar et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/748dc54a89a276639f9d571cdd8e7d1ae3f9a57a) show that reference, baseline, and contrastive choices can change explanations. Fifth, visualize layer-wise and aggregated maps for Contrast-CAT and baselines, including raw attention and rollout, and report Kendall-τ or Spearman correlations among methods and with perturbation scores, as in [(Abnar, and Zuidema, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a9f336481b39515d6cea2920696f11fb686451), [(Mohebbi et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b265827019f420b44c79fd87be1cc6000329c762), and [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8). Sixth, tie each visually salient token to a perturbation effect by reporting MoRF and LeRF curves, token-deletion accuracy, class-switching rates, or blank-out effects, following [(Arras et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/78201113a852abba42a94b6d7b6098fb261f8759), [(Xiong et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ad935b2062a98f85cd7690b50bf21002ea25418), and [(Serrano, and Smith, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/135112c7ba1762d65f39b1a61777f26ae4dfd8ad). Seventh, report reproducibility details for the qualitative evaluation: BERT base version, dataset splits, preprocessing, maximum sequence length, random seeds, hardware, number of visualized examples, sampling method, and software, because many related works omit these details. Least important but useful, consider adding visual quality metrics such as sparsity, noise, local consistency, or contrastiveness, as in [(Vukadin et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/17093c3088bc678b90fc208c5641c1c7d98339c8) and [(Komorowski et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bba254ecfc356a3e383db7af48794ece8bea3f0), but do not let them replace AOPC, LOdds, or Kendall-τ.

## To validate generalizability, the plan will: 'conduct experiments across different transformer architectures to validate the generalizability of our approach.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method, which is to conduct experiments across different transformer architectures to validate the generalizability of Contrast-CAT, comes from [(Zhou et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5d64b7c8b4cfead0c04d3ee5c5bc88575378623), which evaluates DETAIL across a custom recurrent transformer with 10 recurrent layers and 15 attention heads on MNIST in-context learning and across large language models, with an appendix state-space model, using demonstration perturbation, noisy demonstration detection, random projection dimension 1000, 20 demonstrations, 120 test queries, and wall time 4 to 10 seconds; this directly supports architecture-level validation of an attribution method, although the task is demonstration-level ICL rather than token-level text classification. The next strongest support is the iterative move from CNN attribution sanity checks to transformer settings, beginning with [(Schulz et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57d697214af89d8b8f77eeaaf040a43d1a0c651b), which validates attribution across ResNet-50 and VGG-16 using parameter-randomization SSIM, sensitivity-n masking with 100 random index sets for mask sizes 1 to 80 percent on 1000 validation images, and 8x8 or 14x14 occlusion patches, establishing a multi-architecture and sanity-check protocol that the plan can adapt, but without direct transformer or activation-contrast evidence. This is followed by [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1), which compares vanilla saliency, SmoothGrad, and Integrated Gradients on LSTM, QRNN, and Transformer language models trained on WikiText-103, with SmoothGrad 30 samples and variance 0.15 times the L2 norm of the word embedding matrix, Integrated Gradients 100 steps, and distilled Transformer 16 to 4 layers due to memory constraints, reporting quantitative plausibility pass rates such as Transformer SmoothGrad 0.842 on PTB, 0.895 on Syneval, 0.956 on CoNLL, and 0.994 on Winobias, thereby supporting cross-architecture faithfulness-oriented evaluation. [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6) supports the text classification context by evaluating token-level attribution across Transformer, CNN, and LSTM with random saliency, diagnostic properties, and FLOPs, although it includes only one transformer. [(Aken et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be283fc67ecbfe0cb1e50718181ce7b9c0d53a74) supports transformer architecture comparison by probing BERT-base 12 layers, BERT-large 24 layers, and GPT-2 on SQuAD, HotpotQA, and bAbI with macro-averaged F1, but it does not evaluate attribution. [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65) and [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04) support architecture comparison and the plan's use of Kendall-tau by comparing BiLSTM and DistilBERT on SST-2, IMDb, SNLI, MultiNLI XNLI, and Quora, but they include only one transformer. [(Wu, and Ong, 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a560943f23a1e00e26abbd9d84367217b235ff28) supports generalizability validation through BERT token relevance across four text classification datasets, including SST-5, using Pearson correlations for shared words and token-removal ablation, but it varies datasets rather than architectures. [(Jiang et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24967a55839cf9d8c26187888aac67b3ee4999b9) supports token-removal faithfulness and suppression of irrelevant information in BERT on IMDB, AG News, MNLI, and RTE, but only one architecture. [(Prasad et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbd4f99c65d677dc82ac4c051b289626f1e8f435) supports cross-model interpretability evaluation by using ANLI development set, a 60-example subset with 20 examples from each ANLI round where all models failed, five random seeds, and HANS diagnostic, but it does not directly test multiple transformer architectures or activation contrast.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan's confidence evaluation is [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04), which finds weak agreement among explanation methods for DistilBERT regardless of single- or pair-sequence tasks and concludes that low inter-method correlation makes rank correlation a questionable off-the-shelf validity measure for attention-based explanations; this directly challenges using Kendall-tau alone to ensure class-distinct attributions in the plan. This is reinforced by the earlier [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65), which reports mean Kendall-tau 0.2684 across models and tasks, 0.1736 between XAI methods and attention, 0.1088 among non-attention methods for DistilBERT versus 0.4281 for BiLSTM, and lower pair-sequence agreement 0.1883 versus single-sequence 0.273, showing that architecture and task can undermine rank-correlation-based confidence checks. A second important contradiction is [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1), which shows that higher plausibility did not necessarily imply higher faithfulness, that input consistency favored LSTM and Transformer over QRNN, that SmoothGrad improved Transformer but hurt QRNN and LSTM, and that model consistency was lower than input consistency even though Transformer distillation reduced 61 percent of parameters versus less than 20 percent for LSTM and QRNN; this warns that a single faithfulness metric or plausibility test may not generalize across architectures. Third, [(Wu, and Ong, 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a560943f23a1e00e26abbd9d84367217b235ff28) shows that Layerwise Attention Tracing had weak token-removal ablation performance but still strong Pearson correlations across four BERT text classification datasets, suggesting that cross-dataset consistency can be decoupled from faithfulness and should not be treated as primary evidence of generalizability. Fourth, [(Schulz et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57d697214af89d8b8f77eeaaf040a43d1a0c651b) is not directly supportive because it validates attribution on ResNet-50 and VGG-16 rather than transformers, reports no hardware or statistical significance, and shows that some methods such as Guided Backpropagation and LRP remain similar after randomizing large portions of the network, while Occlusion-8x8 performs perfectly for small mask sizes because its scores directly reflect logit drops; this cautions that architecture-generalizability evidence from CNNs and masking metrics may not transfer to Contrast-CAT. Fifth, [(Aken et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be283fc67ecbfe0cb1e50718181ce7b9c0d53a74) shows strong architecture-task interaction, with GPT-2 significantly worse than BERT on SQuAD and HotpotQA but considerably better on bAbI, and BERT errors concentrated in bAbI tasks 17 and 19 requiring positional or geometric reasoning, implying that generalizability across transformer architectures may be task-specific and not guaranteed for text classification attribution. Sixth, [(Jiang et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24967a55839cf9d8c26187888aac67b3ee4999b9) only evaluates fine-tuned BERT and performs degradation on the original model rather than the bottleneck-modified model, with no numeric AOPC, LOdds, confidence intervals, or statistical significance, so it does not directly support cross-architecture validation and highlights a potential confound in token-removal faithfulness. Seventh, [(Prasad et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbd4f99c65d677dc82ac4c051b289626f1e8f435) computes importance alignment only on incorrect predictions and uses a 60-example expert subset, without numeric scores or statistical significance, making its cross-model evidence limited and potentially confounded by example selection. Eighth, [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6) provides only qualitative results, includes only one transformer architecture, and omits diagnostic property scores, FLOPs values, hardware, and hyperparameters, so it cannot strongly support multi-transformer generalizability.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify a concrete multi-transformer architecture matrix for the generalizability experiment, including at least BERT-base, RoBERTa-base, DistilBERT, ALBERT-base, DeBERTa-base, and ELECTRA-base or a recurrent transformer or LLM if feasible, all fine-tuned on Amazon Polarity, Yelp Polarity, SST2, and IMDB with identical preprocessing, tokenization where possible, training hyperparameters, random seeds, hardware, and model parameter counts, because [(Zhou et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5d64b7c8b4cfead0c04d3ee5c5bc88575378623) and [(Schulz et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57d697214af89d8b8f77eeaaf040a43d1a0c651b) show that architecture-level validation requires explicit architecture controls and [(Aken et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be283fc67ecbfe0cb1e50718181ce7b9c0d53a74) shows architecture-task interaction. Second, replace or supplement Kendall-tau with a broader evaluation battery: retain AOPC and LOdds under MoRF and LeRF, but add random attribution baseline, parameter-randomization sanity checks, top-k overlap, input consistency under perturbations, model consistency with distilled or smaller models, and statistical significance or confidence intervals, motivated by [(Neely et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b5dca588dc5006feef49781ac82763b40788ce04), [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65), [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1), and [(Wu, and Ong, 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a560943f23a1e00e26abbd9d84367217b235ff28). Third, use a common set of evaluation examples across architectures, especially examples where models are incorrect or low-confidence, and report per-architecture and per-dataset results with multiple seeds, following [(Prasad et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bbd4f99c65d677dc82ac4c051b289626f1e8f435) and [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6). Fourth, calibrate and report Contrast-CAT hyperparameters per architecture, including the reference confidence threshold gamma, number of reference activations, layer aggregation range, and refinement deletion threshold, and include ablations against random references and same-class references, because [(Jiang et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/24967a55839cf9d8c26187888aac67b3ee4999b9) and [(Schulz et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57d697214af89d8b8f77eeaaf040a43d1a0c651b) show that information-suppression and masking hyperparameters can materially affect attribution quality. Fifth, report computational cost and implementation constraints, including FLOPs, wall time, number of attribution samples or steps, memory usage, and whether distilled models are used, as in [(Ding, and Koehn, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/61c4f280e24d80d09e4f76ed2e31672b970bfbd1), [(Atanasova et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae06bc1e8e67c27b89329ebcfe61b71625d853f6), and [(Zhou et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5d64b7c8b4cfead0c04d3ee5c5bc88575378623). Sixth, treat cross-dataset Pearson or Kendall consistency as a secondary diagnostic rather than primary evidence of generalizability, and report it with significance tests, because [(Wu, and Ong, 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a560943f23a1e00e26abbd9d84367217b235ff28) and [(Neely et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/884dffa4c248663a32cacc1b37cb4a5ecd4d2b65) show consistency can be high even when faithfulness or inter-method agreement is weak.
