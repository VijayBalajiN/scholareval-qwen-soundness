## Context: Core platform design for proteome-wide mechanism-of-action characterization; Method: We will implement PISA-based thermal shift assays in both living cells and native lysates to create a comprehensive platform for drug mechanism characterization.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed cell-based PISA platform is that Gaetani et al. already implemented a 1D PISA workflow in K562 cells, the same cell line specified in the plan, using a single heat step within the 48-58°C window, centrifugation-based soluble/insoluble separation, and TMT-based LC-MS/MS quantification, and recovered known target engagement for MTX on DHFR and 5-FU on TYMS [(Gaetani et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/157a25bd7a9cb5bff789a977bac58a0d9154f7ff). This directly validates the central assumption that compound-dependent soluble protein abundance changes can identify annotated targets in the proposed cell-based arm. A second, somewhat weaker but still important line of support is that the same study simulated 1D PISA outputs from existing TPP melting curves and found that a single-temperature measurement preserved target-engagement signal while reducing thermal measurements, which supports the plan's claim of an 8-fold throughput advantage over full TPP and the feasibility of compressing melting curves into single measurements. The weakest but still relevant support is the use of a 9-compound panel with well-annotated targets, including kinase inhibitors, to generate compound-specific thermal-stability hits consistent with known mechanisms, which provides preliminary evidence that the assay can distinguish target-specific responses in a small library; however, this panel is much smaller than the proposed 96-compound library and does not yet demonstrate the proposed TMTPro 16-plex scale, 10 μM 30-minute treatment, or 384-hour instrument-time design.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that the related work did not include a native lysate arm, so it provides no direct evidence that the proposed lysate-based PISA experiments will detect direct ligand binding or that cell-based versus lysate-based comparisons can separate primary direct engagement from secondary cellular effects. This is a central component of the proposed platform, and the absence of lysate validation weakens the claim that the combined design will distinguish direct from indirect engagement. The next strongest limitation is that the excerpt reports no numeric fold changes, p-values, exact hit counts, or statistical significance, so it does not validate the proposed empirical hit criteria of |log2FC| > 0.2 and >3.5 SD from protein-specific variance, nor does it establish the sensitivity or false-positive behavior needed for a 96-compound screen. A further limitation is that the related work used only a 9-drug panel and did not specify the exact single heat temperature, drug concentrations, treatment duration, or TMT plex size, whereas the proposed plan specifies 10 μM for 30 minutes, TMTPro 16-plex, and a 48-58°C window; therefore, the prior work supports the general concept but does not yet confirm the exact operational parameters, multiplexing strategy, or instrument-time budget required for the proposed large-scale screen. Finally, the related work used a single heat step, while the plan describes thermal gradients, so the operational definition of PISA should be clarified to avoid conflating full TPP with 1D PISA.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a native lysate PISA arm using dounce-homogenized K562 extracts under the same 10 μM, 30-minute, 48-58°C conditions and to benchmark it against the cell-based arm on the 9-compound annotated-target panel before scaling to 96 compounds, explicitly measuring overlap and divergence between cell and lysate hits to test whether lysate enriches for direct binding and cell captures secondary effects. The next priority is to validate the proposed hit criteria and single-temperature choice by rerunning or reanalyzing the 9-drug panel with vehicle controls, reporting fold changes, protein-specific variance, false-positive rates, and statistical significance, and using the TPP-to-1D PISA simulation to select the exact heat temperature within 48-58°C that maximizes target-specific signal while minimizing protein-specific noise. After that, the plan should scale to the 96-compound TMTPro 16-plex design with biological duplicates, dose-response validation for selected compounds, western blot/biochemical confirmation, and network-based correlation analysis, while documenting exact heat temperature, TMT plex composition, instrument time, and reproducibility metrics so that the 871,120 cell and 627,176 lysate measurements are generated under conditions directly traceable to the prior proof-of-concept.

## Context: Cell-based thermal profiling of compound effects; Method: For cell-based experiments, we will treat K562 cells with compounds, subject them to thermal gradients (48°C-58°C), and measure soluble protein abundance after thermal denaturation and centrifugation.

This directly supports the central assumption in the research plan that compound treatment can be inferred from increased soluble protein stability after heat treatment, and it is conceptually aligned with the proposed workflow of treating K562 cells, applying a thermal gradient, and measuring soluble protein abundance. The next strongest support comes from [(Massey, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d968a6dca045ce64533cc039429052e79fe514c6), which demonstrated a cell-based CETSA variant in HT29 cells using controlled heating in an imaging-compatible 96-well plate and single-cell immunofluorescence to assess Chk1 thermostabilization after compound treatment. This supports the feasibility of cell-based thermal profiling in plate-based formats and highlights the importance of heating control, which is relevant to the proposed 48-58°C thermal window and high-throughput TMT-based proteomics design. The weakest support comes from [(Xi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d6201de32fcbd130c5617d3c4f2fba3109691e), whose supplied text only lists a cellular thermal shift assay section without specifying cell line, compound, thermal gradient, quantification method, or statistical outcomes. ### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or cautionary evidence comes from [(Massey, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d968a6dca045ce64533cc039429052e79fe514c6), which showed that the apparent thermal stabilization of Chk1 in intact HT29 cells was highly dependent on the heating method. Specifically, V158411 did not increase Chk1 thermostabilization after immersion in pre-heated PBS, while under sealed low-well-volume heating, V158411 and other Chk1 inhibitors decreased Chk1 nuclear immunofluorescence. This contradicts the assumption that a fixed cell-based thermal gradient, such as the proposed 48-58°C window, will unambiguously reveal compound-protein engagement through increased soluble protein abundance. A weaker contradiction is that [(Tan et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0de8ce01c8cc0c9446d52e9c8f457891718f7b9f) does not provide quantitative fold changes, melting-temperature shifts, IC50 values, statistical significance, cell line, peptide concentrations, thermal gradient range, centrifugation parameters, or quantification instrument details. This does not directly refute the proposed method, but it limits the ability to benchmark the proposed quantitative criteria, including log2 fold changes greater than 0.2 in absolute value, changes greater than 3.5 standard deviations from protein-specific variance, 10 μM 30-minute treatment, and TMTPro 16-plex proteomics. The weakest contradiction is that [(Xi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d6201de32fcbd130c5617d3c4f2fba3109691e) provides no usable experimental details, so it cannot validate or challenge the proposed K562 cell-based PISA workflow, the 48-58°C thermal window, the soluble-proteomics readout, or the large-scale experimental design involving approximately 871,120 cell-based and 627,176 lysate-based thermal stability measurements.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a rigorous pilot validation of the K562 thermal profiling protocol before the full 96-compound screen, using known direct binders and non-binders to test whether the proposed 48-58°C window, 10 μM 30-minute treatment, centrifugation, and TMT-based mass spectrometry reliably detect direct engagement. This should be informed by [(Massey, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d968a6dca045ce64533cc039429052e79fe514c6), which shows that heating method can change the direction of the thermal shift signal, and by [(Tan et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0de8ce01c8cc0c9446d52e9c8f457891718f7b9f), which provides a targeted CETSA precedent for Mdm2/Mdm4 engagement. The pilot should compare multiple heating conditions, including sealed low-volume heating, immersion in pre-heated buffer, and rapid equilibration, and should include vehicle controls, heat-only controls, compound-only controls, lysate-only controls, and known negative compounds to distinguish true direct binding from heating artifacts, secondary cellular effects, or centrifugation-related solubility changes. The next most important action is to strengthen the distinction between direct and indirect engagement by pairing the proposed cell-based PISA assay with lysate-based PISA, targeted CETSA, dose-response experiments, biochemical binding assays, and western blotting, as already outlined in the research plan. This is especially important because the cell-based K562 assay will capture signaling, protein-protein interaction, and post-translational modification effects, while the lysate assay should enrich for direct ligand binding. The proposed statistical criteria, log2 fold changes greater than 0.2 in absolute value and changes greater than 3.5 standard deviations from protein-specific variance, should be validated against known binders and non-binders before being applied to the full library, and the TMTPro 16-plex design, approximately 384 hours of instrument time, and the expected scale of approximately 871,120 cell-based and 627,176 lysate-based measurements should be accompanied by explicit quality-control metrics for normalization, plex effects, missing values, and replicate agreement. The least important but still useful action is to treat [(Xi et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d6201de32fcbd130c5617d3c4f2fba3109691e) only as weak conceptual support, because the supplied text lacks protocol, quantitative, and statistical details needed to justify the proposed method.

## Context: Orthogonal validation of predicted target engagement and signaling effects; Method: We will use biochemical assays and western blotting to validate predicted target engagement and measure downstream signaling effects.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Grailer et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89c611f97f1e25b4f45d07ce186a795e35e019e3) used an orthogonal validation logic directly analogous to the proposed biochemical assay plus western blot strategy: a direct target-expression measurement by flow cytometry was paired with a functional signaling readout, including basal luminescence, fold induction, and EC50, to confirm that a reporter-gene clone retained receptor expression and downstream signaling. This supports the proposed plan's decision not to rely solely on proteome-wide thermal stability changes from PISA, but to require independent confirmation of predicted target engagement and signaling in K562 cells. A secondary support is that the related work emphasized side-by-side testing of parallel expanded passages, with staggered-passage freezing every 5-6 passages and at least 2 weeks of parallel expansion after thaw, rather than day-to-day comparisons; this is directly relevant to the proposed validation because thermal-shift predictions from TMT proteomics should be confirmed by biochemical and western blot measurements performed under matched, contemporaneous conditions to avoid confounding by culture, treatment, or assay variability. A weaker but still relevant support is that the related work monitored multiple stability parameters, including basal signal, fold induction, EC50, receptor expression, population doubling time, and morphology, before accepting a clone, suggesting that the proposed validation should include not only target and phospho-protein western blots but also controls for cell viability, compound exposure, protein loading, and dose-response behavior.

### Contradictions

The main contradiction is that the related work does not validate the specific proposed assays; it used flow cytometry and a luminescent reporter rather than biochemical binding assays or western blotting, so it provides only indirect support for the proposed orthogonal-validation concept and no direct evidence that western blotting will capture the relevant target engagement or signaling changes predicted by PISA. A second tension is that the related work reported no numerical potency, fold-change, or statistical values for the stability testing itself, relying instead on qualitative concordance between receptor expression and functional response; this contradicts an assumption that orthogonal validation can be judged without explicit quantitative acceptance criteria, and it highlights that the proposed method should define measurable thresholds for biochemical binding, western blot band intensity, phospho-protein fold change, EC50/IC50 concordance, and statistical significance. A weaker limitation is that the related work's context was cell-line stability and assay qualification for a reporter-gene bioassay, including antibiotic selection based on parental kill curves, freshly supplemented antibiotics, early banking, and preparation of seed, master, and working cell banks, rather than compound target validation in K562 cells or proteome-wide thermal shift screening, so its evidence is less directly transferable to the proposed large-scale mechanism-of-action workflow.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify a quantitative, side-by-side validation protocol for selected thermal-shift hits: use matched K562 cell or lysate samples treated with compound, vehicle, and positive controls, and measure direct target engagement by a biochemical binding or target-occupancy assay together with western blotting for the predicted target and downstream signaling nodes in the same biological replicates, rather than relying on separate days or uncontrolled comparisons. The second priority is to define explicit acceptance criteria and statistical methods, such as dose-response EC50/IC50 concordance with thermal-shift fold changes, minimum fold-change or p-value thresholds for western blot and biochemical readouts, replicate number, and concordance metrics between predicted targets and validated targets. The third priority is to include stability and assay-quality controls analogous to the related work's monitoring of basal signal, fold induction, receptor expression, growth, and morphology: verify compound stability and exposure, cell viability, protein loading, antibody specificity, and thermal-shift reproducibility before interpreting validation results. The least critical but still useful action is to report validation outcomes in a structured way that compares biochemical, western blot, and thermal-shift measurements for each compound, including dose-response curves, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and failure modes such as reduced signaling with preserved target expression or vice versa.
