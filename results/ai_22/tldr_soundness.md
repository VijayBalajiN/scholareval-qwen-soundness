## Strengths 
The strongest support is direct and method-specific: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) already operationalizes FCoReBench with 40 problems, 596 training and 1354 test instances, natural-language rules, programmatic generators, solvers, verification scripts, and the proposed X/Y/C structure, and shows that SymPro-LM with GPT-4-Turbo, Z3, Python, temperature 0.7, five runs, and four feedback rounds improves over few-shot by 54.04%, Logic-LM by 44.86%, and PAL by 16.97%, with refinement adding 21.02% and five runs adding 11.41%. Solver-augmented LLM pipelines are strongly supported by [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) and [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd), while program-aided execution is supported by [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7). Size-scaled combinatorial evaluation is supported by [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272), and non-first-order generalization datasets are supported by [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the plan's assumptions rather than its feasibility. First, symbolic-aided LLMs can already be strong, challenging the hypothesis that existing approaches will perform poorly: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows SATLM gains over prompting and program baselines, and [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows Logic-LM can outperform standard prompting on several logical datasets. Second, the direct implementation lacks statistical significance, broad size sweeps, and full cost reporting, and still leaves 38% incorrect programs and 12% timeouts after refinement [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). Third, refinement is not guaranteed to improve accuracy: [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) reports rising executable rates with stagnant or reduced accuracy, and [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) shows simple feedback is weak without executable tests. Fourth, model comparisons may not follow a clean frontier-to-small gradient: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports GPT-4-Turbo dominance and Mixtral weakness, while [(Zhu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50257a5a7f9c27835dd5fd774d333109c218f4f9) shows model rankings can invert. Finally, cost evidence is limited to three problems and small sizes [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), and solver overhead can dominate runtime [(Palikareva, and Cadar, 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230ba662b4e2d59a68cba3a2aad9301b62ba7c5e).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Add statistical rigor and reproducibility: report exact model identifiers, temperatures, Z3 and Python versions, hardware, random seeds or API versions, multiple runs, confidence intervals, bootstrap or permutation tests, pass@k or best-of-n results, per-problem and per-size accuracy, and full error rates, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) lacks significance and hardware details while [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) show bootstrap and Wilson intervals are feasible.

Make instance-size scaling and constraint-based verification central: define problem-specific size axes, generate at least five sizes per problem, report accuracy, solver time, timeouts, API cost, and optimality gap where applicable, and use verifiers that accept all legal outputs rather than exact targets, with annotator ambiguity checks and contamination controls, following [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272), [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937), [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), and [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792).

Expand baselines and ablate the refinement loop: include zero-shot and few-shot chain-of-thought, self-consistency, least-to-most, PAL with and without interpreter, per-instance Logic-LM or SATLM, solver-only and symmetry-broken solver baselines, and budget-matched Tree-of-Thoughts; refine programs using solver diagnostics, held-out validation acceptance, and an error taxonomy for planning, encoding, runtime, timeout, and semantic failures, following [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321), [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7), [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd), [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33), and [(Devriendt et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23967fe65d42256e922d852bc13a6550196e8568).
