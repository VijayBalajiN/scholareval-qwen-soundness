## Context: This creates the central testbed and specifies its problem sources. Method: We will develop a comprehensive benchmark called FCoReBench consisting of 40 challenging first-order combinatorial reasoning problems. We will construct FCoReBench by selecting 40 computationally challenging problems from various sources including Wikipedia's NP-hard problems, logical puzzles from publishing houses, and real-world operations research problems.

### Support

The strongest support is direct and newer: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) constructs exactly the proposed FCoReBench testbed, with 40 problems from Wikipedia NP-hard problems, Nikoli-style puzzles, operations research, and SAT competition sources; 30 are NP-hard and the rest unknown complexity; the split is 10 graph-based, 18 grid-based, 5 set-based, 5 real-world, and 2 miscellaneous; it contains 596 training and 1354 test instances, averaging about 15 training and 34 test per problem; two formal-background authors wrote natural-language rules, a second author checked correctness and ambiguity, and a third independent annotator solved all training instances; programmatic generators, solvers, and verification scripts are used, with Z3, a Python interpreter, GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B, temperature 0 for few-shot and Logic-LM and 0.7 for PAL and SymPro-LM, five runs, and four feedback rounds. This directly validates the plan's fcore components X, Y, C, verification-script accuracy, and SymPro-LM refinement, and it reports SymPro-LM average improvements of 54.04% over few-shot and 44.86% over Logic-LM with refinement, plus 100% accuracy at a one-time cost of $0.02 per problem on three problems. The next strongest support is newer solver-augmented work that extends earlier prompting-only methods: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) SATLM uses code-davinci-002 to generate declarative specifications converted to SAT, and it outperforms chain-of-thought and PROGLM by more than 20 points on GSM-SYS, reaching 69.4% greedy and 80.9% self-consistency, while ablations show SAT-NOSOLVER is only on par with standard prompting and SAT-SYMSOLVER adds gains, with bootstrap p less than 0.05. [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) Logic-LM supports the plan's solver-augmented baseline and refinement idea through a problem formulator, symbolic reasoner, result interpreter, and optional self-refiner; with GPT-3.5 it improves PrOntoQA from 51.80 to 85.00, ProofWriter from 36.16 to 71.45, FOLIO from 54.60 to 61.27, and LogicalDeduction from 41.33 to 62.00, and self-refinement raises executable rate. Size-scaled combinatorial evaluation is supported by [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272), which uses six combinatorial tasks, 100 instances per size 5, 10, 15, 20, and 30, GPT-4 and Gemini-1.5, an A100 40GB GPU, and 1 to 3 minutes per instance, showing that SGE outperforms chain-of-thought, Self-Refine, and Decomposition and that advantage over input-output prompting increases as tasks become NP-hard. Controlled generated benchmarks support the plan's instance-generation and formal-verification ethos: [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) PRONTOQA generates 3 to 10 concept ontologies, 1, 3, or 5 hop proofs, 400 examples per condition, and uses recursive-descent parsing with strict, skip, broad, and valid proof accuracy; [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) GPT-3 synthetic word tasks generate 10,000 examples per task, verify exact outputs, and show 175B few-shot performance of 66.9% on random insertion removal but only 15.1% on hard anagramming, supporting generated exact-match evaluation though not first-order combinatorial scaling. Earlier challenging-benchmark work supports the need for hard, curated evaluation: [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792) ReClor uses 1,000 test items, answer-only bias detection, 440 EASY and 560 HARD items, and a graduate-student baseline, showing models score around 75% on EASY but around 30% on HARD; [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) ProofWriter provides D0-D5 100k-question logical proof datasets, strict proof correctness, and out-of-domain transfer, relevant to the plan's non-first-order generalization datasets. Prompting baselines are supported by [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb), [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda), and [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321), which establish chain-of-thought, zero-shot chain-of-thought, and least-to-most as strong direct-LLM baselines, while [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) provides a non-LLM CVRP specification with 1000 instances per size, feasibility masking, beam width 10, optimality gap, and runtime scaling, supporting operations-research problem formalization and solver/heuristic comparison.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan's hypothesis that existing LLM approaches, even when aided by symbolic solvers, will perform poorly is [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33): SATLM with code-davinci-002 and a SAT solver achieves large gains over chain-of-thought and PROGLM, including GSM-SYS 80.9% with self-consistency, more than 20-point gains on ALGEBRA, LSAT 37.4%, BOARDGAMEQA 80.7%, selective accuracy of 77.1% versus 45.2% on GSM-SYS, and bootstrap significance p less than 0.05, indicating that declarative symbolic solving can make LLMs effective on structured constraint tasks, although these are not first-order combinatorial size-scaled instances. A related contradiction is [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd): Logic-LM with GPT-4 reaches 87.63 on LogicalDeduction, 78.92 on FOLIO, and 83.20 on PrOntoQA, and improves over standard prompting by 24.98 points on average, but GPT-4 chain-of-thought is higher on PrOntoQA at 98.79 and AR-LSAT executable rate remains low, rising only from 32.6 to 39.8 with self-refinement, showing that solver pipelines can be strong but not uniformly superior and can be sensitive to formulation executability. [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272) also contradicts a blanket claim that LLMs cannot handle combinatorial scaling: SGE with GPT-4 and Gemini-1.5 outperforms chain-of-thought, Self-Refine, and Decomposition on six combinatorial tasks, with margins over the second-best Decomposition baseline from 7.53 points on Assignment to 37.13 points on Job Scheduling and an advantage over input-output prompting that increases for NP-hard tasks, though it uses cost improvement rather than verification-script accuracy and reports no significance tests. A direct but weaker contradiction to the expectation of clean success is that [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports no formal significance tests, SymPro-LM still has 38% incorrect programs and 12% timeouts after refinement, and Tree-of-Thoughts is much more expensive and less accurate on the three cost-compared problems. Direct prompting evidence also complicates the poor-performance hypothesis: [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb) PaLM 540B with 8-shot chain-of-thought reaches state-of-the-art accuracy on GSM8K, MAWPS, SVAMP, and StrategyQA; [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) zero-shot chain-of-thought with self-consistency reaches 89.0% on MultiArith and 70.1% on GSM8K for PaLM 540B; [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) engineered chain-of-thought and least-to-most reach 68.61% and 68.01% on GSM8K with code-davinci-002; and [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) shows chain-of-thought is emergent around 100B parameters and can more than double GSM8K performance. These tasks are not first-order combinatorial, but they show that prompting baselines may be nontrivial and that failure should be measured against strong chain-of-thought and self-consistency baselines. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows mixed LLM capability on generated exact-match tasks: 175B GPT-3 gets 66.9% on random insertion removal but only 15.1% on hard anagramming and fails word reversal, so generated tasks can be too easy or too hard depending on structure. [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792) ReClor shows fine-tuned models can reach about 50% on HARD logical multiple-choice items, above the around 30% hard-set performance of strong zero-shot transformers, suggesting that benchmark difficulty can be mitigated by task-specific training, which the plan should control for. Finally, [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937) shows a non-LLM pointer network with beam width 10 can get roughly 95% of VRP10 and VRP20 instances within 10% and 13% of optimality and can outperform classical heuristics, indicating that combinatorial operations-research problems are not inherently unsolvable by learned systems, although this does not evaluate LLMs or first-order instance scaling.

### Suggested Action

Most important: implement and validate FCoReBench exactly as in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) with 40 problems, 30 NP-hard, 596 training and 1354 test instances, natural-language rules checked by two authors and an independent annotator, programmatic generators, solvers, and verification scripts, hidden problem names, and Z3/Python evaluation; add formal statistical reporting, including bootstrap or Wilson confidence intervals, per-problem and per-size significance, and effect sizes, because that paper and [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272) lack significance tests. Second: build SymPro-LM as an instance-agnostic program generator that emits conversion-to-symbolic, solver-call, and conversion-back code, with explicit solver-status handling for unsat, ambiguous, and error cases, and refinement on solved examples, using the ablation evidence from [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) that SAT-NOSOLVER is weak while SAT-SYMSOLVER is strong and from [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) that executable rate and self-refinement matter; report executable rate, semantic correctness, runtime errors, and planning versus execution errors. Third: make instance-size scaling central, following [(Akimov et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5605c71465436dd3f0627d5dcd2184ea1d0c9272) and [(Nazari et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0366b6396610708a77540564050a90a761a28937): define problem-specific size axes, generate at least five sizes, report accuracy, optimality gap or solution cost, runtime, timeouts, and API cost; for small instances compute exact, MIP, or Z3 optimal solutions to calibrate gaps, and for large instances use verification scripts rather than exhaustive enumeration. Fourth: include strong prompting and solver baselines: standard few-shot, zero-shot chain-of-thought, 8-shot chain-of-thought, self-consistency, least-to-most, PAL, Logic-LM, Tree-of-Thoughts, and SGE-style decomposition where feasible, using the plan's models GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B and temperature settings, temperature 0 for deterministic baselines and 0.7 for multiple-run program methods, as in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb), [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda), and [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321). Fifth: evaluate generalization on LogicalDeduction, ProofWriter, and PrOntoQA with separate per-instance programs, using answer accuracy plus proof or execution correctness metrics from [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), and compare against GPT-4 chain-of-thought because [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows chain-of-thought can outperform Logic-LM on PrOntoQA. Sixth: document reproducibility and contamination controls, including prompt templates, solver configurations, random seeds, hardware or API versions, cost, n-gram overlap checks as in [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb), fictional, true, and false ontology controls as in [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), and unlikely-pretraining caveats as in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0). Finally, use error taxonomies from [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) planning and execution errors, [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb) semantic, one-step-missing, and symbol-mapping errors, and [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) incorrect, semantically incorrect, runtime-error, and timeout categories to explain failures and guide refinement.

## Context: This is the core proposed approach for combining LLMs with symbolic reasoning. Method: We will propose SymPro-LM, a novel method that combines LLMs with both symbolic solvers and program interpreters.

### Support

The strongest support is direct: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) implements SymPro-LM on FCoReBench with 40 problems, 596 training and 1354 test instances, Z3, Python, GPT-4-Turbo, temperature 0.7, five runs, four feedback rounds, and two solved examples, reporting average gains of 54.04% over few-shot prompting, 44.86% over refined Logic-LM, and 16.97% over PAL, with refinement adding 21.02% and five runs adding 11.41%, directly validating the plan's LLM-program-solver-interpreter loop. Strong support also comes from LLM-to-symbolic pipelines: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows SATLM with code-davinci-002 and a SAT solver reaches 80.9% on GSM-SYS with self-consistency, 84.8% on GSM, 37.4% on LSAT, and 80.7% on BoardgameQA, with solver ablations and p<0.05 gains over PROGLM, supporting the solver front-end; [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows Logic-LM with GPT-3.5 improves PrOntoQA from 51.80 to 85.00, ProofWriter from 36.16 to 71.45, and FOLIO from 54.60 to 61.27, and self-refinement raises executable rate by 5.01 points, supporting solver-augmented LLMs and error-message feedback. Program-aided evidence supports the interpreter side: [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) uses code-davinci-002 to generate executable expressions and reaches 68.61% on GSM8K with engineered chain-of-thought and 68.01% with engineered least-to-most, showing LLMs can produce code but also making interpretation errors. First-order solver-side evidence supports exploiting structure: [(Devriendt et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23967fe65d42256e922d852bc13a6550196e8568) uses IDP, SAUCY 3, lex-leader symmetry breaking, and CLASP 3.1.4 to solve all 16 pigeonhole instances up to 100 pigeons under 1000s and 6GB RAM, showing first-order symmetry breaking can reduce search. Benchmark-design support comes from [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792), whose ReClor 6,138 four-option questions, answer-option-only accuracies of 42.2-43.2, and HARD-set accuracy around 30% show LLMs exploit surface cues and need verifiable, bias-controlled, size-controlled evaluation. [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) supports formal proof-step evaluation on PrOntoQA, where 48 experiments of 400 examples show only text-davinci-002 beats chance and 5-hop top-down falls to chance, motivating solver/program assistance. [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) supports using ProofWriter for generalization, with 99.2% answer accuracy on the 100k-question D5 set but OOD OWA F1 dropping to 44.6 for All-At-Once and complex Electricity abduction to 64% F1, showing templated success does not guarantee robustness. Weaker support includes [(Dodaro et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eebf97d5aa33a59d4dbbc6a9dff83aa493b33f2e), whose WASP external-heuristic interface couples an ASP solver with external control and solves hard Siemens instances under 900s, and [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2), which conceptually frames symbolic solver-aided and program-aided LLM methods but provides no quantitative results.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension is that the direct implementation of SymPro-LM in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports no statistical significance tests, leaves 38% incorrect programs, 20.5% semantically incorrect programs, 5.5% Python runtime errors, and 12% timeouts after refinement, and is only competitive rather than best on PrOntoQA, so the claimed superiority over few-shot, Logic-LM, and PAL is not yet statistically or fully robustly established. A second contradiction is that per-instance symbolic pipelines can already be strong, reducing the necessity of instance-agnostic programs: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows SATLM outperforms PROGLM and chain-of-thought by more than 20 points on GSM-SYS and ALGEBRA, and [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows Logic-LM with GPT-4 is outperformed by chain-of-thought on PrOntoQA (83.20 versus 98.79), indicating that hand-written declarative or per-instance formulations and strong LLMs may suffice on some logical tasks. Third, LLMs can solve some formal reasoning without solvers: [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) reaches 99.2% answer accuracy and 96.2% strict proof correctness on ProofWriter D5, and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) shows text-davinci-002 can solve short PrOntoQA chains, challenging the assumption that LLMs always need external solvers for first-order-like tasks. Fourth, symbolic solvers alone can exploit first-order structure without LLMs: [(Devriendt et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23967fe65d42256e922d852bc13a6550196e8568) solves all 16 pigeonhole instances up to 100 pigeons with first-order symmetry breaking, and [(Dodaro et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eebf97d5aa33a59d4dbbc6a9dff83aa493b33f2e) solves hard Combined Configuration instances with WASP domain heuristics, suggesting LLM program generation may be unnecessary for some combinatorial solving. Fifth, program-aided LLMs can achieve strong math performance without symbolic solvers, as [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) reaches 68.61% on GSM8K, and [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792) shows transformers can approach graduate-student performance on full ReClor inputs, though HARD remains low. The weakest contradiction is conceptual: [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2) provides no empirical results, so it neither validates nor refutes the specific SymPro-LM design.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add statistical rigor and reproducibility to the FCoReBench experiments: report multiple seeds, confidence intervals, significance tests, hardware, solver runtimes, API costs, and one-time versus per-instance costs, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) lacks significance and hardware details, while [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) uses bootstrap p<0.05 and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) uses Wilson intervals. Second, integrate first-order solver-side preprocessing into SymPro-LM or FCoReBench, such as symmetry detection, lex-leader constraints, subdomain interchangeability, or external heuristic callbacks, based on [(Devriendt et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23967fe65d42256e922d852bc13a6550196e8568) and [(Dodaro et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eebf97d5aa33a59d4dbbc6a9dff83aa493b33f2e), and evaluate solver-only and symmetry-broken baselines to isolate the LLM contribution. Third, strengthen FCoReBench against surface bias and memorization by using size-controlled instance families, randomized instance generation, verification scripts, answer-option or format controls, and bias-aware splits inspired by [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792). Fourth, improve the refinement loop by combining solved-example feedback with solver error messages, tracking executable rate, semantic correctness, runtime errors, timeouts, and selective prediction, as in [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) and [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), and select programs using a held-out validation set rather than training accuracy alone. Fifth, add targeted baselines and ablations: per-instance Logic-LM/SATLM, PAL, Tree-of-Thoughts, standard prompting, solver-only, symmetry-broken solver, and feedback-round/run-count ablations, with error taxonomies for planning versus execution errors from [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) and proof-step validity/atomicity/utility metrics from [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a). Sixth, for generalization datasets, use ProofWriter OOD rulebases and PrOntoQA hop/direction/ontology controls to test robustness beyond templated Datalog, as in [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a). Seventh, report model scaling and cost for GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B, including the one-time program-generation cost versus per-instance inference cost, and consider external heuristic interfaces if ASP solvers are added, following [(Dodaro et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eebf97d5aa33a59d4dbbc6a9dff83aa493b33f2e) and the taxonomy in [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2).

## Context: This improves SymPro-LM programs by using training-instance failures as iterative feedback. Method: We will implement a refinement mechanism using solved examples as feedback. When programs fail on training instances, we will provide automated feedback to guide the LLM toward corrections. This process will continue until all training examples are solved correctly or a maximum number of feedback rounds is reached.

### Support

The strongest support is direct and method-specific: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) implements the proposed solved-example refinement for SymPro-LM on FCoReBench, where an LLM generates an input-agnostic Python program that calls Z3, executes on training instances, and receives automated feedback containing error type, actual interpreter/solver error, failing input, and, for incorrect outputs, the solved example and expected solution. On the 40-problem FCoReBench benchmark with 596 training and 1,354 test instances, GPT-4-Turbo with four feedback rounds improves performance by 21.02%, adding five runs improves by another 11.41%, and solved-example count saturates around seven examples; refinement reduces incorrect-program rate from 58% to 38%, semantically incorrect programs from 29% to 20.5%, Python runtime errors from 13.5% to 5.5%, and timeouts from 15.5% to 12%, while average gains over few-shot prompting and Logic-LM are 54.04% and 44.86%. This directly supports the research plan's core refinement loop, the use of training-instance failures, and the ablation choices for feedback rounds, multiple runs, and solved examples. The next strongest support is execution-feedback for code: [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) uses SELF-DEBUGGING with up to 10 debugging turns on Spider, TransCoder, and MBPP, feeding execution outcomes, code explanations, and error feedback to Codex, GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4, and StarCoder; greedy self-debugging matches 16-sample baseline accuracy and 8-sample self-debugging beats 32-sample baseline, showing that automated execution feedback and multiple candidate runs can improve sample efficiency even when one turn is often sufficient. This supports the plan's use of verification scripts and multiple runs, though it does not use solved examples. Solver-error feedback for symbolic formulations provides further support: [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows that Logic-LM self-refinement using solver error messages improves executable formulation rates on PrOntoQA, ProofWriter, FOLIO, LogicalDeduction, and AR-LSAT, with GPT-3.5 executable rates rising from 66.7% to 84.3% on FOLIO and from 11.3% to 21.8% on AR-LSAT, and GPT-4 FOLIO rising from 79.9% to 85.8%; on FOLIO, 0 to 3 refinement rounds increased executable formulations and final performance. This supports using Z3 or solver diagnostics in the proposed feedback, although the feedback there is per-instance syntax repair rather than reusable-program refinement. General iterative feedback also supports the mechanism: [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f) shows feedback-refinement improves GPT-4 code optimization from 27.3% to 36.0% and dialogue preference from 25.4% to 74.6%, and that an external oracle identifying incorrect math answers yields larger gains than self-generated feedback, supporting the plan's preference for automated correctness signals. Symbolic-pipeline evidence is also supportive: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows SATLM's LLM-to-SAT pipeline outperforms PROGLM on GSM-SYS (69.4% greedy, 80.9% self-consistency versus 43.3%) and CLUTRR (68.3% versus 58.9%), with bootstrap p < 0.05, and its UNSAT/AMBIG solver feedback enables selective prediction, supporting solver-augmented verification and error-aware selection. Iterative logical inference supports the ProofWriter evaluation: [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) shows iterative ProofWriter improves strict proof correctness on D5 from 87.1% to 96.2% and depth-5 correctness from 65.1% to 86.4%, and zero-shot Birds-Electricity proof correctness from 80.5% or 84.5% to 97%, supporting structure-exploiting iterative reasoning on the non-first-order dataset in the plan. Formal diagnostic support comes from [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), which evaluates 48 PRONTOQA conditions of 400 examples with 8-shot greedy decoding and shows only text-davinci-002 exceeds chance, while its proof-step validity, atomicity, and utility analysis provides a template for diagnosing SymPro-LM failures. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Mita et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e5edcc692d2c974ec614f3ace9b77148ce0e1a), whose self-refinement denoising for GEC uses a Transformer big model on BEA-train, EFCamDat, and Lang-8, accepts model-generated corrections only when length-normalized GPT-2 perplexity is not worse, and improves CoNLL-2014 F0.5 from 40.3 to 48.4, supporting an automatic acceptance check for refined artifacts even though the domain is far from first-order program synthesis.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that solver-error refinement can improve executability without improving, and sometimes reducing, final accuracy: [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) reports that after self-refinement GPT-4 accuracy on executable samples is unchanged on PrOntoQA and ProofWriter, decreases from 80.4 to 79.9 on FOLIO, and decreases from 60.0 to 58.8 on AR-LSAT, while FOLIO accuracy stagnates in later refinement rounds even as executable rate increases. This newer, domain-specific result tempers the broader claim in [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f) that iterative feedback generally improves outputs, and it contradicts the assumption that more feedback rounds will monotonically improve SymPro-LM accuracy, especially for semantic errors in Z3 encodings or interpreter mappings. A second important contradiction is that feedback quality and executable test coverage determine gains: [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) finds that simple feedback alone does not notably improve Spider when unit tests are unavailable, and that additional debugging after one turn yields within 0.1% accuracy, suggesting that if FCoReBench verification scripts provide only coarse final-output correctness or if many instances are unsolvable within timeout, the proposed loop may add cost without benefit. The same paper's sample-efficiency results also imply that multiple runs may be more valuable than many refinement rounds, so the plan should not assume four rounds are always optimal. A third contradiction concerns model dependence: [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f) shows a weaker 13B model struggles to produce usable feedback and often repeats or hallucinates during refinement, and math gains are modest without an external oracle; this warns that GPT-3.5-Turbo and Mixtral 8x7B in the research plan may benefit less from refinement than GPT-4-Turbo, and that LLM-generated interpretation of feedback can be unreliable. Fourth, LLM reasoning limits may persist even with solved examples: [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) finds that on PRONTOQA only text-davinci-002 achieves better-than-chance proof accuracy across 48 conditions of 400 examples, 5-hop top-down examples fall to chance, and performance is sensitive to traversal direction, suggesting that solved-example prompting and refinement may not fully overcome depth or ordering effects on non-first-order datasets. Fifth, failure-mode evidence shows that solver feedback may not address the dominant errors: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) reports planning errors account for 72.5% of failures on GSM-SYS, while execution errors dominate on GSM (57.5%) and CLUTRR (52.5%), so a feedback loop that only reports solver UNSAT/AMBIG or final incorrect output may miss translation/planning errors in SymPro-LM programs. Sixth, the direct implementation itself has evidentiary gaps that weaken confidence: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) does not report statistical significance, hardware, or full cost across all 40 problems, leaves 38% incorrect programs and 12% timeouts after refinement, and evaluates non-first-order datasets with per-instance programs rather than a single reusable program, so the reported 21.02% and 11.41% gains should not be overgeneralized. The weakest contradiction is from [(Mita et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e5edcc692d2c974ec614f3ace9b77148ce0e1a), whose automatic acceptance check still allowed 10.4% of replacements not to preserve meaning and 8.8% to add unnecessary information, and whose significance claims lack p-values, warning that automatic acceptance of refined programs can introduce subtle semantic regressions.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add rigorous statistical validation and reproducibility to the refinement ablations: report paired bootstrap or permutation tests, 95% confidence intervals, multiple random seeds/API versions, Z3 version, timeout, prompt templates, temperature 0.7 for multiple runs, and per-problem results on the 40 FCoReBench problems with 596 training and 1,354 test instances, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports large gains but no significance, and [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows bootstrap significance can be computed for solver-augmented gains. Second, make the automated feedback richer and ablate its components: include error type, failing input, Z3/Python diagnostics, constraint-violation messages, minimal counterexamples, and the solved example/expected solution only for training instances, then test variants with and without expected solution, with and without solved examples, and with and without solver diagnostics, because [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows solver error messages improve executability but not always accuracy, [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f) shows actionable feedback matters, and [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) shows simple feedback is weak without executable tests. Third, add a regression-aware acceptance rule: after each refinement round, evaluate the candidate program on a held-out subset of training instances or a validation split, accept it only if it improves overall accuracy without increasing runtime errors, timeouts, or semantic errors, and track all-training-solved as a stopping condition rather than the sole objective, because [(Mita et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2e5edcc692d2c974ec614f3ace9b77148ce0e1a) uses a perplexity fail-safe to avoid worse replacements, [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) uses execution-based selection, and [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) uses solver feedback for selective prediction. Fourth, calibrate feedback rounds, multiple runs, and solved examples with early stopping and cost reporting: start from the observed saturation points of four rounds, five runs, and seven solved examples in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), but report round-wise and run-wise curves, stop when marginal gain is below a pre-registered threshold, and report API cost and wall-clock time for all 40 problems, because [(Chen et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e3c493fb09dcd61bb05e8c5659f23327b7b6340) finds one debugging turn often sufficient and [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) finds later rounds stagnate. Fifth, implement an explicit error taxonomy and solver diagnostics: classify failures as planning/translation, SMT encoding, solver timeout, interpreter mapping, output format, and semantic incorrectness, and use Z3 UNSAT/AMBIG, unit tests, property-based tests, and proof-step validity/atomicity/utility analysis for non-first-order datasets, because [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) distinguishes planning from execution errors and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) provides a formal proof-step diagnostic template. Sixth, evaluate model heterogeneity and non-first-order settings separately: report GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B with the same feedback protocol, test whether weaker models need stronger external feedback or a stronger feedback interpreter, and report ProofWriter, LogicalDeduction, and PrOntoQA with per-instance program generation, answer accuracy, proof correctness, and cost, because [(Madaan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aaf6a2cbad5850ad81ab5c163599cb3d523436f) shows weaker models struggle with self-feedback, [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) shows depth and traversal direction matter, and [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) shows iterative proof generation can improve depth generalization. Finally, expand the benchmark and instance-size analysis beyond the current 40 problems and average 15 training instances per problem by adding more problems, larger instance sizes, deeper constraint chains, and train/test splits that control for instance size, because the research plan's central hypothesis is that performance degrades with instance size and because [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) and [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) show depth and structure strongly affect logical reasoning performance.

## Context: This gives the benchmark a formal problem structure that supports instance generation and verification. Method: We will formally define first-order combinatorial reasoning (fcore) problems as having three components: a space of legal input instances (X), a space of legal outputs (Y), and a set of constraints (C) that every input-output pair must satisfy.

### Support

The strongest support is that the proposed X/Y/C formalization has already been operationalized in a benchmark with 40 problems, 596 training instances, and 1354 test instances, where author-written scripts generated instances and verified solutions, and only three problem descriptions required correction after independent annotation, showing that explicit input, output, and constraint spaces are implementable and reproducible [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). This work also shows that the formal structure enables SymPro-LM with GPT-4-Turbo to outperform few-shot prompting by 54.04 percent, Logic-LM with refinement by 44.86 percent, and PAL by 16.97 percent, while few-shot prompting solved less than a third of problems, using Z3, a Python interpreter, temperature 0 for few-shot and Logic-LM, temperature 0.7 for PAL and SymPro-LM, five runs, and four feedback rounds, directly supporting the plan hypothesis that formal first-order structure plus solver and interpreter programs is needed. The same work reports 100 percent accuracy at a one-time cost of $0.02 per problem on three problems versus ToT prompting at 0 to 46.33 percent accuracy and $0.1235 to $1.676 per test instance, and refinement gains of 21.02 percent with four feedback rounds and an additional 11.41 percent with five runs, saturating around seven solved examples, which supports the proposed refinement mechanism. A second strong support is that declarative constraint parsing plus an external SAT solver yields large, statistically significant gains on fixed reasoning datasets: GSM-SYS 69.4 percent greedy and 80.9 percent self-consistency, GSM 84.8 percent self-consistency, ALGEBRA and GSM-SYS improvements over PROGLM by more than 20 percentage points, LSAT from 30.9 to 37.4 percent, BOARDGAMEQA from 73.9 to 80.7 percent, and selective accuracy of 77.1 versus 45.2 percent on GSM-SYS, with p < 0.05 over PROGLM, indicating that explicit constraints and solver verification are more reliable than unconstrained generation [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33). A third support is that SMT-verified counterexample-guided synthesis treats block-world planning as a formal constraint-satisfaction problem with an input state, output action sequence, and transition constraints, and uses verifier counterexamples to refine LLM plans, aligning with the proposed C component and refinement mechanism, although it reports only qualitative success after a few iterations [(Jha et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c89d8672a3742672850fa46f1e8ec51f3261019). Weaker support comes from least-to-most prompting, which decomposes problems and translates symbolic commands to Python, showing that structured input-output decomposition and error analysis are useful, but it does not define X/Y/C or use solver verification [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321). The weakest support is chain-of-thought prompting, whose error analysis on LaMDA 137B GSM8K found 46 percent minor and 54 percent major errors among incorrect answers and showed scale-dependent gains, motivating verification but not formal constraints [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the same formal benchmark work shows that the X/Y/C definition alone is not sufficient: few-shot prompting on the same formal problems still solved less than a third of problems with GPT-4-Turbo, so the formalization is necessary but not sufficient, and the gains depended on SymPro-LM instance-agnostic programs, Z3, a Python interpreter, and refinement [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). A second contradiction is that the SAT-aided declarative work shows that much of the benefit comes from the solver rather than a formal benchmark: removing the solver makes performance on par with standard prompting, greedy decoding on GSM is slightly lower than PROGLM, and the method does not define separate X, Y, and C spaces or generate instances of varying size, so it does not directly validate the plan scaling hypothesis [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33). A third contradiction is that least-to-most prompting without formal X/Y/C or external solvers reaches 68.01 percent on engineered GSM8K, close to chain-of-thought best 68.61 percent, and symbolic manipulation errors are dominated by interpretation errors rather than decomposition failures, suggesting that some compositional structure can be captured by prompting alone, although no statistical significance was reported [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321). A fourth, weaker contradiction is that chain-of-thought prompting without formal constraints reached state of the art on GSM8K, SVAMP, and MAWPS with PaLM 540B and more than doubled standard prompting on GSM8K, showing that direct prompting can be strong on some first-order arithmetic tasks, though its error analysis reveals unreliability and scale dependence [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5). The weakest contradiction is an evidentiary gap rather than direct refutation: the SMT counterexample work provides no quantitative accuracy, iteration counts, problem sizes, prompt token limits, hardware details, or statistical significance, so it cannot confirm that formal verification improves performance at scale [(Jha et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c89d8672a3742672850fa46f1e8ec51f3261019).

### Suggested Action

Most important, instantiate X/Y/C as executable schemas for all 40 problems, with input generators, output validators, constraint checkers, and solver/interpreter scripts, following the 40-problem, 596-training, 1354-test construction and only-three-corrections validation protocol [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). Second, add explicit instance-size scaling by generating families of instances with controlled size parameters such as graph nodes, grid dimensions, set cardinality, and constraint tightness, and report accuracy, solver time, token length, and cost versus size, because the related solver work does not test scaling and the plan central hypothesis is size-dependent degradation [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). Third, report solver ablations and selective accuracy, including with and without solver, with and without program interpreter, greedy versus self-consistency decoding, and statistical significance, mirroring the p < 0.05 comparisons and selective accuracy metrics of 77.1 versus 45.2 percent on GSM-SYS [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33). Fourth, integrate formal counterexample feedback, using SMT or SAT counterexamples or invalid prefixes, and track iterations to convergence, solved-example saturation, and cost, since refinement improved SymPro-LM by 21.02 percent with four rounds and saturated around seven solved examples, and SMT counterexample feedback is conceptually aligned but unquantified [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) [(Jha et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c89d8672a3742672850fa46f1e8ec51f3261019). Fifth, build an error taxonomy mapped to X/Y/C violations, combining minor and major errors and semantic or coherence errors from chain-of-thought, decomposition, copy, and interpretation errors from least-to-most, and planning and execution errors from SAT-aided work [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33). Least important, but still useful, standardize baselines and generalization tests with CoT, least-to-most, PAL, Logic-LM, ToT, and SAT-aided declarative prompting, using matched exemplars, temperatures, runs, and reporting inference time and cost, especially for non-first-order datasets where per-instance programs are required [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75).

## Context: This makes each benchmark problem usable for LLM evaluation and automatic scoring. Method: We will create natural language descriptions of rules and input-output formats for each problem, along with scripts to generate instances and verify solutions.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which directly instantiates the proposed method by constructing FCoReBench from 40 problems selected from Wikipedia NP-hard problems, Nikoli puzzles, operations research, and the SAT competition industrial track, retaining only problems that could be described in natural language and generated programmatically. It reports 596 training and 1,354 test instances, averaging about 15 training and 34 test instances per problem, across 30 known NP-hard and 10 unknown-complexity problems, including 10 graph-based, 18 grid-based, 5 set-based, 5 real-world, and 2 miscellaneous problems, with natural-language rules, input-output formats, instance-generation scripts, solvers, and verifier scripts cross-checked on manually curated cases; a third annotator solved all training instances and only three problems required description corrections in one feedback round. It also reports experiments with GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B, temperature 0 for few-shot prompting and Logic-LM, temperature 0.7 for PAL and SymPro-LM, Z3 as the SMT solver, and Python for PAL and SymPro-LM. This is direct evidence that the proposed method is feasible and that automatic verification can produce clear downstream differences: with GPT-4-Turbo, SymPro-LM outperformed few-shot prompting by 54.04% and Logic-LM with refinement by 44.86%, while few-shot prompting solved less than one-third of the problems. The next strongest support is [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb), which validates the broader pattern of pairing natural-language specifications with automatic checks in code generation: HumanEval, MBPP, GSM8K-Python, TransCoder, and DeepFix are scored by pass@k, with pass@1 using greedy decoding and k greater than 1 using nucleus sampling with p=0.95 and temperature 0.8, and fixed test sets such as 560 TransCoder samples and 1260 DeepFix programs. It uses PaLM 8B, 62B, and 540B models trained on a 780B-token mixture containing 5% GitHub code with 2048-token sequence length, and reports code-token exposure for PaLM, LaMDA, and Codex. Although it does not generate a first-order instance space, it supports the proposed method's use of scripts to verify solutions and shows that automatic scoring is practical for LLM benchmarking. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) provides weaker but still relevant support by showing that synthetic tasks can be defined by simple transformation rules, input-output formats, and 10,000 generated instances per task, with zero-shot, one-shot, and few-shot prompting, automatic target matching, and beam search with beam width 4 and length penalty 0.6 in the general evaluation setup; it evaluates GPT-3 models from 125 million to 175 billion parameters with a 2048-token context window and reports few-shot results for the 175B model of 66.9% on removing random insertions, 38.6% on cycling letters, 40.2% on the easier anagram task, 15.1% on the harder anagram task, and failure to reliably reverse words. These tasks are lexical rather than first-order combinatorial, but they demonstrate that generated tasks can expose model limits. [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) supports the natural-language prompting and automatic scoring aspect, especially for GSM8K where code-davinci-002 improved from 16.38% zero-shot to 68.61% best chain-of-thought and 68.01% best least-to-most, using one-shot prompts with a single two-step example and engineered contexts with 4, 7, or 8 examples, but it does not provide instance-generation or solver-based verification scripts. Finally, [(Ramamonjison et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe6060ced80c1c245a718e6188b6516207bf0a8) supports the need for structured input-output formats and automatic scoring of structured outputs by mapping word problems to typed declarations and canonical optimization tables, with declaration-level mapping accuracy computed from ground-truth declarations, false positives, and false negatives, although it uses BART and Text-to-Table baselines rather than LLM solving or generated combinatorial instances.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the research plan's assumption that the benchmark will reliably expose degraded performance as instance size increases comes from the same work that most directly supports the method, [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). It reports only average 15 training and 34 test instances per problem, no statistical significance tests, and Tree-of-Thoughts evaluated only on a subset because of high cost; the cost and accuracy comparison covers only three problems, where SymPro-LM achieved 100% accuracy at a one-time cost of $0.02 per problem while ToT achieved 46.33%, 32.5%, 26.25%, 8%, 7.5%, and 0% across Latin Squares 3x3, Latin Squares 4x4, Magic Square 3x3, Magic Square 4x4, Sujiko 3x3, and Sujiko 4x4. This limited instance-size range, small per-problem test count, and underpowered significance reporting contradict the plan's goal of analyzing performance as instance size increases and of making fair baseline comparisons. A weaker contradiction comes from [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), where cycling and anagram tasks are non-bijective, meaning the original word is not uniquely determined by the distorted input, yet scoring is based on matching a known target word rather than checking a set of formal constraints. This contradicts the proposed method's requirement that verification scripts check legal constraints, because exact-target scoring can penalize alternative valid outputs and is not a model of first-order constraint satisfaction. Another contradiction is that [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) reports substantial interpretation and decomposition errors, including 13 errors for code-davinci-002 with 6 incorrect twice/thrice interpretations and 7 after-as-and errors, and 20 GSM8K failures categorized as 4 wrong problem decomposition, 13 wrong problem solving, and 3 wrong ground truth; this contradicts the assumption that natural-language rules and input-output formats are automatically sufficient without additional ambiguity testing. A less direct contradiction is that [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb) relies on fixed or manually derived test sets, such as 560 TransCoder samples and 1260 DeepFix programs, rather than a generated instance space, so it does not provide evidence that generated first-order instances will remain unambiguous, scalable, or free of train-test contamination. The weakest contradiction is that [(Ramamonjison et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe6060ced80c1c245a718e6188b6516207bf0a8) reports no quantitative accuracy values, dataset size, hardware, training hyperparameters, or statistical significance, so it cannot by itself support the proposed method's claim that structured input-output formats and automatic scoring will yield reliable LLM evaluation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to strengthen statistical and reproducibility reporting for the proposed method: run multiple seeds or API calls, report mean and standard deviation or confidence intervals, use significance tests where appropriate, and report pass@k or best-of-n results in addition to single-run accuracy, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) does not report significance tests and [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb) shows pass@k with greedy and nucleus sampling as a practical automatic-scoring protocol. The next most important action is to expand and explicitly analyze instance-size scaling: generate parametric families of instances with controlled sizes, report accuracy versus instance size, include larger test instances beyond the average 34 test instances per problem reported by [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), and avoid the absence of instance-size analysis seen in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0). Third, make the verifier constraint-based rather than exact-target based: accept all legal outputs, canonicalize equivalent solutions where possible, and test non-bijective or multiple-solution cases, because [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows exact target matching can be problematic when the input does not uniquely determine the output. Fourth, validate the natural-language descriptions with multiple annotators and ambiguity metrics, including hidden problem names, third-party solving of training instances, inter-annotator agreement, and error taxonomies, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) used a third annotator and only three description corrections, while [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) shows that language ambiguity can cause decomposition and interpretation errors. Fifth, define structured output schemas and intermediate representations, such as JSON, SMT, or Python program interfaces, to reduce parsing failures and make verification more robust, inspired by the two-stage typed-declaration mapping in [(Ramamonjison et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2fe6060ced80c1c245a718e6188b6516207bf0a8) and the code-generation verification setup in [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb). Sixth, control for training-data exposure and contamination by reporting model code-token exposure, using held-out generated instances, and avoiding instances likely present in training corpora, as [(Chowdhery et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/094ff971d6a8b8ff870946c9b3ce5aa173617bfb) reports code-token exposure for PaLM, LaMDA, and Codex. Seventh, make baseline comparisons budget-matched, especially for expensive methods such as Tree-of-Thoughts, by reporting per-instance cost, inference time, solver runtime, and accuracy under equal API or compute budgets, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) evaluated ToT only on a subset due to cost. Finally, include detailed ablations and error analysis for feedback rounds, multiple runs, solved-example counts, and solver versus interpreter components, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports that four feedback rounds improved SymPro-LM by 21.02% and five runs added another 11.41% before saturation, and [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) demonstrates the value of categorizing failures into decomposition, solving, and ground-truth errors.

## Context: This establishes comparison points for SymPro-LM against existing LLM and symbolic-reasoning approaches. Method: We will evaluate four baseline approaches: (1) standard LLM prompting with in-context learning, (2) Program-aided Language Models (PAL), (3) Logic-LM that uses symbolic solvers, and (4) Tree-of-Thoughts prompting.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which directly operationalizes the proposed baseline suite on FCoReBench, a 40-problem first-order combinatorial benchmark with 30 NP-hard problems, 596 training instances, 1354 test instances, and 10 graph-based, 18 grid-based, 5 set-based, 5 real-world, and 2 miscellaneous tasks; it evaluates standard few-shot prompting, PAL, Logic-LM, and Tree-of-Thoughts with GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B, uses two solved examples, problem-specific verification scripts, Z3 for Logic-LM, Python for PAL, temperature 0 for few-shot and Logic-LM, temperature 0.7 for PAL and SymPro-LM, five runs and four feedback rounds, and reports accuracy, cost, inference time, and program error categories; it finds SymPro-LM outperforms few-shot by 54.04%, Logic-LM by 44.86%, and PAL by 16.97%, and shows Logic-LM and few-shot decline sharply with instance size while PAL and SymPro-LM remain more robust; it also finds GPT-4-Turbo far stronger than GPT-3.5-Turbo and Mixtral 8x7B, with Mixtral often worse than random without refinement, supporting model-specific evaluation and refinement ablations. PAL is further supported by [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7), which uses code-davinci-002 with top-1 greedy decoding, temperature 0, in-context examples, interpreter execution, and a no-interpreter control, achieving 72.0 on GSM8K, 96.7 on Object Counting, and large gains on Colored Objects, Penguins, Date, and Repeat Copy, establishing that program-aided execution is a meaningful baseline. Logic-LM is supported by [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd), which uses GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 with temperature 0, 1 to 5 in-context examples, and five datasets including hardest 5-hop PrOntoQA, a 600-example ProofWriter subset, 204-example FOLIO, 300-example LogicalDeduction, and 231-question AR-LSAT; it reports execution rate and executable accuracy, shows Logic-LM outperforms standard prompting on all five datasets, and shows self-refinement increases executable formulation rate by 5.01 points. Solver-aided evaluation is further supported by [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33), which compares SATLM against PROGLM, chain-of-thought, and standard prompting on GSM, GSM-SYS, ALGEBRA, LSAT, BOARDGAMEQA, CLUTRR, PROOFWRITER, COLOR, and STREGEX, uses ablations SAT NOSOLVER, SAT COTSOLVER, and SAT SYMSOLVER, self-consistency decoding, selective accuracy, and bootstrap significance, showing that deterministic solver execution can outperform program execution on several tasks. Standard ICL is supported by [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), which uses 8 fully labeled examples, greedy decoding, 400 test examples per condition across 48 experiments, 95 percent binomial confidence intervals, and controlled hops, ontology direction, and ontology type on linear modus-ponens PrOntoQA, and by [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321), which specifies four in-domain examples and answer post-processing for standard prompting with GPT-3 code-davinci-002 on GSM8K. ProofWriter as an additional dataset is supported by [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1), which defines RuleTaker D0-D5 with 100k questions each, 70/10/20 splits, CWA and OWA variants, strict proof correctness, depth analysis, and iterative proof generation, and by [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) and [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which evaluate on ProofWriter. Prompting configuration and error analysis are supported by [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), which uses eight manually composed exemplars, GPT-3 175B, PaLM 540B, PaLM 62B, and LaMDA 137B, accuracy, and manual analysis of 50 correct and 50 incorrect GSM8K examples, showing that prompting-only baselines must be configured with exemplar choice, scale, and error taxonomy. Finally, [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792) supports the need for controlled input ablations and hard-subset analysis, showing that on ReClor, a 6138-question dataset with 4638 training, 500 validation, and 1000 test examples, older models exploit option-level biases, form a 440-item EASY subset from four-seed agreement, and reach only about 30 percent on HARD items, motivating verification-script-based evaluation and bias controls for FCoReBench.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction to the assumption that existing symbolic-aided LLM baselines will be uniformly weak comes from [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33), where SATLM with a SAT solver outperforms PROGLM, a program-aided baseline analogous to PAL, by 26.1 points on GSM-SYS greedy accuracy, improves LSAT from 30.9 percent to 37.4 percent, improves BOARDGAMEQA from 73.9 percent to 80.7 percent, and reports bootstrap-significant gains over PROGLM where reported, suggesting that solver-aided LLMs can be strong on logical and arithmetic tasks; however, the paper does not report hardware, self-consistency sample counts, computational cost, or inference time, limiting direct transfer to FCoReBench. Next, [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows Logic-LM outperforms standard prompting on all five datasets with GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, and outperforms chain-of-thought on ProofWriter, FOLIO, LogicalDeduction, and AR-LSAT, although GPT-4 chain-of-thought outperforms Logic-LM on PrOntoQA, 98.79 versus 83.20, and execution rate is only 79.9 percent on FOLIO and 32.6 percent on AR-LSAT, indicating that symbolic formulation is not uniformly superior and that solver execution can be a bottleneck. [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) also complicates the hypothesis by showing that trained first-order-style proof generators can reach 99.2 percent answer accuracy and 96.2 percent strict proof accuracy on D5(orig), with Iterative proof generation reaching 97 percent zero-shot proof correctness on Birds-Electricity, although these are templated Datalog theories rather than FCoReBench-style combinatorial instances and no statistical significance tests are reported. The proposed four-baseline suite may also understate prompting-only LLM capability if it omits chain-of-thought and self-consistency: [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) shows chain-of-thought more than doubles GSM8K performance for large models, and [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) shows standard prompting at 17.06 percent versus chain-of-thought at 60.88 percent and least-to-most at 62.39 percent on GSM8K, so a standard-prompting-only baseline may be too weak unless CoT or decomposition is included as an ablation. [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) indicates that Tree-of-Thoughts was evaluated only on a subset because it required specialized prompts and cost per test instance ranged from $0.1235 to $1.676, so a full FCoReBench ToT comparison may be impractical and could leave the baseline suite incomplete; it also shows that on PrOntoQA, PAL and chain-of-thought marginally outperform SymPro-LM, indicating that forward-chaining datasets may favor different baselines and that generalizability claims should be dataset-specific. Finally, older or non-first-order evidence such as [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) uses GPT, BERT, XLNet, RoBERTa, or text-davinci models on multiple-choice or modus-ponens tasks, so their quantitative outcomes cannot be directly transferred to GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Mixtral 8x7B, or first-order combinatorial scaling.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add chain-of-thought and self-consistency decoding as additional baselines or ablations for standard prompting, PAL, Logic-LM, and SymPro-LM, because [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321), and [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) show that CoT, least-to-most, and self-consistency can change rankings and produce large accuracy gains. Second, report execution rate, executable accuracy, solver status, and fallback behavior for Logic-LM and SymPro-LM, following [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) and [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33), including SAT NOSOLVER, SAT COTSOLVER, and SAT SYMSOLVER-style ablations to isolate whether gains come from symbolic execution rather than prompting. Third, adopt the FCoReBench implementation details from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75): two solved examples, temperature 0 for few-shot and Logic-LM and temperature 0.7 for PAL and SymPro-LM, five runs and four feedback rounds, Z3 for symbolic constraints, Python interpreter for programs, problem-specific verification scripts, cost and inference time, and a program error taxonomy separating incorrect programs, semantic errors, runtime errors, and timeouts. Fourth, include statistical significance and uncertainty, using 95 percent binomial confidence intervals as in [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) and bootstrap resampling as in [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33), especially for small test subsets such as ToT, FOLIO, LogicalDeduction, and AR-LSAT. Fifth, manage Tree-of-Thoughts cost by evaluating a stratified subset, reporting cost per instance, and comparing against a fixed budget, as in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), while also reporting accuracy-cost tradeoffs. Sixth, add bias and shortcut controls inspired by [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792), such as input ablations, option or answer-format controls, multiple seeds, and hard-subset analysis, to ensure verification-script accuracy is not confounded by surface features. Seventh, for ProofWriter and PrOntoQA, use depth, ontology type, CWA and OWA, and strict proof metrics from [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), and for PAL include a no-interpreter control and instance-specific versus instance-agnostic variants as in [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7) and [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75).

## Context: This tests the proposed method across a range of model capabilities and scales. Method: We will use three LLMs: GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which directly instantiates the proposed model-capability test on FCoReBench using the exact three LLMs, GPT-4-Turbo (gpt-4-0125-preview), GPT-3.5-Turbo (gpt-3.5-turbo-0125), and Mixtral 8x7B (open-mixtral-8x7b), across 40 first-order problems with 596 training and 1354 test instances, Z3, Python, verification scripts, and SymPro-LM/PAL/Logic-LM/few-shot/ToT baselines; it reports GPT-4-Turbo as hugely better, SymPro-LM improving GPT-4-Turbo by 54.04% over few-shot, 44.86% over refined Logic-LM, and 16.97% over refined PAL, and 100% accuracy on Latin Squares, Magic Square, and Sujiko at 3x3/4x4 with $0.02 per problem versus ToT costs of $0.1235 to $1.676. This directly supports the plan's use of the three models to test whether SymPro-LM depends on frontier-scale proprietary models. The next strongest support is [(Akter et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9ea4ea333eeb8875c3e23637a8dce99038be95c), which benchmarks the same three models under identical prompts and evaluation scripts on MMLU, BIG-Bench Hard, GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDIV, MAWPS, HumanEval, ODEX, FLORES-200, and web-agent tasks, using greedy temperature 0 for most tasks and 5-shot/CoT prompts; it finds GPT-4-Turbo strongest on MMLU, BIG-Bench Hard, and math word problems, GPT-3.5-Turbo generally stronger than Mixtral on reasoning, math, code, and translation, and Mixtral higher than GPT-3.5-Turbo on MMLU overall, supporting the model set and the need for a common protocol. [(Zhu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50257a5a7f9c27835dd5fd774d333109c218f4f9) further supports the exact model set by evaluating GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral-8x7B on FanOutQA with 1,034 top-level questions, 7,305 sub-questions, closed-book, BM25+ open-book, and evidence-provided conditions, showing GPT-4-Turbo strongest in evidence-provided loose accuracy 62.8 and model-judged 41.3, while humans scored 68.5 loose and 45.2 model-judged with p < 0.05, supporting the plan's emphasis on verification and human baselines. Older multi-model work provides weaker but relevant support: [(Sawada et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c29dbfbc17fa190b787a2662d49f08a38c8bd166) shows GPT-4 outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo on ARB text-only items, with GPT-3.5 unparsable on about 25% of Law questions, and motivates the plan's verification scripts, partial credit, and error taxonomy; [(Zhu et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d7f414983eb847c4618489baa44e99b01162f98) shows GPT-4 and GPT-3.5-Turbo on dynamically generated DAG tasks with 500 samples per dataset, three seeds, temperature 0, and relative precision 0.01%, supporting repeated evaluation and complexity-level analysis. Weaker task-specific support comes from [(Iga, and Silaghi, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70ed836bac30f80b039508f482cd2f5f90061bc5), where Mixtral-8x7b-Instruct-v0.1, GPT-3.5-Turbo-0125, and GPT-4o are compared on ontology-anchored triple extraction, and prompt rephrasing improves Mixtral by 14% average, and from [(Ouédraogo et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15d4c3c7ee39c26903544e4098879dd5ca72ca52), where GPT-4, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Mixtral-8x7B, and Mistral-7B are compared on JUnit test generation, with CoT and guided tree-of-thought improving structural correctness but reliability remaining challenging.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method's implicit assumption that the three-model comparison will yield a clean frontier-to-small capability gradient is [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which reports that GPT-4-Turbo is hugely better than GPT-3.5-Turbo and Mixtral 8x7B on FCoReBench, that Mixtral often performs worse than random without refinement, and that Logic-LM performs poorly with smaller LLMs; this contradicts the generalizability of SymPro-LM to smaller/open-weight models and suggests the plan must not claim uniform transfer. The next strongest contradiction is [(Zhu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50257a5a7f9c27835dd5fd774d333109c218f4f9), which uses the exact three models on FanOutQA and shows that closed-book loose accuracy is 47.0 for Mixtral-8x7B, 46.0 for GPT-4-Turbo, and 39.8 for GPT-3.5-Turbo, while open-book GPT-3.5-Turbo drops to 15.5 loose accuracy and GPT-4-Turbo reaches 47.0, contradicting a simple scale ordering and showing that retrieval, context length, and refusal rates (5% for GPT-4-Turbo, 10% for GPT-3.5-Turbo) can invert model rankings. [(Iga, and Silaghi, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70ed836bac30f80b039508f482cd2f5f90061bc5) further contradicts frontier-model superiority by showing GPT-3.5-Turbo surpasses GPT-4o by about 4% on Templates Hard, while prompt rephrasing helps Mixtral-8x7b by 14% and hurts GPT-4o by 33%, indicating prompt sensitivity and task-specific inversions. [(Akter et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9ea4ea333eeb8875c3e23637a8dce99038be95c) contradicts the assumption that GPT-3.5-Turbo is uniformly stronger than Mixtral 8x7B because Mixtral outperforms GPT-3.5-Turbo on MMLU overall, although it remains weaker on many reasoning, math, code, and translation tasks, and it reports no statistical significance tests, weakening inferential claims. Older work contradicts direct LLM solving and self-evaluation: [(Sawada et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c29dbfbc17fa190b787a2662d49f08a38c8bd166) finds GPT-4 still struggles with arithmetic and symbolic manipulation over long contexts, GPT-3.5-Turbo has about 25% unparsable Law answers, and GPT-4 self-evaluation sometimes gives partial credit to solutions outside the rubric, contradicting reliance on LLM self-grading; [(Zhu et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d7f414983eb847c4618489baa44e99b01162f98) finds performance declines sharply from D1 to D4, GPT-3.5-Turbo arithmetic drops 23% with complexity, several smaller models score 0, and GPT-4 struggles with linear equations and abductive logic, contradicting the assumption that current LLMs can solve complex first-order combinatorial instances directly. The weakest contradiction is [(Ouédraogo et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15d4c3c7ee39c26903544e4098879dd5ca72ca52), which qualitatively reports that LLM-generated tests still suffer dependency mismatches, redundant logic, test smells, and hallucination-induced errors, and its lack of exact GPT-4-Turbo identifier, temperature, max tokens, and hardware limits reproducibility of the proposed three-model setup.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should report a complete per-model, per-problem, and per-instance-size accuracy table for GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B on FCoReBench, with exact model identifiers (gpt-4-0125-preview, gpt-3.5-turbo-0125, open-mixtral-8x7b), verification-script accuracy, random-chance baselines, confidence intervals or significance tests, and refinement ablations, because [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) provides only a qualitative per-model comparison and GPT-4-Turbo gains, while [(Akter et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9ea4ea333eeb8875c3e23637a8dce99038be95c) reports no significance tests. ## Context: This diagnoses how problem difficulty, iterative refinement, and repeated sampling affect performance. Method: We will analyze the effect of problem instance size on performance, the impact of feedback rounds and multiple runs, and conduct error analysis to understand failure modes.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which directly instantiates the proposed diagnostic on FCoReBench by varying instance size for sudoku, sujiko, and magic square, using five independent runs, four feedback rounds, verification-script accuracy, and program-level error categories; it reports SymPro-LM outperforming few-shot prompting by 54.04%, Logic-LM by 44.86%, and PAL by 16.97% with refinement, refinement improving performance by 21.02%, five runs adding 11.41%, saturation at seven solved examples, and size curves showing SymPro-LM and PAL relatively robust while Logic-LM and few-shot prompting decline. Next, [(Chen et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7ecb703d810dfb0794b7a168eb5e2a6b381a534) supports the feedback-round and repeated-sampling analysis with MAGICORE on MMLU-Math, MATH, SVAMP, GSM8K, and SAT, showing Llama3-8B-Instruct accuracy 74.2, 75.1, and 75.6 over three iterations versus best-of-120 at 71.0 and Self-Refine at 57.1, GPT-3.5-Turbo accuracy 80.3, 80.6, and 80.9 versus best-of-120 at 77.3, and k=15 outperforming best-of-120, with token and iteration analyses. [(Hupkes et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fbcfbb396f6c0391674c8637a373b729e5e531) supports structural difficulty analysis by decomposing PCFG SET accuracy by input length, parse-tree depth, and number of functions, reporting Transformer 0.92, ConvS2S 0.85, and LSTMS2S 0.79, productivity drops to 0.50, 0.31, and 0.30, three runs, and error containment rates of 22%, 6%, and 8%. [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) supports trace-level error analysis and scale effects, with 46% of 50 incorrect LaMDA 137B GSM8K traces almost correct and 54% major errors, and chain-of-thought gains appearing only for models around 100B parameters or larger. [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) supports failure-mode taxonomy and step-difficulty analysis, with symbolic errors split into decomposition, twice/thrice, after-as-and, left/right, and copy errors, and GSM8K accuracies from 16.38% zero-shot to 68.61% best chain-of-thought. [(Petruzzellis et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77338785e65aacf4d378034ff9822c00af45506c) supports controlled difficulty and error incidence by nesting depth, operands, operator, result sign, and ListOps atomic operation across Llama2 7B, 13B, and 70B and fine-tuned variants. Weakest but still relevant support is [(Srivastava, and Gandhi, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cca77ff5dd95e395ff0fc725982199340f774c6c), which shows decomposition plus up to three human refinement rounds outperformed a no-refinement baseline for GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, Llama2-7B, and Llama3-8B on 250 GSM8K problems, though without numerical accuracy.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the most direct implementation, [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), does not report statistical significance tests, hardware details, or a broad size sweep; its size analysis covers only sudoku, sujiko, and magic square at 3x3 and 4x4, its Tree-of-Thoughts comparison is limited to three problems, and refinement still leaves GPT-4-Turbo SymPro-LM with 38% incorrect programs, 20.5% semantically incorrect programs, 5.5% runtime errors, and 12% timeouts, while PAL refinement can increase timeouts from 1% to 3%, so the proposed diagnostic conclusions are not yet statistically or computationally settled. Next, [(Chen et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c7ecb703d810dfb0794b7a168eb5e2a6b381a534) contradicts direct transfer of its iterative-sampling gains to FCoReBench because it uses math and general reasoning benchmarks, reward models, and no symbolic solvers or instance-size scaling, and it also lacks significance tests. [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) contradicts applying chain-of-thought or refinement uniformly across scales and difficulties: smaller models did not benefit and could perform worse, gains appeared only near 100B parameters or larger, and SingleOp MAWPS gains were negative or very small. [(Zhou et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5437e8adab596d7294124c0e798708e050e25321) contradicts the assumption that decomposition or least-to-most prompting always helps: least-to-most was only moderately higher than chain-of-thought overall and did not improve when test questions did not require more steps than demonstrations, with engineered GSM8K accuracies of 62.39% for one-shot least-to-most versus 68.61% for best chain-of-thought. [(Hupkes et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3fbcfbb396f6c0391674c8637a373b729e5e531) contradicts assuming that a single surface failure mode explains difficulty: early end-of-sequence emission was not the main cause, incorrect outputs were contained in correct outputs in only 22% of LSTMS2S errors, 6% for ConvS2S, and 8% for Transformer, and most manual errors involved function applications to long string inputs, so the proposed error analysis must include composition and chunking failures. [(Srivastava, and Gandhi, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cca77ff5dd95e395ff0fc725982199340f774c6c) contradicts automated, reproducible refinement because it used manual code execution, human-written refinement prompts, only 250 GSM8K problems, no multiple independent runs, no statistical tests, and no exact accuracy percentages. Weakest contradiction is [(Petruzzellis et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77338785e65aacf4d378034ff9822c00af45506c), which is qualitative and limited to single-nesting formulas, and shows nonmonotonic scale effects, with base Llama2 70B sum modulo 10 becoming hardest and deteriorating relative to smaller models, and negative-result arithmetic errors driven by modulo calculations.

Finally, expand baselines beyond the limited Tree-of-Thoughts comparison to include Self-Refine, best-of-k, self-consistency, solver-only, and timeout-aware PAL and Logic-LM variants, and report hardware, seeds, temperatures, and reproducibility details.

## Context: This tests whether SymPro-LM generalizes beyond the first-order benchmark. Method: We will also evaluate SymPro-LM on three additional logical reasoning datasets (LogicalDeduction, ProofWriter, and PrOntoQA) to assess generalizability beyond first-order problems.

### Support

The strongest support is direct evidence from a closely matching implementation of the proposed non-first-order evaluation, which reports that SymPro-LM outperforms all other baselines on ProofWriter and LogicalDeduction, with the largest advantage over Logic-LM, and remains competitive on PrOntoQA where PAL and CoT are marginally better; its error analysis says Logic-LM logical errors decrease when the LLM is prompted to produce programs, and that programming constructs such as dictionaries and loops reduce syntactic translation errors as rules and facts increase, using Z3, Python, GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). The next strongest support comes from Logic-LM, which evaluates per-instance FOL or CSP formulations with an FOL prover or SAT solver on the same three datasets and generally improves over standard prompting and chain-of-thought: PrOntoQA 61.00, 85.00, and 83.20 versus standard 47.40, 51.80, and 77.40 and CoT 67.80, 83.00, and 98.79; ProofWriter 58.33, 71.45, and 79.66 versus standard 35.50, 36.16, and 52.67 and CoT 49.17, 48.33, and 68.11; LogicalDeduction 65.67, 62.00, and 87.63 versus standard 40.00, 41.33, and 71.33 and CoT 42.33, 48.33, and 75.25, with self-refinement raising executable rates such as ProofWriter GPT-3.5 from 87.3 to 95.6 [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd). LAMBADA supports LLM plus symbolic search on the target datasets, with ProofWriter-PD depth-5 label accuracy 0.81 versus CoT 0.54, ProofWriter-PUD depth-5 0.72 versus 0.46, PrOntoQA depth-5 0.96 versus 0.45, proof accuracy 0.94 versus 0.28, and fewer inference calls than selection-inference, 18.53 versus 219.34 at depth 5 [(Kazemi et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03fb95e6be583ca954c3d00812a9e9a40f118e51). PrOntoQA in-context results support the need for symbolic assistance and a controlled evaluation design, because only text-davinci-002 performs better than chance, 5-hop top-down examples fall to chance, and the study uses 48 experiments of 400 examples with 95 percent Wilson confidence intervals [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a). ReClor provides older evidence that conventional LLMs struggle with hard logical reasoning, with about 75 percent accuracy on EASY items but about 30 percent on HARD items, answer-only accuracies of 41.7 to 43.2 versus 25 percent chance, and transfer learning improving HARD accuracy only to about 50 percent [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792). ### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that symbolic pipelines do not uniformly outperform strong LLM chain-of-thought on the target datasets: Logic-LM with GPT-4 scored 83.20 on PrOntoQA while GPT-4 chain-of-thought scored 98.79, and self-refinement improved executable rates by an average of 5.01 but accuracy largely stagnated, with PrOntoQA GPT-3.5 executable-sample accuracy moving from 84.9 to 85.0, ProofWriter GPT-3.5 from 73.6 to 74.1, ProofWriter GPT-4 remaining at 79.6, and LogicalDeduction remaining at 62.0 and 87.6 for GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd). The next strongest contradiction concerns solver choice and executability: Lam et al. show that GPT-4o with Z3 produced 73.50 percent executable correct instances, 17.62 percent parse errors, and 8.06 percent runtime errors, whereas Prover9 produced 93.44 percent executable correct instances, 3.00 percent parse errors, and 0.75 percent runtime errors, and Pyke produced 85.50 percent executable correct instances but 14.36 percent executable incorrect instances, with Pyke competitive only on PrOntoQA and unable to solve FOLIO [(Lam et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e5f6be9f94c4b72e07c27eef567b371cdbd70e3d). A direct but weaker contradiction is that the closest SymPro-LM adaptation reports PAL and CoT marginally better than SymPro-LM on PrOntoQA, so the method is not uniformly superior across all three datasets [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75). Older ProofWriter work contradicts the assumption that LLM-generated formal or proof artifacts will generalize smoothly to complex theories, because all-at-once and iterative models degrade on open-world and complex settings, with depth-5 proof correctness 86.4, lower OWA performance, depth-5 abduction recall of 36.84 on D3-Ab and 78.13 on D5-Ab, and complex Electricity abduction at 64 F1 and 62 accuracy [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1). Semantic parsing work contradicts the expectation that larger LLMs and more complex output formats will generalize, because in-context learning was worse than a smaller fine-tuned model on most splits, original complex formats hurt all cases, COGS entity-type confusion caused a 29 percent absolute accuracy loss, CFQ had more than 60 percent missing-conjunct errors, and T5-11B overfit cross-domain content [(Qiu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e10343767ab09dde83cf99ea3442907402a9810). Theorem-proving work provides a weaker contradiction to neural sequence generalization, because transformer agents deteriorated as test problems became more complex while GNN agents showed no obvious trend, although no exact success rates were provided [(Wu et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/016863a86189c4e8ccecf9a36c4406c439a8a84c). PrOntoQA in-context results also pose a risk to SymPro-LM if the LLM parsing stage is unreliable, because only text-davinci-002 performed better than chance and 5-hop top-down examples fell to chance, meaning solver access cannot compensate for incorrect fact or rule extraction [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a). ReClor provides the weakest contradiction to the broader claim that LLMs can solve hard logical reasoning, because even after transfer learning the best HARD-set accuracy remained around 50 percent, below graduate students and far below the 100 percent ceiling [(Yu et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65723a96ff5b15f124bf3b534503950b537b4792).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the generalizability evaluation directly comparable to the strongest existing baselines by using the same dataset subsets, models, and metrics: evaluate ProofWriter open-world depth-5, PrOntoQA hardest 5-hop or fictional-character subsets, and LogicalDeduction full 300-example test set against standard prompting, CoT, PAL, Logic-LM, and if feasible LAMBADA, reporting accuracy, executable rate, proof accuracy, label accuracy, inference calls, and cost for GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) [(Kazemi et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03fb95e6be583ca954c3d00812a9e9a40f118e51) [(Lam et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e5f6be9f94c4b72e07c27eef567b371cdbd70e3d). The next most important action is to ablate the symbolic backend rather than assuming Z3 is optimal, comparing Z3, Prover9, Pyke, or SAT solvers with solver-specific prompts and a parse-error, execution-error, and runtime-exception taxonomy, because Prover9 achieved 93.44 percent executable correct instances for GPT-4o versus 73.50 for Z3 and SAT-aided declarative formulas improved logical tasks [(Lam et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e5f6be9f94c4b72e07c27eef567b371cdbd70e3d) [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) [(Moura, and Bjørner, 2008-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3960dda299e0f8615a7db675b8e6905b375ecf8a). For PrOntoQA, the evaluation should use controlled variables and proof-step metrics, including 1, 3, and 5 hops, top-down versus bottom-up ordering, fictional, true, and false ontologies, 400 examples per condition, 95 percent Wilson confidence intervals, strict, skip, broad, and valid proof accuracy, and label accuracy, plus out-of-demonstration deduction rules to test unseen generalization [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) [(Saparov et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c58325547156a70cb27c148e5b57738ca9ce79aa). The refinement mechanism should be ablated separately from program generation, tracking executable rate, executable-sample accuracy, logical-error rate, and feedback-round stagnation, because self-refinement improved executable rates but not accuracy in Logic-LM [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd). Sampling or self-consistency should be tested for program and parse generation, with multiple programs or parses and majority voting on the final answer or solver output, because majority voting improved Minerva models and self-consistency was used in SAT-aided evaluation, but the cost and k value must be reported [(Lewkowycz et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab0e3d3e4d42369de5933a3b4c237780b41c0d77) [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33). The output format should be simplified and typed, using a constrained JSON or typed Z3 API, variable-free or reversible intermediate representations where possible, and syntax-error analysis, because complex original formats hurt semantic parsing and caused missing-conjunct or entity-type errors [(Qiu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e10343767ab09dde83cf99ea3442907402a9810). The plan should include contamination and out-of-distribution checks, such as fictional entity names, modified instances, unseen deduction rules, and BLEU or overlap checks where appropriate, following Minerva memorization checks and PrOntoQA-OOD design [(Lewkowycz et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab0e3d3e4d42369de5933a3b4c237780b41c0d77) [(Saparov et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c58325547156a70cb27c148e5b57738ca9ce79aa). Computational resources, inference calls, latency, and API cost should be reported, because LAMBADA reports average inference calls and Lam et al. report instance-level executable outcomes, making cost a key practical metric [(Kazemi et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03fb95e6be583ca954c3d00812a9e9a40f118e51) [(Lam et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e5f6be9f94c4b72e07c27eef567b371cdbd70e3d). Statistical significance should be added, using bootstrap tests or Wilson confidence intervals and multiple seeds, because Ye et al. report p less than 0.05 and Saparov et al. report 95 percent Wilson intervals [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a). Finally, iterative proof generation or backward-chaining baselines should be considered as ablations or comparisons, because ProofWriter iterative proof generation and LAMBADA backward chaining show that proof structure and rule selection matter beyond one-shot program generation [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) [(Kazemi et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03fb95e6be583ca954c3d00812a9e9a40f118e51).

## Context: This adapts SymPro-LM to datasets where a single instance-agnostic program is not assumed to exist. Method: For these non-first-order datasets, we will generate separate programs for each test instance since no single program can solve all problems.

### Support

The strongest support is direct: [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) implements the same adaptation, generating a new Python/Z3 program for each ProofWriter, LogicalDeduction, and PrOntoQA instance with GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B, and reports that this per-instance SymPro-LM outperforms all baselines on ProofWriter and LogicalDeduction while remaining competitive on PrOntoQA, which directly validates the plan's non-first-order generalizability experiment. A second strong line of support is that per-instance program generation plus an interpreter is a proven mechanism: [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7) shows PAL with code-davinci-002, greedy decoding, and an external Python interpreter reaches 72.0 on GSM8K versus 23.2 when the generated Python is not executed, and improves over chain-of-thought by 8.8, 14.1, 11.4, 23.7, and 21.8 points on colored objects, penguins, date, object counting, and repeat copy, supporting the plan's inclusion of PAL as a baseline and the necessity of execution. Third, per-instance symbolic artifacts with solvers help on the exact non-first-order datasets in the plan: [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) reports Logic-LM accuracy of 58.33, 71.45, and 79.66 on ProofWriter for ChatGPT, GPT-3.5, and GPT-4, improving over chain-of-thought by 9.16, 23.12, and 11.55 points, and 65.67, 62.00, and 87.63 on LogicalDeduction, improving over chain-of-thought by 23.34, 13.67, and 12.38 points, with temperature 0 and 1-5 examples. Fourth, per-instance derivation is beneficial on ProofWriter-style tasks: [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) obtains 97% full-accuracy proof correctness zero-shot on Birds-Electricity versus 84.5% all-at-once and 80.5% PRover, and high implication-enumeration F1 on D3/D5 from 100k-example D* datasets with proof depths up to 5, supporting instance-specific derivation rather than one fixed answer string. Fifth, symbolic constraint handling is needed for fixed natural-language puzzles: [(Zhong et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c6f5ffd4d2d43ae7684601f607eae26a759a5a) shows AR-LSAT, with 2,046 five-option questions and a 1,585/231/230 split, requires extracting participants, positions, facts, and rules into constraints, while fine-tuned RoBERTa reaches only 23.1% test accuracy, motivating per-instance symbolic encoding even though ARM uses a shared constraint-function library rather than a generated program. Finally, [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) supports using PrOntoQA as a controlled non-first-order testbed and using proof-step validity metrics, because its 8-shot in-context evaluation with 400 examples per variable and 48 experiments isolates hops, traversal direction, and ontology type.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that a declarative per-instance solver specification can outperform program generation: [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) reports SATLM improves LSAT from 30.9% to 37.4% and BOARDGAMEQA from 73.9% to 80.7%, and on CLUTRR achieves 68.3% versus 58.9% for PROGLM, with selective accuracy 89.9% versus 73.7% and p<0.05 under greedy or self-consistency decoding with code-davinci-002, implying that the plan should not assume generated programs are the best per-instance symbolic interface. A second strong contradiction is that instance-specific code can be brittle and can underperform a reusable task-level prompt: [(Chae et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/505013ca939bf7e6138b38c58a24d80726c669ee) finds Zero-shot PoT achieves only 0.4% on Dyck Languages and Temporal Sequences with GPT-3.5-Turbo in zero-shot settings without demonstrations, while THINK-AND-EXECUTE outperforms PoT and direct prompting on all seven Big-Bench Hard tasks and outperforms Chain-of-Code by 32.3 points, suggesting that per-instance program generation is not automatically superior when a task-level pseudocode template can be reused. Third, per-instance symbolic methods do not dominate strong LLM chain-of-thought on all non-first-order datasets: [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) reports Logic-LM at 83.20 on the PrOntoQA hardest fictional 5-hop subset with GPT-4 versus 98.79 for chain-of-thought, and self-refinement can reduce execution accuracy on FOLIO from 80.4 to 79.9 and AR-LSAT from 60.0 to 58.8, so the plan's refinement and solver assumptions need empirical validation on the 600-example ProofWriter depth-5, 300-example LogicalDeduction, and 231-example AR-LSAT settings. Fourth, LLM-generated reasoning can be locally invalid even when labels look plausible: [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a) finds only text-davinci-002 performs better than chance on PrOntoQA, 5-hop top-down accuracy falls to chance, and label accuracy is poorly correlated with strict or broad proof accuracy, contradicting the assumption that per-instance programs will automatically produce valid derivations without formal step-level checking. Fifth, iterative per-instance derivation is not uniformly better than all-at-once generation: [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) reports all-at-once D5-trained proof correctness of 97.2% CWA and 98.0% OWA versus 95.4% and 97.6% for iterative trained only on D0-D3, so the plan should not overstate the benefit of per-instance iterative construction. Finally, [(Zhong et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c6f5ffd4d2d43ae7684601f607eae26a759a5a) suggests that a shared constraint-function library can be sufficient for AR-LSAT and that major errors come from extraction and missing commonsense, which may not be solved merely by generating a separate program for each instance.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a broader per-instance symbolic baseline suite and report task-level comparisons: compare SymPro-LM against PAL, Logic-LM, chain-of-thought, Tree-of-Thoughts, self-consistency, declarative SATLM-style SAT/CSP/FOL specifications, and task-level pseudocode such as THINK-AND-EXECUTE on ProofWriter, LogicalDeduction, PrOntoQA, and optionally AR-LSAT, because [(Ye et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f27f6d1d521d189e78f5623098ced0deea613d33) shows SATLM can beat PROGLM, [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) shows GPT-4 CoT can beat Logic-LM on PrOntoQA, and [(Chae et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/505013ca939bf7e6138b38c58a24d80726c669ee) shows reusable task-level pseudocode can beat instance-specific code. Second, evaluate not only final accuracy but executable rate, execution accuracy, proof correctness, selective accuracy, and PRONTOQA-style strict, skip, broad, and valid proof-step metrics, as in [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd) and [(Saparov, and He, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7028cd7ea838ab8294ecf26d5a2c0dbb8cfa81a), because label accuracy can mask invalid reasoning. Third, refine the program-generation loop with both syntactic and semantic feedback: use solver error messages, failed unit tests, and small per-instance test cases, but monitor for the negative accuracy effects of self-refinement reported by [(Pan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e9e4df2996bac794c4f04cb887df3e553bae4fd), and specify temperature, decoding, maximum rounds, and multiple independent runs, since [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) leaves non-first-order sampling details unclear and many related works omit confidence intervals. Fourth, consider a hybrid architecture that uses a shared task-level pseudocode or constraint template, as in [(Chae et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/505013ca939bf7e6138b38c58a24d80726c669ee) and [(Zhong et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c6f5ffd4d2d43ae7684601f607eae26a759a5a), while still allowing instance-specific parameter extraction and solver calls, to reduce code-generation variance and address extraction/commonsense failure modes. Fifth, report computational cost, inference time, solver runtime, number of generated programs, and dataset sizes, using the plan's GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, and Mixtral 8x7B with Z3/Python, because per-instance generation is more expensive than a single instance-agnostic program and prior work such as [(Gao et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1e1cc1e0e1f8fd026fe517607b2d4535565fa7) and [(Tafjord et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87c45a908537ffe1d2ab71a5d609bd7b4efa4fe1) does not fully specify hardware or cost.

## Context: This assesses practical deployment constraints of the proposed approach. Method: We will also analyze computational costs and inference times to understand the practical implications of our approach.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed computational-cost and inference-time analysis is direct evidence from [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75), which evaluates SymPro-LM against Tree-of-Thoughts on FCoReBench-style problems including Latin Squares, Magic Square, and Sujiko at 3x3 and 4x4 sizes. That work reports 100% accuracy for SymPro-LM on the listed instances, whereas Tree-of-Thoughts accuracy ranges from 46.33% on 3x3 Latin Squares to 0% on 4x4 Sujiko, and it reports per-instance Tree-of-Thoughts costs of $0.1235, $0.5135, $0.4325, $0.881, $0.572, and $1.676 versus a one-time SymPro-LM cost of $0.02 per problem. This directly supports the research plan’s need to distinguish one-time program-generation cost from per-instance inference cost, and it supports the hypothesis that SymPro-LM can be more cost-effective than search-based LLM prompting. The next strongest support comes from [(Palikareva, and Cadar, 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230ba662b4e2d59a68cba3a2aad9301b62ba7c5e), which demonstrates how to measure practical deployment constraints in a solver-integrated system using 12 GNU Coreutils 6.10 benchmarks, KLEE, STP, Boolector, and Z3, with controlled one-hour runs, recorded instruction counts, 30-second query timeouts, 2 GB memory limits, depth-first search, disabled ASLR, and deterministic memory allocation. Its measurements of instruction throughput, query throughput, solver time, cache misses, and solver timeouts are highly relevant to the proposed plan because SymPro-LM also depends on Z3 and a Python interpreter, and solver or interpreter overhead may dominate end-to-end inference time. Supporting evidence also comes from [(Zankl, and Korp, 2014-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b662e283e60a1bb1f695bf38aa56f43b638f9feb), which evaluates computational cost and runtime for proving polynomial complexity bounds on 2132 term rewriting systems using a 60-second timeout, parallel criteria, bound tightening, and hardware with eight dual-core AMD Opteron 885 processors and 64 GB RAM; this supports measuring proof-search or solver-search cost as a first-class experimental variable. Additional support is provided by [(Rustenholz et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf872bf95bc90262db80fc9b397590b596a0d18), which measures solving time for recurrence benchmarks on a weak laptop with a 1.1 GHz Intel Celeron N4500 CPU and 4 GB RAM, reporting lasso regression times of 1.4s to 8.4s with a mean of 2.5s and symbolic regression times of 56s to 133s with a mean of 66s, thereby supporting the need to report timeouts, hardware, and solver-specific runtime behavior. Weaker but still useful support comes from [(Kosarev, and Boulytchev, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11043b0ebaa3923e39d3e1010fd8f87bda5552e), which uses controlled benchmarking with ten natively compiled runs, average user time, an Intel Core i7-4790K CPU, 16 GB memory, OCaml 4.04.0, and Chez Scheme 9.4.1, supporting the plan’s need to report hardware, implementation, and runtime conditions. Finally, [(Strubell et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6a083dad7114f3a39adc65c09bfbb6cf3fee9ea) and [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2) provide weaker but relevant support: the former offers a template for measuring computational resource use through GPU/CPU power sampling, PUE, and emissions conversion, while the latter qualitatively supports the claim that search-augmented LLM reasoning increases inference-time computational cost, which is directly relevant to the Tree-of-Thoughts baseline in the research plan.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly relevant evidence is still too limited to support broad conclusions about the practical cost of SymPro-LM on FCoReBench. [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) reports only 3x3 and 4x4 instances for three problems, uses GPT-4-Turbo, does not report statistical significance for the cost comparison, and does not provide the numerical results of its additional inference-time analysis in the supplied excerpt. Its one-time $0.02 SymPro-LM cost may also understate total cost if refinement requires multiple feedback rounds, failed programs, repeated solver calls, or larger instances, so the proposed method cannot assume that the cost advantage will generalize across all 40 FCoReBench problems, all three LLMs, and larger instance sizes. A second important contradiction comes from [(Palikareva, and Cadar, 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230ba662b4e2d59a68cba3a2aad9301b62ba7c5e), which shows that solver-integrated systems can have highly benchmark-dependent overhead: caching improved dircolors from 8,090 to 1,091,795 instructions per second but degraded mkfifo from 25,331 to 3,895 instructions per second, and for 9 of 12 benchmarks more than 98% of time was spent solving queries. This contradicts any simplistic analysis that treats LLM inference time or nominal solver availability as the main cost, because Z3 query volume, solver time, caching behavior, timeouts, and nondeterminism can materially change the practical cost of SymPro-LM. Third, [(Zankl, and Korp, 2014-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b662e283e60a1bb1f695bf38aa56f43b638f9feb) contradicts the assumption that more modular or refined symbolic reasoning is automatically cheaper: its modular setting proved more polynomial bounds but was slower because it typically required more proofs to succeed, implying that SymPro-LM’s refinement mechanism may increase total cost even when it improves accuracy. Fourth, [(Rustenholz et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf872bf95bc90262db80fc9b397590b596a0d18) warns that program-synthesis or symbolic-regression components can be expensive, with symbolic regression taking 56s to 133s per subdomain and requiring timeouts, which contradicts the assumption that generating and refining instance-agnostic programs will always be low-cost. Fifth, [(Kosarev, and Boulytchev, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11043b0ebaa3923e39d3e1010fd8f87bda5552e) shows that performance can depend strongly on implementation details such as tagless representation and language runtime, so cost results from one Python/Z3/LLM stack may not transfer to another without controlled benchmarking. Sixth, [(Strubell et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6a083dad7114f3a39adc65c09bfbb6cf3fee9ea) is less directly contradictory but limits the evidence because it focuses on training energy rather than inference latency, token cost, or solver interaction, so it cannot by itself justify the proposed inference-time analysis. Finally, [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2) provides only a qualitative claim that greater search exploration increases inference-time cost, with no quantitative outcomes, dataset sizes, hardware specifications, or statistical significance, so it cannot serve as strong evidence for specific cost estimates in FCoReBench.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to define a precise cost-accounting protocol that separates one-time SymPro-LM program-generation and refinement costs from per-instance inference costs and from baseline per-instance LLM costs. For each of the 40 FCoReBench problems, the plan should report LLM API calls, input/output tokens, monetary cost, wall-clock time, number of feedback rounds, number of solved examples used, number of failed refinement attempts, and whether the final program is instance-agnostic, while also reporting per-instance program execution time, Z3 solving time, output-formatting time, and total end-to-end latency; this extends the direct but limited cost comparison in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) to the full benchmark, all LLMs, and larger instance sizes. The second most important action is to instrument solver and runtime behavior with the same granularity as [(Palikareva, and Cadar, 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/230ba662b4e2d59a68cba3a2aad9301b62ba7c5e) and [(Kosarev, and Boulytchev, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11043b0ebaa3923e39d3e1010fd8f87bda5552e), including Z3 version, Python version, CPU/GPU/RAM specifications, timeout values, query counts, solver time, memory usage, cache hits/misses if any caching is used, API latency, temperature, max tokens, seeds, retries, and deterministic controls where possible. Third, the plan should add statistical and scaling analyses rather than relying on point estimates: report distributions, confidence intervals, paired comparisons or bootstrap tests where appropriate, cost-accuracy Pareto curves, per-instance and total cost, timeout/failure rates, and performance as instance size increases, thereby addressing the lack of statistical significance and small-size limitation in [(Mittal et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9e5f85bb02f1871a21f38e98a9f6c8db3eed8b75) and the qualitative-only cost claim in [(Yang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f77a6a1d6ca64c81349f696abd3106db8a0467a2). Fourth, the plan should explicitly ablate the cost of refinement and feedback, measuring how total cost changes with the number of feedback rounds, number of solved examples, multiple runs, and solver verification, because [(Zankl, and Korp, 2014-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b662e283e60a1bb1f695bf38aa56f43b638f9feb) and [(Rustenholz et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf872bf95bc90262db80fc9b397590b596a0d18) show that additional proof-search or symbolic-regression steps can substantially increase runtime. Fifth, the plan should report full reproducibility details and, if feasible, optional environmental cost metrics such as energy use or carbon estimates using the measurement template in [(Strubell et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6a083dad7114f3a39adc65c09bfbb6cf3fee9ea), including model versions, API endpoints, solver versions, hardware, timeouts, and any cost caps used for expensive baselines such as Tree-of-Thoughts.
