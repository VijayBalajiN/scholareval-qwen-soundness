## We will develop a benchmark called Linguini based on problems extracted from the International Linguistic Olympiad (IOL), a secondary school competition where participants solve Rosetta Stone-style linguistic puzzles using only their understanding of linguistic concepts. This is the central planned system design, creating the benchmark artifact that all later evaluations will use.

### Support

Strongest support is from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which directly evaluates the Linguini benchmark, compares open-source and proprietary models, uses in-context examples, and reports a performance gap between the best open model, llama-3-70b, and multiple proprietary tiers, with chrF deferred to an appendix; this validates the central benchmark artifact and the planned exact-match/chrF evaluation. This newer result builds on the olympiad-extraction precedent in [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), which converted UKLO puzzle sheets into JSONL rows containing preamble, context, subquestions, and exhaustive acceptable answers, then scored 12 models with exact match and a language-resourcing-adjusted score, yielding 20.8% average exact match, 12.0% adjusted score, Claude Opus 46.3%/28.8%, and best open Mixtral 8x7B 14.2%/6.4%; that evidence supports the feasibility of extracting olympiad problems, using exact match, and observing open/proprietary gaps. The older [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) further supports the IOL/NACLO extraction route by constructing 96 puzzles from 81 languages into 2311 parallel instances and treating exact match as meaningful for unambiguous solutions, although it evaluated PBSMT, FastAlign, and low-resource Transformers rather than LLMs. Finally, [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) supports the language-agnostic, exact-match, extremely low-resource design with 48 newly authored puzzles and 272 questions across 19 languages, human difficulty ratings, and strong performance by GPT-4o, Mistral-8x22B, and Llama-3-70B versus near-zero performance for smaller models, but it is newly authored rather than IOL-extracted.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is from [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4): the language-resourcing-adjusted score averaged 12.0% versus 20.8% exact match, and failures were often attributed to instruction-following or auxiliary-task errors, showing that exact match can conflate linguistic reasoning with format compliance, memorization, and high-resource familiarity; this challenges the proposed language-agnostic assumption that all necessary information in context isolates reasoning. Second, [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) contradicts the assumption that more in-context examples are uniformly beneficial: examples helped llama-3-70b-it but degraded claude-3-opus, possibly because examples introduce unrelated linguistic tokens, so the planned 0-5 few-shot and leave-one-out protocol must be treated as a controlled ablation rather than a guaranteed performance boost. Third, [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) shows that IOL/NACLO sequence-transduction puzzles can be extremely difficult under exact match, with maximum exact match only 3.4%, PBSMT outperforming Transformers, and best Transformer exact match 0.0 in English-to-foreign, suggesting that the sequence-transduction category may be too hard or too sensitive to lexical errors if only exact match is used. Weakest contradiction is from [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), whose newly authored 48-puzzle/272-question benchmark is limited to four problem types, lacks IOL extraction, leave-one-out, script transliteration, and grammar-book integration, and reports no exact per-model accuracies or significance tests in the excerpt; this does not refute Linguini but warns that a narrow or newly authored puzzle set may not capture the full IOL taxonomy and may understate construction risk.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the language-resourcing-adjusted score and resource-level analysis from [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4): record speaker count and online presence for the 75 low-resource languages, report exact match alongside an adjusted score, and use no-context prompting plus same-format/same-language exclusion to separate reasoning from memorization. Second, implement Bean-style exhaustive acceptable answers and strict normalization for exact match, with chrF as a secondary softer metric, and add subquestion-level scoring plus an error taxonomy for instruction-following, segmentation, and auxiliary-task failures. Third, based on [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), design the 0-5 in-context example protocol as a controlled ablation: use leave-one-out same-format examples, exclude same-language examples, test format-only examples, report per-model few-shot curves, and include confidence intervals or significance tests because examples can hurt some models. Fourth, expand and validate the IOL taxonomy beyond the translation/matching sequence-transduction focus of [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) to include fill-in-blanks and number transliteration, using human difficulty ratings and Olympiad solution files as in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), and include a small newly authored holdout to check contamination and format robustness. Fifth, include non-LLM baselines or difficulty anchors from [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), such as PBSMT, FastAlign, and low-resource Transformers, and report dataset size, prompt templates, hardware, and leakage checks to improve reproducibility.

## We will build a comprehensive taxonomy of IOL problems from 2003 to 2023, excluding instances that appear only once, require images, or have explanation-only responses. We will focus on problems requiring morphosyntactic segmentation, morphosemantic alignment, derivation, morphophonological segmentation, and graphophonemic transcription. This defines the linguistic reasoning task space and filters the source problems into a coherent benchmark scope.

### Support

Strongest method-level support comes from [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), which constructs a machine-readable Olympiad-puzzle benchmark from a public puzzle database by selecting English-language IOL and local competition puzzles with expert solutions, converting two-step matching puzzles into parallel translation pairs using solution files, and evaluating random, FastAlign, phrase-based SMT, and Transformer NMT baselines with exact match as the primary meaningful metric; its 96 puzzles, 81 languages, 39 language families, and 2311 parallel instances show that IOL-style problems can be operationalized into a coherent, language-agnostic benchmark, although it covers only translation and matching. Read iteratively, this 2020 work is followed by [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which evaluates an IOL-derived linguistic reasoning benchmark with instructions, context, problems, and varying in-context examples, using exact match and chrF and a no-context ablation, directly supporting the proposed language-agnostic IOL task space and the plan's open/proprietary model comparison, including a reported gap between proprietary tiers and the best open model llama-3-70b. [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) further supports the low-resource, single-answer, exact-match design with MODELING, a 48-problem, 272-question IOL-style Rosetta Stone benchmark over 19 extremely low-resource languages, difficulty-rated by two IOL medalists and tested under leakage control without target/reference pairs, although its four typological categories are narrower than the proposed five. [(Laverghetta et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3177b63f5d6ce3aa2e172eb5c94412fdaf3be5fd) supports the filtering and diagnostic-validation logic by filtering GLUE broad coverage diagnostic items to single-sub-category questions, leaving 811 items across 20 sub-categories with at least 15 per sub-category, and using classical test theory and Rasch IRT to compare 27 humans, 240 neural models, and a random baseline, showing that transformer models often tracked human item difficulty more strongly than LSTM or random baselines. [(Chen, and Gao, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32c6607346e0bbe21844275f55fb368bbffd4699) supports the curation and ontology approach by selecting 36 English NLI/NLU datasets that target specific linguistic phenomena, requiring public availability, excluding domain-specific knowledge, and recasting them into a unified premise-hypothesis format. Finally, [(Pinter et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03826718c8c47d8f1f25e437fd6ff15165162e8c) supports the morphosemantic alignment and segmentation focus by annotating 142 blends, 121 transparent compounds, and 49 opaque compounds with constituent bases, character spans, and semantic relations, with 7% first-round disagreement resolved using originating context, although it is English-specific and does not evaluate models.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is to the assumption that the proposed filtered IOL task space will yield a stable exact-match signal: [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) reports maximum exact match of only 3.4% for phrase-based SMT in foreign-to-English and 3.0% in English-to-foreign, while character-based metrics such as CharacTER and ChrF can be inflated by morpheme overlap, and the benchmark covers only translation and two-step matching rather than derivation, morphophonological segmentation, or graphophonemic transcription. Next, [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) contradicts a simple 0-5 in-context protocol by showing mixed effects of in-context examples, with llama-3-70b-it benefiting while claude-3-opus degraded as examples increased, and it omits problem count, language count, dataset size, hardware, and exact prompt hyperparameters, creating a reproducibility gap for the proposed benchmark. [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) contradicts the proposed historical extraction approach by using newly authored puzzles rather than 2003-2023 IOL problems, and its four categories omit the proposed morphosyntactic segmentation, derivation, morphophonological segmentation, and graphophonemic transcription; its small category counts, such as 5 possessive problems/26 questions and 5 semantic problems/32 questions, also show the risk of underrepresented subtasks. [(Laverghetta et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3177b63f5d6ce3aa2e172eb5c94412fdaf3be5fd) contradicts a purely content-driven taxonomy if category selection is not psychometrically validated, because it selected seven sub-categories based on transformer performance improvement after pre-training and finetuning, and its English NLI, finetuned-model setting is not directly transferable to low-resource IOL. [(Chen, and Gao, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32c6607346e0bbe21844275f55fb368bbffd4699) is less directly contradictory but limits transferability by being English-only NLI, using MCC/accuracy rather than exact match, and not addressing low-resource language-agnostic puzzles. Finally, [(Pinter et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03826718c8c47d8f1f25e437fd6ff15165162e8c) provides no downstream model evaluation, no statistical significance, and is English-specific, so it cannot support the proposed language-agnostic IOL taxonomy beyond annotation practice.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed taxonomy should be built with an explicit, reproducible annotation protocol: define operational criteria for morphosyntactic segmentation, morphosemantic alignment, derivation, morphophonological segmentation, and graphophonemic transcription, organize them into an explicit ontology as in [(Chen, and Gao, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32c6607346e0bbe21844275f55fb368bbffd4699), exclude one-off, image-dependent, and explanation-only items only after documenting counts by year, language, and category, and report inter-annotator agreement, as [(Pinter et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03826718c8c47d8f1f25e437fd6ff15165162e8c) did with 7% disagreement resolved by context, and [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) did by using solution files to align shuffled sentences into parallel pairs. Second, validate the taxonomy psychometrically rather than by model performance alone: use classical test theory and Rasch IRT as in [(Laverghetta et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3177b63f5d6ce3aa2e172eb5c94412fdaf3be5fd), ensure a minimum number of items per category, and report item difficulty, discrimination, and human expert ratings, following the IOL-medalist difficulty ratings in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02). Third, balance category coverage and avoid underrepresented subtasks: report the number of problems and questions per category, as MODELING's 41% noun-adjective, 37% word-order, 10% possessive, and 12% semantic distribution shows, and add or downweight categories that have too few 2003-2023 instances. Fourth, keep exact match as primary but add chrF and normalized/partial-credit metrics, and explicitly analyze character-metric inflation from morpheme overlap, as shown by [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) and the exact-match/chrF setup in [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960). Fifth, design the in-context protocol to control for format and language leakage: use leave-one-out examples of the same format but different language, include no-context and format-only conditions, and test whether examples help or hurt as in [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) and the no target/reference leakage condition in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02). Finally, report full reproducibility details, including dataset size, language list, prompt templates, model versions, hardware, seeds, and evaluation code, because [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) and [(Chen, and Gao, 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32c6607346e0bbe21844275f55fb368bbffd4699) leave key implementation details unspecified.

## We will compile problems across 75 mostly extremely low-resource languages, organizing them into three categories: sequence transduction (translation and matching tasks), fill-in-blanks (morphophonological derivation), and number transliteration (digit/text conversion). This constructs the actual benchmark dataset and ensures coverage of low-resource languages and distinct task formats.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which evaluates the Linguini benchmark with exact match as primary and chrF as secondary, 0-5 in-context examples, no-context and character-wise substitution ablations, directly matching the proposed dataset's language-agnostic IOL design and evaluation plan. Next, [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) shows IOL-derived puzzles can be parsed into JSONL with exhaustive acceptable answers and scored by exact match, with average exact match 20.8% and ΔNC 12.0%, Claude Opus 46.3% exact match and 28.8% ΔNC, and closed models outperforming open models, supporting the feasibility of Olympiad-style benchmarking and the need for contamination-aware scoring. [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) provides a Rosetta Stone-style ELR benchmark with 48 puzzles, 19 extremely low-resource languages, 272 questions, exact match, prompting conditions, and expert difficulty ratings, supporting zero/few-shot linguistic reasoning and difficulty alignment. [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) directly supports the sequence-transduction category by converting IOL translation and matching puzzles into 2311 parallel instances across 81 languages, using Moses SMT, small transformers, BPE, and exact match as the most meaningful metric, though maximum exact match was only 3.4%. [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96) supports low-resource transduction and transliteration benchmarking with an 8-hour annotation budget, TPU training, chrF and character error rate, showing ByT5-base 71.4 chrF for translation and 14.6 CER for transliteration. [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8), [(Dabre et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f17b380a684d369850533c423c83f20a3fc3f3f0), [(Kumar et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc62f2b13cc3d1384e8c4a85dc4fc8201ce1d5a6), and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507) provide weaker but relevant support: [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8) used 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs, Bible parallel data, and resource-efficient IndoBART; [(Dabre et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f17b380a684d369850533c423c83f20a3fc3f3f0) used 450 million sentences and 9 billion tokens with Devanagari script mapping; [(Kumar et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc62f2b13cc3d1384e8c4a85dc4fc8201ce1d5a6) used 11 Indic languages, 244M IndicBART and 300M mT5, and human evaluation; and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507) covered 101 languages with 3001 translation sentences and 70 languages across 16 generative datasets. Read iteratively, the field moves from early IOL translation benchmarking to low-resource multilingual transduction, then to under-represented-language transduction, and finally to IOL LLM benchmarks, making the proposed 75-language, three-category benchmark a plausible extension.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that none of the related works directly validates the proposed fill-in-blanks morphophonological derivation or number transliteration categories; [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96) only offers autocomplete as a prefix completion analogue, and [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) covers translation/matching only, so the novel categories remain under-evidenced. A second major concern is that exact match can be overly strict and character-level metrics can overestimate puzzle performance: [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) found maximum exact match 3.4% and simple baselines competitive on character metrics, while [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8) found automatic metrics sometimes contradict human judgments on Sundanese-Indonesian and Javanese-Indonesian translation. Third, resourcefulness and memorization can confound language-agnostic reasoning: [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) introduced ΔNC because memorization reduced performance, and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507) showed high-resource languages substantially outperform low-resource ones, so the proposed 75-language coverage must control for resource level. Fourth, in-context examples are not uniformly beneficial: [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) reports llama-3-70b-it improved with examples while claude-3-opus degraded, contradicting a simple assumption that 0-5 examples monotonically help. Fifth, script transliteration effects may not generalize: [(Dabre et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f17b380a684d369850533c423c83f20a3fc3f3f0) studied related Indic scripts and Devanagari mapping, not 75 unrelated ELR languages, and does not specify hardware or exact model sizes. Sixth, supervised fine-tuned baselines often outperform generative LLMs in low-resource settings, as in [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8), where pretrained models outperform Scratch and mBART LARGE reaches 31.45, [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96), where fine-tuned ByT5 and mT5 outperform 5-shot Flan-PaLM on translation and transliteration, and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507), where fine-tuned task-specific models often outperform generative LLMs, so zero-shot LLM scores may reflect benchmark difficulty rather than pure reasoning. Finally, several studies have reproducibility gaps: [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) lacks per-model exact-match percentages and statistical significance, [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96) reports no significance tests and has an autocomplete table/text inconsistency, [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) is a smaller UK-based precedent, and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507) does not provide exact scores or hardware details.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add explicit expert validation and a separate validation protocol for fill-in-blanks morphophonological derivation and number transliteration, including multiple acceptable answers, difficulty ratings by IOL medalists, and human spot-checks, because no related work directly supports these categories. Second, adopt a contamination- and resource-adjusted evaluation alongside exact match, such as [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) ΔNC or a speaker-count/online-presence regression, and use no-context prompting as a contamination proxy, because [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507) show memorization and resource level can dominate. Third, retain chrF as secondary but add character error rate for transliteration and human evaluation for a subset, while guarding against character-metric overestimation shown by [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) and [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8). Fourth, standardize dataset construction in JSONL with metadata for language, script, resourcefulness, source year, task category, difficulty, and exhaustive acceptable answers, using validation scripts and deduplication as in [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), and [(Kumar et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc62f2b13cc3d1384e8c4a85dc4fc8201ce1d5a6). Fifth, test in-context example selection carefully, including leave-one-out same-format different-language examples and ablations over 0-5 examples, because [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) shows examples can help or hurt depending on model and token leakage. Sixth, report per-model, per-category, per-language scores with confidence intervals or bootstrap significance tests, plus hardware and hyperparameter details, addressing gaps in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96), and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507). Seventh, include supervised low-resource baselines such as mT5, ByT5, or IndicBART-style fine-tuned models where feasible, to contextualize zero-shot LLM performance, as supported by [(Ruder et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39bca01efce8765f0a5d3a8981bc30d56f196b96), [(Cahyawijaya et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe0b1f6194b490f6bbc41c716a58901c1049ccd8), and [(Sankalp et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53f60a6bfb7e01cc2ca75f1098331340b3773507). Eighth, design the script transliteration ablation to include Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, and Armenian while controlling for script familiarity and tokenization, extending [(Dabre et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f17b380a684d369850533c423c83f20a3fc3f3f0) without overgeneralizing from Indic scripts.

## We will ensure that all information required to solve each linguistic puzzle is provided within the context, eliminating the need for prior knowledge of the tested languages. This implements the language-agnostic design principle that allows the benchmark to test reasoning rather than memorized language knowledge.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed language-agnostic design comes from [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), which directly operationalized the same principle by building an IOL-derived corpus of 96 unique puzzles from 81 languages, 39 language families, two creoles, and two artificial languages, with 2,311 parallel instances split into 1,559 training and 752 test pairs, and explicitly selected self-contained, unambiguous puzzles solvable without prior knowledge of the target language. Its evaluation of random guessing, FastAlign, phrase-based SMT, a standard Transformer, and frozen RoBERTa features showed a maximum average exact match of only 3.4 percent, with phrase-based SMT best at 3.0 exact match English-to-foreign and 3.4 exact match foreign-to-English, supporting the plan's use of exact match for short, unambiguous IOL-style items and the need for a benchmark that isolates meta-linguistic reasoning. The next strongest support is [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), which evaluated 12 models on a UK Linguistics Olympiad JSONL benchmark with preambles, contextual information, subquestions, and exhaustive acceptable answers, reporting 20.8 percent average exact match and 12.0 percent average ΔNC, with Claude Opus at 46.3 percent exact match and 28.8 percent ΔNC and Mixtral 8x7B at 14.2 percent exact match and 6.4 percent ΔNC; this supports the plan's Olympiad-style context, exact-match scoring, and open-versus-proprietary comparison, although it is not IOL-derived and does not explicitly certify full self-containedness. Further support comes from [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), which used 48 newly authored puzzles and 272 questions across 19 extremely low-resource languages, exact-match accuracy, and a no-target/reference data-leakage condition, showing that GPT-4 and large open models such as Mistral-8x22B and Llama-3-70B performed strongly while smaller models were near zero and that difficulty followed human ratings, with possessive and semantic problems harder at 5.0 and 4.54 than noun-adjective and basic word-order at 2.61 and 3.18; this supports the planned no-context ablation and low-resource exact-match evaluation. Additional support is provided by [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which studied in-context examples for IOL-style puzzles, found proprietary tiers outperforming llama-3-70b, and reported mixed ICE effects, including improvement for llama-3-70b-it and degradation for claude-3-opus as examples increased, supporting the plan's 0-5 few-shot design and chrF as a secondary metric. The weakest support is conceptual rather than empirical: [(Littell et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/acbc0f99df7499ffe0099b4c62c6a137d28fdd61) describes puzzle-design patterns in which the puzzle statement supplies the formal machinery, such as finite-state machines, context-free grammars, 4-gram models, and two-dimensional notation, but reports no accuracy, baselines, dataset size, or statistical significance, so it supports the design intuition without providing quantitative validation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the claim that providing all required information in context eliminates prior knowledge is [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), which used Olympiad-style contextual puzzles but still introduced ΔNC to reduce the influence of language resourcefulness and possible memorization, and found that easier problems, often higher-resource, yielded higher exact match and ΔNC scores, with average exact match 20.8 percent versus average ΔNC 12.0 percent; this suggests that contextual Olympiad items may still be confounded by memorized or resource-dependent knowledge, so the proposed method needs explicit self-containedness verification and resource-adjusted analysis rather than assuming context alone is sufficient. The next strongest contradiction is [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which shows that in-context examples can both standardize answer format and introduce tokens from potentially unrelated languages, creating a confound for language-agnostic reasoning, and that ICE effects were mixed, improving some models such as llama-3-70b-it while degrading others such as claude-3-opus as the number of examples increased; this contradicts a simple assumption that adding contextual examples will monotonically improve language-agnostic performance and requires tighter control over example language, script, morphology, and format. A weaker but important contradiction is [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), whose no-target/reference condition demonstrates that models can rely on prior language knowledge and that performance varies sharply by model scale, with smaller models near zero and larger models strong; although this work supports the planned no-context ablation, it also warns that even low-resource or newly authored puzzles may not fully separate reasoning from memorization unless the context-withheld condition is used as a baseline. A still weaker limitation is suggested by [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), where self-contained IOL puzzles produced very low automatic performance, with maximum average exact match 3.4 percent, phrase-based SMT outperforming Transformers, and frozen RoBERTa features worsening English-to-foreign performance; this does not contradict the design principle directly, but it contradicts the assumption that self-contained context will yield easily interpretable model differences without careful metric choice, difficulty calibration, and error analysis. The weakest potential contradiction is [(Littell et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/acbc0f99df7499ffe0099b4c62c6a137d28fdd61), which provides no empirical outcomes, so it cannot demonstrate that self-contained puzzle design reliably prevents prior-knowledge leakage in modern language models.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add an explicit self-containedness audit to the LINGUINI construction pipeline: for every IOL item, require expert annotation that the solution can be derived solely from the provided preamble, context, subquestions, and answer format, using official solutions where available, multiple independent solvers or solvers without target-language knowledge, and documented exclusion of puzzles requiring external knowledge, images, explanation-only responses, or ambiguous references; this directly addresses the gap in [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) and aligns with the stricter selection in [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e). The next most important action is to operationalize the no-context ablation and add a resourcefulness/memorization control, such as a ΔNC-style resourcing-adjusted metric or explicit stratification by speaker count and online presence, comparing exact match and chrF with and without context to quantify how much performance comes from provided context versus training-data knowledge, as motivated by [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4) and [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02). Third, tighten the 0-5 in-context example protocol by excluding not only same-language examples but also same-script, same-lexical-item, same-morphological-paradigm, and unrelated-language-token examples, standardizing answer format, randomizing example order, and reporting example-count curves to detect the mixed ICE effects reported by [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960). Fourth, strengthen evaluation robustness by using exhaustive acceptable answers, a validated JSONL schema, bootstrap confidence intervals or significance tests, item-level difficulty ratings, and manual error analysis, following the exact-match and ΔNC evaluation in [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), the exact-match, BLEU-2, CharacTER, ChrF-3, and Chickasaw error analysis in [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e), and the exact-match difficulty analysis in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02). Fifth, document reproducibility details that prior work only partially reports, including dataset size, splits, preprocessing, prompt templates, model versions and API dates, temperature, seeds, hardware or API provider, and residual parsing errors, as needed to make the benchmark comparable to the 96-puzzle, 2,311-instance setup in [(Şahin et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e0fab543b49b315d29b0d81e8f958707456f69e) and the 12-model Olympiad evaluation in [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4). The least important but still useful action is to incorporate the self-contained design patterns from [(Littell et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/acbc0f99df7499ffe0099b4c62c6a137d28fdd61), such as Broken Machine, Troublemaker, Assembly Required, Black Box, and Combinatorial Problems, when authoring or selecting puzzles, to ensure the context supplies the formal machinery needed for reasoning.

## Zero-shot to few-shot evaluation (0-5 in-context examples) across the complete dataset. This is the primary experimental condition for measuring how model performance changes as contextual examples are provided.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 0-5 in-context evaluation comes from [(Gao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/894ed1aba8e42a4ec27ba53ecde383b14c5128ca), which directly compares 0, 1, 3, and 5 examples and shows non-monotonic accuracy, e.g. Llama-2-13B MedMCQA 37.25, 37.17, 38.90, and 38.94 percent and PubMedQA 55.6, 72.2, 70.0, and 68.2 percent, while BELLE-7B SciQ drops from 92.8, 83.0, 80.7, and 81.7 percent to 79.7, 27.3, 46.5, and 40.2 percent under system meta-instructions; this validates the need for a full 0-5 sweep, controlled example selection, exact-match scoring, and contamination checks in LINGUINI. Next, [(Wan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/717392dac099d1506b766787382d61b277863163) supports the leave-one-out-like design by selecting K=5 demonstrations from other test questions while excluding the current question, using exact-match accuracy, m=7 sampled paths, lambda=0.2, and self-consistency, with GPT-3 average exact-match accuracy of 38.32 for zero-shot chain-of-thought, 46.96 for 5-shot chain-of-thought, and 53.98 for self-adaptive prompting, showing that moving from zero to five contextual examples can improve reasoning, although it uses chain-of-thought demonstrations on arithmetic and logical tasks rather than IOL puzzles. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) provides foundational support for zero/one/few-shot in-context evaluation using GPT-3 models from 125M to 175B with a 2048-token context and K from 0 to 100, and supports LINGUINI's low-resource, language-agnostic motivation because it shows that in-context examples can reveal reasoning but also that benchmark overlap and task format matter; for example, Winograd was 88.3 percent zero-shot, 89.7 percent one-shot, and 88.6 percent few-shot, Winogrande rose from 70.2 to 73.2 to 77.7 percent, and synthetic character manipulation tasks were weak at 66.9, 38.6, 40.2, and 15.1 percent. [(Black et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e37018d3cfab9cfc29a7b78404e6c86ea18a907e) supports the central 0-versus-5 comparison and the use of confidence intervals, reporting that GPT-NeoX-20B, with 20B parameters, 44 layers, hidden dimension 6144, and 64 attention heads, outperforms FairSeq 13B on 22 of 32 evaluations and improves substantially with five examples, although it does not sweep all intermediate counts. [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) supports the proposed emphasis on same-format examples and exact-match scoring by showing that zero-shot chain-of-thought improved MultiArith from 17.7 to 78.7 and GSM8K from 10.4 to 40.7, while task-matched few-shot chain-of-thought reached 39.0 and 88.2 versus 31.9 and 27.0 for cross-task examples on AQUA-RAT and MultiArith. [(Lauscher et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85dc7829455819283270eb643817bcf97133464d) supports the hypothesis that small amounts of target-language information can improve linguistic structure tasks, with gains even below five annotated sentences for dependency parsing and part-of-speech tagging, up to 17 UAS points for dependency parsing and 15 points for part-of-speech tagging with 10 sentences, although it uses fine-tuning rather than in-context examples. [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) supports the broader premise that in-context reasoning demonstrations can change performance, especially for large models and harder tasks, with fixed eight exemplars and GSM8K performance more than doubling for the largest GPT and PaLM models, but its protocol is not a 0-5 sweep. [(Wei et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff0b2681d7b05e16c46dfb71d980cc2f605907cd) supports zero-shot and few-shot instruction prompting across 62 datasets and 12 task clusters, showing that zero-shot FLAN outperformed zero-shot GPT-3 on 20 of 25 datasets and surpassed GPT-3 few-shot performance on 10 datasets, which is relevant to LINGUINI prompt construction. [(Scao et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb15f3727f827a3cb88b5d3ca48415c09b40a88f) supports zero-shot evaluation as informative and motivates reporting the full 0-5 curve because its observed 0.93 zero/few-shot correlation may not hold for extremely low-resource linguistic puzzles; its 1.3B Pile model outperformed GPT-Neo 1.3B but underperformed the 1.3B OpenAI API model by 1.6 percentage points. [(Sinha et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97833e2aa0da5240e62436373b58af988a4ab6ab) supports the need to control prompt position and formatting, because zero-shot and five-shot accuracy on OPT models from 125M to 30B can be sensitive to absolute starting position in the context window. Weaker support comes from [(Johnson et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a486e2839291111bb44fa1f07731ada123539f75), which shows zero-shot multilingual translation can be strong for related languages and that incremental fine-tuning using about 3 percent of total training time recovers much of the direct-data gain, but it uses a GNMT-style encoder-decoder, a 32,000-piece shared vocabulary, and up to 100 GPUs rather than in-context examples; [(Guo et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b88526a0f0337e3a6b632b4af8fd0882eb4b470), which shows five-shot chain-of-thought can change model rankings on a 20 percent FinEval subset, with Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct at 69.4 weighted average and moving to first place in five-shot CoT; [(Cheng et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9a5f82751d8349d5554a5f1a99d7b90a93677e3), which shows a fixed five-shot MMLU condition can reveal differences not visible zero-shot, with MINI-LLM pruned LLaMA-7B at 26.60 percent versus SparseGPT 25.80 percent and LLM-Pruner 25.30 percent; and [(Yang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54fb839f621e3fe787437ab8ca5f37e7e4726bfe), which uses few-shot prompting for base Qwen2 models but provides no quantitative details for the proposed 0-5 condition.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that adding 0-5 in-context examples may not produce a clean, monotonic performance curve. [(Gao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/894ed1aba8e42a4ec27ba53ecde383b14c5128ca) reports non-monotonic accuracy across 0, 1, 3, and 5 examples, with Llama-2-13B MedMCQA at 37.25, 37.17, 38.90, and 38.94 percent, Vicuna-13B at 33.92, 36.58, 37.49, and 35.98 percent, and BELLE-7B SciQ dropping from 92.8, 83.0, 80.7, and 81.7 percent to 79.7, 27.3, 46.5, and 40.2 percent under system meta-instructions, which challenges any assumption that LINGUINI scores will improve smoothly as examples are added. A second major contradiction is that in-context performance can be confounded by prompt position and formatting rather than by example count alone; [(Sinha et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97833e2aa0da5240e62436373b58af988a4ab6ab) shows that zero-shot and five-shot accuracy on OPT models from 125M to 30B can deteriorate or become erratic under non-zero phase shifts, with larger models more affected, so the proposed 0-5 sweep must control absolute prompt position, context length, and answer formatting. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) contradicts a simple reading of in-context gains by showing that examples do not uniformly help: Winograd was 88.3 percent zero-shot, 89.7 percent one-shot, and 88.6 percent few-shot, synthetic character manipulation tasks were weak at 66.9, 38.6, 40.2, and 15.1 percent, and pretraining contamination could affect results, implying that LINGUINI must use low-resource languages, leave-one-out examples, and contamination checks. [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) also complicates the 0-5 design because zero-shot chain-of-thought can outperform cross-task few-shot chain-of-thought, with zero-shot chain-of-thought reaching 78.7 on MultiArith versus 27.0 for cross-task few-shot chain-of-thought, and task-matched few-shot chain-of-thought reaching 88.2, meaning that example relevance and reasoning format may interact with example count. [(Scao et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb15f3727f827a3cb88b5d3ca48415c09b40a88f) provides a weaker contradiction by reporting a 0.93 correlation between zero-shot and few-shot performance across model sizes, which could suggest that a full 0-5 sweep is less necessary, although this correlation may not transfer to language-agnostic IOL puzzles. [(Lauscher et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85dc7829455819283270eb643817bcf97133464d) suggests that small target-language data can be more effective through fine-tuning than through in-context prompting, especially for low-level structured tasks, with average dependency parsing gains of about 30 to 35 points but high-level gains of only about 2.52 to 4.57 points after 1,000 sentence pairs or 10 articles, so the proposed in-context-only primary condition may understate the benefit of language-specific information. [(Johnson et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a486e2839291111bb44fa1f07731ada123539f75) warns that zero-shot performance can be inflated by language relatedness and shared parallel data, with zero-shot models already obtaining relatively high BLEU on Russian-Belarusian and Russian-Ukrainian directions, which could undermine the language-agnostic interpretation if LINGUINI examples or target languages are too similar to training data. [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) contradicts the assumption that in-context examples help all models by showing that smaller models may not benefit from chain-of-thought exemplars and that gains emerge mainly around 100B parameters, so LINGUINI should not overgeneralize from large proprietary models to smaller open-source models. [(Wei et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff0b2681d7b05e16c46dfb71d980cc2f605907cd) shows that template selection and instruction tuning can dominate zero/few-shot comparisons, with zero-shot FLAN outperforming zero-shot GPT-3 on 20 of 25 datasets and surpassing GPT-3 few-shot on 10 datasets, so the proposed method must avoid dev-selected template bias or report template sensitivity. Weaker contradictions or limitations include [(Guo et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b88526a0f0337e3a6b632b4af8fd0882eb4b470), which evaluates only a 20 percent test subset and only zero/five-shot conditions, showing that incomplete evaluation can change rankings; [(Cheng et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9a5f82751d8349d5554a5f1a99d7b90a93677e3), which uses only a fixed five-shot MMLU condition and therefore cannot establish the full 0-5 curve; and [(Yang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54fb839f621e3fe787437ab8ca5f37e7e4726bfe), which provides insufficient methodological detail to support or refute the proposed 0-5 evaluation.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, implement the full 0-5 in-context sweep across the complete LINGUINI dataset with a fixed prompt template, leave-one-out example selection, same-format examples, exclusion of same-language examples, and multiple random seeds, reporting per-example curves for 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 examples rather than only 0 and 5, because [(Gao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/894ed1aba8e42a4ec27ba53ecde383b14c5128ca) shows non-monotonic 0, 1, 3, and 5-shot accuracy, [(Black et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e37018d3cfab9cfc29a7b78404e6c86ea18a907e) shows substantial 0-versus-5 improvements with 95 percent confidence intervals, and [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows task-dependent gains and contamination risk. Second, add explicit prompt-position and formatting controls, including fixed absolute starting position, consistent context-window usage, answer-format constraints, and optionally a phase-shift or template ablation, because [(Sinha et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97833e2aa0da5240e62436373b58af988a4ab6ab) shows OPT zero-shot and five-shot accuracy can be sensitive to absolute starting position and [(Gao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/894ed1aba8e42a4ec27ba53ecde383b14c5128ca) shows system meta-instructions can improve or hurt accuracy. Third, strengthen contamination and language-resource controls by combining the planned no-context prompting, script transliteration, and resource-level analysis with n-gram or token-overlap checks, exclusion of same-language examples, and careful selection of extremely low-resource languages, as motivated by [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), [(Johnson et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a486e2839291111bb44fa1f07731ada123539f75), and [(Lauscher et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85dc7829455819283270eb643817bcf97133464d). Fourth, retain exact match as the primary metric but report chrF as a secondary softer metric for sequence transduction and fill-in-blank outputs, and consider self-consistency or majority voting only as an ablation, because [(Wan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/717392dac099d1506b766787382d61b277863163) uses exact-match accuracy and self-consistency to compare zero-shot, 5-shot, and self-adaptive prompting, and [(Black et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e37018d3cfab9cfc29a7b78404e6c86ea18a907e) reports two-standard-error bars corresponding to 95 percent confidence intervals. Fifth, add targeted baselines and ablations, including zero-shot chain-of-thought, five-shot chain-of-thought, self-adaptive demonstration selection, and optionally a fine-tuning or grammar-book condition, because [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) shows zero-shot chain-of-thought can reach 78.7 on MultiArith, [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) shows chain-of-thought gains are scale-dependent, [(Wan et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/717392dac099d1506b766787382d61b277863163) shows self-adaptive 5-shot prompting can reach 53.98 average exact-match accuracy, and [(Lauscher et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85dc7829455819283270eb643817bcf97133464d) shows fine-tuning can produce large low-level gains. Sixth, report statistical uncertainty and ranking stability, including confidence intervals, significance tests where appropriate, multiple seeds, and per-task as well as aggregate scores, because [(Black et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e37018d3cfab9cfc29a7b78404e6c86ea18a907e) uses error bars and [(Guo et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b88526a0f0337e3a6b632b4af8fd0882eb4b470) shows that five-shot chain-of-thought can change model rankings on a 20 percent subset. Finally, document all implementation details, including prompt templates, example-selection algorithm, random seeds, context length, sampling parameters, hardware or API versions, and dataset sizes, to avoid the reproducibility gaps seen in [(Yang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/54fb839f621e3fe787437ab8ca5f37e7e4726bfe), [(Gao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/894ed1aba8e42a4ec27ba53ecde383b14c5128ca), and [(Scao et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb15f3727f827a3cb88b5d3ca48415c09b40a88f).

## Leave-one-out cross-validation to maximize in-context candidates per task. This evaluation design controls how in-context examples are selected and ensures broad task coverage during few-shot testing.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed leave-one-out cross-validation design is that [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) shows in-context examples are not neutral in Linguini-style linguistic reasoning: llama-3-70b-it improved with in-context examples, while claude-3-opus degraded as the number of examples increased. This directly motivates a controlled candidate-selection procedure rather than arbitrary few-shot prompting, because the proposed method would select in-context candidates to maximize task coverage while controlling format and language contamination. A secondary supporting point is that the same work attributes part of the benefit of examples to setting a useful answer format, which aligns with the research plan's requirement to include same-format examples and use exact match as the primary metric. A weaker but still relevant support is that the related work evaluates open and proprietary models on a language-agnostic linguistic reasoning benchmark and reports a performance gap between the best open model, llama-3-70b, and higher proprietary tiers, matching the proposed model coverage and the need to compare few-shot behavior across model families. The weakest support is that chrF is mentioned as a secondary metric in Appendix E, consistent with the plan's softer metric, although no numerical chrF values are provided.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct refutation of leave-one-out, but a caution against the assumption that maximizing in-context candidates is uniformly beneficial: [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) reports that claude-3-opus degraded as the number of in-context examples increased, suggesting that adding more examples can introduce harmful tokens or distract from the provided context. This contradicts a naive interpretation of the proposed method if maximize in-context candidates per task is implemented as increasing example count without strict relevance, format, and language controls. A second contradiction is that the related work does not explicitly describe a leave-one-out cross-validation selection procedure, so it cannot validate the specific claim that leave-one-out candidate selection improves task coverage or reduces contamination; it only evaluates varying numbers of in-context examples. A weaker contradiction is the absence of exact accuracy values, dataset size, prompt construction details, hardware, hyperparameters, and statistical significance tests, which limits the ability to confirm that the observed model-dependent few-shot effects are robust enough to justify the proposed selection design. As the only provided related work, it also cannot be contrasted with earlier or later work to establish whether leave-one-out selection is an iterative improvement over prior few-shot protocols.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the leave-one-out cross-validation protocol explicit and empirically validated: define a candidate-selection algorithm that holds out one item per task, selects in-context examples from the same task format, excludes same-language examples, and maximizes task coverage using a documented coverage metric such as stratified sampling over task type, language, script, and answer format. This should be compared against random selection, nearest-neighbor selection, task-balanced selection, and zero-shot baselines for 0-5 examples. The next priority is to report exact match and chrF with per-task and per-model breakdowns, confidence intervals or significance tests, and ablations over example count, because [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) shows that few-shot effects differ between llama-3-70b-it and claude-3-opus. A further action is to add contamination controls beyond same-language exclusion, such as lexical overlap filtering, script transliteration, no-context prompting, and resource-level analysis, since the related work attributes degradation to tokens from potentially unrelated languages. Finally, include reproducibility details such as dataset size, prompt templates, decoding settings, API or model versions, hardware, random seeds, and statistical tests so that the leave-one-out design can be independently evaluated.

## Include examples of the same format while excluding same-language examples to avoid contamination. This controls for format familiarity and reduces the chance that performance comes from memorizing the target language.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which evaluates the Linguini benchmark on IOL-derived puzzles across 75 mostly extremely low-resource languages and uses in-context examples to establish expected answer format in sequence transduction, fill-in-blanks, and number transliteration tasks; this directly validates the proposed LINGUINI plan's use of same-format examples and its exact-match primary metric with chrF as a secondary softer metric. The next strongest support is [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), which used a full chain-of-thought condition with a Spanish worked example while evaluating newly written Rosetta Stone puzzles without target-language reference pairs, showing that a same-format, non-target-language example can be used to make the puzzle format familiar while reducing reliance on memorized target-language data. A further supporting signal is [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), whose LINGOLY contamination-aware protocol reports average exact match of 20.8% and average ΔNC of 12.0% across 12 models, with Claude Opus at 46.3% exact match and 28.8% ΔNC and best open Mixtral 8x7B at 14.2% exact match and 6.4% ΔNC, demonstrating that exact match alone can overstate reasoning when language resourcing and memorization are not controlled. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a), which preserves task format while changing surface content through semantic and context reconstruction of 373 unique puzzles into 1,119 samples, using human verification and group-based accuracy, thereby supporting the broader principle that format-preserving surface variation can reduce memorization, although it does not directly test same-language exclusion in in-context examples.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), because in-context examples had a mixed effect in Linguini: some models improved, such as llama-3-70b-it, while others degraded as the number of examples increased, such as claude-3-opus, and the examples could introduce tokens from potentially unrelated languages that hurt instruction-following models; this challenges the assumption that same-format examples, even when same-language examples are excluded, will uniformly improve format familiarity without adding cross-lingual noise. The next strongest contradiction is [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), which found that across minimal, hand-tuned, basic chain-of-thought, and full chain-of-thought with a Spanish example, accuracies were roughly similar, suggesting that the non-target same-format example did not produce a large reported performance shift, and the excerpt provides no exact numeric accuracy or significance test for that condition. A further contradiction to the sufficiency of the proposed method is [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), where the gap between average exact match 20.8% and ΔNC 12.0%, and the much lower open-model ΔNC of 6.4% for Mixtral 8x7B, indicates that memorization and language resourcing can substantially inflate exact match, but the paper does not show that same-format examples or same-language exclusion alone resolve this confound. The weakest contradiction is [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a), which reports only overall ChatGPT accuracy of 53% on word puzzles and 63% on sentence puzzles against 25% random chance and 92% human performance, with about one-third of models at or below random, and does not provide instance-based or group-based accuracy broken down by original, semantic, or context reconstruction, so the specific benefit of format-preserving surface changes cannot be quantified.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to operationalize and audit the same-language exclusion in the LINGUINI few-shot protocol: for each target problem, define language identity using ISO codes, script, and lexical or typological similarity, then select 0-5 leave-one-out examples that match task format (sequence transduction, fill-in-blanks, number transliteration) but exclude the target language and any source problem overlap, while logging example language, script, task, and prompt position; this directly addresses the cross-lingual token risk identified in [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) and the non-target example design in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02). The next most important action is to add a contamination-aware evaluation layer, reporting exact match alongside a language-resourcing-adjusted score analogous to ΔNC from [(Bean et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/299cbb7e4c7e676e81d87d28efb8701df8892ec4), plus chrF, resource-level analysis, and no-context prompting, with confidence intervals and significance tests so that format examples are not mistaken for memorization reduction. A further action is to validate that examples and puzzles preserve the intended linguistic answer through human verification and group-based accuracy, inspired by [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a), by checking that all acceptable answers, subquestions, and distractors remain invariant when examples or surface forms change. Finally, report detailed reproducibility information missing from the related work, including prompt templates, example counts, model versions, hardware or API dates, and per-task, per-language, and per-model-size results, so that the mixed effects observed in [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) and the small reported prompting differences in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) can be tested quantitatively.

## Use exact match accuracy as the primary metric, with chrF as a secondary softer metric. This defines the scoring criteria for the benchmark and provides both strict and character-level evaluation of model outputs.

### Support

The strongest support is direct: [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) evaluates IOL-derived Linguini problems with exact match accuracy as the primary strict metric and chrF as a secondary character-level softer metric, using zero/few-shot prompting, same-format but different-language in-context examples, item-level averaging, and a reported gap between the best open model, identified as llama-3-70b, and proprietary models, which matches the proposed scoring criteria and contamination controls; however, the excerpt lacks numeric exact-match or chrF values, so this is design-level support. The next strongest support for the exact-match primary component is [(Madhani et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b4e59fb2dde89d88c67da499d36385817b1f129), which uses top-1 word-level exact match as the primary metric for Indic transliteration, with top-3, top-5, and F1 as secondary signals on Dakshina and Aksharantar test sets after strict no-word-overlap filtering from a 554k-pair, 19-language dataset split into 451k training and 103k test pairs, directly supporting exact match for Linguini number transliteration and sequence transduction tasks. The next strongest support for the chrF secondary component in low-resource settings is [(Bapna et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66de7ac94dd13ec041659b06041c6ff27b1e55f2), which uses chrF on a 0-100 scale for 38 languages over 1,200 English sentences split into 600 development and 600 test sentences, shows many xx->en languages above 40 chrF, reports that scaling from 1.5B to 6B parameters improved xx->en by 2.7 chrF but reduced en->xx by 1.0 chrF, and shows data filtering improving polluted pairs such as en-pcm from 59.3 to 64.6 chrF and en-kl from 27.8 to 35.8 chrF, supporting chrF as a softer diagnostic for extremely low-resource scripts. The chrF component is further supported by the iterative trajectory from early n-gram precision to character F-scores: [(Popovic, 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/285c165c81fc9275955147a892b9a039ec8b1052) found 6-gram character n-grams strongest, spaces did not help, and recall-weighted CHRF3 with beta = 3 performed best overall on WMT12-14 system-level correlations and document-level win rates, while [(Popovic, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a30e0193889fca9c34cbf4e4e42ac2fe3db9f79) found beta = 2 and beta = 3 comparable and better than beta = 1, with character 3-grams and 4-grams and word unigrams and bigrams performing best, providing a more conservative chrF configuration for a secondary metric. More recent low-resource work continues to support character-level overlap as a useful signal: [(Puduppully et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b6e5855b6a4e425ba251a93516f2bccffe5ba403) uses chrF++ with SacreBLEU and paired bootstrap significance for morphologically rich low-resource FLORES devtest pairs, reporting an average 13.8 chrF++ improvement for DecoMT over Standard Prompting with mT5; [(B et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c0fe24dd7a620419693a4cd74b2d08339d0233d) finds chrF++ the best overlap-based metric for five Indian languages, with system-level Pearson correlations of 0.923 Gujarati, 0.67 Hindi, 0.90 Marathi, 0.895 Malayalam, and 0.756 Tamil on 1,400 MQM-annotated segments per language; and [(Singh et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eed4140dfa751319c2952acf46a64ad79df545e2) finds chrF++ the best word- or character-overlap metric on Assamese, Punjabi, Kannada, and Maithili, though weaker than trained metrics. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Husain et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ed2da4a2c64c4ffdfe4fa38176b61d688a2b0d21), which uses SacreBLEU chrF to compare native-script and romanized Llama 2 7B outputs on FLORES-200, showing differences such as 46.87 versus 37.40 chrF for 0-shot romanized versus native out-of-English translation and an average about 8 chrF-point gain for romanized instruction-tuned models, and [(Lo, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/731682fb8bc04b382e9e2db9156147a9479bcc4b), which uses chrF as a baseline and shows character-level matching can be a resource-free signal in some directions. The earliest and weakest support is [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), which validates n-gram precision and BLEU against human judgments on a 500-sentence Chinese-to-English corpus with correlations of 0.99 and 0.96 and paired t-tests on 25-sentence blocks, establishing the general value of automatic overlap scoring but not exact match or chrF for IOL-style reasoning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that chrF can overestimate quality in low-resource or closely related settings: [(Bapna et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66de7ac94dd13ec041659b06041c6ff27b1e55f2) reports Nigerian Pidgin with chrF around 60 while 20% of outputs were judged nonsense or wrong language by humans on a 0-6 scale, and token hit-rate analysis shows high-chrF languages such as Fulfulde can mask rare-token errors, which directly cautions against treating chrF as a reliable secondary correctness signal for Linguini extremely low-resource languages without human validation. The next strongest contradiction is that character-level overlap metrics may not capture low-resource quality well enough to stand in for reasoning correctness: [(Singh et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eed4140dfa751319c2952acf46a64ad79df545e2) finds no metric achieved strong correlations with human judgments on Assamese, Punjabi, Kannada, and Maithili, and although chrF++ was the best overlap metric, trained COMET variants generally outperformed it, suggesting chrF alone may be too coarse for the proposed benchmark. A strong methodological contradiction is that automatic sequence metrics are sensitive to preprocessing: [(Post, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4bfadfca9742bb3ee98a0cd322d5ce4e59a3ceb) shows BLEU can vary by 0.5 to 1.8 points across 12 WMT 2017 language arcs due to tokenization, case, unknown-word masking, and reference handling, with case treatment producing the largest variation, such as en-fi increasing from 22.2 to 25.4 cased and en-ru dropping from 33.3 to 32.0 cased, which means exact match and chrF scores for Linguini could be non-comparable if normalization, script conversion, case, diacritics, whitespace, and dataset version are not fixed. A related but weaker contradiction is that chrF-family metrics can be outperformed by other character or trained metrics: [(B et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c0fe24dd7a620419693a4cd74b2d08339d0233d) shows chrF++ is the best overlap metric but weaker than COMET and BLEURT-20, and its Hindi Pearson correlation of 0.67 was not significant at p < 0.05, weakening the claim that chrF-family scores are robust across all low-resource languages. Another weaker contradiction is that plain chrF is not automatically the best character-level secondary metric: [(Lo, 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/731682fb8bc04b382e9e2db9156147a9479bcc4b) reports YiSi-0, a resource-free longest common character substring metric with IDF weighting, as more stable than chrF and BLEU across directions and significantly better for Turkish-English and English-Turkish. Earlier configuration evidence also contradicts a single default chrF: [(Popovic, 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/285c165c81fc9275955147a892b9a039ec8b1052) favors recall-weighted CHRF3 with beta = 3 and 6-gram character n-grams, whereas [(Popovic, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a30e0193889fca9c34cbf4e4e42ac2fe3db9f79) finds beta = 2 comparable to beta = 3 and selects beta = 2, with best character 3-grams and 4-grams rather than 6-grams, so the proposed chrF secondary metric is under-specified if it does not state beta, n-gram order, and whether chrF or chrF++ is used. Weaker but relevant is [(Freitag et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/476d79d1f5650c5361104ed468e75bfc4732622d), which shows metric rankings can change completely when gold scores shift from WMT ratings to MQM annotations, with chrF achieving only a system-level Kendall's tau of 0.8 for English-German and lower segment-level correlations, indicating that chrF validity depends on the human judgment target and may not transfer to IOL-style exact-answer reasoning.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to fix and document all preprocessing before scoring: specify exact benchmark item version, normalization, case sensitivity, diacritic handling, whitespace, punctuation, script conversion, and whether answers are canonicalized, because [(Post, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4bfadfca9742bb3ee98a0cd322d5ce4e59a3ceb) shows metric scores can shift by 0.5 to 1.8 points from preprocessing alone and [(Madhani et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b4e59fb2dde89d88c67da499d36385817b1f129) shows exact-match transliteration evaluation depends on strict test-set filtering and word-level matching. The next most important action is to specify the chrF variant and hyperparameters, ideally reporting both chrF and chrF++ as secondary diagnostics: use SacreBLEU or a pinned version, state beta = 1, 2, or 3, character n-gram orders, word n-gram orders, and whether spaces are included, because [(Popovic, 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/285c165c81fc9275955147a892b9a039ec8b1052) favors beta = 3 and 6-grams, [(Popovic, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a30e0193889fca9c34cbf4e4e42ac2fe3db9f79) favors beta = 2 with character 3-grams and 4-grams plus word unigrams and bigrams, and [(Puduppully et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b6e5855b6a4e425ba251a93516f2bccffe5ba403), [(B et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c0fe24dd7a620419693a4cd74b2d08339d0233d), and [(Singh et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eed4140dfa751319c2952acf46a64ad79df545e2) use chrF++ for low-resource morphologically rich outputs. A third important action is to add human or native-speaker validation for a stratified subset of low-resource items, especially close varieties, dialects, and ambiguous fill-in-blank answers, because [(Bapna et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66de7ac94dd13ec041659b06041c6ff27b1e55f2) shows chrF can overestimate quality and mask rare-token errors, and [(Singh et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eed4140dfa751319c2952acf46a64ad79df545e2) shows automatic metrics have weak correlations on low-resource languages. The plan should also report statistical significance and uncertainty: use paired bootstrap or paired t-tests with p < 0.05 and confidence intervals for model differences, per task type, per language, and per resource level, following [(Puduppully et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b6e5855b6a4e425ba251a93516f2bccffe5ba403), [(B et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c0fe24dd7a620419693a4cd74b2d08339d0233d), and the earlier paired-test practice in [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9). Finally, the benchmark should report exact match and chrF separately by sequence transduction, fill-in-blank, and number transliteration, include top-k exact match or multiple-reference handling where valid answers are not unique, and retain the no-context, leave-one-out, and same-format-different-language contamination controls, because [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) directly implements the dual-metric IOL setup and [(Madhani et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b4e59fb2dde89d88c67da499d36385817b1f129) supports exact match for transliteration-style tasks.

## We will evaluate major open-source models (Llama, Mistral, Gemma series) and commercially available state-of-the-art models (GPT-4 variants, Claude-3 series) to identify performance gaps between open and proprietary systems. This specifies the model population for the main evaluation and supports comparison across the current model landscape.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed model-coverage method comes from [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), which directly evaluates open-source Gemma 7B, Alpaca 7B, Llama 2 7B, Llama 3 8B and 70B, Vicuna 13B, Mistral 7B, Mixtral 8x7B and 8x22B against GPT-3.5 and GPT-4o on 48 newly written Rosetta Stone-style puzzles and 272 questions across 19 extremely low-resource languages, using exact match accuracy and four prompting conditions, with open models run through Together.AI and GPT models queried on June 16, 2024. This is the closest analogue to LINGUINI's IOL-derived, language-agnostic design and supports the plan's choice to compare Llama, Mistral, and Gemma with proprietary GPT models, while also showing that the largest open models, specifically Mistral-8x22B and Llama-3-70B, can be competitive with GPT-4, so the proposed evaluation should report model-size and family effects rather than assume a uniform open-proprietary gap. A strong, more general precedent is [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4), which compares LLaMA-2-7B, LLaMA-2-70B, Mistral-7B, Claude2, GPT-3.5-Turbo, GPT-4, and GPT-4-Turbo on SCIBENCH with zero-shot, few-shot chain-of-thought, and few-shot Python settings at temperature zero, and finds that proprietary GPT-4 models lead by large margins, with GPT-4-Turbo outperforming Mistral-7B by 34.76 percent in zero-shot and LLaMA-2-70B improving from 2.41 to 8.40 percent from zero-shot to few-shot. This supports the proposed open-versus-proprietary comparison and the need to fix decoding and scoring conditions, although its scientific-computing domain and 5 percent numeric tolerance are not directly transferable to LINGUINI's exact-match and chrF linguistic scoring. Earlier work provides the methodological foundation: [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) established controlled zero-, one-, and few-shot evaluation across GPT-3 sizes from 125M to 175B with 2048-token contexts and K typically 10 to 100 demonstrations, showing performance gains with scale and examples, and found that synthetic word-manipulation tasks depended on in-context demonstrations rather than memorized task instances, with no model reversing words; its contamination analysis and filtering bug also motivate LINGUINI's no-context ablation and same-format, different-language example selection. [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) and [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) refine this by showing that chain-of-thought prompting can substantially improve multi-step reasoning, with Kojima reporting zero-shot chain-of-thought improving MultiArith from 17.7 to 78.7 percent and GSM8K from 10.4 to 40.7 percent, PaLM 540B reaching 89.0 percent on MultiArith with self-consistency, and Wei's error analysis showing that 46 percent of incorrect GSM8K answers had almost-correct chains, supporting detailed error analysis in LINGUINI. More recent but less directly relevant work adds weaker support: [(Wang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2920a36dd777df75792d97d3abb18ea8fa42906) uses synthetic, configurable tasks to reduce leakage and compares open-source models, supporting LINGUINI's synthetic IOL-style design; [(Ng, and Markov, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d10ca1f42346551dfa81a9e6bb14cfa3a086054e) compares GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 with open-source models on native-language identification using TOEFL11 and ICLE-NLI and averages open-source runs over three trials, supporting repeated inference reporting; [(Ochieng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/80d1cebc2dbbc03772fdd277b4fee4ad82b9c3aa) compares seven models, including GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-3.5-Turbo, Llama-2-70b, Mistral-7b, Mixtral-8x7b, and Gemma-7b, on a 3,719-message WhatsApp sentiment task with weighted F1 and qualitative review, and [(Kabongo et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56b85b523c8a935917a124588650c2a82bb8126a) compares Mistral 7B, Llama-2, GPT-4-Turbo, and GPT-4.o on leaderboard extraction with a 50 percent fuzz.ratio threshold, but both provide limited quantitative evidence for the specific linguistic-reasoning gap that LINGUINI targets.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to a simplistic open-versus-proprietary framing is that the closest linguistic benchmark, [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02), reports that GPT-4 and the largest open-source models, specifically Mistral-8x22B and Llama-3-70B, show strong performance, while smaller open-source models such as Gemma, Alpaca, and Llama 2 perform much worse, with accuracies described as closer to 0. This newer linguistic evidence complicates the older scale-based narrative in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), and [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda), because the gap is not simply a function of proprietary access; LINGUINI must therefore report per-model, per-size, per-family results and avoid collapsing Llama, Mistral, and Gemma into a single open-source category. A second important contradiction is that prompting and decoding can change the apparent gap: [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) found no universally effective prompting setting, with GPT-4 improving from 30.36 to 43.22 percent when using Python but GPT-4-Turbo underperforming GPT-4 in few-shot Python, and [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) showed that smaller models sometimes perform worse with chain-of-thought because they produce fluent but illogical chains. For LINGUINI, if the main evaluation uses 0-5 in-context examples, leave-one-out candidates, or any chain-of-thought-style prompting, the open-proprietary gap may partly reflect prompting sensitivity rather than linguistic reasoning, so the plan should include minimal, hand-tuned, chain-of-thought, and no-context conditions as controlled ablations. A third contradiction is contamination and data leakage: [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) found benchmark overlaps during contamination analysis and a filtering bug that left some overlaps unremoved, and [(Wang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2920a36dd777df75792d97d3abb18ea8fa42906) explicitly designs synthetic tasks to avoid leakage. Since LINGUINI uses IOL problems from 2003 to 2023, some problems, languages, or linguistic patterns may have been seen in pretraining, so the proposed no-context prompting and same-format, different-language example selection are necessary but not sufficient; the method should also report n-gram or embedding overlap checks and, where possible, newly authored or held-out IOL-style items. A fourth contradiction is metric brittleness: [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) justifies exact match because each puzzle has a single correct answer, but LINGUINI includes sequence transduction, fill-in-blanks, and number transliteration, where surface-form variation, transliteration conventions, and multiple acceptable segmentations can make exact match overly harsh; the plan's chrF secondary metric is therefore important, and the method should specify normalization, acceptable variants, and perhaps token-level F1 for transliteration. Weaker contradictions or limitations include the absence of Claude-3 in the cited evidence, with only Claude2 appearing in [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4), so the proposed Claude-3 series inclusion is not directly validated by the related work; the closest linguistic study also omits several planned LINGUINI controls, including the 0-5 in-context example range, leave-one-out cross-validation, chrF, script transliteration, and grammar-book integration, so its protocol cannot be copied wholesale; the lack of statistical significance, confidence intervals, or exact per-model accuracies in several recent papers, including [(Kabongo et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56b85b523c8a935917a124588650c2a82bb8126a), [(Ochieng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/80d1cebc2dbbc03772fdd277b4fee4ad82b9c3aa), [(Ng, and Markov, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d10ca1f42346551dfa81a9e6bb14cfa3a086054e), and the excerpt of [(Wang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2920a36dd777df75792d97d3abb18ea8fa42906), means that open-proprietary gaps cannot be inferred without rigorous repeated inference and item-level statistical tests; the operational details in [(Kabongo et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/56b85b523c8a935917a124588650c2a82bb8126a), including an NVIDIA 3090, 20 hours of training, and 10 minutes of inference for 2,000 test instances, are useful for reproducibility but are undermined by conflicting test-set sizes, showing the need for precise split reporting; and the older model populations in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), and [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) predate Llama 3, Mistral/Mixtral, Gemma, GPT-4o, and Claude-3, so their quantitative gaps should not be extrapolated to the current model landscape.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to operationalize the model-coverage method as a controlled matrix of exact model versions, sizes, and inference settings, including Llama 2 and Llama 3 variants, Mistral 7B and Mixtral 8x7B/8x22B, Gemma 2B/7B/27B if available, GPT-4, GPT-4-Turbo, GPT-4o, and Claude-3 Haiku/Sonnet/Opus, with API dates, weights, temperature, top-p, max tokens, decoding mode, and computational resources recorded, because [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) shows that model version and prompting can change rankings, and [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) shows that open-source performance is highly size-dependent. The next priority is to align the evaluation protocol with LINGUINI's language-agnostic design by using the planned 0-5 in-context examples, leave-one-out cross-validation, same-format but different-language examples, and a no-context condition, while also reporting an optional 8-example condition if context length permits, because [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) used eight manually composed exemplars and [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) showed that performance often improves with more demonstrations, so 0-5 examples may understate capability on complex IOL puzzles. The method should then specify scoring in detail: exact match as primary for single-answer fill-in-blanks and number transliteration, chrF as secondary for sequence transduction, normalized exact match or multiple acceptable answers where orthographic or transliteration variants are plausible, and chance-level baselines, following [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) for exact match on single-answer linguistic puzzles and [(Wang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4993258852711c4e3d0011325ac3db680eae84f4) for controlled scoring with tolerance where appropriate. Statistical reporting should be strengthened by averaging scores across items within each problem as planned, but also by running multiple inference samples for stochastic open-source models, reporting standard deviations or confidence intervals, and using paired bootstrap or mixed-effects tests over problems and questions, motivated by [(Ng, and Markov, 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d10ca1f42346551dfa81a9e6bb14cfa3a086054e) averaging open-source runs over three trials and by the lack of significance testing in several related evaluations. Prompting ablations should be made explicit and balanced across models: minimal prompt, hand-tuned prompt, basic chain-of-thought, full chain-of-thought with a high-resource example, and no-context, following [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02) and [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5), while monitoring that chain-of-thought does not disadvantage smaller open models as reported in [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) and [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda), and, where appropriate, using self-consistency or majority voting as in [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda). Finally, the plan should add contamination and leakage controls beyond no-context prompting, such as n-gram overlap checks against public corpora, newly authored or held-out IOL-style items, and script transliteration ablations, because [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) documents benchmark overlap and [(Wang et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2920a36dd777df75792d97d3abb18ea8fa42906) uses synthetic configurability to reduce leakage; these controls, together with stratification by language resourcefulness and grammar-book integration ablations, will make the open-versus-proprietary gap claim more defensible and reproducible.

## We will prompt models with instructions, contextual information, and problems, averaging scores across items within each problem to provide single task scores. We will analyze both individual model performance and cross-model comparisons to understand the current state of linguistic reasoning capabilities in language models. This defines the operational evaluation pipeline and the comparative analysis used to interpret the benchmark results.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which operationalizes the proposed LINGUINI pipeline by prompting open and proprietary models with instructions, contextual linguistic information, and IOL-style problems, using 0 to 5 leave-one-out in-context examples, same-format examples while excluding same-language examples, exact-match accuracy as the primary metric and chrF as a secondary metric, and reports an open-proprietary gap with llama-3-70b as the best open model and several proprietary tiers above it; this directly validates the proposed operational evaluation and cross-model comparison, although it does not provide numeric scores, confidence intervals, or significance tests. The next strongest support is [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02e), which evaluates newly authored Rosetta Stone-style puzzles across 19 extremely low-resource languages and 272 questions using exact-match accuracy, prompting ablations, and cross-model comparisons, finding that GPT-4o and large open models such as Mistral-8x22B and Llama-3-70B perform strongly while smaller models perform near zero and difficulty follows IOL-medalist ratings; this supports the language-agnostic design, exact-match scoring, and model-tier analysis, but it does not explicitly report item-to-task averaging, confidence intervals, or significance. Further support comes from [(Handa et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ccc3c79860db684baedcc4351c42f5eee75302e7), which uses a prompt-based benchmark with instructions, domain context, action sequences, and questions, scores binary and free-form items, aggregates item-level accuracies by length, category, domain, and fluent type, and reports standard errors of the mean for GPT-4o, Llama-3.1-8B/70B, fine-tuned Llama-3.1-8B, and o1-preview, with fine-tuning using 8 H100 GPUs and o1-preview restricted to the ramifications subset due to cost; this supports the proposed item-level averaging and cross-model comparison, though it uses LLM-judged free-form scoring rather than exact match and chrF. Constructed-benchmark evidence also supports the pipeline: [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cb249077fa9fdd7e18db396f5e053febaa83889) and [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a) score item-level correctness on lateral-thinking puzzles, aggregate to task-level accuracy, compare original, semantic, and context reconstructions, and show that group-based all-variant accuracy is stricter and often lower than instance accuracy, supporting the need for controlled item aggregation and contamination or robustness checks. [(Raihan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95b59d6cc01d2b92d14c169d8f8cd47bd0ac6626) supports zero-to-eight-shot prompting, chain-of-thought, and ensemble conditions on a 1,100-question multiple-choice benchmark, with GPT-4 Turbo reaching 0.93 on sentence puzzles and 0.95 on word puzzles and Claude 2.1 reaching 0.86 and 0.95 under eight-shot chain-of-thought ensemble, but it lacks significance tests and item-level averaging. Earlier iterative work supports the core assumptions: [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) shows zero-shot chain-of-thought improves multi-step reasoning, e.g., MultiArith from 17.7% to 78.7% and GSM8K from 10.4% to 40.7%, while [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) shows chain-of-thought gains emerge around 100B parameters and are larger on harder problems; both support prompt-based reasoning evaluation but not language-agnostic linguistic puzzles. [(Wallace et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32afdb07021fda775ceaedd231c58bfed0aa980a) supports item-level scoring, puzzle-level aggregation, baseline comparison, and statistical testing, with an 11.2 percentage-point paired t-test improvement over Dr.Fill on New York Times puzzles and 89.5% perfect-puzzle accuracy on themeless New York Times puzzles. [(Ding et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea987db9daa0af1e495b29e2a03dff975641181b) supports the prompt-to-answer-extraction workflow and cross-model comparison, although it evaluates general reasoning rather than IOL-style linguistic puzzles. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Rozner et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2ce4d4be33379cbc3b4b295d93e098637638761), [(Liu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59641c10ed7431a3cf841f308367dc2dc0281b74), [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), and [(Koncel-Kedziorski et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2bdbb07bc12b8d8c332b7a84aa05e76218c07cd9), which establish contextual constraints, in-context examples, controlled overlap or grammaticality, and cross-validation; for example, [(Liu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59641c10ed7431a3cf841f308367dc2dc0281b74) used RoBERTa-large CLS retrieval with GPT-3 at temperature 0 and reported KATE_sst-2 accuracy of 93.43 on IMDB, [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) used eight GPT-3 models from 125M to 175B with 2048-token contexts, and [(Koncel-Kedziorski et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2bdbb07bc12b8d8c332b7a84aa05e76218c07cd9) used 5-fold cross-validation with a random same-size subset, although they are English-specific, fine-tuned, or symbolic rather than low-resource IOL-style prompting. The weakest support is [(Koubâa et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ed1c09e27fb35e0b8da4e1afa9ad23c0b517f3d), which compares 0.1B and 0.3B ArabianGPT models with AraGPT, Bloom-7b1, and Llama-7B on ARC, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA, but provides no numeric scores, confidence intervals, or task-specific linguistic-reasoning evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method comes from [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960), which reports that in-context examples had mixed effects in the LINGUINI pipeline: llama-3-70b-it benefited from in-context examples, while claude-3-opus degraded as the number of examples increased, and the authors attribute this to examples imposing a useful answer format but also introducing tokens from languages potentially unrelated to the evaluated problem; this contradicts the assumption that 0 to 5 leave-one-out examples will uniformly improve performance and shows that same-format selection alone may be insufficient unless language, script, token overlap, and answer-format contamination are explicitly controlled, and it also contradicts the older assumption in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) and [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) that more in-context examples or chain-of-thought generally improves performance. A second major contradiction comes from [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cb249077fa9fdd7e18db396f5e053febaa83889) and [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a), where group-based accuracy requiring all original, semantic, and context variants to be correct was substantially lower than instance-based accuracy, with drops of 0.025 to 0.175 in sentence puzzles and 0.031 to 0.281 in word puzzles for original-and-semantic groups, and larger drops when context reconstruction was included; this contradicts the sufficiency of averaging item scores within each problem as the sole task score, because averaging can mask instability across items or variants. A third contradiction is that [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02e) reports only qualitative outcomes, with different prompting approaches producing roughly similar accuracies and smaller models near zero, and no exact accuracy values, baselines, confidence intervals, or significance tests; this weakens the expectation that prompt ablations and exact-match scoring will cleanly separate model capabilities without additional statistical and item-level reporting. A fourth contradiction is that [(Sarti et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a5867cef53cf5a0efa481bdcbb70655dbe3ac05) found prompted models performed poorly on 2,000 Italian rebus examples, with the best prompted system Claude 3.5 Sonnet solving only 24% and open-source prompted systems producing correct first passes for only 4%, while a fine-tuned system achieved 51%; this suggests that zero- or few-shot prompting may understate linguistic puzzle-solving ability and that item-level solution accuracy can be low even when partial linguistic cues are present. A fifth contradiction is that [(Handa et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ccc3c79860db684baedcc4351c42f5eee75302e7) shows free-form reasoning can be brittle, with GPT-4o scoring 0% on free-answer ramification questions while o1-preview scored 25.00% at length 1, and it relies on a Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct judge for free-form answers; this contradicts the assumption that exact match alone is sufficient for open-ended linguistic outputs, although chrF and constrained answer formats can mitigate the issue. A sixth contradiction is that [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) found one-shot performance dropped by roughly half or more compared with few-shot, zero-shot performance was very weak, some anagram tasks were non-bijective and required search, and no statistical significance tests were reported; this contradicts a simple monotonic zero-to-few-shot evaluation and shows that item determinism, shot count, and scoring can materially affect task scores. A seventh contradiction is that [(Koncel-Kedziorski et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2bdbb07bc12b8d8c332b7a84aa05e76218c07cd9) found performance declines as lexical and template overlap are reduced, with most datasets having lexical overlap around 7% and about a 20% reduction when selecting half the data, while a random same-size subset showed that data size alone has a weaker effect than overlap or grammaticality; this contradicts the idea that reducing overlap or contamination is always neutral or beneficial, because it can also change task difficulty and may require difficulty calibration. An eighth contradiction is that [(Rozner et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2ce4d4be33379cbc3b4b295d93e098637638761) found that a word-initial disjoint split reduced T5-base top-1 accuracy from 16.3% on a random split to 1.1% before curricular training, even though the disjoint split was intended to reduce answer overlap; this shows that contamination controls can make the benchmark much harder and may require larger datasets or easier item calibration. A ninth, weaker contradiction is that [(Ding et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea987db9daa0af1e495b29e2a03dff975641181b) found that zero-shot chain-of-thought and break-the-chain prompts were often comparable, and shortcut prompts produced large relative gains on ShortcutQA, including about 22% in arithmetic, suggesting that the proposed pipeline should not assume that standard contextual examples or chain-of-thought prompting are the best or necessary condition for linguistic reasoning.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make in-context example selection and ablation explicit and model-tier-aware: use leave-one-out cross-validation, same-format examples, and exclusion of same-language examples as planned, but also control lexical and template overlap, script, tokenization, answer format, and unrelated-language tokens, and report 0 to 5 shot curves, no-context prompting, random-example baselines, and retrieved-example baselines, because [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) shows examples can harm stronger proprietary models, [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows shot count can be non-monotonic, and [(Liu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59641c10ed7431a3cf841f308367dc2dc0281b74) shows retrieval quality affects in-context performance. Second, report both item-level and problem-level scores rather than only an averaged task score: use exact-match accuracy as primary, chrF as secondary, normalized exact match or token-level F1 as diagnostics, all-items-correct per problem, and a group-based metric when a problem contains multiple items or variants, because [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cb249077fa9fdd7e18db396f5e053febaa83889) and [(Jiang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ee2d23968b8ee97c958d75ae2b6ae13c84da1a) show group accuracy is stricter and often lower than instance accuracy, [(Handa et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ccc3c79860db684baedcc4351c42f5eee75302e7) shows item-level aggregation with standard errors is useful, and [(Sarti et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a5867cef53cf5a0efa481bdcbb70655dbe3ac05) shows partial first-pass metrics can explain solution failures. Third, add statistical and reproducibility reporting: confidence intervals, standard errors, paired bootstrap or paired t-tests, multiple-comparison corrections, random seeds, temperature, max tokens, API versions, hardware or cost, and answer-extraction prompts, following [(Wallace et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32afdb07021fda775ceaedd231c58bfed0aa980a), [(Handa et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ccc3c79860db684baedcc4351c42f5eee75302e7), and [(Ding et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea987db9daa0af1e495b29e2a03dff975641181b). Fourth, strengthen contamination and robustness controls by adding no-context prompting, script transliteration, resource-level analysis, grammar-book integration, and reconstruction or paraphrase variants, plus human and random baselines and IOL-medalist difficulty ratings, because [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cb249077fa9fdd7e18db396f5e053febaa83889), [(Wallace et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32afdb07021fda775ceaedd231c58bfed0aa980a), [(Rozner et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2ce4d4be33379cbc3b4b295d93e098637638761), and [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02e) show that overlap, disjoint splits, reconstruction, and difficulty ratings materially affect interpretation. Fifth, test prompt conditions without assuming chain-of-thought superiority: compare standard prompting, zero-shot chain-of-thought, few-shot chain-of-thought, self-consistency, and ensemble only where cost permits, because [(Kojima et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7ad08848d5d7c5c47673ffe0da06af443643bda) and [(Wei et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b6e810ce0afd0dd093f789d2b2742d047e316d5) show scale-dependent gains, while [(Ding et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea987db9daa0af1e495b29e2a03dff975641181b) and [(Raihan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95b59d6cc01d2b92d14c169d8f8cd47bd0ac6626) show that alternative prompts or ensembles can be competitive. Sixth, calibrate dataset difficulty and size: report the number of problems, items, languages, and task types, exclude singletons, image-dependent, and explanation-only items, and include enough items per language to support leave-one-out selection and resource-level analysis, following the IOL-derived design in [(Sánchez et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9b6c35db256e38496ad81c189783529ab4209960) and the difficulty-rated Rosetta Stone design in [(Chi et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e35c5f517e547bde2ae4d77a535d3e5584805f02e).

## No-Context Prompting: Remove contextual information to assess reliance on training data versus provided context, serving as a proxy for data contamination detection. This ablation tests whether models depend on the supplied linguistic context or on pre-existing knowledge.

### Support

The strongest support for No-Context Prompting comes from [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), which uses a zero-shot K=0 protocol for GPT-3 175B trained for 300 billion tokens with a 2048-token context window and also performs a contamination-overlap analysis, making it the closest analogue to LINGUINI's goal of separating supplied context from pre-existing or memorized knowledge. In that work, zero-shot performance was generally weaker than one-shot and few-shot performance, as on Winogrande where zero-shot accuracy was 70.2 percent versus 73.2 percent one-shot and 77.7 percent few-shot, and on commonsense tasks such as ARC Challenge where zero-shot was 51.4 versus 78.5 for fine-tuned state of the art, but it remained high on Winograd at 88.3 percent and PIQA at 80.5 percent; the authors also found some Winograd schemas in the training data and flagged the PIQA few-shot test-server result as potentially contamination-affected, showing that no-context performance can reveal reliance on memorized benchmark content while also requiring explicit overlap checks. The next strongest support is [(Nie et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa21fd30134b3a35449cc65795598846c4339060), whose Direct baseline removes retrieved cross-lingual context and evaluates a multilingual model on machine-translated low-resource test sets for 10 languages across six language families; on AG News the Direct no-context baseline averaged 36.3 percent, with per-language values ranging from 27.4 for Javanese to 46.9 for Urdu, while labeled retrieval improved it by 31.3 points and on unseen AG News languages Direct averaged 30.8 percent versus 60.7 percent for labeled retrieval with five examples, directly supporting LINGUINI's hypothesis that low-resource linguistic puzzles will depend heavily on supplied context rather than prior language knowledge. [(Min et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4df78183261538e718066331898ee5cad7cad05) provides strong methodological support for ablation design by comparing no demonstrations, no labels, labels only, random labels, random English words, and out-of-distribution inputs, finding that replacing in-distribution demonstration inputs with out-of-distribution inputs caused a 3 to 16 percentage-point absolute drop for Channel MetaICL, Direct GPT-J, and Channel GPT-J, and that no-format variants were not better than no demonstrations, which supports LINGUINI's need to compare no-context with corrupted-context and format-preserving controls rather than only removing context. [(Dai et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ca0894cb4d165eb5ef45b73526ca8b4cdd725) supports the conceptual distinction between zero-shot and example-driven learning by comparing zero-shot, 32-shot in-context learning, and finetuning on the same 32 examples using GPT 1.3B and 2.7B on NVIDIA V100 GPUs with 32 GB memory, and by showing that SimAOU between ICL and finetuning updates was substantially positive, with averages of 0.186 for GPT 1.3B and 0.225 for GPT 2.7B versus random-update baselines near zero, although its English classification datasets are less directly aligned with LINGUINI's low-resource IOL puzzles. [(Kumar et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3f387fa3cbfcb6255ac93786b6027eb86eba580) supports the use of no-context ablations by showing that removing context from label descriptions can reveal prior label knowledge, with GPT-J 6B no-context GS-N outperforming context-augmented GS on SST5 35.6 versus 34.8, AGNews 72.0 versus 64.9, DBPedia 78.9 versus 71.7, Ethos SO 55.3 versus 52.7, and Emotions 37.8 versus 30.3, while also underperforming the full contextualized GEN-Z on SST2 72.3 versus 91.7, AGNews 72.0 versus 77.0, and HS18 29.9 versus 62.6. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Han et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a483cd1cbecd66150c9bbcd01606723950281bc), which uses 0-shot prompting as a baseline on nine classification datasets with GPT-2-large, GPT-2-XL, GPT-neo, GPT-J, and Bloom, and shows that PROCA calibration can improve zero-shot and few-shot accuracy by an average of 13 percentage points and up to 40 percentage points, indicating that no-context scores are informative but sensitive to decision-boundary and prompt effects; from [(Liu et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28692beece311a90f5fa1ca2ec9d0c2ce293d069), which uses context-free placeholder prompts to estimate prior answer distributions and thereby separates pre-existing priors from supplied context; from [(Reynolds, and McDonell, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac3cdb50606f7770eef8e4cd951840a4f71287a0), which shows that zero-shot prompting can expose catastrophic task-location failures that mean metrics obscure; and from [(Wei et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff0b2681d7b05e16c46dfb71d980cc2f605907cd), whose zero-shot unseen-task cluster holdout supports the broader goal of separating memorized task data from generalization, although it controls contamination at the task-cluster level rather than by removing linguistic context. The remaining works provide weaker support: [(Ubani et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee1fdc0e4391f2ef7d3222d47d8677c574fcd95c) supports combining zero-shot prompting with overlap auditing, [(Sivarajkumar et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5a6fc6aa139066e3b66ba63002e7d84c109aebc) and [(Carolan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa99a3b9a4174f1bbd9cb12e28171c4b183949c0) support zero-shot benchmarking but provide limited quantitative evidence, and [(Garg et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de32da8f5c6a50a6c311e9357ba16aa7d05a1bc9) is conceptually related to memorization but lacks usable quantitative details in the provided excerpt.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that No-Context Prompting is not, by itself, a reliable contamination detector because contamination and generalization require direct overlap or held-out out-of-domain evidence. [(Ziegler et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5616be414472881c170abca7ac2472378c63fc74) uses weighted 5-gram Jaccard similarity and out-of-domain MMLU biology subsets rather than no-context prompting, finding that CRAFT datasets had less than 0.4 percent weighted 5-gram Jaccard similarity with test sets while in-domain datasets had higher overlap, such as BioQA 16.6 percent, CSQA 4.4 percent, MedQA 1.1 percent, and Summarization 0.3 percent, and that the in-domain model scored 92.7 on ScienceQA versus 80.4 for CRAFT XL while CRAFT XL averaged 66.5 on MMLU biology versus 65.8 for the in-domain model; this shows that memorization and generalization must be assessed with lexical overlap and unseen-task transfer, not merely by removing context. A similarly strong contradiction comes from [(Ubani et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee1fdc0e4391f2ef7d3222d47d8677c574fcd95c), which audits zero-shot ChatGPT data augmentation with MiniLM, TF-IDF, and word-overlap similarity in a low-resource setting with 10 examples per class and 15 repeated runs, and finds little evidence of memorization, with generated data only 7 percent more similar to training than test data and word-overlap statistics such as SNIPS generated-to-test 0.7 percent above 66 percent overlap versus training-to-test 11.0 percent, indicating that zero-shot or no-context behavior alone does not measure contamination. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) also contradicts a simple contamination-proxy interpretation because zero-shot GPT-3 can be strong even when some benchmark overlap exists, as with Winograd zero-shot 88.3 percent and PIQA zero-shot 80.5 percent, while the authors judged the Winograd contamination effect small and only flagged the PIQA few-shot test-server result as potentially affected. [(Kumar et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3f387fa3cbfcb6255ac93786b6027eb86eba580) contradicts the assumption that high no-context performance necessarily indicates memorized benchmark content, because no-context variants can outperform context-augmented baselines due to prior label knowledge, such as GPT-J 6B GS-N on SST5 35.6 versus GS 34.8, AGNews 72.0 versus GS 64.9, DBPedia 78.9 versus GS 71.7, Ethos SO 55.3 versus GS 52.7, and Emotions 37.8 versus GS 30.3. [(Wei et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff0b2681d7b05e16c46dfb71d980cc2f605907cd) further weakens the contamination-proxy claim by showing that zero-shot instruction-tuned models can outperform zero-shot and sometimes few-shot GPT-3 on many held-out task clusters, meaning strong no-context performance may reflect instruction tuning or broad generalization rather than memorized LINGUINI items. [(Han et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a483cd1cbecd66150c9bbcd01606723950281bc) contradicts treating raw no-context accuracy as a stable capability measure because PROCA calibration improved zero-shot and few-shot performance by an average of 13 percentage points over the conventional approach and up to 40 percentage points, with GPT-J 0-shot on DBpedia cited as a large gain, showing that prompt, label, and decision-boundary effects can substantially change no-context scores. [(Reynolds, and McDonell, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac3cdb50606f7770eef8e4cd951840a4f71287a0) provides a weaker but important contradiction by showing that zero-shot failures can be catastrophic task-location failures, such as continuing in French, blanks, or underscores, rather than evidence of contamination, and that mean BLEU can obscure these failure modes. [(Liu et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28692beece311a90f5fa1ca2ec9d0c2ce293d069) adds a methodological contradiction that context-free placeholder choice and miscalibrated probability distributions can confound the estimated prior, so a no-context condition may measure prompt artifacts as much as training-data reliance. The remaining works provide weaker contradictions or limitations: [(Min et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4df78183261538e718066331898ee5cad7cad05) shows that no-format context is not equivalent to no demonstrations, so a poorly specified no-context prompt may conflate format removal with context removal; [(Dai et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ca0894cb4d165eb5ef45b73526ca8b4cdd725) does not directly measure contamination; and [(Garg et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de32da8f5c6a50a6c311e9357ba16aa7d05a1bc9), [(Sivarajkumar et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d5a6fc6aa139066e3b66ba63002e7d84c109aebc), and [(Carolan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa99a3b9a4174f1bbd9cb12e28171c4b183949c0) provide insufficient quantitative evidence to validate no-context prompting as a contamination proxy.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to stop treating No-Context Prompting as a standalone contamination detector and add explicit contamination and generalization controls to LINGUINI. Following [(Ziegler et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5616be414472881c170abca7ac2472378c63fc74) and [(Ubani et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee1fdc0e4391f2ef7d3222d47d8677c574fcd95c), the benchmark should report weighted n-gram or Jaccard overlap, semantic embedding overlap, and word-overlap statistics between LINGUINI prompts, contexts, and answers and any accessible training corpora or model-specific data, with thresholds and per-language breakdowns; if training data are inaccessible, the plan should use held-out unseen low-resource languages, paraphrased or synthetic variants of IOL puzzles, and script-transliterated versions as out-of-domain probes, while documenting that no-context performance is only a behavioral proxy. The second most important action is to define the no-context condition precisely and add corrupted-context baselines, because [(Min et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4df78183261538e718066331898ee5cad7cad05) shows that removing format or using out-of-distribution inputs changes performance differently than removing demonstrations. LINGUINI should compare full context, no context with the same task template and answer format, shuffled or irrelevant context, random labels or random target strings, label-only or input-only conditions, and out-of-distribution linguistic inputs, so that a drop or rise can be attributed to context reliance, task-location failure, format reliance, or prior knowledge. The third most important action is to align the ablation with LINGUINI's low-resource, language-agnostic design by reporting no-context performance separately by language resourcefulness, script, task type, and problem category, using the Direct low-resource baseline logic of [(Nie et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa21fd30134b3a35449cc65795598846c4339060) and the zero-shot contamination-aware protocol of [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0); this should include exact match and chrF, multiple random seeds, confidence intervals or significance tests, and failure-mode analysis for non-attempts, blanks, or malformed outputs as highlighted by [(Reynolds, and McDonell, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac3cdb50606f7770eef8e4cd951840a4f71287a0). The fourth action is to reduce prompt and label bias, because [(Han et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a483cd1cbecd66150c9bbcd01606723950281bc) and [(Liu et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/28692beece311a90f5fa1ca2ec9d0c2ce293d069) show that calibration, placeholder choice, and answer-space specification can materially change no-context scores; LINGUINI should use multiple templates, normalized answer spaces, logprob or PMI calibration where appropriate, and report variance across templates and seeds. The fifth action is to use internal or behavioral diagnostics as secondary evidence, such as SimAOU-style representation similarity from [(Dai et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d86ca0894cb4d165eb5ef45b73526ca8b4cdd725) or memorization probes inspired by [(Garg et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de32da8f5c6a50a6c311e9357ba16aa7d05a1bc9), but only after the primary contamination and context-reliance controls are established. Finally, the paper should explicitly state that no-context prompting tests reliance on supplied context and may reveal contamination, but it does not prove contamination without overlap, held-out unseen data, or out-of-domain transfer evidence, consistent with the limitations in [(Kumar et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3f387fa3cbfcb6255ac93786b6027eb86eba580) and [(Wei et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff0b2681d7b05e16c46dfb71d980cc2f605907cd).

## Script Transliteration: Convert high-performing problems from Latin script to non-Latin scripts (Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, Armenian) to test script-independence of reasoning. This ablation examines whether linguistic reasoning persists when the writing system changes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed LINGUINI script transliteration ablation comes from [(Jung et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc125af6e7071aae2ed2b46f1d45ea9adc42d916), which extends the earlier controlled transliteration design in [(Muller et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29599829d26b4acaaf0ad88698ed4362d33b2ae7) with quantitative representation-level evidence. Jung et al. directly manipulate writing-system representation by comparing subword, character, and phoneme inputs for multilingual language models and evaluate POS tagging, NER, and XNLI. On XNLI, the phoneme-based model had lower English accuracy than the subword and character baselines, 71.89 versus 80.80 and 75.02, but it showed smaller performance gaps to low-resource languages: for Swahili the relative and absolute gap from English was 16.59 and 11.01, compared with 24.87 and 17.87 for subword and 22.71 and 15.30 for character, while for Urdu the gap was 24.67 and 15.79, compared with 27.01 and 19.23 for subword and 28.08 and 18.47 for character. The subword model outperformed the phoneme model by 8.91 points in English, 2.05 points in Swahili, and 5.47 points in Urdu. Across POS and NER, the phoneme-based model reported lower standard deviation and lower average percentage difference across languages, and a correlation analysis of 45 phoneme-based fine-tunes found that performance gap was weakly correlated with English performance, Spearman 0.111 with p=0.560 and Kendall 0.104 with p=0.430, but strongly correlated with representation similarity and linguistic gap metrics, including Sinkhorn distance Spearman 0.681 with p=3.5e-5 and Kendall 0.457 with p=2.0e-4, and CKA Spearman -0.782 with p=3.4e-7 and Kendall -0.577 with p=2.1e-6. The experimental setting used 10 typologically diverse languages, 15 random seeds, 10 percent of the XNLI training set for the correlation study, and hardware including an NVIDIA A5000 for POS, an NVIDIA A40 for NER, and an NVIDIA A6000 for XNLI, with batch size 128, AdamW, and cosine learning-rate scheduling. This supports the LINGUINI plan because it shows that surface writing-system representation is a measurable variable that can change downstream linguistic task performance, which is exactly the confound that the proposed transliteration ablation seeks to test. The next strongest support is methodological and comes from [(Muller et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29599829d26b4acaaf0ad88698ed4362d33b2ae7), which includes a controlled transliteration experiment that changes the writing system of high-resource language data and then evaluates the same downstream linguistic tasks, namely universal POS tagging, dependency parsing, and named entity recognition. This is directly analogous to the LINGUINI proposal to convert Latin-script IOL problems into Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, and Armenian while keeping the reasoning task fixed. The broader Muller et al. setting uses 15 unseen languages not included in mBERT pretraining, raw OSCAR data where available, Wikipedia or other collected sources for a few languages, task-specific fine-tuning, 8-fold cross-validation for small training sets, UDPipe for parsing and POS tagging, and an LSTM-CRF model for NER. However, the supplied excerpt does not report numerical UPOS, LAS, or NER results, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance values for the controlled transliteration experiment, so this support is strong in design but not quantitative. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Zhao et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2507a6924007efbe0c3116048a85108398f23007), which transliterates Lezgian from Cyrillic to Latin and Tsez from Latin to Cyrillic so that typologically related low-resource languages can share a script during training for cross-lingual multi-source transformer gloss generation. The model uses 2 encoder layers, 2 decoder layers, 2 attention heads, 128-dimensional embeddings and hidden states, and batch size 20 for Lezgian, Tsez, and subsampled Arapaho, while the full Arapaho model uses 4 encoder layers, 4 decoder layers, 4 attention heads, 256-dimensional embeddings and hidden states, and batch size 50. Training uses Adam with initial learning rate 0.0005, dropout 0.3, early stopping at a minimum learning rate of 1.0e-6, and inference uses beam search with beam size 5. The paper reports that the multi-source model significantly outperforms a statistical baseline on all evaluation metrics in all three languages in the word-level setting, improves lemma and grammatical tag generation in Tsez and Arapaho compared with a single-source model, but shows no substantial difference in Lezgian, which the authors attribute to very limited training data. This supports the feasibility of Latin-Cyrillic script manipulation and shows that script alignment can affect cross-lingual transfer, but it does not test language-agnostic IOL reasoning. The weakest direct support is [(Gorman et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/278f4f68309fc4ffb83bfc4ba86a0ea005106682), which evaluates grapheme-to-phoneme conversion across fifteen language/script pairs, including Armenian, Georgian, and Modern Greek, using 4,500 examples per language split into 3,600 training, 450 development, and 450 test examples. It compares a pair n-gram finite-state model, an encoder-decoder LSTM with attention, and a transformer sequence-to-sequence model, and evaluates word error rate and phone error rate. The LSTM baseline achieved the best word error rate in nine of fifteen languages, the transformer baseline in four languages, and the pair n-gram baseline was outperformed by both neural baselines on all languages, with at least ten word error rate point gaps in Bulgarian, Georgian, and Korean. The IMS team achieved the lowest macro-averaged word error rate, reported as a three percentage-point absolute and eighteen percent relative improvement over the strongest baseline. This supports the practical viability of the target non-Latin scripts and graphophonemic transcription, but it is a G2P shared task rather than a Latin-to-non-Latin reasoning-problem conversion, and it does not report hardware details, statistical significance tests, or meta-linguistic reasoning performance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caveat is that the newer [(Jung et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc125af6e7071aae2ed2b46f1d45ea9adc42d916) results complicate the earlier conceptual transliteration control in [(Muller et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29599829d26b4acaaf0ad88698ed4362d33b2ae7) by showing that writing-system changes can materially alter model performance rather than leaving linguistic reasoning invariant. The phoneme representation lowered English XNLI accuracy to 71.89 versus 80.80 for subword and 75.02 for character, and the subword model outperformed the phoneme model by 8.91 points in English, 2.05 points in Swahili, and 5.47 points in Urdu, even though the phoneme model reduced low-resource gaps. The correlation analysis, using 45 phoneme-based fine-tunes, 15 seeds, and 10 percent of the XNLI training set, found that performance gaps were weakly correlated with English performance but strongly correlated with representation similarity metrics such as Sinkhorn distance and CKA. This contradicts a simplistic assumption that transliterating LINGUINI problems into Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, or Armenian will isolate reasoning; it may instead introduce tokenization, character-inventory, and representation-gap effects that confound script-independence. A second contradiction is that [(Gorman et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/278f4f68309fc4ffb83bfc4ba86a0ea005106682) demonstrates that grapheme-phoneme conversion is script-specific and error-prone. Across fifteen language/script pairs, the best word error rate system was not always the best phone error rate system, with four languages showing divergent rankings, and neural baselines were not uniformly superior, with LSTM best in nine languages, transformer in four languages, and near ties in Modern Greek and Hungarian. If LINGUINI relies on transliteration or graphophonemic transcription, imperfect script conversion can contaminate the reasoning evaluation, especially for sequence transduction and graphophonemic transcription tasks. The paper also does not convert Latin reasoning problems to non-Latin scripts, so it does not provide direct evidence that reasoning persists after script change. A third contradiction is that [(Zhao et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2507a6924007efbe0c3116048a85108398f23007) shows transliteration can produce weak or inconclusive effects when data are limited. In Lezgian, no substantial difference was observed between multi-source and single-source models, attributed to very limited training data; in the subsampled Arapaho setting with only 2,000 training examples, the baseline achieved slightly higher BLEU and lower word error rate, although both systems performed poorly, and the full Arapaho statistical baseline could not be trained on GPU and completed less than 50 percent of training after 7 days. This suggests that LINGUINI's script ablation could be underpowered if only a small number of high-performing problems are transliterated per script, and that transliteration quality, data size, and model capacity can dominate the measured script effect. The weakest contradiction is that [(Muller et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29599829d26b4acaaf0ad88698ed4362d33b2ae7) does not provide numerical results for its controlled transliteration experiment, so it cannot confirm whether script change improves, harms, or leaves unchanged performance on UPOS, dependency parsing, or NER. Its high-resource, downstream-task setting also differs from LINGUINI's IOL-style language-agnostic reasoning puzzles, limiting direct generalizability.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the transliteration ablation a controlled, high-fidelity manipulation of script only. LINGUINI should keep problem content, contextual clues, answer keys, and task format identical between Latin and Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, and Armenian versions, and should use deterministic orthographic transliteration tables or validated grapheme-to-phoneme and phoneme alignment tools rather than ad hoc model-generated transliteration. Following [(Gorman et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/278f4f68309fc4ffb83bfc4ba86a0ea005106682), the team should report transliteration fidelity with word error rate, character error rate, chrF, and phone error rate where applicable, and should exclude or flag ambiguous, many-to-one, or irreversible mappings that could change the puzzle. This is essential because the proposed method uses exact match accuracy as the primary metric and chrF as a secondary softer metric, so transliteration errors would otherwise be indistinguishable from reasoning failures. The next most important action is to add explicit statistical and baseline controls. Inspired by [(Muller et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29599829d26b4acaaf0ad88698ed4362d33b2ae7) and quantified by [(Jung et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc125af6e7071aae2ed2b46f1d45ea9adc42d916), the experiment should compare Latin and non-Latin versions on the same tasks and report per-script, per-task, and per-model deltas rather than only aggregate accuracy. It should include high-resource control transliterations if possible, use the planned 0-5 in-context examples and leave-one-out cross-validation, and test significance with paired bootstrap confidence intervals, McNemar tests, or mixed-effects models over problems, models, and shots. If open-source models are run with stochastic decoding, multiple seeds or repeated generations should be reported, mirroring the 15-seed and correlation-based analysis in Jung et al. A third action is to add representation-level controls to disentangle script from phonology and tokenization. Jung et al. show that phoneme-based input can reduce cross-lingual gaps while lowering high-resource accuracy, so LINGUINI should consider an IPA or character-level condition, or at least report tokenization and character-inventory statistics for each script. If feasible, include subword, character, and phoneme prompts for a subset of problems, and use representation similarity or linguistic gap metrics to interpret whether performance changes track script, phonology, or model representation. A fourth action is to ensure adequate sample size and transparent implementation details. [(Zhao et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2507a6924007efbe0c3116048a85108398f23007) shows that low data can make transliteration effects inconclusive, so LINGUINI should report the number of high-performing problems per script and task, avoid making strong claims from very small transliterated subsets, and use macro-averaged and micro-averaged exact match and chrF. The paper should also report model names, sizes, hardware, batch size, decoding parameters, temperature, top-p, beam size, and prompt templates, because script effects can be sensitive to tokenizer and inference setup. A final, less critical action is to integrate the transliteration ablation with the other LINGUINI ablations, especially no-context prompting and resource-level analysis, to determine whether script effects interact with contamination, language resourcefulness, or in-context grammar learning. This will help distinguish whether non-Latin script performance drops reflect script-specific priors, transliteration artifacts, or genuine limits in language-agnostic linguistic reasoning.

## Resource Level Analysis: Examine correlation between language resourcefulness (measured by speaker count and online presence) and model performance. This analysis tests whether model performance is driven by language resourcefulness rather than language-agnostic reasoning.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Chang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d23b8bce8d8abeab50fce08b6b7628ebb7de4d08), which directly demonstrates that language-resource level, operationalized as available monolingual training-data size, strongly shapes model performance across 350 languages and 1154 Transformer models up to 125M parameters. Its FLORES log-perplexity results show a clear data-size gradient: mean log-perplexity of 76.9 for 73 languages with 1000MB, 102.7 for 22 languages with 100MB, and 130.5 for 5 languages with 10MB/5MB, versus bigram baselines of 112.3, 132.6, and 148.3, and its MultiBLiMP results show monolingual models outperforming several multilingual baselines on average and achieving highest accuracy in 25 of 74 languages. This supports the proposed Resource Level Analysis because it shows that performance differences across languages can be tied to measurable resource quantities rather than only to model architecture or task difficulty, and its contamination control (excluding FLORES-200/AmericasNLI and finding <10/2000 FLORES sequences in training for 98% of languages) is a useful template for ensuring LINGUINI performance is not driven by memorized benchmark items. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Hasan et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fcf5e276368c17d74e55d626a5ce5841bf64cdc), which evaluates GPT-4, Llama 2, and Gemini Pro zero-shot on XNLI, SemEval-2017 Task 4 sentiment, and hate speech across English, Bangla, Hindi, and Urdu, and reports large English advantages in NLI and hate speech (e.g., GPT-4 English 18.04%, 17.38%, and 22.81% better than Bangla, Hindi, and Urdu; Llama 2 English F1 32.52%, 31.28%, and 29.94% higher) as well as sentiment gaps for GPT-4 and Llama 2. Although this work does not measure speaker count or online presence and uses only four languages, it supports the broader hypothesis that LLM performance varies with language resourcefulness and motivates stratifying LINGUINI by resource level.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither paper directly validates the proposed claim that performance on language-agnostic linguistic reasoning is driven by resourcefulness rather than language-agnostic reasoning; both evaluate language-specific or knowledge-dependent tasks, not controlled IOL-style puzzles where all necessary information is in context. [(Chang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d23b8bce8d8abeab50fce08b6b7628ebb7de4d08) uses FLORES perplexity and MultiBLiMP, which can rely on language-specific lexical, syntactic, and cultural knowledge, and its resource variable is training-data size, not speaker count or online presence, so it cannot establish that sociolinguistic resourcefulness predicts LINGUINI exact-match or chrF performance. A second contradiction is that resourcefulness is not a uniform predictor: [(Hasan et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fcf5e276368c17d74e55d626a5ce5841bf64cdc) reports that Gemini sentiment performance was nearly flat across languages and that Bangla, Hindi, and Urdu F1 were 0.49%, 0.89%, and 0.60% higher than English, and it describes low-resource differences among Bangla, Hindi, and Urdu as insignificant, suggesting that task subjectivity, cultural context, prompt handling, and model-specific artifacts can obscure or reverse resource-level effects. A third, weaker contradiction is that [(Chang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d23b8bce8d8abeab50fce08b6b7628ebb7de4d08) shows small monolingual models can outperform much larger multilingual models (e.g., lower perplexity than XGLM 4.5B for 60 of 102 languages, XGLM 7.5B for 32 of 46, BLOOM 7.1B for 2 of 30, and MaLA-500 10B for 111 of 175), indicating that model specialization and data-size scaling can dominate simple resource rankings; if LINGUINI uses heterogeneous open and proprietary models, speaker count or online presence alone may be confounded by model-specific language support, prompt sensitivity, and safety filtering.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to operationalize resourcefulness with multiple directly measurable variables and test them statistically rather than relying on a single qualitative notion: collect speaker-count estimates, online-presence metrics (e.g., Common Crawl/web token counts, Wikipedia article size, or language-specific corpus size), script/orthography type, and model support status for each of the 75 LINGUINI languages, then compute Spearman/Pearson correlations and mixed-effects regressions of exact-match and chrF on resource variables while controlling for model family, task category, script, context length, few-shot count, and no-context ablation. The second priority is to adopt the contamination and data-control practices from [(Chang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d23b8bce8d8abeab50fce08b6b7628ebb7de4d08): verify that IOL items and their source languages are absent from training data where possible, exclude same-language examples in few-shot prompts, report per-language sample sizes, and use the no-context prompting ablation to separate memorized language knowledge from in-context reasoning. The third priority is to address the task-confound and model-artifact issues highlighted by [(Hasan et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fcf5e276368c17d74e55d626a5ce5841bf64cdc): standardize prompts across models, explicitly handle refusals, unpredicted outputs, repeated labels, and safety blocks, report accuracy/precision/recall/macro-F1 or at least exact-match with confidence intervals, and use statistical tests or permutation-based significance because the 75-language, multi-model design will produce many comparisons. Finally, use Chang's data-size gradient as a sanity check by stratifying LINGUINI languages into resource bins and reporting whether performance changes monotonically with resource level; if speaker count and online presence do not predict performance after controlling for training-data availability or task type, the method should report that resourcefulness is not the dominant driver of language-agnostic reasoning.

## Grammar Book Integration: Test models' ability to acquire linguistic proficiency through in-context textbooks, scaling previous single-language studies to multiple languages and task types. This ablation evaluates whether models can learn language-specific rules from in-context instructional material.

### Support

The strongest direct support for the Grammar Book Integration ablation is [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af705d648b5b16daa3dcc593bc593f2574d76c07), which places a formal grammar G in the prompt and uses Earley-based constrained decoding to generate outputs in L(G); the paper reports that grammar prompting improves over standard prompting and that constraints guarantee syntactic validity, which is directly analogous to giving models an in-context rule book for IOL-style sequence transduction, fill-in-blank, and transliteration tasks. The strongest quantitative support for in-context rule acquisition more generally is [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), where GPT-3 models from 125M to 175B with a 2048-token context learned character-manipulation rules from zero, one, or few demonstrations, with the 175B model reaching 66.9% on random insertion removal, 38.6% on cycling letters, 40.2% on easier anagram recovery, and 15.1% on harder anagram recovery, while Winogrande improved from 70.2% zero-shot to 77.7% few-shot; this supports the plan's zero-to-few-shot design but also shows that rule learning is task-dependent. Mechanistic support comes from [(Oswald et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/525d93a382f6e7873b5d8a2e0713eb3dff7fb250), which shows that a single linear self-attention layer trained on synthetic linear regression tasks approximates one step of gradient descent and can apply the inferred rule to a new query token, with N=10, noiseless teacher data, and Adam optimization; this supports the hypothesis that attention models can infer contextual rules, although the setting is far simpler than natural-language morphology. Additional support is provided by [(Dinu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b02e8ce41cab6656af38dc0d65b61f56bbb91072), whose SymbolicAI associative-prediction tasks use pseudo-grammars and few-shot examples, with at most 4096-token context and 8 seeds per test, and reports GPT-4 Turbo on par with Gemini-1.0 Pro while open-source models such as LLaMA3-Chat 70B lag, indicating that in-context associations can be evaluated but that model class matters. Finally, [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8) provides the closest single-language implementation precedent by inserting Quechua grammar descriptions into Spanish prompts for 50 Quechua-Spanish sentence pairs and comparing them with baseline, MORPH, CORPUS, and combined conditions, showing that the proposed ablation is feasible even if its effect is not guaranteed.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8), whose GRAMMAR condition inserted Quechua grammar descriptions into Spanish prompts for 50 Quechua-Spanish sentence pairs and found only a minor or negative effect on BLEURT relative to baseline, while MORPH improved BLEURT for all models; this directly challenges the assumption that adding a grammar book will improve in-context linguistic proficiency, especially for translation-like sequence transduction. A second major contradiction is [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), which shows that in-context rule learning is fragile: zero-shot performance was near chance, one-shot performance dropped by half or more relative to few-shot, the 175B model failed to reverse words, achieved only 15.1% on harder anagram recovery, and Winogrande few-shot performance of 77.7% remained below fine-tuned RoBERTA at 79% and T5 at 84.6%; this warns that 0-5 IOL examples plus a grammar book may not be enough for complex morphophonological or graphophonemic tasks. Third, [(Oswald et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/525d93a382f6e7873b5d8a2e0713eb3dff7fb250) shows out-of-distribution degradation when input range or teacher-weight scale is changed, with no statistical significance tests and only N=10 synthetic regression tokens, suggesting that in-context rule inference may not transfer across the 75 low-resource languages and task types unless the grammar book and examples are tightly aligned with the test distribution. Fourth, [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af705d648b5b16daa3dcc593bc593f2574d76c07) provides only qualitative evidence and relies on formal grammars plus Earley constrained decoding, so it does not support the stronger claim that natural-language grammar books alone will improve unconstrained IOL puzzle solving. Finally, [(Dinu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b02e8ce41cab6656af38dc0d65b61f56bbb91072) reports a large gap between closed-source and open-source models on associative-prediction tasks with pseudo-grammars, without exact VERTEX values, implying that open-source models in the proposed Llama, Mistral, and Gemma coverage may struggle and that symbolic association success may not translate to linguistic proficiency.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make Grammar Book Integration a controlled ablation over instructional format and decoding constraints rather than a single raw-textbook condition: provide full textbook, formal rule table, morpheme glosses, examples, and combined conditions, and where IOL outputs are deterministic (number transliteration, script-constrained sequence transduction, fill-in-blanks with known inventories) use constrained decoding, Earley/regex validation, or post-hoc filtering, following [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af705d648b5b16daa3dcc593bc593f2574d76c07) and addressing the negative grammar result in [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8). Second, expand the few-shot schedule beyond 0-5 when context permits, including 10 or 20 examples and reporting per-shot curves, because [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) found one-shot can be worse than few-shot and zero-shot near chance; use leave-one-out but also relevance-ranked examples to avoid contamination, maximize rule coverage, and calibrate the distribution of in-context rules to the test items to reduce out-of-distribution degradation identified by [(Oswald et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/525d93a382f6e7873b5d8a2e0713eb3dff7fb250). Third, add retrieval baselines from [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8), including no-context, examples only, grammar only, MORPH/lexicon only, CORPUS examples, manual versus automatic retrieval, and combined grammar-plus-lexicon-plus-examples, so the benchmark can distinguish whether gains come from grammar, lexical evidence, or examples. Fourth, strengthen statistical and reproducibility reporting by using multiple seeds (at least 8, as in [(Dinu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b02e8ce41cab6656af38dc0d65b61f56bbb91072)), confidence intervals, significance tests, per-language and per-task breakdowns, exact model versions, context windows, prompt templates, grammar-book construction, retrieval parameters, and hardware; keep exact match as primary and chrF as secondary, but add error typology or human validation for a subset, as in [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8). Fifth, explicitly test tokenization and script effects by including the planned Cyrillic, Greek, Georgian, and Armenian transliterations and character-level controls, because [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) showed BPE tokenization can hinder character manipulation, and because the proposed low-resource IOL tasks may depend on script-specific segmentation. Finally, analyze resource level and open/closed model gaps, since [(Dinu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b02e8ce41cab6656af38dc0d65b61f56bbb91072) found open-source models lagged on associative tasks and [(Court, and Elsner, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e0bf0c7af09a17738458ae1059d9e08b4cdc9ea8) used a single low-resource language pair, so the benchmark should report whether grammar-book gains vary with speaker count, online presence, script, and model class.
