## Core neural data collection from approximately 100 MT neurons: We will record extracellularly from isolated neurons in area MT of macaque monkeys while they perform fixation tasks. For each neuron, we will characterize direction tuning, map receptive fields, and measure speed tuning to single speeds before testing responses to bi-speed stimuli.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed core data-collection method comes from the newer macaque recording study [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb), which documents extracellular neural recordings from three macaque animals using a general setup, spatial mapping, tuning, and reverse correlation, and provides firing-rate, interspike-interval, eye-data, raster-plot, trial-histogram, and example spatial-mapping/tuning analyses. This directly parallels the proposed plan to record isolated macaque neurons during fixation, first map receptive fields, characterize direction tuning, and then proceed to bi-speed testing, because it shows that the same preliminary characterization pipeline is feasible in macaques and that eye data and trial-level firing-rate summaries are appropriate for fixation-based single-neuron work. The next strongest support is from [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512), which provides a likelihood-based cross-validation framework for probabilistic spike-response models p(y|x,theta) fitted by maximum likelihood, comparing pA(y*|x*,thetaA) and pB(y*|x*,thetaB) on independent test data or held-out data. This supports the downstream computational modeling and model-selection components of the plan, especially after collecting approximately 100 MT neurons, because it gives a principled way to compare candidate bi-speed encoding rules such as averaging, soft-max, slow-speed bias, fast-speed bias, and the modified divisive normalization model without relying on training-set fit alone. The weakest support is that both works are indirect for the specific proposed method: [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb) does not explicitly report area MT, speed tuning, bi-speed stimuli, or a 100-neuron target, and [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512) is a conceptual validation method with a toy Gaussian/mixture example rather than an empirical MT dataset, so neither provides direct quantitative evidence that the proposed 100-neuron MT speed-tuning and bi-speed protocol will succeed.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction, or more precisely the strongest limitation that undermines the proposed method as stated, is that the newer macaque recording study [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb) does not provide evidence for the critical MT-specific and speed-specific steps in the plan. It reports spatial mapping, direction tuning, reverse correlation, firing rates, ISIs, eye data, raster plots, and trial histograms, but it does not report area MT, speed tuning to single speeds, bi-speed stimuli, speed ratios, fixation-task performance, electrode type, sampling rate, neuron count, or statistical outcomes; therefore it cannot be used to validate the assumption that preliminary direction and receptive-field characterization is sufficient before testing bi-speed encoding in MT. The next strongest contradiction is the overfitting warning in [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512): its toy example shows that a more parameterized model can obtain higher training likelihood while assigning very low probability to new data, which contradicts any analysis of the proposed weighted-sum or modified normalization models that relies only on fit to the same 100-neuron dataset without independent held-out or newly generated bi-speed test trials. This is especially relevant because the plan compares several candidate encoding rules and a modified divisive normalization model, all of which can be overfitted if model selection is not based on independent likelihood evaluation. The weakest contradiction is the absence of quantitative reproducibility details in both works: [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb) gives no neuron count, hardware/system specs, hyperparameters, or statistical significance, and [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512) gives no dataset size, species, recording hardware, stimulus parameters, regularization strength, or empirical performance comparison, so neither provides direct evidence that the proposed approximately 100 MT neuron experiment can be reproduced with the stated precision.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add an explicit likelihood-based cross-validation protocol, motivated by [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512), for every candidate encoding model tested on the approximately 100 MT neurons. The plan should fit averaging, soft-max, slow-speed bias, fast-speed bias, and modified divisive normalization models on a training subset of single-speed and bi-speed trials, then compare their log-likelihoods on independent held-out trials or newly presented bi-speed stimuli, reporting parameter counts, confidence intervals, and significance tests such as likelihood-ratio tests, permutation tests, or AIC/BIC where appropriate. The second most important action is to operationalize the macaque recording and preliminary characterization pipeline using the methodological details implied by the newer empirical work [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb), but extended to MT and speed tuning: specify electrode type, sampling rate, spike-sorting criteria, fixation thresholds, eye-data quality control, receptive-field mapping metrics, direction-tuning indices, speed-tuning curves, and inclusion criteria for the 100 neurons. The third action is to align the speed-tuning and bi-speed stimulus design with the psychophysical speed pairs, using the stated 4:1 and 2:1 ratios and speed ranges from 1.25 to 80 deg/s, with single-speed and bi-speed trials randomly interleaved and sufficient trial counts to estimate response weights. The fourth action is to add the proposed control conditions, including 90-degree direction-separation controls and attention-control trials in the opposite visual field, and to evaluate population-level encoding with cross-validated linear classifiers and pseudo-population analyses. The least important but still useful action is to report full reproducibility details, including hardware/system specs, number of monkeys, neuron count per monkey, trial numbers, stimulus parameters, fixation requirements, and data availability, so that the method can be compared with the macaque recording precedent in [(Wild et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/07aa74fcc4bb9da49fdb117f36166988d74769eb) and the model-validation standard in [(Pillow, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371b1a357007fb7c42763c8bd95d8c9ac9f8c512).
