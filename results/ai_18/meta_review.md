## Context: This is the central plan that organizes the entire research effort around a reusable urban embodied-AI benchmark. Method: We will construct a comprehensive benchmark platform consisting of three main components: a 3D simulation environment, agent interfaces, and systematic benchmark tasks.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed EmbodiedCity platform is that reusable 3D embodied benchmarks with a simulator, agent interface, task sampling, and objective metrics are feasible and informative. [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) built a Matterport3D visual-language navigation benchmark with 90 scenes, 10,800 viewpoints, 21,567 instructions, RGB observations, success within 3 m, RANDOM 13.2%, human 86.4%, and a sequence-to-sequence agent 20.4% test success, with seen 38.6% and unseen 21.8%, directly supporting the proposed embodied action task, instruction annotation, success threshold, and seen/unseen evaluation. [(Gupta et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbdcf09754b9b96694f88c8fb3fbebc2d338fc2) supports the proposed SR, SPL, and NE metrics and the need for sampled navigation tasks, showing CMP reached 62.5% RGB and 78.3% depth success, SPL 59.4% and 73.7%, while nearest-neighbor transfer had 22 and 25 step mean and median distance to goal, demonstrating train-test mismatch and the value of depth over RGB. [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) supports embodied question answering by grounding questions in scene context and agent situation, using 650 ScanNet scenes, 20.4k situations, and 33.4k questions, with multiple-choice accuracy and localization thresholds; ScanQA reached 47.2% versus 90.06% human, and zero-shot GPT-3 reached 39.67% only on What questions with ScanRefer captions, supporting the proposed QA and LLM evaluation while also requiring spatial metrics. [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) supports urban LLM benchmarking through a real-city data pipeline, API and natural-language interfaces, and three-level urban tasks, showing that general LLMs lack urban domain knowledge and that task-specific success rates are needed, which aligns with the proposed urban tasks and MLLM evaluation. [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151) supports the 3D simulation environment and agent interfaces by providing a 572-building, 1447-floor, 211k m^2 scanned-space simulator with Gym-style APIs, view synthesis, and RL tasks; it reports 77.7, 30.6, and 14.5 FPS on a Tesla V100 and sim-real action-logit L2 of 0.204 for filler-to-backward-mapped real images, supporting reproducible simulation and sim-real validation. [(Bellemare et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f82e4ff4f003581330338aaae71f60316e58dd26) supports standardized benchmark protocol, baseline agents, normalization, and the finding that no single method dominates, with learning agents beating baselines in 40/55 games and search methods in 49/55 games, which justifies multiple baselines and cross-task normalization in EmbodiedCity. Weaker but useful support comes from [(Chang et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8337441971f941716a9e525a67f37088eb01fd13) for real RGB-D scene capture and scene splits, [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d) for synthetic scene ground truth and voxel-level evaluation, [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224), [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), and [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) for city-scale geometry, OSM-derived semantic maps, multi-view camera trajectories, and visual realism metrics, and from [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) for aspect-level MLLM evaluation of embodied task planning. The remaining works, [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c), [(Savinov et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0d96d1ea69855a5a3abf614f17095c29b3339a4), [(Savva et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a35e548de27b6924e5f2ee41d37238a5c4a1d5), [(Kadian et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f1f19bfddf103e4e3ac74610f84d944496406b3), [(Ramakrishnan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14c88059503461afdab6d59c3e56914b6690eb02), and [(Zhao et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4820eb7607c8f8de2b9bdab3918035ef1c038), provide weaker but relevant support for disentangling navigation from QA, unseen-environment trials, generic 3D APIs, sim2real correlation, exploration metrics, and digital-twin validation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that current MLLMs and LLMs are still weak on grounded embodied QA and planning, so the proposed evaluation may reveal large gaps rather than strong performance. [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) reports best trained 3D model 47.2% versus 90.06% human, zero-shot GPT-3 not reasonable overall, and only 39.67% on What questions with ScanRefer captions, while [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) reports GPT-4V planning success of 19% on 100 general-description tasks, MiniCPM 0/270, temporal perception below 30% for most models, and only 80% planning accuracy after injecting object, spatio-temporal, and task knowledge. This contradicts any assumption that Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, or GPT-4 Turbo will reliably solve urban embodied tasks without explicit spatial context, tools, or fine-tuning. A second major contradiction is that simulation-based evaluation can be misleading if physics, collisions, rendering, or sim-real transfer are not validated. [(Kadian et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f1f19bfddf103e4e3ac74610f84d944496406b3) identifies cheating by sliding and proposes SRCC to measure whether simulation ranks predict real robot ranks, while [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151) shows view-synthesis artifacts and sim-real gaps, with filler-to-real action-logit L2 of 0.300 and source-to-real 0.242 versus 0.204 for backward-mapped real images. The proposed plan does not yet specify real-world validation, SRCC, collision or anti-cheating checks, or traffic-dynamics validation, which is a risk for a 2.8 km by 2.4 km urban benchmark. Third, indoor navigation evidence contradicts the sufficiency of static, manually annotated trajectories and RGB-centric MLLM evaluation. [(Gupta et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbdcf09754b9b96694f88c8fb3fbebc2d338fc2) shows depth improves success from 62.5% to 78.3% and SPL from 59.4% to 73.7%, and train-test mismatch causes large distance to goal, while [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) shows unseen-environment success drops to 21.8% versus 38.6% seen. The proposed plan should therefore include unseen streets or buildings, dynamic traffic, sensor noise, and depth, segmentation, or LiDAR ablations rather than relying only on manual optimal trajectories. Fourth, the proposed text-generation metrics are not sufficient for embodied spatial reasoning. [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) uses multiple-choice accuracy and localization thresholds, and [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) uses aspect-level scores and manual plan scoring, whereas BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT can reward fluent but spatially wrong answers. Fifth, city-scale realism is not guaranteed by manual UE modeling or generative assets. [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) reports FID 108.47 and KID 0.084 for satellite-view synthesis, [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) provides only qualitative realism evidence, and [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) reports relative FID, KID, depth-error, and GPT-4 preference gains without agent-task validation, indicating that visual realism metrics and semantic consistency checks are needed. Weaker contradictions include the fact that many related benchmarks are indoor, static, graph-based, or API-based rather than outdoor urban embodied settings, as in [(Chang et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8337441971f941716a9e525a67f37088eb01fd13), [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d), [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c), [(Savinov et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0d96d1ea69855a5a3abf614f17095c29b3339a4), [(Savva et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a35e548de27b6924e5f2ee41d37238a5c4a1d5), [(Ramakrishnan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14c88059503461afdab6d59c3e56914b6690eb02), and [(Zhao et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4820eb7607c8f8de2b9bdab3918035ef1c038), so their direct transfer to the proposed Beijing city, Mirage traffic, AirSim sensors, and drone or vehicle action spaces is limited.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize the benchmark evaluation protocol before model testing: define train, validation, and test splits by street, building, or district, specify repeated trials, success thresholds, episode lengths, action frequencies, and baseline agents such as random, shortest-path, rule-based, SLAM, oracle, and MLLM with or without tools, and report cross-task normalization and statistical significance, following [(Bellemare et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f82e4ff4f003581330338aaae71f60316e58dd26), [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc), and [(Gupta et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbdcf09754b9b96694f88c8fb3fbebc2d338fc2). Second, replace or supplement BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT with embodied-specific metrics: multiple-choice accuracy, distance, position, and counting error, localization Acc@0.5m, Acc@1.0m, Acc@15 degrees, and Acc@30 degrees, aspect-level QA scores, manual plan satisfaction, SR, SPL, NE, collision rate, and time-to-success, as in [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b), [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f), and [(Gupta et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbdcf09754b9b96694f88c8fb3fbebc2d338fc2). Third, validate the simulation environment and sim2real transfer: add environment realism metrics such as FID, KID, depth error, camera error, semantic or instance IoU, and human evaluation; compare rendered views with Baidu or Amap street view; run SRCC or a small real drone or robot validation; and audit Mirage traffic, collision, and anti-cheating behavior, motivated by [(Kadian et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f1f19bfddf103e4e3ac74610f84d944496406b3), [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224), [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), and [(Zhao et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37c4820eb7607c8f8de2b9bdab3918035ef1c038). Fourth, ground tasks in urban semantics and agent state: use OSM, POI, land-use, mobility, and semantic or instance labels to generate distance, position, counting, dialogue, and planning questions, and include situation descriptions, agent pose, and optional API access, following [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17), [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), and [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b). Fifth, design difficulty and generalization controls: sample starts and goals with path-length and edge-count constraints, include seen and unseen environments, dynamic traffic, occlusion, sensor noise, and depth, segmentation, or LiDAR ablations, as supported by [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc), [(Gupta et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5cbdcf09754b9b96694f88c8fb3fbebc2d338fc2), and [(Ramakrishnan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14c88059503461afdab6d59c3e56914b6690eb02). Sixth, provide reproducible interfaces and documentation: a Gym-like or AirSim Python SDK, HTTP proxy, observation and action schemas, headless FPS, hardware, hyperparameters, annotation protocol, and public baselines, following [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), [(Savva et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a35e548de27b6924e5f2ee41d37238a5c4a1d5), and [(Bellemare et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f82e4ff4f003581330338aaae71f60316e58dd26). Seventh, use generative or scanned urban assets to supplement manual UE modeling, but only after validating interactive consistency, using [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f), [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), and [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) as references for OSM-to-3D, MLLM asset refinement, and multi-view consistency. Finally, following [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c) and [(Savinov et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c0d96d1ea69855a5a3abf614f17095c29b3339a4), report navigation and QA separately, use repeated trials in unseen environments, and include human baselines and failure-mode analysis, because human performance in [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) and [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) is far above current models, and failure modes in [(Bellemare et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f82e4ff4f003581330338aaae71f60316e58dd26) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) show that no single method or prompt strategy dominates.

## Context: This creates the core outdoor city environment needed to move embodied agents beyond indoor rooms. Method: We will build a highly realistic simulation based on a 2.8km × 2.4km commercial district in Beijing, China, using Unreal Engine 5.3.

### Support

The strongest support for building the proposed 2.8km by 2.4km Beijing commercial district in Unreal Engine 5.3 comes from [(Crosato et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f11da10357164348d5d03073995efa53fefd03c), which used Unreal Engine 4.26 and an HTC Vive to create a 1:1-scale single-lane loop road 3.65m wide, collect 8 hours of effective driver-pedestrian trajectory data from 16 pairs, split the data into 11 training, 3 validation, and 2 test pairs, and train an ST-GCNN/TXP-CNN model with PReLU activations, dropout, regularization, batch size 32, stochastic gradient descent for 250 epochs, initial learning rate 0.01 with linear decay, and an ablation favoring 2 ST-GCNN and 5 TXP-CNN layers with reciprocal L2 distance weighting; the best model achieved 0.17 m ADE and 0.25 m FDE for pedestrians and 0.85 m ADE and 1.39 m FDE for vehicles over a 2.4 s horizon. This directly demonstrates that an Unreal Engine outdoor environment can support embodied agent interaction, trajectory recording, and quantitative evaluation, which is relevant to the proposed AirSim-based RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, and LiDAR interfaces and embodied action tasks. The next strongest support is [(Dubs et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/472f789f8bd2f38294b45fc506b7bc0d5b7a92d1), which used Unreal Engine to model a photo-realistic Florida Polytechnic campus and electric golf cart while running vehicle dynamics externally on a Speedgoat accelerator over 1-GigE links, using real drive-by-wire data from a Ford Fusion Plug-In Hybrid collected on May 4, 2022, with throttle, brake, and speed inputs, a smoothed speed differentiation step, and a linear acceleration model with a -0.190 air-friction constant. This supports the proposed separation of Unreal Engine 5.3 rendering from Mirage traffic dynamics, although the Dubs system is campus-scale and lacks city-scale validation. Further support comes from [(Dosovitskiy et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf0615fc4d98cf1dbe527c79146ce1e50dce9af), which evaluated modular pipeline, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning agents on four urban navigation tasks across two towns and weather sets using 25 test episodes per task, town, and weather set, with a time budget equal to optimal-path travel time at 10 km/h and infractions logged but not terminating episodes; its observation that weather generalization was easier than town generalization supports the proposed need for realistic urban geometry, street layout, and traffic patterns. The weakest support is [(Xu et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06a8b5869a1e22ead1cf6b4479b9ff53f8a4f1df), which built a Unity-based shared-street VR environment using SketchUp, Blender, Substance textures, and scripted pedestrian-vehicle interaction, and found statistically significant scenario differences in human ratings from 32 valid questionnaires with Cronbach alpha 0.86, ANOVA p-values of 0.0031 for pedestrians and 0.0002 for drivers, and a Spearman safety-walking correlation of 0.744, but it does not use Unreal Engine 5.3, does not scale to 2.8km by 2.4km, and does not provide agent APIs or LLM/VLM benchmark evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that a single 2.8km by 2.4km Beijing district may be insufficient for a comprehensive embodied benchmark, because [(Dosovitskiy et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf0615fc4d98cf1dbe527c79146ce1e50dce9af) showed that performance on the two most challenging navigation tasks fell by at least a factor of two when moving from the training town to the test town, while new-weather generalization was comparatively easier; on the hardest dynamic-obstacle task, the best modular pipeline and imitation learning methods were below 90% success in all conditions and below 45% success in the test town, implying that urban geometry, street layout, and local visual context matter more than weather alone, so the proposed benchmark should include held-out subregions, unseen streets, or a second district to test transfer. A second major contradiction is the lack of evidence that Unreal Engine-based outdoor simulation can be scaled to the proposed 200 buildings, 100 streets, 50km of streets, and over 6,000 urban elements while supporting AirSim-style multimodal observations and Mirage traffic; [(Crosato et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f11da10357164348d5d03073995efa53fefd03c) used only a 3.65m-wide single-lane loop with two agents and no traffic lights or zebra crossings, and [(Dubs et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/472f789f8bd2f38294b45fc506b7bc0d5b7a92d1) used a campus-scale golf cart testbed with no quantitative accuracy metrics, baseline comparisons, statistical significance tests, or city-scale validation. A third contradiction is that realistic street simulation alone does not guarantee a valid embodied benchmark, because [(Xu et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06a8b5869a1e22ead1cf6b4479b9ff53f8a4f1df) demonstrated human perception evaluation in a small Unity/VR shared-street environment but reported participant dropout due to dizziness, limited hardware details, and no agent interfaces, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, depth, segmentation, or LLM tasks. The weakest contradiction is that the related work does not evaluate the proposed multi-modal large language models such as Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, or GPT-4 Turbo on urban embodied tasks, so the assumption that BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, Sentence-BERT, SR, SPL, and NE will be sufficient for the five proposed tasks remains unsupported by these studies.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to redesign the benchmark evaluation to include generalization tests beyond one Beijing district: split the 2.8km by 2.4km district into training, validation, and test zones, add at least one unseen commercial district or street subgraph, vary weather, time of day, and dynamic traffic, and report SR, SPL, NE, BLEU-1 to 4, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT separately for each condition, following the town- and weather-generalization evidence in [(Dosovitskiy et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebf0615fc4d98cf1dbe527c79146ce1e50dce9af). The second most important action is to add quantitative validation of the Unreal Engine 5.3 environment itself: report GPU/CPU/memory specifications, rendering FPS, AirSim latency, Mirage traffic throughput, building count, street length, element count, and realism comparisons against Baidu Map and Amap streetview references, using statistical tests where appropriate, because [(Dubs et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/472f789f8bd2f38294b45fc506b7bc0d5b7a92d1) and [(Crosato et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f11da10357164348d5d03073995efa53fefd03c) show that Unreal Engine outdoor systems need explicit performance and accuracy reporting. The third action is to document the full pipeline for reproducibility: Blender modeling workflow, Unreal Engine 5.3 asset and lighting settings, Mirage vehicle and pedestrian parameters, AirSim observation and action APIs, Python SDK/HTTP proxy configuration, random location sampling, annotation protocol, and model evaluation code. The fourth action is to include human or VLM-based realism and usability validation, inspired by [(Xu et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06a8b5869a1e22ead1cf6b4479b9ff53f8a4f1df), using questionnaires, ANOVA, and correlation analysis to assess comfort, safety, and realism, while mitigating VR discomfort if head-mounted displays are used. The least important but still useful action is to add trajectory-level metrics such as ADE and FDE from [(Crosato et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f11da10357164348d5d03073995efa53fefd03c) for embodied action or navigation subtasks, especially when evaluating drone or ground-vehicle control, so that the benchmark can measure both language quality and physical interaction accuracy.

## Context: This provides realistic building geometry and visual fidelity for the urban simulation. Method: We will manually create 3D models of approximately 200 buildings using Blender, referencing streetview services from Baidu Map and Amap.

### Support

The strongest support for manually creating approximately 200 Blender buildings from Baidu Map and Amap street view is that newer work validates Blender and street-view references as a route to realistic urban assets: [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) uses Blender 3.2.2 with Blosm, a fine-tuned urban MLLM trained on about 300K Google Maps street-view images, Stable Diffusion 1.5 with ControlNet-Depth, IP-Adapter, ControlNet-inpainting, and ControlNet-tile on a single NVIDIA Tesla A100, four camera views per asset, 30 inference steps, and smart-projection UV unwrapping, and reports 5.4% FID improvement, 26.7% KID improvement, 44.2% depth-error improvement, 10.4% homogeneity-index improvement, and higher GPT-4 preference over SGAM, PersistentNature, SceneDreamer, and CityDreamer. This supports the proposed method's core assumption that Blender-based urban assets and street-view references can provide the visual fidelity needed for an embodied city benchmark, although the related work is automated rather than manual. The next strongest support is large-scale real-imagery texturing: [(Buyukdemircioglu, and Elberink, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf68a6229c21760e2ecd4abb9d278ae2b40637bb) maps 48,497 textures for 1,346 LOD2.2 CityJSON buildings in 3 minutes 16 seconds at 247 surfaces per second on a Dell Precision 3561 with i7 11800H and 16 GB RAM, using 35 oblique and 6 nadir aerial images of Enschede with 5 cm GSD, showing that real imagery can rapidly improve visual realism for many buildings. This supports the plan's visual-fidelity goal but also suggests that manual texture creation in Blender should be supplemented by automated texture extraction. Data-driven street-view and CAD pipelines further support the feasibility of using real-world references at city scale: [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) builds a 3D city from GPS-registered CAD and 14,612 HoliCity London street-view images, uses octrees, top-down category/depth/normal maps, and reports satellite-map synthesis FID 108.47 and KID 0.084 ± 0.001 versus GSN FID 333.92 and KID 0.325 ± 0.002, although it does not report final 3D building fidelity or embodied-task performance. Automated generation also supports the broader goal of realistic urban simulation: [(Deng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b538c3e66f1456f5c7b4f7cb34523905d297a43) uses DiT-B/2, GPT-4o-2024-05-13, GPT-4-1106-preview, and GPT-4-vision-preview on 8 Nvidia 4090-24GB GPUs, achieving layout FID 27.60 and KID 0.022 versus CityGen FID 88.38 and KID 0.089, user preference 8.6, and scene Depth Error 0 and Camera Error 0, while [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce90b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) generates unbounded cities from OpenStreetMap data covering 80 cities and more than 6,000 km2 plus 15,000 Google Earth evaluation images, using a 512-code 512-dimensional codebook, 16-level hash grid with 2^19 entries, and qualitative comparisons against SGAM, PersistentNature, SceneDreamer, and InfiniCity. The weakest but still relevant support is early semi-automated photo reconstruction: [(Vezhnevets et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6ddd51b226b95208e8f947b620f9432dd9daaf3) reconstructs LoD1 buildings from more than 50 Moscow and Seoul images, reports that 50% of cases required only a few mouse clicks, and uses semantic segmentation, vertical vanishing-point correction, and image completion, showing that street-view-like photos can guide simple building geometry. The oldest procedural work, [(Lipp et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/936f2a0f79a7b27d43439f1f51f41a76b35b5d15), provides only qualitative evidence that tagged urban layouts can generate CityEngine geometry, so it supports the general need for systematic building generation but offers little direct support for manual Blender modeling or street-view fidelity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly comparable newer work shows that manual modeling is not necessary to achieve realistic Blender-based urban assets: [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) automates Blender 3.2.2 asset generation from street-view-conditioned MLLM descriptions and diffusion rendering, outperforming baselines by 5.4% FID, 26.7% KID, 44.2% depth error, 10.4% homogeneity index, and GPT-4 preference, which undermines the proposed plan's reliance on manually creating 200 buildings without a quantitative fidelity protocol or reproducibility metrics. The next strongest contradiction is that automated layout-and-asset pipelines can produce scene consistency with explicit metrics, whereas the proposed method does not specify how the 200 buildings will be validated: [(Deng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b538c3e66f1456f5c7b4f7cb34523905d297a43) reports FID 27.60, KID 0.022, user preference 8.6, Depth Error 0, and Camera Error 0 on 8 Nvidia 4090-24GB GPUs, suggesting that a manual Blender workflow should be benchmarked against automated baselines rather than presented as the sole route to realism. Large-scale automated texturing also contradicts the efficiency of purely manual visual-fidelity work: [(Buyukdemircioglu, and Elberink, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf68a6229c21760e2ecd4abb9d278ae2b40637bb) textures 1,346 buildings and 48,497 surfaces in 3 minutes 16 seconds at 247 surfaces per second on a laptop, implying that manual texturing of 200 buildings in Blender may be slower and less reproducible unless automated texture mapping is used. City-scale data-driven generation further contradicts the assumption that a fixed set of 200 manually modeled buildings is sufficient for a 2.8 km by 2.4 km commercial district: [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) uses 14,612 street-view images and CAD-derived octrees for a London subset, and [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce90b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) uses OpenStreetMap data from 80 cities covering more than 6,000 km2 and 15,000 Google Earth images, both indicating that realistic urban environments can be generated at much larger scale and density than 200 buildings over 6.72 square kilometers. Earlier semi-automated and procedural work provides weaker contradictions: [(Vezhnevets et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6ddd51b226b95208e8f947b620f9432dd9daaf3) shows that photo-based LoD1 reconstruction can reduce manual clicks in 50% of cases, and [(Lipp et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/936f2a0f79a7b27d43439f1f51f41a76b35b5d15) shows that tag-driven CityEngine grammars can generate city geometry without manual modeling, but neither provides strong quantitative evidence against the proposed manual method.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative validation protocol for the 200 manually created Blender buildings before using them as the EmbodiedCity environment: render multi-view RGB, depth, segmentation, and LiDAR-compatible observations in Unreal Engine 5.3/AirSim, then compute FID and KID against real Baidu Map or Amap street-view references, depth error and camera error using the definitions in [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce90b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) and [(Deng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b538c3e66f1456f5c7b4f7cb34523905d297a43), texture fidelity with PSNR/SSIM/LPIPS where aligned images exist, geometry accuracy against OSM/CityJSON/CAD footprints if available, and user or GPT-4 preference scores as in [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f); include confidence intervals or paired significance tests and compare against at least one procedural baseline such as [(Lipp et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/936f2a0f79a7b27d43439f1f51f41a76b35b5d15) and one automated baseline such as [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) or [(Deng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b538c3e66f1456f5c7b4f7cb34523905d297a43). Second, augment the manual workflow with automated geometry and texture tools to improve reproducibility and reduce labor: import OSM/CityJSON/CityGML or use Blender Blosm as in [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f), apply CityEngine-style tag-driven generation as in [(Lipp et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/936f2a0f79a7b27d43439f1f51f41a76b35b5d15) for base massing, use semi-automated photo reconstruction as in [(Vezhnevets et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6ddd51b226b95208e8f947b620f9432dd9daaf3) where street-view photos can guide LoD1 walls, and use automated texture mapping from aerial or street-view imagery as in [(Buyukdemircioglu, and Elberink, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf68a6229c21760e2ecd4abb9d278ae2b40637bb), with diffusion/ControlNet/MLLM refinement as in [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) for facade realism; retain manual Blender editing for the 200 key buildings but document LOD, UV precision, materials, collision meshes, and UE5.3 export settings. Third, justify and possibly increase the building count and urban-element density: 200 buildings in a 2.8 km by 2.4 km commercial district is only about 30 buildings per square kilometer, so the plan should report buildings per street segment, landmark density, and coverage of the 100 streets and 50 km road network, and should test whether 6,000 urban elements are sufficient for navigation, counting, and scene-understanding tasks. Fourth, document all reproducibility constraints: Baidu/Amap image counts, capture dates, GPS or map tile identifiers, camera intrinsics/extrinsics where available, Blender version, texture resolution, hardware, time per building, and UE5.3/AirSim parameters, mirroring the level of detail reported in [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224), [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce90b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), and [(Buyukdemircioglu, and Elberink, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf68a6229c21760e2ecd4abb9d278ae2b40637bb). Finally, tie asset quality to embodied-task evaluation by measuring whether Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo performance on scene understanding, QA, dialogue, navigation, and planning changes when asset fidelity is varied, using BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR/CIDEr/Sentence-BERT for language tasks and SR, SPL, and NE for navigation.

## Context: This adds street-scale connectivity and dynamic traffic to support navigation and perception tasks. Method: The environment will include 100 streets with a combined length of 50km, incorporating realistic traffic patterns using the Mirage Simulation System for vehicle and pedestrian dynamics.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231), which builds CityBench with CityData, CitySimu, and CityBench, integrating OpenStreetMap road, POI, and AOI data, Google and Baidu street-view images, Esri satellite imagery, rule-based lane and lane topology reconstruction, building-lane connections, and dynamic human simulation for eight urban tasks; it also evaluates LLM and VLM models with VLMEvalKit and vLLM against task-specific baselines such as GeoCLIP, RSVA, Remote-CLIP, LSTPM, and MaxPressure, directly supporting the plan's multi-source urban data, dynamic simulation, and LLM/VLM benchmarking, although it does not specify 100 streets, 50 km, Mirage, or hardware. As newer iterative work, it extends the static street-view and street-graph line of [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) and [(Mirowski et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86430b50921c7aecb212b4cd0a43a73b091cf95f) by adding dynamic simulation and multi-model evaluation, supporting the proposed shift from static street-view to a dynamic embodied city. Next, [(Serrano et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91409f2bc5102535318efce84143f6f1f6b73943) supports dynamic vehicle-pedestrian interaction in a UE4.24 and CARLA street scene using a Python API, TCP socket plugin for actor locations and scene parameters, positional pedestrian and vehicle audio, and environmental sounds, with presence results of self-presence M=3.97 SD=0.528, autonomous vehicle presence M=3.60 SD=0.657, and environmental presence M=4.63 SD=0.266, supporting the plan's need for dynamic traffic and embodied perception, but only in a single AV-pedestrian crossing with n=6. Further support comes from [(Griffith et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73c4e75dbe22fbaa06b70f969b446580a2a1dc24), which injects dynamic 3D vehicles and pedestrians into a rendered city using X-Plane 10, per-timestep XML, a custom UDP plugin, frustum culling, shared-memory capture with a named mutex, TIF storage on an SSD, and 10-by-10 patches of 2048-by-2048 USGS tiles with 5-polygon overlap, producing a 41888 by 41888 pixel mosaic and processing about 27 GB of 15 cm imagery, showing feasibility of large-area dynamic traffic rendering, but it is stop-motion and not Mirage or UE5.3. Weaker but relevant support is from [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962), which provides a large static NYC Street View graph with 29,641 panoramas and 61,319 edges, ResNet18 features, crowdsourced validation, and quantitative navigation and spatial description results such as RCONCAT 10.7 TC, 19.5 SPD, 0.104 SED and LINGUNET 37.81/16.11 at 120 pixels, supporting street-scale connectivity and task design, and from [(Mirowski et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86430b50921c7aecb212b4cd0a43a73b091cf95f), which supports street-graph navigation design with 644 landmarks, 100 m goal radius, 200 m reward shaping, curriculum from 500 m to 3.5 or 5 km, and transfer by freezing the convolutional encoder and policy LSTM while training only the goal LSTM, supporting the proposed 100-street and 50-km connectivity and navigation evaluation, but without dynamic traffic, 3D Unreal, or LLM evaluation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the closest dynamic UE vehicle-pedestrian evidence, [(Serrano et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91409f2bc5102535318efce84143f6f1f6b73943), is only a preliminary single-scenario study with n=6, no statistical significance tests, no collision or reaction-time metrics, no 100-street or 50-km scale, and no Mirage integration; its presence scores and feedback that the vehicle image was not clear until close, braking felt conservative, and engine sound produced disagreement do not validate the proposed Mirage traffic realism or navigation performance. Next, [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) contradicts the assumption that one urban simulator or one model will dominate: no LLM or VLM consistently outperformed across eight tasks, GPT-4o led only the first two visual tasks while GLM-4v-9B, LLaVA-NeXT-34B, and InternVL2-8B led the remaining visual tasks, Llama3-70B led mobility prediction and urban exploration, and VLMs underperformed their LLM backbones on text-only tasks, with LLaVA-NeXT-8B averaging 4.10 percent below LLaMA3-8B and MiniCPM-V2.5-8B more than 1.88 percent below, so the plan must report task-specific baselines and model rankings rather than assume a single best model; it also does not specify Mirage, 100 streets, 50 km, or hardware. [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) contradicts the idea that a static street-level environment alone is sufficient for strong embodied performance: the combined navigation-plus-spatial-description pipeline reached only 4.5 percent full-task accuracy at an 80-pixel threshold versus estimated human performance of 92 percent, and baselines such as STOP and FREQUENT had 0.0 TC, showing that street-scale tasks are already difficult and that adding dynamic traffic without rigorous metrics may not improve embodied performance. [(Mirowski et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86430b50921c7aecb212b4cd0a43a73b091cf95f) contradicts the proposed rich multimodal LLM benchmark by using only a 60-degree cropped 84 by 84 RGB image, five discrete actions, 1000-step episodes, and no depth, LiDAR, IMU, GPS, dynamic traffic, 3D Unreal scene, or LLM evaluation, so its street-graph navigation results cannot directly support the plan's AirSim multi-modal interface or LLM tasks. Weaker contradiction comes from [(Griffith et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73c4e75dbe22fbaa06b70f969b446580a2a1dc24), which is a stop-motion X-Plane 10 imager pipeline rather than a real-time Mirage or UE5.3 embodied benchmark, reports no traffic realism or navigation metric, and leaves static vehicles embedded in textures that could contaminate perception, even though its 41888 by 41888 mosaic and 27 GB USGS imagery show scale.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add quantitative traffic-realism and safety validation for Mirage in UE5.3, reporting vehicle and pedestrian density, speed, headway, lane compliance, crossing compliance, collision rate, time-to-collision, reaction time, path-following error, and traffic-flow statistics across the 100 streets and 50 km, with statistical significance and baselines such as no traffic, random traffic, CARLA, or Mirage if available, because [(Serrano et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91409f2bc5102535318efce84143f6f1f6b73943) lacked objective metrics and [(Griffith et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73c4e75dbe22fbaa06b70f969b446580a2a1dc24) reported no traffic realism metric. Second, adopt and extend the street-graph and navigation task design of [(Mirowski et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86430b50921c7aecb212b4cd0a43a73b091cf95f) and [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) by using OSM or lane topology, 100 m goal radius, 200 m reward shaping, curriculum from 500 m to 3.5 or 5 km, landmarks, and SR, SPL, and NE metrics, while adding dynamic traffic, depth, LiDAR, IMU, GPS, and LLM/VLM evaluation. Third, follow [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) by integrating multi-source data such as OSM, Baidu and Amap street view, and satellite imagery, rule-based lane reconstruction, dynamic human and vehicle simulation, and a multi-task LLM/VLM evaluation harness using VLMEvalKit or vLLM, reporting task-specific model rankings and degradation rather than assuming a single best model, and including Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, GPT-4 Turbo, and relevant baselines. Fourth, incorporate embodied perception, usability, and audio from [(Serrano et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91409f2bc5102535318efce84143f6f1f6b73943), including positional engine, pedestrian, and environmental sounds, presence scales, and larger human studies with objective collision and reaction-time metrics. Fifth, implement dynamic actor injection and rendering robustness inspired by [(Griffith et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73c4e75dbe22fbaa06b70f969b446580a2a1dc24), such as frustum culling, UDP or shared-memory actor synchronization, texture and tile patch management, removal of baked static vehicles, and documented hardware, hyperparameters, and reproducibility details.

## Context: This enriches the scene with fine-grained objects that affect perception, reasoning, and agent interaction. Method: We will model over 6,000 urban elements including street furniture, vegetation, and urban amenities.

### Support

Strongest support for modeling over 6,000 urban elements comes from [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f), which shows that asset-level urban appearance and material descriptions can be generated from OSM or semantic/height layouts in Blender 3.2.2 using a fine-tuned urban MLLM trained on approximately 300K street-view images with GPT-4-generated and manually reviewed captions, then rendered with Stable Diffusion 1.5, ControlNet-Depth, IP-Adapter, ControlNet-inpainting, and ControlNet-tile on a single NVIDIA Tesla A100 using four camera views and 30 diffusion steps. This directly supports the research plan's goal of enriching a 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing commercial district with fine-grained street furniture, vegetation, and amenities, because it demonstrates that asset-level visual specifications and textures can be produced at city scale and that image-conditioned generation outperformed text-only generation. It also outperformed SGAM, PersistentNature, SceneDreamer, and CityDreamer by 5.4 percent FID, 26.7 percent KID, 44.2 percent depth error, and 10.4 percent homogeneity index, with a higher GPT-4 preference score, indicating that newer MLLM-plus-diffusion work can improve upon the neural city generation of [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae). Next, [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) supports category-aware urban 3D content creation from CAD and GPS-registered street-view images, using a HoliCity London CAD subset with 14,612 registered images, 64 points per 1-meter voxel, depth-6 octrees with 64 voxels per edge, top-down categorical, depth, and normal maps, an infinite-pixel image synthesis model, a contrastive patch discriminator, voxel completion, and SPADE-based texturization. Its FID of 108.47 and KID of 0.084 +/- 0.001 versus GSN's 333.92 and 0.325 +/- 0.002 support the use of street-view-derived category and geometry priors to populate urban scenes, which can inform placement and texturing of the proposed 6,000 elements. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), which generated unbounded city scenes from OSM semantic maps and height fields across 80 cities covering more than 6,000 km2, rasterized at EPSG:3857 zoom 18 with about 0.597 meters per pixel, and Google Earth Studio orbit images with 400 trajectories, 60 images per trajectory, orbit radii 125 to 813 meters, and altitudes 112 to 884 meters. Its codebook layout generator, hash-grid background generator, building instance generator, and compositing pipeline support the feasibility of large-scale urban scene construction, although it focuses on roads, buildings, green land, construction sites, and water rather than fine-grained street furniture. The weakest support for the specific proposed method is [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), which constructed 572 buildings, 1447 floors, and 211k m2 of scanned indoor space with RGB panoramas, camera poses, and reconstructed meshes, and showed that perceptual agents outperformed non-perceptual agents, distant visual navigation succeeded only with visual state after 1700 episodes, and a perceptual stair-climbing ant outperformed a non-perceptual agent by 70 percent. This supports the broader research plan's emphasis on detailed 3D geometry for perception and embodied tasks, but it is indoor and scan-based rather than a manually modeled outdoor commercial district.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the iterative literature from [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) to [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) to [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) shows that city-scale 3D content can be generated or completed from CAD, OSM, semantic maps, height fields, and street-view images without manually modeling thousands of individual assets. Lin et al. used 14,612 registered images, octrees, voxel completion, and SPADE texturization; Xie et al. used 80 cities, more than 6,000 km2, codebook size 512, code dimension 512, 512x512 patches compressed by a factor of 16, hash-grid parameters N_LH=16, T=2^19, N_CG=8, and loss weights of 10, 10, and 0.5; Shang et al. used a single A100, approximately 300K street-view MLLM training images, Stable Diffusion 1.5, ControlNet variants, and 30 diffusion steps. None of these papers provides evidence that manually modeling over 6,000 street furniture, vegetation, and amenity instances is necessary for an embodied benchmark, and the trend suggests that a purely manual approach may be less scalable, less reproducible, and harder to extend than a hybrid generative pipeline for the proposed 2.8 km by 2.4 km district. Next, the same newer work contradicts the assumption that visual realism alone validates the proposed method. Xie et al. reported qualitative comparisons with SGAM, PersistentNature, SceneDreamer, and InfiniCity, noting 3D consistency and building structural distortion problems, but the excerpt provides no FID, KID, depth error, camera error, or statistical significance values. Shang et al. reported FID, KID, depth error, homogeneity index, and GPT-4 preference, but no statistical significance tests and no downstream embodied-agent metrics such as Success Rate, SPL, Navigation Error, counting accuracy, or scene-understanding BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, or Sentence-BERT similarity. Therefore, their evidence supports visual scene generation, not the claim that 6,000 manually modeled elements will improve the five proposed embodied tasks in Unreal Engine 5.3 with AirSim, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, and Mirage traffic. Further contradiction comes from the lack of task-relevant geometry in these generative methods. Lin et al. did not report hardware, training hyperparameters, or downstream embodied validation, and its voxel completion and texturization were not evaluated for collision, affordances, semantic segmentation, or agent interaction. Xie et al. focused on coarse semantic categories and building instances, not fine-grained interactive street furniture or urban amenities. Shang et al. produced textured urban assets and scenes, but did not create an embodied-agent benchmark or manually model 6,000 elements. The weakest contradiction is from [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), which shows that large scanned indoor environments can support embodied validation without manual outdoor asset modeling, and that dense reconstructed geometry can reduce rendering speed to 26.5, 14.5, and 21.2 FPS at 512x512 on a Tesla V100 depending on filler configuration. This does not directly contradict outdoor street furniture modeling, but it warns that adding 6,000 fine-grained assets to 200 buildings, 100 streets, 50 km of roads, and traffic simulation may create real-time rendering and memory constraints that the proposed method has not yet measured.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to convert the proposed manual modeling of over 6,000 urban elements into a hybrid, data-driven pipeline that uses OSM, Baidu Map, Amap, street-view, and semantic/height data to auto-layout and texture assets, with manual refinement reserved for task-critical objects. Following [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae), rasterize the Beijing district at a high-resolution web-mercator scale such as EPSG:3857 zoom 18 with about 0.597 meters per pixel, and use semantic colors and height fields to place roads, buildings, green land, and candidate vegetation. Following [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224), create top-down categorical, depth, and normal maps from CAD or reconstructed geometry, use octree or voxel completion to fill missing urban content, and use street-view segmentation to guide category assignment. Following [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f), use an MLLM fine-tuned on street-view images with GPT-4-generated and manually reviewed captions to produce asset-level appearance and material descriptions for benches, bus stops, hydrants, trees, planters, signage, and amenities, then render multi-view textures with Stable Diffusion 1.5, ControlNet-Depth, IP-Adapter, ControlNet-inpainting, and ControlNet-tile, using four camera views and 30 diffusion steps on a single A100-class GPU where possible. This would reduce manual labor, improve reproducibility, and align the 6,000-element target with the research plan's need for realistic perception, reasoning, and agent interaction. Next, add explicit environment-construction metrics and ablations before using the scene for the five embodied tasks. Report asset counts by category, spatial density per square kilometer, specific surface area or clutter measure inspired by [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), and visual realism metrics such as FID, KID, depth error, homogeneity index, and GPT-4 preference where applicable. Also benchmark rendering performance in Unreal Engine 5.3 and AirSim at the observation resolutions used by agents, for example 128x128, 256x256, and 512x512, on a specified GPU, and compare against the V100 FPS ranges reported by Xia et al. to ensure that 6,000 assets plus traffic remain real-time. Then run ablations comparing no assets, a smaller asset set, 6,000 manually modeled assets, 6,000 generated assets, and a hybrid set, measuring downstream effects on distance, position, counting, dialogue, navigation SR, SPL, NE, and text-generation metrics such as BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT similarity. Finally, ensure that every asset exported from Blender to Unreal Engine 5.3 has semantic labels, collision meshes, LODs, material IDs, metadata for task grounding, and compatibility with LiDAR, GPS, IMU, segmentation, and Mirage pedestrian/vehicle dynamics; document hardware, hyperparameters, random seeds, data licenses, and human review protocols so that the benchmark can be reproduced and compared against generative baselines such as CityDreamer and UrbanWorld.

## Context: This gives agents a standardized way to perceive and act inside the city simulation. Method: We will develop input/output systems based on AirSim to enable first-person observations and realistic control actions.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed AirSim-based input/output systems comes from [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2), which directly demonstrates AirSim's capacity to expose realistic sensor observations and a reproducible control interface for embodied agents. That work implements a GPS sensor model with 200 ms latency, 50 Hz updates, and separate first-order low-pass decay rates for horizontal and vertical position error, and uses AirSim's MavLinkTest application to run the same offboard control loop for real Pixhawk v2/Flamewheel flights and simulated flights, which is directly relevant to the plan's goal of providing first-person observations and realistic control actions for drones. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9), which shows that standardized simulator interfaces for perception and control can be built through a modular architecture, Python wrapper, and OpenAI-Gym-style API, supporting the plan's intention to expose RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, and LiDAR through a Python SDK and HTTP proxy, although it does not validate AirSim itself.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work provides quantitative evidence that AirSim can support the full scale and sensor suite of the proposed EmbodiedCity benchmark. [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) reports only that simulated quadrotor flight characteristics and sensor models were evaluated against real-world measurements, without numerical error values, statistical comparisons, or significance, and it covers a single quadrotor GPS model rather than the plan's 2.8 km × 2.4 km Beijing district, 100 streets, 50 km road length, 6,000 urban elements, traffic, ground vehicles, LiDAR, depth, segmentation, and first-person urban tasks. This absence of quantitative validation undermines the assumption that AirSim's default or lightly configured sensors are sufficient for benchmark-grade embodied evaluation. A weaker contradiction is architectural: [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9) demonstrates that a scalable robot-learning simulator benefits from decoupling photo-realistic rendering from parallelized dynamics and using asynchronous ZeroMQ communication plus an OpenAI-Gym-style Python API, which suggests that a monolithic AirSim-based I/O layer may be less suitable for a large, multi-agent, multi-vehicle urban benchmark unless the plan explicitly adds modular, asynchronous, and performance-tested interfaces. This does not prove AirSim is unsuitable, but it contradicts the implicit assumption that AirSim alone provides the necessary standardized, scalable I/O architecture.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative validation protocol for the AirSim I/O layer before using it as the benchmark backbone: compare simulated GPS, IMU, depth, segmentation, and LiDAR outputs against ground truth or real sensor data in the target Beijing district, reporting latency, update rates, noise/error distributions, packet loss, and statistical significance, extending the 200 ms/50 Hz GPS model from [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) to all modalities and documenting hyperparameters. The next priority is to make the interface modular and asynchronous in the spirit of [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9), separating rendering, dynamics, sensor simulation, and agent communication, and benchmarking the Python SDK/HTTP proxy for FPS, end-to-end latency, memory, and concurrent agent throughput in the 2.8 km × 2.4 km environment with 100 streets and 6,000 urban elements. Finally, the plan should explicitly define and test action spaces for both drones and ground vehicles, not only AirSim/MavLink-style offboard control, and include reproducibility details such as hardware specs, Unreal Engine 5.3/AirSim versions, sensor parameters, traffic model settings, and evaluation metrics for the I/O layer itself.

## Context: This broadens the benchmark to multiple sensor modalities and embodied platforms. Method: We will support multiple observation types (RGB images, depth images, segmentation images, IMU data, GPS data, LiDAR data) and various action spaces for both drones and ground vehicles.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Guerra et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d7bdedb161a6bafb7eee3432db76565e5d1eb78), which implements a game-engine-based simulator with a Python interface, cloud simulation support, multiple-vehicle support, photogrammetric asset creation, and performance optimizations, and directly simulates exteroceptive observations including RGB, depth, semantic segmentation, and optical-flow cameras plus an inertial measurement model, while also implementing drone motor dynamics, forces and moments, physics integration, an acro/rate-mode controller with a low-pass filter, and car dynamics. This is the closest prior evidence that the EmbodiedCity plan can expose multiple observation types and action spaces for both drones and ground vehicles through AirSim-based interfaces, although the excerpt does not include GPS or LiDAR. The next strongest support is [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2), which provides a concrete AirSim GPS sensor model for a quadrotor with 200 ms latency, 50 Hz updates, and first-order low-pass filter decay for horizontal and vertical fix, and validates the approach against a real Pixhawk v2 Flamewheel quadrotor on a Gigabyte 5500 Brix running Ubuntu 16.04 using MavLinkTest, supporting the proposed GPS observation type. The third support is [(Liu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3765f1577e9e9e4ed919bf13e8b4515dee3f9f3), which evaluates four drone aerial path-planning methods for urban scene acquisition, including oblique photography with a fixed-height S-shaped complete-coverage trajectory, a reconstructability-based optimizer, a redundancy-reduction planner, and a smoothness-aware rapidly-exploring random tree planner, on UrbanScene3D with 10 synthetic CAD scenes and 6 real-world scenes, 100 synthetic paths and 26 real paths, using a professional five-camera drone for oblique photography and a single-camera drone for the other planners, and reports reconstruction accuracy as 90 and 95 percent closest-point distances and completeness as ground-truth vertices within 0.02 m, 0.05 m, and 0.075 m, with the School scene at 90 percent overlap showing that reconstructability-based and redundancy-reduction planners generally achieved higher accuracy and completeness than oblique photography and the smoothness-aware planner. This supports the proposed drone action-space and RGB observation acquisition, but only for aerial RGB rather than the full multi-sensor set. The weakest support is [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9), which describes Flightmare as a modular quadrotor simulator that separates a photo-realistic Unity-based rendering engine from parallel quadrotor dynamics simulation, uses asynchronous messaging, and exposes a Python-based OpenAI-Gym-style interface, supporting the broader simulator-to-agent interface idea but not specifically the proposed urban-scale, multi-sensor, or ground-vehicle scope.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed GPS observation support is not quantitatively validated in the closest GPS-related work. [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) specifies a 200 ms latency, 50 Hz update rate, and first-order low-pass filter decay for horizontal and vertical fix, but the provided excerpt reports no quantitative GPS error values, latency measurements, flight-characteristic differences, or statistical comparisons, so the EmbodiedCity plan cannot assume that AirSim GPS will be realistic for distance, position, counting, navigation, or dialogue tasks in the 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing district without additional calibration. The next strongest contradiction is that the multi-sensor and ground-vehicle evidence is architectural rather than performance-validated and incomplete. [(Guerra et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d7bdedb161a6bafb7eee3432db76565e5d1eb78) supports RGB, depth, semantic segmentation, optical-flow cameras, IMU-like data, drone motor dynamics, acro/rate-mode control, and car dynamics, but the excerpt contains no numerical experimental outcomes, baseline comparisons, statistical significance, hardware specifications, rendering resolution or frame rate, sensor noise parameters, course dimensions, dataset size, or hyperparameters, and it does not include GPS or LiDAR, two observation types explicitly required by the proposed method. The third contradiction is that smoother drone action spaces may not uniformly improve embodied perception. [(Liu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3765f1577e9e9e4ed919bf13e8b4515dee3f9f3) found that the smoothness-aware rapidly-exploring random tree planner produced paths with higher quality and lower energy consumption, but its reconstruction accuracy and completeness were less consistent with proxy refinement, and the work is limited to aerial RGB path planning without depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, LiDAR, or ground-vehicle action spaces. The weakest contradiction is that the modular simulator evidence does not establish city-scale multi-platform performance. [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9) reports no quantitative experimental outcomes, no ground vehicles, no urban-scale environment, and no evidence for the specific RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, or LiDAR modalities required by the EmbodiedCity benchmark.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative sensor-validation protocol for GPS and LiDAR before using them in the five embodied tasks. For GPS, adopt the AirSim model from [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) as a baseline, configure 200 ms latency, 50 Hz updates, and first-order low-pass filters for horizontal and vertical fix, then compare simulated and real quadrotor or ground-vehicle GPS traces using position error CDFs, horizontal and vertical error, latency, update-rate jitter, fix decay, and statistical significance, and document the hardware such as Pixhawk v2 or equivalent, frame, and computer. For LiDAR, define range, angular resolution, point-cloud rate, noise, dropout, and coordinate frame, and validate against real LiDAR or published sensor specifications. The second most important action is to integrate and document the multi-sensor and dynamics components shown in [(Guerra et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d7bdedb161a6bafb7eee3432db76565e5d1eb78), including RGB, depth, semantic segmentation, optical-flow cameras, IMU-like inertial measurement, drone motor dynamics, forces and moments, physics integration, acro/rate-mode control with a low-pass filter, and car dynamics, and then extend them to GPS and LiDAR while reporting frame rates, resolutions, control rates, physics timestep, sensor noise, and system requirements for the 2.8 km by 2.4 km Unreal Engine 5.3 city with about 200 buildings, 100 streets totaling 50 km, over 6,000 urban elements, and Mirage traffic. The third action is to use the trajectory and reconstruction evaluation from [(Liu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3765f1577e9e9e4ed919bf13e8b4515dee3f9f3) to benchmark drone action spaces in EmbodiedCity, generating oblique S-shaped, reconstructability-based, redundancy-reduction, and smoothness-aware RRT trajectories, and measuring closest-point distance, completeness at 0.02 m, 0.05 m, and 0.075 m, path energy, smoothness, and downstream task metrics such as Success Rate, SPL, Navigation Error, BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT, so that action-space smoothness is not assumed to improve perception without evidence. The least important but still useful action is to adopt the modular architecture of [(Song et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34b144320e73a4e0c7789df2f6f5b90d726c76a9), separating rendering from dynamics, using asynchronous messaging, and exposing a Python SDK or OpenAI-Gym-style interface, while measuring simulation speed, frame drops, and interface latency under the full city scene to ensure the AirSim-based Python and HTTP proxy can support real-time embodied agents.

## Context: This lowers the barrier for researchers to integrate models and run experiments on the platform. Method: We will create a Python SDK and HTTP-based proxy server to facilitate easy access and development.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Python SDK and HTTP-based proxy server is that [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) demonstrates AirSim can serve as an interface layer for repeatable offboard control and sensor-state access, using MavLinkTest to issue commands and read state on a real Pixhawk v2/Flamewheel quadrotor and in simulation. This directly supports the feasibility of wrapping AirSim with a higher-level SDK/proxy for the EmbodiedCity plan, because the related work already treats the simulator/vehicle boundary as an abstraction that can be driven programmatically. A second, more specific support is the GPS sensor model configuration with 200 ms latency, 50 Hz update rate, and first-order low-pass decay for horizontal and vertical position-error estimates, which aligns with the research plan's need for realistic GPS observations and suggests the SDK should expose such sensor hyperparameters rather than hide them. A weaker but still relevant support is the documented hardware/software context, including a Gigabyte 5500 Brix running Ubuntu 16.04 and sensor measurements recorded on the Pixhawk, which shows that reproducible interface-level experiments are possible with modest embedded and desktop hardware, although the plan's Unreal Engine 5.3 urban scene will likely require more substantial GPU and CPU resources.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2) does not evaluate a Python SDK or HTTP-based proxy server; it uses a MavLinkTest-based workflow, so it provides no direct evidence that an HTTP proxy will be low-latency, thread-safe, easy to use, or suitable for multi-modal agent experiments. A second contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes, numerical comparisons to baselines, statistical significance, or reported metrics, meaning the related work cannot support claims about interface reliability, sensor fidelity, or reproducibility beyond qualitative statements. A third contradiction is scope: the related work focuses on a quadrotor, Pixhawk v2, Flamewheel frame, and GPS fix acquisition, whereas the proposed platform must support RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, LiDAR, drone and ground-vehicle action spaces, a 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing commercial district, 100 streets totaling 50 km, approximately 200 buildings, over 6,000 urban elements, and Mirage traffic dynamics. A weaker contradiction is temporal and platform mismatch: the 2017 AirSim workflow may not map cleanly to the plan's Unreal Engine 5.3 environment, modern multi-modal LLM evaluation, or HTTP service architecture, so older implementation details may need revalidation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the Python SDK and HTTP proxy as an explicit, documented abstraction over AirSim and, where useful, MavLink-style offboard control, with endpoints for all planned observations (RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, LiDAR) and actions for drones and ground vehicles, and to expose sensor configuration parameters such as the 200 ms GPS latency, 50 Hz update rate, and first-order low-pass decay used in [(Shah et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20f3dfc9142b48b924e68ee22ba259a0d621bb2). The next most important action is to add quantitative interface validation: measure command latency, observation delivery rate, packet loss, concurrency, memory/CPU/GPU usage, and reproducibility across repeated flights or episodes, comparing the SDK/proxy against direct AirSim or MavLinkTest baselines with confidence intervals and significance tests. A further action is to extend validation beyond the quadrotor/GPS case to ground vehicles and the full urban scene, including Mirage traffic, and to report hardware specifications, AirSim/UE5.3 versions, hyperparameters, and dataset sizes so that the interface can be reproduced. Finally, include tutorials, API examples, and failure-mode documentation for the five benchmark tasks, ensuring that the SDK supports data collection for BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, Sentence-BERT, SR, SPL, and NE evaluations without becoming a bottleneck.

## Context: This defines the task suite used to evaluate multi-dimensional urban embodied intelligence. Method: Our benchmark methodology will encompass five essential embodied tasks covering perception, reasoning, and decision-making abilities: (1) embodied first-view scene understanding, (2) embodied question answering, (3) embodied dialogue, (4) embodied action (visual-language navigation), and (5) embodied task planning.

### Support

The strongest support comes from mature indoor VLN and EQA benchmark protocols that directly instantiate proposed tasks 2 and 4. [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) built R2R with 7,189 paths, 90 scenes, 21,567 instructions, success below 3 m, seen/unseen splits, human success 86.4%, RANDOM 13.2%, seq2seq 20.4%, and seen 38.6% versus unseen 21.8%, validating instruction-based navigation, human baselines, and generalization splits. [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c) separated navigation from QA in EQA v1 with 100,000 questions across 1,000 scenes, reporting QA 38.1% versus random 10.6%, shortest-path 18.8%, and oracle 44.7%, while navigation was 20.4% versus oracle 100%, supporting separate embodied QA and navigation evaluation plus oracle upper bounds. [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e) and [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234) support embodied task planning and action: ALFRED uses goal-condition success, path-weighted success, sub-goal evaluation, and human 91% task success/86% path-weighted success, while the AI2-THOR agent uses Mask R-CNN, U-Net, a semantic spatial graph, PDDL, 500-step random exploration, and achieves ALFRED seen 0.40/unseen 0.36 with +6/+11 points over FILM, plus failure analysis of depth and object localization. [(Zhu et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2fab8c96e202d0aac3f68721e6d3f1f1f11bfd) supports the proposed SR/SPL/NE metrics and auxiliary reasoning, with AuxRN improving SPL by 3.02 and 2.78 points on seen/unseen validation and reaching 71% SR with beam search. Newer LLM/VLM works support evaluating large models and planning: [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) generated filtered ALFWorld tasks, fine-tuned LLM/LMMs on four Tesla V100 GPUs, and found OPT-6.7B at 66.43% seen/79.10% unseen versus weaker LMMs; [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) shows GPT-4V 19% planning success and information injection improving 22/34/72/80; [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718) shows a few-shot GPT-3 planner using 100 examples, 9 in-context examples, 7 LLM calls, and grounded replanning with +1.83% success; [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443) supports situated QA generation with 251K instances, GPT 1-5 scoring, and human QC; [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3) supports multi-round embodied QA in a UE5 cafe, with accuracy 70 to 72, exploration 22 to 19.3, unreachable planning 3.5 to 1, and path length 4769.9 to 3800.7 cm; [(Hu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/498decc50ccea9293f63a98c30d7c3439be074b7), [(Fried et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/893186c6bc08a17cb3f9f94fa3f14e9ad20b0525), and [(Pashevich et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25ddddbd0bd1cfebf1548b2ee91bb1bbd05fdff1) provide weaker but relevant support for first-view planning, synthetic route/instruction augmentation, and long-horizon episodic memory.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is domain mismatch: all cited works are indoor or bounded, including Matterport3D, AI2-THOR, ALFWorld, ScanNet, and a UE5 cafe, and none directly validates the proposed 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing district, 100 streets, 50 km roads, Mirage traffic, drone/ground vehicle action spaces, or GPS/LiDAR/IMU observations, so the central urban-scale claim is not directly supported. Second, the proposed reliance on BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT is weak for grounded embodied QA and dialogue; related work uses exact-match QA [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c), GPT-based 1-5 scoring and human QC [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443), manual 1/0 planning scores [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f), and capability-based scores, indicating that n-gram metrics may miss grounded correctness. Third, navigation evaluation is incomplete: related work shows the need for oracle success, goal-condition success, subgoal success, path-weighted success, and stopping behavior [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc), [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e), and [(Zhu et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2fab8c96e202d0aac3f68721e6d3f1f1f11bfd), whereas the plan lists only SR, SPL, and NE. Fourth, first-view scene understanding may be insufficient: [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234) attributes failures to depth estimation and object localization, [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3) uses RGB, depth, and segmentation from multiple cameras, and [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443) uses point clouds and situated descriptions, suggesting that a single first-view RGB input may understate urban perception difficulty. Fifth, embodied dialogue evidence is thin: only [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3) reports multi-round gains, and they are small, with no urban multi-turn dialogue validation. Sixth, free-form task planning may be unreliable without structured constraints: GPT-4V achieved only 19% general-description planning and MiniCPM 0/270 in [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f), while [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) and [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234) use PDDL or structured task metadata to reduce hallucination. Seventh, automated data collection needs filtering and QC: [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) filters invalid tasks, [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443) uses human quality control, and [(Fried et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/893186c6bc08a17cb3f9f94fa3f14e9ad20b0525) shows synthetic route-instruction data is useful but not quantitatively validated in the excerpt. Eighth, the proposed model set and evaluation lack compute, cost, and statistical rigor: related work sometimes reports hardware such as four Tesla V100 GPUs [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5), but most cited works do not report significance, and the plan does not specify API costs, inference budgets, or significance tests.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt the strongest existing VLN/EQA evaluation protocols: use seen/unseen splits, human baselines, oracle navigation, goal-condition success, subgoal success, path-weighted success, and stopping/oracle stopping, while reporting SR, SPL, and NE with confidence intervals [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc), [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c), [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e), and [(Zhu et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2fab8c96e202d0aac3f68721e6d3f1f1f11bfd). Second, replace or supplement BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT with grounded correctness metrics: exact match for distance, position, and counting; GPT-4 judge 1-5 scoring with human calibration for open-ended QA; manual 1/0 or capability scores for planning [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443), [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f), and [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c). Third, ground task generation in scene graphs, PDDL, or simulator metadata with explicit filters for urban incompatibility, invalid objects, unreachable goals, already completed tasks, and traffic constraints, followed by human QC [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5), [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234), and [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443). Fourth, include multi-sensor and multi-view observations, including RGB, depth, segmentation, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, and possibly panoramic or multi-camera inputs, and run sensor ablations to test whether first-view RGB alone is sufficient [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234), [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3), and [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443). Fifth, design urban-specific navigation with dynamic traffic, drone and ground vehicle action spaces, difficulty by distance and environmental complexity, and oracle trajectories, while reporting path length and stopping behavior [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) and [(Zhu et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2fab8c96e202d0aac3f68721e6d3f1f1f11bfd). Sixth, add a dedicated multi-turn dialogue protocol with memory, context maintenance, clarification, and turn-level accuracy, using the multi-round EQA evidence as a starting point [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3). Seventh, broaden baselines to include text-only LLMs, open-source 7B models, fine-tuned Q-LoRA agents, few-shot GPT-3/4 planners, grounded replanning, and synthetic-data-augmented agents, and report API calls, cost, compute, and inference latency [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5), [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718), [(Fried et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/893186c6bc08a17cb3f9f94fa3f14e9ad20b0525), and [(Pashevich et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25ddddbd0bd1cfebf1548b2ee91bb1bbd05fdff1). Eighth, add statistical significance, ablations, and a failure taxonomy covering depth, object localization, traffic, language grounding, planning, and memory, following the failure analysis in [(Liu, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c23a6c46aaf00c2881829f9b5cc2ac6c15a42234) and the capability dimensions in [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f).

## Context: This tests current large model capabilities on the proposed urban embodied benchmark. Method: We will conduct comprehensive evaluations using popular multi-modal large language models including Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo across all five benchmark tasks.

### Support

The strongest support for evaluating Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo across five urban embodied tasks comes from [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3), which demonstrates a closed-loop UE5 embodied pipeline with an LLM planner and VLM for spatial understanding, navigation, robot motion control, and embodied question answering in a cafe simulator. Its 90-question, 6-scene EQA set, single-round versus multi-round comparison, and metrics including average accuracy, exploration count, unreachable planning count, and path length directly validate the proposed use of embodied simulation, multi-turn dialogue, memory, and navigation-style efficiency metrics; multi-round QA improved accuracy from 70% to 72%, reduced exploration from 22 to 19.3, reduced unreachable planning from 3.5 to 1, and reduced path length from 4769.9 cm to 3800.7 cm. A second strong line of support is [(Liu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf108758d87ce26f7b9258a79772dbe9dfa4e50), which evaluates embodied planning in AI2-THOR with a fixed skill library, memory, and step-wise success metrics, showing that Initial Plan Success Rate, Step-wise Success Rate, and overall Success Rate are useful for multi-step action execution and that a fine-tuned ReLEP system reached 100.0%, 99.8%, and 99.0% on 10 tasks with 10 trials each. Together, these newer embodied evaluations extend the static multi-task protocols of [(Dai et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bd6a2a89503be083176f2cc26fabedb79238cbd) and [(Li et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4309d572a37d655779f9dce6a2c98c66334132de) into closed-loop action and planning. The evaluation protocol is further supported by [(Fu et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09ff7cdc583b28ec63dfc85c7142f2cb0e5e4eef), which benchmarks Qwen-VL-Max, Claude 3 Opus, and GPT-4 Turbo on Blink with 1,906 test samples, temperature 0, retry 10, no image resizing, rule-based plus GPT-3.5-turbo answer extraction, and a human baseline of 95.70%, providing a reproducible template for zero-shot MLLM evaluation and task calibration. [(Ying et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10d3c1cfb98f4e547f579a30b31f2361297983f1) supports the proposed failure-mode analysis by showing that a 162-subtask, 30-model benchmark can be interpreted with an error taxonomy and Kendall tau task-map clustering, with perception error dominating for GPT-4V, GeminiProVision, and InternVL. Older but still relevant support includes [(Dai et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bd6a2a89503be083176f2cc26fabedb79238cbd), which evaluates InstructBLIP zero-shot across 26 datasets and 11 task categories with held-in and held-out splits, CIDEr, iVQA accuracy, AUC, MRR, and top-1 accuracy, reports 15.0% average relative improvement and 47.1% relative improvement on MSRVTT-QA, and was trained on 16 Nvidia A100 40G GPUs, demonstrating that multi-task zero-shot evaluation can expose generalization differences. [(Li et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4309d572a37d655779f9dce6a2c98c66334132de) supports the proposed scene understanding and QA dimensions by measuring 12 spatial and temporal dimensions with likelihood-based multiple-choice ranking, finding top overall accuracy of 60.29% for InstructBLIP but many dimensions below 50%. [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/124d4d374fbef2016fa9880489871a58a7450644) supports benchmarking MLLMs with explicit response-format prompts and data-efficiency checks, showing that LLaVA-1.5 at 448x448 improves detail-sensitive tasks and that 50% of training data retains more than 98% performance, with 8 A100 GPUs, about 6 hours pretraining, and 20 hours visual instruction tuning for the 336x336 13B model. [(Das et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60db77acc65d178221afe36f85cfa3d4003017c3) supports evaluating Qwen-VL and GPT-4V on multiple-choice visual exam questions with 4,208 test samples, 11 languages, and random baselines. [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) supports the proposed automated generation plus human refinement by generating 158K instruction samples from COCO captions and bounding boxes with GPT-4 or ChatGPT and manually designed seeds, using 8 A100 GPUs and Vicuna hyperparameters, though it does not report benchmark scores. The weakest but foundational support is [(Srivastava et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd1331b233e84bab7eba503abc60b31ac08e7881), which shows that large multi-task benchmarks can compare models against expert human baselines and reveal scale-dependent breakthroughs, although it is text-only and uses author-normalized 0-100 scores.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that current MLLMs are not reliable visual perception systems, which directly threatens the proposed embodied first-view scene understanding, QA, and navigation. [(Fu et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09ff7cdc583b28ec63dfc85c7142f2cb0e5e4eef) reports that on Blink, random guessing is 38.09%, open-source 7B and 13B models average 35-42%, the best open-source LLaVA-v1.6-34B reaches 45.05%, GPT-4V reaches 51.26%, GPT-4 Turbo 53.89%, Gemini Pro 45.72%, Claude 3 Opus 44.11%, and Qwen-VL-Max 41.94%, while humans average 95.70%; several tasks are below random, and a caption-only ablation reaches random on Blink, indicating that language priors do not substitute for visual perception. A second strong contradiction is that off-the-shelf MLLMs fail at complete embodied planning and action execution even when initial plans look plausible. [(Liu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf108758d87ce26f7b9258a79772dbe9dfa4e50) reports GPT-4V* with manually crafted examples achieved 100.0% Initial Plan Success Rate but only 29.0% overall Success Rate, ReLEP+GPT-4V achieved 32.0% overall Success Rate, and VILA achieved 26.0%, whereas a fine-tuned ReLEP system achieved 99.0%, implying that the proposed evaluation of Claude 3, GPT-4 Turbo, Qwen-VL, and Fuyu-8B may need constrained action spaces, skill libraries, memory, or fine-tuning to be meaningful. A third contradiction is that perception errors dominate MLLM failures and out-of-domain visual tasks have low accuracy and low ranking correlation. [(Ying et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10d3c1cfb98f4e547f579a30b31f2361297983f1) reports perception error as the largest error type for GPT-4V at 51%, GeminiProVision at 76.9%, and InternVL at 67.2%, and identifies clusters 8, 9, and 11 as low-accuracy out-of-domain regions such as fine-grained visual cognition, localization, detection, GUI, and table tasks. This newer error analysis extends the older weakness reported by [(Li et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4309d572a37d655779f9dce6a2c98c66334132de), which found that many MLLMs remain below 50% on spatial and temporal dimensions, with instance counting top at 40.33%, action prediction top at 31.88%, and weak text recognition, and it contains an apparent inconsistency in spatial-relation reporting. [(Das et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60db77acc65d178221afe36f85cfa3d4003017c3) contradicts the assumption that Qwen-VL is a strong general-purpose MLLM in this evaluation, because Qwen-VL-7B performed close to the random baseline in supported languages and had limited language support, while GPT-4V reached only 42.78% overall on EXAMS-V versus random baselines of 19% to 26%. Weaker contradictions concern metric and data design: [(Srivastava et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd1331b233e84bab7eba503abc60b31ac08e7881) shows that author-normalized 0-100 aggregate scores can be misleading, best models scored below 20, low-resource tasks were poor, and social bias often worsened with scale, so the proposed BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, Sentence-BERT, SR, SPL, and NE metrics must be task-calibrated and supplemented with human baselines. [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/124d4d374fbef2016fa9880489871a58a7450644) also warns that static benchmark evaluation can be confounded by training-data overlap, such as GQA images observed during training, and it does not include depth, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, action spaces, or urban simulation, limiting direct transfer to EmbodiedCity. [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8) provides the weakest contradiction because its GPT-assisted data generation used captions and bounding boxes rather than direct image understanding and did not report quantitative benchmark scores, so it does not by itself validate the quality of VLM-generated urban descriptions or questions.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add explicit perception baselines and task calibration before interpreting model rankings: report random-chance baselines, human expert baselines, oracle caption or text-only baselines, per-task sample sizes, difficulty strata, and confidence intervals, using the Blink-style protocol of temperature 0, retry 10, no image resizing, and deterministic answer extraction from [(Fu et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09ff7cdc583b28ec63dfc85c7142f2cb0e5e4eef). For open-ended scene understanding and dialogue, do not rely only on BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT; add human-rated accuracy, semantic correctness, and task-specific preferred metrics with documented low and high anchors, following the metric-design caution in [(Srivastava et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd1331b233e84bab7eba503abc60b31ac08e7881). The second most important action is to make embodied action and planning executable rather than purely generative: define a fixed skill library or action primitives, use memory, constrain decoding to valid actions, and report Initial Plan Success Rate, Step-wise Success Rate, overall Success Rate, executable success rate, path length, and unreachable planning count, as in [(Liu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf108758d87ce26f7b9258a79772dbe9dfa4e50) and [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3). The third action is to evaluate single-round versus multi-round dialogue and environment memory explicitly, measuring accuracy, exploration count, unreachable planning count, and path length, because multi-round QA improved accuracy from 70% to 72% and reduced exploration from 22 to 19.3 in [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3). The fourth action is to build an error taxonomy and task map for urban tasks, classifying failures into perception, reasoning, knowledge, capability, refusal, instruction-following, and annotation errors, and using Kendall tau or clustering to identify out-of-domain urban subtasks, following [(Ying et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10d3c1cfb98f4e547f579a30b31f2361297983f1). The fifth action is to prevent data contamination and validate generated data: hold out city locations, avoid overlap with public training data, use multiple perspectives, and manually verify VLM-generated descriptions, questions, and ground-truth trajectories, motivated by the GQA overlap warning in [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/124d4d374fbef2016fa9880489871a58a7450644), the held-out evaluation design in [(Dai et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bd6a2a89503be083176f2cc26fabedb79238cbd), and the GPT-assisted generation pipeline in [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5036f31f0e629dc661f120b8c3b1f374d479ab8). The sixth action is to specify model and API details for reproducibility, including exact versions of Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo, image resolution, observation modalities, temperature, retries, decoding parameters, random seeds, number of episodes, hardware, and cost, because Fuyu-8B was not evaluated in [(Fu et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/09ff7cdc583b28ec63dfc85c7142f2cb0e5e4eef) and Qwen-VL language support was limited in [(Das et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60db77acc65d178221afe36f85cfa3d4003017c3). The seventh action is to include high-resolution and multi-modal observations, such as depth, segmentation, LiDAR, GPS, and IMU, rather than only RGB, because urban navigation and spatial QA require metric grounding and because prior static evaluations used 224x224 inputs in [(Dai et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bd6a2a89503be083176f2cc26fabedb79238cbd) or 336x336/448x448 image benchmarks in [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/124d4d374fbef2016fa9880489871a58a7450644). The least important but still useful action is to report statistical significance and variance across repeated trials, since several related embodied evaluations, including [(Liu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9bf108758d87ce26f7b9258a79772dbe9dfa4e50) and [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3), do not report significance tests.

## Context: This establishes the overall pipeline for creating reliable benchmark data at city scale. Method: For data collection, we will employ a combination of automated generation and human refinement.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed data-collection method, automated generation plus human refinement for a city-scale embodied benchmark, is [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5), which constructs MuEP by aligning ALFWorld/Ai2-THOR PDDL/TextWorld metadata with egocentric observations, then uses text-davinci-003 with 30 manually crafted task examples, object dictionaries, room-specific templates, and four filters (room compatibility, valid items, sufficient attributes, not already completed) to generate 140 seen and 134 unseen tasks; downstream evaluation with a 30-step limit on four Tesla V100 GPUs shows the pipeline yields usable benchmark data, with OPT-6.7B reaching 66.43% seen and 79.10% unseen success, while MiniGPT-4 reaches 21.43% and 25.37%, validating that human-curated examples and programmatic filtering can support embodied task annotation. As an iterative progression, this 2024 embodied-task work extends the 2023 city-generation pipelines by moving from scene data to task annotation. This directly supports the plan's template-based QA, dialogue, navigation, and planning annotation, although it is indoor rather than the proposed 2.8 km x 2.4 km Beijing district. The next strongest support is [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f), which uses an urban MLLM trained on about 300,000 street-view images with LLM-generated captions that were manually reviewed and filtered, then a Stable Diffusion 1.5 renderer with depth, inpainting, tile, and image-reference controls, and a final MLLM refinement stage; on a single NVIDIA Tesla A100 it reduces FID by 5.4%, KID by 26.7%, depth error by 44.2%, and improves homogeneity by 10.4% over the strongest baseline, supporting the plan's use of VLM-generated scene descriptions followed by manual review and correction for first-view scene understanding. This is more directly urban than Li but does not create AirSim-style agent interfaces or the five embodied tasks. [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32ef08a51d2a79334d4d088947768ae15af00db2) further supports iterative automated generation with human feedback: CityX uses an LLM multi-agent framework over PCG modules, Blender Cycles rendering, 50 semantic maps, 50 height fields, 50 OSM XML files, and 50 style/weather descriptions, and GPT-4 achieves ER@1 91.00 and SR@1 81.32, while adding Description/Input/Limitation fields improves SR@1, with Description alone raising SR@1 by 57.00%; its 30 volunteers and 5 3D-modeling experts show human evaluation can be incorporated, although only for aesthetics. Older city-scale work supports the automated-generation component: [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) rasterizes OSM for 80 cities over >6,000 km2 at zoom 18 (0.597 m/px), collects 400 Google Earth Studio orbit trajectories with 60 images each, and automatically generates semantic and instance segmentation via connected components and camera projection, which is useful for the plan's segmentation, GPS, and multi-view observations but lacks human refinement and embodied tasks. Finally, [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) demonstrates an earlier automated city-scale pipeline from CAD and GPS-registered street view, using octrees, top-down category/depth/normal maps, and a contrastive patch discriminator, achieving FID 108.47 and KID 0.084 +/- 0.001 versus GSN's FID 333.92 and KID 0.325 +/- 0.002; this supports automated multi-modal city data creation but does not provide first-person embodied observations, human refinement, or task annotation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caveat is also [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5): when PDDL-style textual scene state is available, LLMs outperform LMMs, with OPT-6.7B at 66.43% seen and 79.10% unseen versus MiniGPT-4 at 21.43% and 25.37%, and LLaMA-2-7B scores 0% on seen Pick two and place tasks due to a 4.17% language-control score; this contradicts the plan's assumption that visual RGB/depth/LiDAR observations and VLM-generated descriptions alone will be sufficient for reliable urban embodied evaluation, and suggests the benchmark must expose structured metadata, object states, and programmatic task validation. A second contradiction is that human refinement in related work is often limited to aesthetics or preference rather than task correctness: [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32ef08a51d2a79334d4d088947768ae15af00db2) uses 30 volunteers and 5 experts only for aesthetic scoring, while LLM control varies widely (GPT-4 ER@1 91.00/SR@1 81.32, GPT-3.5-turbo 72.00/75.00, Mistral 78.00/61.54, Gemma-7B 39.00/69.23, Llama2-7B 27.00/59.26, Gemma-2B 9.00/33.33), so aesthetic human review does not guarantee valid navigation trajectories, counting answers, or planning ground truth. [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) also reports no statistical significance or confidence intervals, relies on GPT-4 preference as one metric, and does not produce embodied task annotations, agent interfaces, or sensor-based ground truth, so its support for human refinement is limited to visual asset quality rather than benchmark validity. The 2024 human-refinement papers extend the 2023 automation evidence but also show that human review has been limited to aesthetics or preference. Older automated city pipelines further contradict the idea that automation alone yields benchmark-ready embodied data: [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) automatically generates semantic/instance labels from OSM and projected layouts, but the excerpt reports no FID/KID/depth/camera error for the dataset, uses orbit imagery rather than first-person AirSim observations, and omits human refinement, task annotation, or navigation ground truth. [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224) is the weakest contradiction but still shows constraints: it uses a LOD-2 CAD subset of 14,612 registered images because of accessibility, trains on color-coded categorical maps due to limited real RGB satellite imagery, and does not include human refinement or embodied-task annotation, indicating that automated city-scale generation may require substantial manual curation to become a reliable benchmark.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5)'s structured metadata and filtering approach: build a machine-readable city scene graph for the 2.8 km x 2.4 km Beijing district, with object identities, attributes, states, GPS/semantic zones, reachability, and task preconditions, then generate QA, dialogue, navigation, and planning candidates with templates/LLMs and filter them programmatically for valid objects, zone compatibility, count consistency, trajectory feasibility, and non-completion before human annotation. Second, make human refinement task-specific and measurable: for VLM scene descriptions, use manual review as in [(Shang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65145427d8dad66577b61225e169f8003819257f) but add inter-annotator agreement, double annotation, and error-rate reporting; for navigation, manually verify start/target/optimal trajectories in UE5.3/AirSim with distance, occlusion, traffic, and pedestrian constraints; for planning, require expert validation of multi-step ground truth, and use GPT-4 preference or aesthetic scores only as secondary diagnostics. Third, integrate automated city-scale observation generation from [(Xie et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddc64a5ce980b7b97e9bcad122682f289ac854ae) and [(Lin et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feef7e0835d93b17faa04461917888e304efe224): use OSM/height fields, CAD/voxel/octree representations, and camera intrinsics/extrinsics to render RGB, depth, segmentation, and LiDAR from first-person drone/ground-vehicle viewpoints, then validate with FID/KID, depth error, camera error, segmentation IoU, and sensor consistency rather than relying only on qualitative realism. Fourth, use [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32ef08a51d2a79334d4d088947768ae15af00db2)'s LLM multi-agent/PCG protocol to manage Blender/UE5 asset generation and refinement, with structured Description/Input/Limitation fields and visual feedback, but evaluate it on task correctness, trajectory validity, and sensor ground truth rather than aesthetics alone. Finally, report reproducibility and statistical rigor: dataset sizes (200 buildings, 100 streets, 6,000 elements, sampled viewpoints), hardware, hyperparameters, random seeds, annotation protocols, confidence intervals, and significance tests for BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR/CIDEr/Sentence-BERT, SR/SPL/NE, and model comparisons across Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo.

## Context: This provides diverse first-person visual inputs for perception and language tasks. Method: We will randomly sample locations within our city environment to capture RGB observations from multiple perspectives.

### Support

Strongest support comes from newer [(Marathe et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e32f870d06dc71bf5683cb024d8f448b1869bdac), which directly uses a random sampling function in Habitat to initialize an agent in 3D indoor scenes from HM3D, Gibson, and Matterport, move it in random steps and directions, and render multiple views of the same scene; the resulting MIMIC-1M and MIMIC-3M datasets contain 1.3M and 3.1M image pairs and improve dense visual pretraining, with CroCo pretrained on MIMIC-3M reaching 91.79 delta1 on NYUv2 depth and 53.02 L1 on Taskonomy surface normals, improving over MultiView-Habitat CroCo 85.60 delta1 and 54.13 L1, and MAE reaching 85.32 delta1 and 58.72 L1 versus 79.00 delta1 and 59.76 L1. This supports the proposed random-location, multi-perspective RGB capture as a scalable way to create diverse first-person observations, although the evidence is indoor and dense-vision rather than city-scale language. It also extends earlier object-level random viewpoint sampling in [(Insafutdinov, and Dosovitskiy, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59b013e5f39f1f09aba67f59d7d39671dd449282) and large-scale indoor RGB-D capture in [(Dai et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e52e37cd91366f07df1f98e88f87010f494dd16e) to a broader multi-view RGB setting. A closely related newer embodied-data effort, [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55d184e7bcf467f2824cd9603194e523b206f8e7), supports first-person multi-perspective visual data for embodied perception and language-grounded tasks by curating ego-centric RGB-D captures with camera poses from ScanNet, 3RScan, and Matterport3D into EmbodiedScan and showing that training improves a multi-modality baseline by 2.74% AP on ScanNet and 5.93% AP on the overall validation split, with head-category gains of 4.01% and 7.55% AP. This is relevant because the proposed city benchmark also needs multi-perspective observations for perception and language tasks, but the related work is indoor and uses RGB-D rather than RGB-only city sampling. [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927) provides direct support for random location initialization in embodied language and navigation: agents are randomly spawned in novel indoor test environments, advanced along ground-truth shortest paths to targets 10, 30, or 50 primitive actions away, and trained with first-person RGB, point clouds, and question text; question answering top-1 accuracy reaches 46.2% for spatial+RGB+Q, and inflection-weighted behavior cloning improves navigation over unweighted and forward-only/random baselines. This supports the idea that random spawns plus first-person RGB can support embodied QA and navigation, but the observations are target-conditioned shortest-path views rather than arbitrary multi-perspective captures at each sampled city location. More recent urban capture by [(Dai et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fb8204ba37eaad41b76e7c0e717d83db821ddf7) supports the use of real urban first-person RGB observations by capturing helmet-mounted wide-angle RGB video, 128-beam LiDAR, and 17 IMUs in 10 urban locations, producing 15 sequences, 100k LiDAR frames, 300k video frames, and 500k IMU frames over more than 8 km; downstream pose estimation improves when combining datasets, reducing LiDAR test MPJPE from 228.7 to 79.2 and PA-MPJPE from 149.9 to 60.1, and camera-based MPJPE from 102.5 to 67.6 after fine-tuning. This shows that urban first-person RGB is usable for downstream perception, but the capture is activity-driven and not a random-location language benchmark. Earlier view synthesis by [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151) supports multi-view first-person RGB generation from 3D environments by selecting nearest k reference views, projecting point clouds into target equirectangular views, filtering depth differences above 0.1 m, aggregating RGB with a locally weighted mixture, and using a filler network plus Goggles to reduce sim-to-real appearance gaps; rendering speeds are 77.7, 30.6, and 14.5 FPS after the full filler network at 128x128, 256x256, and 512x512, and L2 action-logit discrepancy is lowest at 0.204 when training on synthesized views and testing on Goggles-mapped real views versus 0.300 on raw real views. This supports the proposed capture of multiple perspectives, but only for indoor scanned spaces and paired source-target views. The foundational ScanNet pipeline in [(Dai et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e52e37cd91366f07df1f98e88f87010f494dd16e) supports large-scale multi-perspective observation collection by building 1,513 scans from handheld RGB-D video, estimating 6-DoF poses with BundleFusion at 1 cm voxel resolution, extracting 4 mm TSDF meshes, aligning scans with planar regions, IMU gravity, and PCA, and producing 3,391 crowdsourced annotation tasks; however, it is indoor, user-driven, and centered on 3D semantic reconstruction rather than random city-scale RGB sampling. Earlier synthetic indoor scene completion in [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d) supports controllable synthetic scene rendering for perception evaluation by creating 45,622 indoor scenes, 404,058 rooms, 5,697,217 object instances, and 84 object categories, rendering depth observations with volumetric ground truth, and achieving 30.5% voxel-level prediction score versus 19.6% for prior RGB-D approaches with 7 seconds per image runtime versus 127 seconds for a mesh-fitting baseline; this supports synthetic viewpoint sampling but is depth-only, indoor, and not language-oriented. Weaker support comes from [(Insafutdinov, and Dosovitskiy, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59b013e5f39f1f09aba67f59d7d39671dd449282), which samples five random RGB views per object with azimuth 0 to 360 degrees and elevation -20 to 40 degrees, and [(Fei et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba8b0fa4b2b32df3af77c013e53fe7bbc1845d3c), which uses ShapeNetRender colored single-view RGB images to increase camera-angle diversity and improves point cloud pretraining, with DR-Point reaching 93.6% ShapeNet classification and 84.6% on ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS; both support viewpoint diversity but are object-level and do not validate city-scale embodied language tasks.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is the newer rendered-to-real transfer gap reported by [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55d184e7bcf467f2824cd9603194e523b206f8e7): on a large-vocabulary multi-view 3D detection benchmark, a model trained on rendered images transfers poorly to real captures, showing a 5.99% AP drop on head categories and being 5.89% AP lower than a model trained on real images, while oriented 3D boxes cause a significant performance drop and replacing reconstructed point clouds with aggregated multi-view depth maps has minor AP25 effects but heavily impacts AP50. This directly challenges the proposed plan's reliance on synthetic UE5.3 RGB observations from a simulated Beijing commercial district as sufficient for real-world city embodied perception and language tasks, especially if the benchmark is intended to reflect real urban conditions. A second important contradiction is that multi-view RGB rendering can introduce artifacts and appearance gaps that require explicit correction, as shown by earlier view synthesis in [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), where view synthesis from sparse RGB-D panoramas requires depth filtering at 0.1 m, a filler network, and Goggles mapping; the best L2 action-logit discrepancy is 0.204 only when testing on Goggles-mapped real views, compared with 0.300 on raw real views, and rendering speed drops from 109.1 to 77.7 FPS at 128x128 after the full filler network. This suggests that randomly sampled city RGB views, especially with traffic, pedestrians, weather, and large-scale disocclusions, may not be clean enough for language grounding without additional rendering or appearance-adaptation controls. Third, [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927) shows that first-person RGB is not uniformly beneficial for embodied navigation: adding vision improves gaze-direction metrics and question answering and reduces collisions, but it worsens distance-based navigation metrics, and adding the question provides only limited benefit for behavior-cloned navigation; question answering top-1 accuracy is 46.2% for spatial+RGB+Q, 40.0% for attention+RGB+Q, and 38.3% for bag-of-words question-only. This contradicts a simple assumption that more random RGB perspectives will automatically improve all embodied tasks in the proposed city benchmark. Fourth, older depth-only synthetic scene completion in [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d) is limited to indoor depth-only observations and dense voxel prediction, with 30.5% voxel-level prediction score and 7 seconds per image runtime, and does not provide evidence that synthetic RGB city observations support language tasks; its test set is 500 depth images from 184 scenes and real evaluation uses 1,449 Kinect depth maps, so it does not validate RGB-only, city-scale, or LLM-based evaluation. Fifth, [(Dai et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e52e37cd91366f07df1f98e88f87010f494dd16e) demonstrates large-scale indoor RGB-D capture and reconstruction, but the data are user-driven handheld scans from 20 users over roughly one month, not randomly sampled city locations, and the excerpt reports no quantitative benchmark performance for 3D object classification, semantic voxel labeling, or 3D object retrieval, so it does not support the proposed random-location RGB language protocol. Sixth, although [(Marathe et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e32f870d06dc71bf5683cb024d8f448b1869bdac) strongly supports random multi-view sampling, its evidence is limited to indoor and object-centric dense vision pretraining, with no outdoor city environment, navigation, embodied language, or large language model evaluation; scaling from MIMIC-1M to MIMIC-3M improves delta1 by 2.33, ADE20K mIOU by 3.73, and Taskonomy L1 by 4.10, but these gains do not directly transfer to the proposed urban embodied tasks. Seventh, [(Fei et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba8b0fa4b2b32df3af77c013e53fe7bbc1845d3c) uses RGB images only as an auxiliary modality for object-level point cloud pretraining, with ShapeNet over 50,000 models, 1,024 points per model, and 64 groups of 32 points, and reports 93.6% ShapeNet classification and 84.6% ScanObjectNN PB-T50-RS; this does not contradict the city plan directly but provides no evidence for city-scale first-person language grounding. Finally, [(Insafutdinov, and Dosovitskiy, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59b013e5f39f1f09aba67f59d7d39671dd449282) provides no quantitative outcomes, only planned Chamfer distance and pose accuracy metrics for object-level shape and pose estimation, so it cannot support or refute the proposed city-scale random RGB capture method.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a real-world validation and sim-to-real mitigation protocol because newer [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55d184e7bcf467f2824cd9603194e523b206f8e7) shows rendered images underperform real captures by 5.99% AP on head categories and 5.89% AP overall in a multi-view 3D detection setting; collect or license real RGB observations from the same 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing commercial district, align them to the UE5.3 coordinate frame, and report synthetic-only, real-only, and mixed training/evaluation results for all five tasks. Use appearance adaptation or view synthesis controls inspired by earlier [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151), such as a filler network for disocclusions and a Goggles-style mapping between synthetic and real views, and report L1, SSIM, FID, or action-logit discrepancy metrics to quantify the visual gap. Second, formalize the random sampling method with a reproducible specification: define the spatial domain as walkable sidewalks, drivable streets, intersections, building entrances, and public plazas; sample locations using a seeded grid or Poisson-disk process with minimum separation, sample perspectives using explicit azimuth and elevation ranges, camera height, FOV, resolution, exposure, and traffic/pedestrian states, and report coverage metrics such as street length covered, building facade coverage, occlusion rate, and duplicate-view rate. This addresses the lack of camera sampling detail in [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d) and extends the random azimuth/elevation sampling in [(Insafutdinov, and Dosovitskiy, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59b013e5f39f1f09aba67f59d7d39671dd449282) and the random agent movement in [(Marathe et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e32f870d06dc71bf5683cb024d8f448b1869bdac) to city scale. Third, separate observation protocols by task: use random multi-perspective captures for scene understanding, QA, dialogue, and planning, but use target-conditioned shortest-path or manually annotated trajectories for navigation, following [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927), and evaluate navigation with SR, SPL, and NE while reporting 90% bootstrap confidence intervals. Fourth, include auxiliary modalities and ablations because related work shows geometry helps perception: [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55d184e7bcf467f2824cd9603194e523b206f8e7) uses RGB-D and camera poses, [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d) uses depth and volumetric labels, and [(Xia et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e89a4fe6e8286eccedd702216153f0f248adb151) uses depth, surface normals, semantics, and non-visual sensory outputs; therefore, even if RGB is the primary LLM input, the benchmark should expose depth, segmentation, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, and camera pose for grounding, error analysis, and ablations. Fifth, scale the dataset and report dataset statistics comparable to prior work: number of sampled locations, number of RGB views per location, total image count, scene/building/street coverage, object and category counts, annotation tasks, and human review rates, using the scale of [(Dai et al., 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e52e37cd91366f07df1f98e88f87010f494dd16e), [(Song et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a05db7a75c65ee61c3ca7a6e5401b946166290d), and [(Marathe et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e32f870d06dc71bf5683cb024d8f448b1869bdac) as benchmarks. Sixth, improve language annotation quality by combining VLM generation with human correction, as planned, but add inter-annotator agreement, category-specific QA templates for distance, position, and counting, and negative examples for occluded or dynamic objects; this is important because none of the cited works directly validates city-scale embodied dialogue or LLM evaluation. Finally, include baselines and statistical comparisons: compare random sampling against uniform street sampling, target-conditioned path sampling, and human-selected viewpoints; compare RGB-only against RGB plus depth/segmentation/LiDAR; and report BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, Sentence-BERT, SR, SPL, and NE with significance tests, following the evaluation rigor suggested by [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927) and the quantitative dense-task comparisons in [(Marathe et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e32f870d06dc71bf5683cb024d8f448b1869bdac).

## Context: This produces grounded scene descriptions while ensuring quality through human validation. Method: For scene understanding tasks, we will generate descriptions using vision-language models followed by manual review and correction.

### Support

Viewed as an iterative line from early captioning to modern VLMs, the strongest direct methodological support for the proposed VLM-description-plus-manual-review method comes from [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a25a41e40d7b2e09292d9f6651cb27e940a567), which explicitly proposes using large language model image-description capability to generate street-view scene descriptions and annotations, then having disciplinary experts review, verify, and correct output for visual auditing; this matches the EmbodiedCity plan's use of VLM-generated descriptions followed by manual review for scene understanding, although it provides no model, prompt, dataset split, review protocol, computational setup, or quantitative outcomes, so its empirical support is conceptual. The next strongest quantitative support for the human-review component is [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4), where five human raters labeled 3,669 Oxford IIT Pets test images across 37 breeds and average per-class accuracy rose from 54% zero-shot to 76% with one example per class, with gains concentrated on initially uncertain images; this supports using human correction selectively on uncertain VLM outputs in EmbodiedCity, but it evaluates classification, not free-form scene descriptions, and human accuracy remains imperfect. Further support comes from [(Qian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee156428803c5bd6e7372f6b27d74bcf88390db3), which constructs 281,000 NuScenes-QA pairs from structured 3D annotations and templates and shows a BEV-based model reaches 71.2% answer accuracy versus BLIP 53.6% and LLaVA 57.8%, indicating that scene-understanding data benefit from structured curation and that RGB-only VLMs may need correction or 3D/BEV assistance for spatial, counting, and motion questions in the proposed benchmark. [(Liao et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0314cfdc5fac4f9a9b9195c73dea9e4a1203b471) supports the need for human-validated outdoor urban labeling by describing a 300k-image, 80k-scan, 73.7 km 3D-to-2D street-scene pipeline with 379 annotation batches, annotator training, pilot tasks, regular quality checks, and cross-checks, which is directly transferable to the manual review and correction workflow for EmbodiedCity's sampled RGB observations, although the excerpt reports no numerical segmentation metrics or significance tests. [(Ma et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/891c84adbd2fe154180be9710b8de31fa6333665) supports language-model-based urban scene understanding with human validation by fine-tuning BERT on MIT Place Pulse 2.0's 110,988 street-view images and 1,169,078 pairwise perception scores, outperforming LightGBM, XGBoost, Decision Tree, Random Forest, and GBDT by about 42% overall, and validating against 15 residents at 71 Hong Kong locations with R2 0.42, but this is perception scoring rather than VLM description generation. [(Gao et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fa415c5052a2d98712d6b8f5ec5a1b61758fed) supports grounded visual-language data construction by generating 494K questions from 96K images using scene graphs and a 3.4K-item knowledge graph, and shows attention supervision and explicit knowledge improve grounding, which aligns with manually correcting VLM descriptions to be grounded in EmbodiedCity's objects, attributes, relations, and spatial layout. Finally, [(Anderson et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a82c1d1ccaa3a3d1d6ee6677de0eed2e93ddb6e8) supports the proposed evaluation metrics and grounded captioning objective by training an Up-Down attention LSTM on MSCOCO 2014's 113,287 training images, 5,000 validation, and 5,000 test images, using BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and SPICE, and reporting CIDEr 117.9 for a single model and 120.5 for a 4-model ensemble, but it predates modern VLMs and does not use manual review.

### Contradictions

Viewed iteratively, the strongest contradiction is newer quantitative evidence that RGB-only VLMs underperform on structured scene questions: [(Qian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee156428803c5bd6e7372f6b27d74bcf88390db3) reports BLIP 53.6% and LLaVA 57.8% overall accuracy on NuScenes-QA versus 71.2% for a BEV-based model using 3D perception features, BEV cropping, and pooling; this suggests that VLM-generated descriptions from EmbodiedCity's RGB observations may miss 3D spatial, counting, distance, and motion details that the plan's scene understanding, question answering, navigation, and planning tasks require, and manual review may be insufficient unless reviewers have access to depth, segmentation, LiDAR, GPS, or structured scene graphs. A second major contradiction is that grounding remains difficult: [(Gao et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fa415c5052a2d98712d6b8f5ec5a1b61758fed) reports MAC grounding accuracy of only 31.46% on its 494K-question, 96K-image scene-graph dataset, compared with 82.2% on GQA, and shows that even with attention supervision and knowledge items, functions such as Find, Find_Hypernym, Find_KG, and Recognition remain difficult; this warns that VLM descriptions may be semantically fluent but poorly grounded, so manual correction must explicitly verify object, attribute, relation, and location grounding. Third, human reviewers are not infallible: [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4) found human zero-shot accuracy of 54% and one-shot accuracy of 76% on 3,669 OxfordPets images, and CLIP zero-shot errors correlated with human errors, implying that manual review in EmbodiedCity can introduce or retain errors, especially for fine-grained urban objects, and requires inter-rater reliability and adjudication. Fourth, outdoor urban perception has persistent ambiguity: [(Liao et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0314cfdc5fac4f9a9b9195c73dea9e4a1203b471) reports qualitative errors in far buildings where 3D points are absent, porous objects such as fences and trees, and dynamic objects whose multi-frame 3D points are dense but less accurate, which are exactly the kinds of errors VLM descriptions may make in a 2.8 km by 2.4 km city benchmark; the paper also does not report numerical metrics or significance, limiting its evidential strength. Fifth, human validation of urban language models can be moderate and underpowered: [(Ma et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/891c84adbd2fe154180be9710b8de31fa6333665) validates a BERT perception model against only 15 residents at 71 Hong Kong locations with R2 0.42, indicating that small human-review panels may not establish strong agreement for EmbodiedCity's description quality. Sixth, earlier automatic captioning work shows that trained models can be optimized directly for caption metrics without manual review: [(Anderson et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a82c1d1ccaa3a3d1d6ee6677de0eed2e93ddb6e8) reports Up-Down CIDEr 117.9 single and 120.5 ensemble on MSCOCO Karpathy test, but without statistical significance, so it is weak evidence against manual review and mainly highlights that the proposed method must justify human correction beyond automatic metric optimization. Finally, the most direct human-in-the-loop proposal lacks empirical validation and raises feasibility constraints: [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a25a41e40d7b2e09292d9f6651cb27e940a567) discusses LLM-generated street-view descriptions with expert review but reports no description accuracy, annotation speed, inter-rater agreement, BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR/CIDEr, review time savings, model, prompt, split, or computational setup, and notes privacy, storage, processing, environmental, and financial costs, which contradicts the assumption that VLM-plus-manual-review is automatically scalable and reliable for a large EmbodiedCity benchmark.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify and validate the manual review and correction protocol before using it as ground truth for EmbodiedCity's scene understanding tasks: recruit and train reviewers, use pilot qualification, batch-level QC, double annotation for a subset, adjudication, correction taxonomy for object presence, attribute, count, spatial relation, distance, motion, safety, and context, and report inter-rater agreement such as Cohen's kappa, Fleiss kappa, or Krippendorff alpha, following the batch training, pilot tasks, regular checks, and cross-checks described in [(Liao et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0314cfdc5fac4f9a9b9195c73dea9e4a1203b471) and the expert review concept in [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a25a41e40d7b2e09292d9f6651cb27e940a567). Second, because [(Qian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee156428803c5bd6e7372f6b27d74bcf88390db3) shows RGB-only BLIP and LLaVA underperform a BEV-based model on NuScenes-QA, and [(Gao et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/59fa415c5052a2d98712d6b8f5ec5a1b61758fed) shows grounding is a bottleneck, the method should not rely on RGB VLM descriptions alone; EmbodiedCity should provide depth, segmentation, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, structured scene graphs, or BEV features to VLMs and reviewers, and use these cues to correct counting, distance, position, relation, and motion errors. Third, use uncertainty or confidence-based review, inspired by [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4), where human gains were concentrated on uncertain cases: have VLMs output confidence, ensembles, or self-consistency scores, and route only low-confidence or high-risk descriptions to human correction, while auditing a random high-confidence subset to estimate error rates. Fourth, strengthen evaluation beyond BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT, while retaining the captioning metric suite validated in [(Anderson et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a82c1d1ccaa3a3d1d6ee6677de0eed2e93ddb6e8), by adding factual error rate, grounding IoU or object-set accuracy, SPICE, human-rated description quality, and task-specific accuracy for distance, position, counting, navigation, and planning, with confidence intervals and significance tests, because none of the cited works report robust significance and the plan currently lacks statistical validation. Fifth, compare baselines systematically: VLM-only, VLM plus structured 3D/BEV cues, VLM plus manual review, structured/template generation, and human-only annotation, measuring accuracy, cost, time per image, and scalability, as in [(Qian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee156428803c5bd6e7372f6b27d74bcf88390db3) and [(Ma et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/891c84adbd2fe154180be9710b8de31fa6333665). Finally, address privacy, storage, compute, and sustainability constraints highlighted by [(Chowdhury et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a25a41e40d7b2e09292d9f6651cb27e940a567) and [(Liao et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0314cfdc5fac4f9a9b9195c73dea9e4a1203b471) by documenting model versions, prompts, hardware, batch sizes, learning rates if fine-tuning, data splits, and review workload, and by using active sampling or model ensembles to reduce manual review cost in the 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing commercial district.

## Context: This builds structured spatial and counting questions that test embodied reasoning. Method: For question answering tasks, we will create three categories: distance questions, position questions, and counting questions, using both template-based generation and human annotation.

### Support

Read iteratively, the literature moves from human-annotated video multiple-choice QA to embodied 3D QA to template- and LLM-assisted city-scale QA, and the strongest method-level support for the proposed distance, position, and counting question-answering categories comes from newer template-and-review pipelines that show structured spatial QA can be generated at scale and then quality-controlled. The strongest support is [(Rawal et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b65e39d681d901e956a8116353e224ebd4c0fb), which derives reusable templates from human questions, generates four distractors, applies degeneracy and educated-guessing checks, adversarial refinement with a local 70B LLM for up to five attempts, vision-reliance scoring, hardness scoring, and human review; it produced 298,887 training and 4,941 test multiple-choice questions from 9,396 clips, fixed 90.94% of weak training items and 90.24% of weak test items, and showed Gemini 1.5 Pro at 60.12% average accuracy and 48.22% hard-split accuracy, directly supporting the proposed use of template-based generation plus human annotation for distance, position, and counting items. Next, [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7530ff872ad718828fe44fe4610d50a5c48e7063) supports the exact category logic by building TOPVIEWRS with templates for counting, relative spatial relations, localization, and dynamic action counting, using four-option multiple-choice items and exact or partial match, although its indoor top-view maps and sub-50% vision-language-model performance show the need for first-person urban grounding. [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) provides strong support for human-annotated, situation-dependent position and counting QA: its multi-stage pipeline collected 20.4k situation descriptions and 33.4k questions from 650 ScanNet scenes, used multiple-choice answers, reported 47.2% model accuracy versus 90.06% human accuracy, and introduced localization metrics such as Acc@0.5m, Acc@1.0m, Acc@15 degrees, and Acc@30 degrees, which are directly transferable to EmbodiedCity position questions. [(Chou et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d27640ce75557156de13fb827b64446ef9cc0e4) supports template-based position and count-like question construction with placeholders for object, scene, color, and direction, negative or zero-answer items, balanced answer distributions, and location indicators, while its 1,490 images and 16,945 question-answer pairs show that template generation can reduce language bias. [(Azuma et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f6c652a392995bd047a2f7b94474ab1e6e23ff0) supports automated generation plus human editing and 3D grounding, with 41,363 questions, 58,191 answers, object boxes, and EM@1 23.45 versus human EM@1 51.6, indicating that human refinement and multiple valid answers are necessary. [(Sun et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc75c405cd54761197dff8f8599daba7326dd267) is the closest city-scale support, constructing 450k question-answer pairs over 193 city scenes, 2.5 billion points, and 10.78 square kilometers, with 32.5% spatial comparison and 52.8% multi-hop questions, supporting large-scale urban spatial QA even though it is point-cloud rather than embodied. [(Datta et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71762d71cac45f14a835f89ed048bf038be97382) supports position questions through top-down 2 cm maps and IoU, recall, and precision, with 36.09 top-down IoU on short tours and pose-noise analyses. [(Yu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/68a2eb5890eb67989df5fb42b929d10e5e8e5a47) and [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927) support distance and position reasoning by evaluating multi-target spatial comparisons, distance-change, hit accuracy, dT, IoUT, and QA top-1, showing that spatial QA should be paired with navigation metrics. Older work provides weaker but foundational support: [(Xiao et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b937ff4b05e2b56c2c2fcdfa5baa3085cd5a08c) shows human-annotated counting and binary descriptive questions and a large human-model gap, [(Tapaswi et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bdd75a37f7c601f01e9d31c2551fa9f2067ffd7) established human multiple-choice QA with counting and location types, [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c) motivated embodied QA, [(Ye et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77f079943af7b62209824aea9db335449f3648e0) supports 3D spatial and number QA with geometry and appearance, [(Yan et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c00846f91f071606cbad60be5125d56eaa1acd49) suggests structured question families, [(Patraucean et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42585728685d7b2567e62b03463d1520f1bbe47e) supports position accuracy thresholds, [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3) supports UE5 embodied QA and multi-round memory, [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443) supports scalable LLM-assisted situated QA with quality control, and [(Bohus et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5f14073579c345058447220f30e8536de439a56) supports situated human annotation and the need for action-history context.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that counting questions can become guessable or low-value if they are simple. [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) found that How questions, mostly counting questions, were an exception where question-only guessing could be competitive, so the proposed counting category must include anti-guessing controls. [(Ye et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77f079943af7b62209824aea9db335449f3648e0) explicitly rejected simple predefined patterns such as known-object existence, counts, and colors in its ScanNet collection pipeline, implying that a naive counting category may not require scene evidence. This newer evidence contradicts the older assumption that human-annotated counting or location multiple-choice QA is automatically valid, as in [(Tapaswi et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bdd75a37f7c601f01e9d31c2551fa9f2067ffd7) and [(Xiao et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b937ff4b05e2b56c2c2fcdfa5baa3085cd5a08c). [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7530ff872ad718828fe44fe4610d50a5c48e7063) contradicts the assumption that template-based multiple-choice spatial or counting evaluation is automatically clean: some vision-language models performed below random in parts of TOPVIEWRS, mainly due to difficulty following four-option instructions, and the benchmark lacks explicit distance questions and first-person urban navigation. [(Tapaswi et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bdd75a37f7c601f01e9d31c2551fa9f2067ffd7) shows that video QA baselines can be near chance and late fusion of visual and subtitle information can be poor, with SSCB visual-text at 22.3% versus all-text at 27.7%, warning that long visual context and counting or location questions may not be answerable without strong grounding. [(Xiao et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b937ff4b05e2b56c2c2fcdfa5baa3085cd5a08c) reports that counting questions are folded into descriptive questions and that blind language models remain far below humans, with fine-tuned BERT BlindQA at 43.76% overall versus 88.38% human, suggesting that counting evaluation needs separate metrics and human baselines. [(Azuma et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f6c652a392995bd047a2f7b94474ab1e6e23ff0) shows low model EM@1 23.45 and a large human-model gap, indicating that automatically generated plus human-edited questions can still be ambiguous or require multiple valid answers. [(Datta et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71762d71cac45f14a835f89ed048bf038be97382) contradicts robust position-question evaluation under ideal sensors by showing 39% to 43% IoU drops when ground-truth-pose-trained models are evaluated under noisy pose, so EmbodiedCity position questions must account for GPS, IMU, and pose noise. [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927) warns that distance-based navigation metrics can be confounded by stopping behavior, collisions, and behavior cloning, and that question input did not produce large improvements under behavior cloning, so distance questions must be evaluated separately from navigation policy artifacts. Weaker contradictions or gaps include [(Sun et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc75c405cd54761197dff8f8599daba7326dd267), whose city-scale point-cloud QA lacks embodied navigation metrics and reported no quantitative results in the excerpt; [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3), whose 90-question UE5 cafe study lacks category breakdowns, template details, and significance tests; [(Bohus et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5f14073579c345058447220f30e8536de439a56), whose situated assistance pilot is not spatial or counting and shows text alone is insufficient; [(Yan et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c00846f91f071606cbad60be5125d56eaa1acd49) and [(Das et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc5ac1d3083b6663482ba6830dfa3bf65343286c), which provide limited quantitative or methodological detail for direct comparison; [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443), which relies on LLM-assisted generation and GPT-based scoring rather than explicit template-based distance, position, and counting categories; and [(Patraucean et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42585728685d7b2567e62b03463d1520f1bbe47e), which lacks reported QA accuracy and counting tasks in the supplied excerpt.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the three question categories non-trivial and scene-grounded rather than template-only. For distance questions, generate templates only from verified 3D scene-graph relations, GPS, LiDAR, depth measurements, visibility, and path constraints, and include absolute, relative, and multi-hop variants with calibrated answer ranges; for position questions, require agent situation descriptions, target bounding boxes, top-down or semantic map evidence, and negative, zero, or distractor locations; for counting questions, avoid simple known-object counts by requiring occlusion, scale, category ambiguity, traffic or pedestrian dynamics, or multi-region aggregation, and balance answer distributions. This should follow [(Rawal et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b65e39d681d901e956a8116353e224ebd4c0fb) with degeneracy, educated-guessing, adversarial LLM refinement, vision-reliance, hardness scoring, and human review; [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7530ff872ad718828fe44fe4610d50a5c48e7063) with template-based counting, localization, and partial match; [(Chou et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8d27640ce75557156de13fb827b64446ef9cc0e4) with balanced answers and negative or zero items; and [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b) with situation descriptions and curation against template-like or guessable items. The second priority is to define category-specific metrics and baselines before model evaluation. Use top-1 accuracy, exact match, partial match, random, language prior, question-only, and human baselines; report counting exact accuracy and MAE, distance absolute or relative error and tolerance bins, and position Acc@0.5m, Acc@1.0m, Acc@15 degrees, Acc@30 degrees, IoU, recall, precision, and NE, as in [(Ma et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1a4fb211cf995b1af1247a152fcc145594ec13b), [(Datta et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71762d71cac45f14a835f89ed048bf038be97382), [(Patraucean et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42585728685d7b2567e62b03463d1520f1bbe47e), and [(Xiao et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b937ff4b05e2b56c2c2fcdfa5baa3085cd5a08c). Because the research plan includes navigation, QA should be evaluated both with a ground-truth navigator and with the agent's own navigator, reporting SR, SPL, and NE to disentangle perception and reasoning from navigation, following [(Yu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/68a2eb5890eb67989df5fb42b929d10e5e8e5a47) and [(Wijmans et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2d3580faaa56be0eb7243fa119e6a7191924927). The third priority is to ground answers in 3D evidence and multiple modalities. Use object boxes, scene graphs, top-down or semantic maps, depth, LiDAR, GPS, IMU, and segmentation, and optionally provide bird's-eye-view or point-cloud inputs, as supported by [(Azuma et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f6c652a392995bd047a2f7b94474ab1e6e23ff0), [(Sun et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc75c405cd54761197dff8f8599daba7326dd267), [(Datta et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71762d71cac45f14a835f89ed048bf038be97382), and [(Linghu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8d405189f4b5556d2882c879454b65bfa66f443). The fourth priority is to scale and split the benchmark appropriately for a 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing district: use scene-wise or city-wise splits, sample locations across 100 streets and 50 km, create difficulty bins by path length, visibility, traffic density, and sensor noise, include a hard split, randomize answer positions, and support multiple valid answers or open-ended variants with BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT as in [(Sun et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc75c405cd54761197dff8f8599daba7326dd267), [(Rawal et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b65e39d681d901e956a8116353e224ebd4c0fb), and [(Azuma et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f6c652a392995bd047a2f7b94474ab1e6e23ff0). The fifth priority is to add robustness and reproducibility controls: test pose, GPS, and IMU noise, drone versus ground-vehicle action spaces, multi-round memory, and annotation protocol details such as annotator training, inter-annotator agreement, compensation, answerability checks, and release of templates, scene graphs, boxes, and code, informed by [(Datta et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71762d71cac45f14a835f89ed048bf038be97382), [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/adbdddcfc97ce2aa15e1c1ce126a14d51ab5b7d3), [(Bohus et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f5f14073579c345058447220f30e8536de439a56), and [(Ye et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77f079943af7b62209824aea9db335449f3648e0).

## Context: This evaluates whether agents can sustain coherent embodied dialogue over time. Method: For dialogue tasks, we will extend the question-answering framework to multi-turn conversations requiring context maintenance.

### Support

The strongest methodological support for extending the question-answering framework to multi-turn embodied dialogue is that long temporally structured dialogue can be converted into QA-style evaluation, as shown by [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/112293d48fa7aa453cad5564cdddd8005f89ffa6), which constructs LongDialQA from five consecutive seasons of Friends, The Big Bang Theory, and The Office, assigns dates to scenes, filters and matches questions to evidence scenes, and evaluates conversational LLM agents as multiple-choice items at temperature 0.2 and top-p 0.1 on NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPUs. The strongest quantitative support is that LLMs can maintain multi-turn conversational targets, as shown by [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472), where ChatGPT with standard prompting achieved 97.5% and 96.3% target success on easy and hard TGConv targets in 2.26 and 2.30 turns, and one-shot proactive chain-of-thought prompting improved Vicuna-13B from 63.0% to 72.3% easy success and from 62.5% to 59.8% hard success, with coherence scores of 0.52 and 0.48. This newer LLM evidence builds on older sequence-to-sequence work showing that context-awareness improves dialogue: [(Tian et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cac08d4eddc03d8d38656e1e57a430afb37670b) found weighted sequential concatenation achieved BLEU-1 5.322, BLEU-2 1.883, BLEU-3 0.9966, and BLEU-4 0.6897 versus 4.611, 1.488, 0.657, and 0.311 for context-insensitive models on 500,000 training samples, 2,000 validation samples, and 4,000 test samples, supporting the premise that preserving multi-turn context improves generated replies. Additional support comes from [(Xing, and Gulla, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8dda0c5777ec601f44ad1a20e7c96b352abcdeb), whose DAS ratio and distraction tests on Ubuntu chatlogs with about 0.48 million training dialogues, 20,000 validation dialogues, and 10,000 test dialogues show that context maintenance can be measured by attention to relevant versus distracting history, with distraction-trained models reducing DAS ratios by about 10% at 0.5 or 0.7 insertion probability. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Santhanam, and Shaikh, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b94dcac41325a03956402ff7862fa80936f9ddb) for hierarchical context encoding, [(Yu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/789c5dd71fb6085161931955339de2172539cdc8) for multi-turn role and dialogue consistency metrics, [(Chu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bf19d876b64922d41152a04955b309fb5ed8e929) for multi-turn consistency and factualness over 16-turn GPT-3.5-turbo dialogues, and [(Zhang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c35ff61df76580117326d10c86faa85869dcdaf7) for human ACUTE-Eval and expert-advised multi-turn dialogue, although these are text-only and do not validate embodied city observations.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the plan's proposed metrics, BLEU-1 to BLEU-4, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT, are unlikely to capture the main failure modes of multi-turn dialogue, especially when context maintenance is the target. [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472) shows that ChatGPT can reach target topics with 97.5% and 96.3% success but has low automatic coherence of 0.38 and 0.41, and human evaluation found lower global coherence, indicating that lexical or target-success metrics alone can miss conversational smoothness. [(Xing, and Gulla, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8dda0c5777ec601f44ad1a20e7c96b352abcdeb) further contradicts reliance on generic text-generation metrics by showing that models with similar perplexity can differ in attention allocation to distracting utterances, and that DAS ratio is needed to measure whether models attend to relevant history. Older work also warns against overinterpreting lexical scores: [(Tian et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cac08d4eddc03d8d38656e1e57a430afb37670b) reports BLEU improvements for context-aware models but no statistical significance, and its best model still had lower length, entropy, and diversity than ground truth. A second contradiction is that extending a QA framework may under-specify interactive dialogue: multi-turn dialogue requires topic steering, role consistency, factual consistency, and robustness to irrelevant history, not only answerable questions. [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472) shows aggressive target transitions can reduce global coherence; [(Zhang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c35ff61df76580117326d10c86faa85869dcdaf7) reports failure cases of short, non-empathetic responses when history is limited; [(Yu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/789c5dd71fb6085161931955339de2172539cdc8) shows dialogue stability and knowledge consistency can vary across role-playing methods, with Neeko expansion averaging 5.62 and LLaMA-chat RAG stability 3.03; and [(Chu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bf19d876b64922d41152a04955b309fb5ed8e929) shows multi-turn regeneration must address consistency, factualness, and keyword repetition, with reductions of up to 47% for creativity and 44% for collaboration. A third contradiction is that none of the cited related work validates the embodied city setting: all are text-only dialogue, scripted dialogue, or role-playing, without RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, LiDAR, navigation, or action execution, so the plan's assumption that a QA extension will transfer to first-person urban observations is not directly supported. Weaker contradictions include limited reproducibility and missing significance in several studies, such as [(Santhanam, and Shaikh, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b94dcac41325a03956402ff7862fa80936f9ddb) reporting hierarchical context encoding without numeric score differences, [(Zhang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c35ff61df76580117326d10c86faa85869dcdaf7) lacking numerical win percentages, and [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/112293d48fa7aa453cad5564cdddd8005f89ffa6) not providing quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpt.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to redesign the dialogue benchmark as an interactive, observation-grounded multi-turn QA/dialogue task rather than a simple extension of single-turn QA. Each dialogue should include a target topic or goal, spatial/temporal references, answerable facts tied to RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, or LiDAR observations, and explicit context-maintenance probes that require the agent to recall or integrate earlier turns, locations, object identities, or user constraints. This would combine the long-context QA structure of [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/112293d48fa7aa453cad5564cdddd8005f89ffa6) with the target-guided multi-turn evaluation of [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472), while adding embodied state that the text-only related work lacks. The second most important action is to replace or supplement BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT with dialogue-specific metrics: target success rate, turns to target, context retention accuracy, answer accuracy or F1, DAS-style distractor attention or probe accuracy, factual consistency, hallucination rate, keyword repetition, local and global coherence, and human or LLM-as-judge ratings for Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo, with confidence intervals and significance tests. This directly addresses the evidence that lexical metrics miss coherence and attention failures in [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472), [(Xing, and Gulla, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8dda0c5777ec601f44ad1a20e7c96b352abcdeb), and [(Tian et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cac08d4eddc03d8d38656e1e57a430afb37670b). The third action is to test context-handling strategies explicitly, including hierarchical or role-preserving context encoding, proactive chain-of-thought prompting, memory or retrieval over prior observations, and distraction robustness, following [(Santhanam, and Shaikh, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b94dcac41325a03956402ff7862fa80936f9ddb), [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13c85adfa950651ffcd91ef3018fa30801b74472), and [(Xing, and Gulla, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8dda0c5777ec601f44ad1a20e7c96b352abcdeb). The fourth action is to add human annotation and pairwise evaluation, such as ACUTE-Eval-style win/lose judgments and self-chats, to validate coherence, empathy, appropriateness, and factual grounding, as in [(Zhang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c35ff61df76580117326d10c86faa85869dcdaf7) and [(Chu et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bf19d876b64922d41152a04955b309fb5ed8e929). The fifth action is to include text-only baselines and ablations that remove visual, GPS, or action state, so the benchmark can isolate whether embodied observations improve multi-turn context maintenance beyond text dialogue. Finally, report model versions, prompt templates, sampling temperature and top-p, hardware, compute, dataset splits, random seeds, and statistical significance to avoid the reproducibility gaps seen in [(Tian et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cac08d4eddc03d8d38656e1e57a430afb37670b), [(Santhanam, and Shaikh, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b94dcac41325a03956402ff7862fa80936f9ddb), and [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/112293d48fa7aa453cad5564cdddd8005f89ffa6).

## Context: This creates controlled navigation instances for measuring embodied action performance. Method: For navigation tasks, we will manually annotate starting points, target locations, and optimal trajectories, ensuring varied difficulty levels based on distance and environmental complexity.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962), which manually constructs real-world urban navigation instances in a Google Street View graph of 29,641 New York City panoramas and 61,319 edges, with a leader hiding a target at a route endpoint, writing instructions, and a follower validating the example; the retained 9,326 examples, average 35.2-panorama paths, 108-token instructions, 89.1% validation rate, and human accuracy of 92% show that human-constructed start-target-path triples are feasible in outdoor city settings and that non-learning baselines are weak, directly supporting the EmbodiedCity plan to manually annotate starting points, target locations, and optimal trajectories for LLM evaluation. The iterative literature also supports the method: [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) established controlled start-goal constraints by retaining only paths longer than 5 meters with 4 to 6 edges, producing 7,189 paths with average length 10 meters and 21,567 instructions, and [(Kim et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a00369391b59b4edfc19dd02bc2f81a93f9cb9da) later showed that longer dialogue-path instances, with 1,653 NDH-FULL examples, average path length 25.05 versus 7.59, and average dialogue length 5.69 versus 3.78, create harder navigation and require nDTW, nDTW+, success rate, SPL, trajectory length, and goal progress. [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b) supports city-scale route construction by extracting 43,630 street locations and 80,000 road edges in a 10 km by 10 km New York area, generating shortest routes with OpenStreet Routing Machine and A*, annotating 10,714 routes with landmarks and directional segments, and stratifying difficulty by one to four landmarks, which validates the proposed use of distance and environmental complexity to create varied navigation instances, although it relies more on graph routes than fully manual trajectories. [(Jain et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/68ccecb380ecfc0a4b294b84e3d0b6ff6884c4df) supports the need for path-fidelity evaluation when manually annotated optimal trajectories are not simply shortest-to-goal, because fidelity-oriented training improved CLS by more than 14 percentage points on R4R and SPL could be misleading. Additional support comes from urban or aerial human-trajectory work such as [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e), which uses 32,637 human demonstration trajectories over 4.65 square kilometers with goal distances from 50 to 500 meters and human success of 87 to 90 percent; [(Liu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25d8e3c541f996e366d8fa48cba248ca330e8a78), which uses human pilot traces, 25,338 instructions, and a 20-meter success radius in large scenes; and [(Fan et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfa1ef7a6bc0d2f413694608202a93da66368e22), which pairs sampled destinations with starts within 1.5 km and collects 3,064 trajectories with average path length 287 meters, all of which support start-target trajectory construction with distance-based difficulty, although they are aerial or demonstration-based rather than ground-level manual optimal annotation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the closest city-scale and indoor benchmark work generally does not manually annotate optimal trajectories; instead it uses algorithmic shortest paths, sampled starts/goals, or human demonstrations, which makes the proposed fully manual optimal-trajectory method less established and potentially less reproducible. [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b) constructs 10,714 city routes with K-means node selection, OpenStreet Routing Machine, and A*, then annotates landmarks and directions, suggesting that scalable route generation can substitute for manual trajectory annotation. [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) creates R2R by sampling start-goal pairs and retaining shortest paths longer than 5 meters with 4 to 6 edges, showing that controlled difficulty can be achieved without manually drawing optimal urban trajectories. [(Mirowski et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/86430b50921c7aecb212b4cd0a43a73b091cf95f) uses randomly sampled starts and goals in city graphs, distance-based rewards, and curriculum learning from 500 meters to 3.5 or 5 kilometers, further indicating that distance-based difficulty can be generated algorithmically rather than manually. A second contradiction is that the proposed EmbodiedCity environment includes dynamic traffic and pedestrians via Mirage, while most related navigation instances are static: [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) uses static Street View panoramas, [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b) assumes no dynamic environment changes or actuator noise, and [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e) and [(Liu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25d8e3c541f996e366d8fa48cba248ca330e8a78) use aerial settings where ground traffic is absent, so a single manually annotated optimal trajectory may become unsafe, illegal, or invalid under traffic conditions. Newer aerial work also contradicts the sufficiency of manual trajectories: [(Liu et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/25d8e3c541f996e366d8fa48cba248ca330e8a78) reports random 0 percent, action sampling below 1 percent, oracle success below 3 percent on unseen splits, long-path success 1.8 percent versus short-path 7.4 percent, and human success about 80 percent, while [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e) reports human success 87 to 90 percent but model gaps, indicating that stopping precision, generalization, and scene complexity remain unresolved even with human demonstrations. A third contradiction concerns metrics: the plan uses SR, SPL, and NE, but SPL can be misleading when the annotated optimal trajectory is not the shortest path; [(Jain et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/68ccecb380ecfc0a4b294b84e3d0b6ff6884c4df) shows SPL can favor goal-oriented agents over fidelity-oriented agents on R4R, and [(Kim et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a00369391b59b4edfc19dd02bc2f81a93f9cb9da) shows goal progress can be misleading because a global-target model achieved goal progress 5.51 versus 3.82 while having lower success rate 19.8 versus 37.2 and lower nDTW 0.253 versus 0.518, so SR, SPL, and NE alone may not capture whether an LLM follows the manually annotated trajectory. A fourth contradiction is scale and cost: manual or crowdsourced construction in related work required substantial annotation effort, such as [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) with 11,019 instruction-writing tasks and 12,664 validation tasks, [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) with more than 400 workers and about 1,600 hours, [(Qi et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bdd8f6d3900e6e7fe492ea21fcdd87f3d8c857f) with over 1,000 workers and about 2,648 hours, and [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b) with 150 workers and route descriptions taking about 3 minutes, while the EmbodiedCity plan does not specify how many trajectories will be manually annotated across 50 km of streets, 200 buildings, and 6,000 urban elements, making feasibility uncertain. Weaker contradictions include the fact that many related works evaluate specialized indoor or aerial agents rather than the proposed multi-modal LLMs, and that some reports lack statistical significance or detailed hyperparameters, for example [(Ma et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b4439694205d007972af49b423d2ea9f28f7e2b) reports rollback effects without significance tests and [(Huang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c351e405660f09ded4ba2bd05a2362be292a3da3) provides detailed training settings but no quantitative outcomes in the excerpt, so the proposed method should not assume that manual trajectory annotation alone will yield reliable LLM benchmarking without explicit validation and statistical controls.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace purely manual trajectory annotation with a hybrid protocol that uses algorithmic route generation and human validation: sample start and target nodes from the 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing graph, generate candidate routes with A* or OpenStreet Routing Machine as in [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b), then have annotators refine legality, safety, and naturalness, and retain only examples that pass a follower validation step similar to [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962), reporting validation rate, inter-annotator agreement, and multiple optimal trajectories where ambiguity exists. Second, define explicit difficulty bins and metric controls: stratify instances by distance, number of intersections, traffic density, crosswalks, occlusion, landmark density, and path length, and report SR, SPL, NE, path length, oracle success, stopping precision, and path-fidelity metrics such as nDTW, nDTW+, CLS, or SDTW, because [(Jain et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/68ccecb380ecfc0a4b294b84e3d0b6ff6884c4df) and [(Kim et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a00369391b59b4edfc19dd02bc2f81a93f9cb9da) show that goal reaching and SPL can hide poor trajectory following; also define whether SPL uses the manually annotated optimal length or the shortest-path length to avoid metric ambiguity. Third, integrate the dynamic Mirage traffic and pedestrian system into trajectory validity: annotate time-dependent safe trajectories or constraints, simulate traffic during validation, and add safety metrics such as collision rate, illegal crossing, stop-line violation, and time-to-collision, because static related work such as [(Vasudevan et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490a7473715e9136de8a62fee16704eb4631b69b) and [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) does not address dynamic urban traffic. Fourth, specify dataset scale, splits, and reproducibility: report the number of annotated instances, train/validation/test splits by unseen streets or buildings, observation modalities, action space, UE5 and AirSim versions, random seeds, hardware, and statistical tests such as bootstrap confidence intervals or paired tests with false-discovery-rate control, following the statistical practice in [(Thomason et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b9ce27801c077433245ad0f9e43e3c38441cecd) and the multi-seed confidence intervals in [(Nguyen et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03fdf3abf8d6bb3ff35dc87742ad66722997caeb). Finally, include strong baselines and human evaluation: random, forward, shortest-path, target-only, no-dialogue, and LLM baselines such as Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo, with human performance estimates similar to the 92 percent human accuracy in [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) or 87 to 90 percent in [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e), and analyze failure modes by difficulty bin to ensure the benchmark measures embodied urban navigation rather than only goal proximity.

## Context: This tests higher-level planning and decision-making in urban settings. Method: For task planning, we will design scenarios requiring multi-step reasoning and provide ground truth through human annotation.

### Support

The strongest support comes from ALFRED, which directly validates the proposed human-annotated multi-step task-planning design by providing 25,743 human-written directives and 8,055 replayable expert episodes in AI2-THOR 2.0, with tasks decomposed into goal-conditions and sub-goals; human participants achieved 91.0% task success and 94.5% goal-condition success on 100 unseen directives, showing that human-annotated embodied plans can be executable and measurable, although the setting is indoor rather than the proposed 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing district, AirSim drone and ground-vehicle interfaces, and multi-modal LLM evaluation [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e). A second strong support is the LLM planner evaluated on ALFRED, which used GPT-3 with 100 stratified examples, 9 in-context examples, temperature 0, logit bias 0.1, and object-detector confidence above 80%, achieved few-shot performance competitive with the full-data HLSM planner, outperformed E.T., HiTUT, and M-TRACK, required about 7 GPT-3 calls per task versus about 22 for SayCan, and improved unseen success by 1.83 percentage points with grounded re-planning, supporting the plan to evaluate Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo on human-annotated urban plans [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718). Zhang et al. further support human-scored planning ground truth by constructing 100 household tasks from 20 types using BEHAVIOR-100 and VirtualHome frames, scoring plans as 1 or 0 by experienced AI researchers, and showing that GPT-4V achieved 19% general-description success while object plus spatio-temporal prompting raised success to 72% and 80% on 50 tasks, indicating that human-annotated scenarios can isolate object and spatio-temporal reasoning [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f). Li et al. support multi-step embodied planning evaluation with 134 unseen and 140 seen ALFWorld tasks, a 30-step limit, goal-condition success, formatted control-language scores, and reasoning-disorientation scores, with OPT-6.7B reaching 79.10% unseen success, although the ground truth is programmatic PDDL rather than human annotation [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). Padmakumar et al. support hierarchical human-annotated task completion with 3,047 human-human AI2-THOR sessions, a Progress Check action, Commander-Follower language, and state-change evaluation, but the excerpt reports no quantitative success rates [(Padmakumar et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ec6fa4e34200e13d80ee79b95d1cc6ec0f6b424). Shi et al. support designing multi-step difficulty by hop count, training on k=1-5 and testing on k=6-10, with monotonic degradation as k increases, although the method is textual spatial QA and automatically generated rather than embodied urban planning [(Shi et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a1acf6dce142d9a87d353c7edbda0127dbe69d7). Xu et al. support urban multi-step planning and decision making through a city simulator API with next-location prediction, point-of-interest navigation, daily schedule planning, and society simulation, but the paper uses simulator-defined success criteria rather than human-annotated ground truth and reports no quantitative outcomes [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that current multi-modal models may perform poorly on human-annotated planning: Zhang et al. report GPT-4V success on only 19 of 100 household tasks, MiniCPM with 0 correct plans out of 270 cases, binary human scoring, and no statistical significance tests, suggesting that the proposed evaluation of Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo may yield low and noisy urban task-planning scores unless grounding, annotation scale, and scoring rubrics are strengthened [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f). Li et al. contradict the emphasis on multi-modal LLMs by showing that fine-tuned 7B LLMs substantially outperform LMMs, with OPT-6.7B at 79.10% unseen success and 66.43% seen success, Bloomz-3B at 48.51% unseen, LLaMA-2-7B at 38.81% unseen, MiniGPT-4 at 25.37% unseen, and the hardest Pick two & place task reaching only 52.94% for the best model, implying that vision-language models may need fine-tuning, stronger grounding, or text-only planning components [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). Xu et al. contradict reliance solely on human-annotated optimal plans for urban tasks because their city agents are evaluated by simulator-defined success criteria and domain-general laws rather than human ground truth, and the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes, suggesting that open urban environments with traffic, pedestrians, and multiple valid routes may require state-based or simulator-based success checks in addition to human plans [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17). Shi et al. contradict the expectation that models will generalize smoothly to longer multi-step scenarios because all models degrade monotonically as k increases and most fail to generalize from k=1-5 training to k=6-10 testing, so EmbodiedCity must control step count and report zero-shot longer-chain performance [(Shi et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a1acf6dce142d9a87d353c7edbda0127dbe69d7). Song et al. provide a weaker contradiction because their LLM planner is competitive but limited to ALFRED's fixed high-level action set, indoor household tasks, and GPT-3 text prompts, with only a 1.83 percentage point improvement from grounded re-planning and about 7 API calls per task, indicating that urban open-world planning may require more actions, sensors, and API budget than indoor LLM planning [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718). Padmakumar et al. are a weak contradiction because the excerpt lacks quantitative success rates, baseline comparisons, and statistical significance, and the benchmark remains indoor rather than validating the proposed urban scale [(Padmakumar et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ec6fa4e34200e13d80ee79b95d1cc6ec0f6b424). Shridhar et al. are the weakest contradiction because baseline agents performed poorly, with SEQ2SEQ about 8% goal-condition success and task success from about 0.1% to 2.4%, and sub-goal successes were uneven, with Goto about 51% and Pickup about 32%, but the high human success shows that the human-annotated benchmark itself is sound [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e).

### Suggested Action

Most important, define a rigorous human-annotation and evaluation protocol for task planning that accepts multiple valid urban plans, uses multiple annotators with inter-annotator agreement, and reports goal-condition success, sub-goal success, path-weighted success, step limits, and state-change completion checks, following ALFRED's 2.55 goal conditions and 7.5 sub-goals, 1000-step and 10-failure termination, and human validation, TATC's state-change criteria, and Zhang's binary human scoring but with larger samples and significance [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e) [(Padmakumar et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ec6fa4e34200e13d80ee79b95d1cc6ec0f6b424) [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f). Second, construct a controlled difficulty ladder by number of reasoning steps or hops, with seen and unseen splits and zero-shot longer-chain tests, inspired by StepGame's k=1-5 training and k=6-10 testing and Li's 134 unseen and 140 seen 30-step evaluation, and report monotonic degradation [(Shi et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a1acf6dce142d9a87d353c7edbda0127dbe69d7) [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). Third, incorporate grounded re-planning and explicit high-level action vocabularies, object and spatio-temporal grounding, and tool or API use, using Song's 9 in-context examples, temperature 0, logit bias 0.1, object-detector confidence above 80%, Zhang's object plus spatio-temporal prompting gains, and Xu's City Simulator API constraints [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718) [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17). Fourth, combine human-annotated ground truth with simulator-defined success criteria for open urban tasks, especially traffic, pedestrian dynamics, and multiple valid routes, using Xu's domain-general laws and TATC's state-change evaluation [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) [(Padmakumar et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ec6fa4e34200e13d80ee79b95d1cc6ec0f6b424). Fifth, include baselines and cost analyses, such as few-shot prompts, in-context retrieval, API call counts, text LLM versus MLLM comparisons, and fine-tuned small models, following Song's 7 versus 22 GPT-3 calls and Li's Q-LoRA 7B experiments on four Tesla V100 GPUs [(Song et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee45aeb7c97e3346cc62f216f673b91277ac718) [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). Sixth, scale the task-planning set beyond 100 instances, report confidence intervals and statistical significance, and validate human executability with human participants, addressing Zhang's 100-task limitation and ALFRED's 100-directive human evaluation [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33539cd2a03a1ebcc9625ba10ecdc6e7e3b4c42f) [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e). Finally, document reproducibility details for the 2.8 km by 2.4 km Beijing environment, including scene metadata, action spaces, observation modalities, prompt templates, detector thresholds, and traffic constraints, so that the benchmark can be compared with indoor PDDL and human-annotated baselines [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) [(Shridhar et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4cf4246f3882aa6337e9c05d5675a3b8463a32e).

## Context: This quantifies the quality of generated scene descriptions, answers, and dialogue. Method: We will evaluate performance using established metrics: BLEU scores (1-4), ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr for text generation tasks, and Sentence-BERT for semantic similarity.

### Support

The related work forms an iterative line from n-gram metrics to consensus and semantic metrics, and the strongest support comes from [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f), which evaluated BLEU-1/4, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and SPICE on COCO 2014 (123,293 images; 5 references for most images; 5,000 test images with 40 references; 255,000 AMT judgments) and found SPICE Pearson 0.88 versus CIDEr 0.43 and METEOR 0.53, showing that n-gram metrics miss semantic detail and detailedness; this directly supports adding Sentence-BERT semantic similarity and human-aligned evaluation to EmbodiedCity scene descriptions, QA, and dialogue. Next, [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) validated CIDEr on PASCAL-50S (1,000 images, 50 crowd-written references) and ABSTRACT-50S (500 images, 50 references), achieving 82% consensus-prediction accuracy, 0.98 Pearson ranking correlation, and CIDEr-D Spearman 0.94, supporting CIDEr when multiple references are available, although it used 24 references for scoring and 24 for consensus. [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) supports METEOR on DARPA/TIDES 2003 Chinese and Arabic MT (920 Chinese and 664 Arabic sentences, four references, two judges rating adequacy and fluency 1-5), with system-level Pearson 0.964 versus BLEU 0.817 and NIST 0.892, and recall/F1 correlations of 0.941/0.948, supporting sentence-level METEOR for generated answers and dialogue. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) supports BLEU-1/4 in Chinese-English MT (127 sentences with four references, 500 sentences with two references, 20 blocks of 25 sentences, 20 judges rating 1-5, paired t-statistics of 1.7 or above treated as 95% significant), with human-BLEU regression 0.99 for monolingual judges and 0.96 for bilingual judges, supporting n-gram overlap as a baseline. [(Denkowski, and Lavie, 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5e4377d2149a8167d89383d785793967cf74602) supports using METEOR without language-specific tuning via 101,099 WMT12 judgments and held-out Russian/Hindi evidence, useful if EmbodiedCity text is multilingual or references are limited. [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008) supports ROUGE in DUC 2001-2003 summarization with 12-18 systems and 29-624 judged summaries per system, using Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall protocols, though the excerpt lacks coefficients, so this is weaker. Domain-adjacent works [(Chen et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4ba78d377eea9650e5e399a0878e30bd22f648), [(Chen et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8abaaa14c7b2f089c081bf031cc4b144a60a6759), [(Chen et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d3f7cea95bba8cb905454324c3eeb84d2b6e58), [(Zhang et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d237fa9155311740ecca679313fc1df7e7e4a2c8), [(Phạm et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c45c74dcbe4139fdb6b42206b1049c16f0111d2c), and [(Fan et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fadd6e5a8e877884dccb7ca5c8167f32f65ec5c4) show BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and CIDEr are standard for 3D, traffic, image, and sensor captioning; for example, [(Zhang et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d237fa9155311740ecca679313fc1df7e7e4a2c8) used MS-COCO, Flickr30k, and a 10,000+ image traffic dataset with five captions per image on four Titan V GPUs, [(Phạm et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c45c74dcbe4139fdb6b42206b1049c16f0111d2c) used KTVIC with 4,327 images and 558 test images, and [(Fan et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fadd6e5a8e877884dccb7ca5c8167f32f65ec5c4) reported BLEU-1 62.3, BLEU-4 23.5, METEOR 21.1, ROUGE-L 28.9, and CIDEr 26.7 on RCD with 1,035 clips and 3,610 sentences averaged over 5 trials, supporting transfer to urban scene descriptions but not dialogue or navigation.

### Contradictions

Newer semantic and 3D grounding work contradicts older reliance on n-gram metrics. Strongest contradiction is that the proposed metric suite may over-reward surface overlap and under-reward semantic correctness: [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f) found CIDEr 0.43 and METEOR 0.53 Pearson with human overall quality, BLEU and ROUGE-L penalized detailedness, and only SPICE rewarded detailedness and ranked human captions first, so using BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, and CIDEr alone for EmbodiedCity scene descriptions, QA, and dialogue is risky. [(Ma et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc3965a843b80e720aa03116d3f2cf3896e40592) cautions that 3D VQA text-level evaluation can miss hallucinations because models may use linguistic priors rather than genuine 3D scene understanding, directly threatening open-ended urban scene understanding and dialogue. The proposed Sentence-BERT component is also not directly validated by any supplied work; the closest semantic evidence is SPICE in [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f), not Sentence-BERT, so its calibration to EmbodiedCity human judgments is unproven. [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) shows CIDEr requires many references (50 per image, with 24 used for scoring) and can be gamed; CIDEr-D was introduced for robustness, so EmbodiedCity with few manually refined references may get unstable CIDEr. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) shows BLEU depends on reference count (human 0.3468 with four references versus 0.2571 with two references) and was validated on constrained MT, not open-ended embodied text. [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) notes human-rating noise reduces metric correlation and METEOR was validated on MT with four references, not city-scale QA or dialogue. [(Wang et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0af4274ddc948bb175be7662a699a8270878ced2) shows automatic BLEU, METEOR, ROUGE-L, and CIDEr margins were smaller than Turing-test margins in visual storytelling, implying text metrics may not capture human-likeness in dialogue. [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008) provides no numerical ROUGE-human correlations in the excerpt, so ROUGE remains the least directly validated component for EmbodiedCity. [(Chen et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4ba78d377eea9650e5e399a0878e30bd22f648), [(Chen et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8abaaa14c7b2f089c081bf031cc4b144a60a6759), and [(Chen et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d3f7cea95bba8cb905454324c3eeb84d2b6e58) condition captioning metrics on IoU matching, indicating that without grounding constraints text scores may not verify that descriptions correspond to observed urban objects, attributes, or spatial relations.

### Suggested Action

Most important: add a human-validated metric calibration protocol for EmbodiedCity: sample a stratified subset of scene descriptions, QA, and dialogue across drone and ground-vehicle views, task complexity, and environment conditions; collect multiple human references or consensus judgments for adequacy, fluency, correctness, detailedness, and hallucination; compute Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlations, paired t-tests, or bootstrap confidence intervals for BLEU-1/4, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT, following [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5), [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534), and [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f). Second, make evaluation grounding-aware: for scene descriptions and QA, couple text metrics to object, attribute, spatial, or IoU/scene-graph constraints, inspired by [(Chen et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4ba78d377eea9650e5e399a0878e30bd22f648), [(Chen et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8abaaa14c7b2f089c081bf031cc4b144a60a6759), [(Chen et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92d3f7cea95bba8cb905454324c3eeb84d2b6e58), and SPICE in [(Anderson et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f90d9c5615f4a0e3f9a1ce2a0075269b9bab6b5f), to reduce hallucination risk noted by [(Ma et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc3965a843b80e720aa03116d3f2cf3896e40592). Third, use CIDEr only with enough references and report CIDEr-D or robust variants, as in [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534), while monitoring metric gaming. Fourth, specify METEOR configuration: use universal parameters from [(Denkowski, and Lavie, 2014-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5e4377d2149a8167d89383d785793967cf74602) or tune on EmbodiedCity human judgments, report precision, recall, F1, stemming, paraphrase, and language-specific resources if non-English. Fifth, report BLEU and ROUGE implementation details: reference count, tokenization, stemming, stopword handling, brevity penalty, and ROUGE variant such as ROUGE-L or SU-4, following [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) and [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008). Sixth, calibrate Sentence-BERT with human semantic similarity judgments and compare against SPICE-like semantic-graph or human/LLM judges, because no supplied work validates Sentence-BERT specifically. Finally, for dialogue add multi-turn context and human-likeness evaluation such as Turing-style or pairwise human/LLM judgments, motivated by [(Wang et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0af4274ddc948bb175be7662a699a8270878ced2), and keep navigation metrics SR, SPL, and NE separate from text metrics.

## Context: This measures embodied action success and path efficiency in the city environment. Method: For navigation tasks, we will use Success Rate (SR), Success weighted by Path Length (SPL), and Navigation Error (NE).

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e), which evaluates urban aerial visual-language navigation on CityNav/CityFlight using the same core metrics as the proposed EmbodiedCity navigation method, SR, SPL, and NE, plus OSR. The benchmark covers 4.65 km2 across two real cities, 32,637 human demonstration trajectories, and splits of 22,002 train descriptions, 2,498 val-seen, 2,826 val-unseen, and 5,311 test-unseen, with goal distances from 50 to 500 m and a typical distance around 200 m; human evaluators achieve 87-90% success while Seq2Seq, CMA, and AerialVLN remain far below, and adding a five-layer convolutional geographic semantic map improves all baselines, showing that SR, SPL, and NE can separate model performance and generalization in a real city-scale setting. This newer urban result directly supports the proposed metric protocol and extends the older indoor metric lineage, although the evidence is aerial rather than ground-level and the excerpt lacks exact metric values and significance tests. The next strongest support is methodological: [(Gao, and Huang, 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cb7fd11450540ca82efcb8cda3029cbe9669f95) reviews 177 papers from 2005 to 2021 and identifies success rate and path efficiency as conventional navigation-performance measures, with SPL as a useful composite metric when the task setting is held constant; this supports the proposed metric family but also requires careful comparability. Earlier indoor work provides the metric lineage: [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) established R2R SR with a 3 m final-position threshold and NE as shortest-path distance, reporting random 13.2%, human 86.4%, and student-forcing seq2seq 20.4% on test, with 38.6% seen and 21.8% unseen validation success across 90 Matterport3D scenes, 10,800 viewpoints, 7,189 paths, and 21,567 instructions; [(Landi et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64d1b545f586d930cbe67402e853ab26a8f6e18d) explicitly adopts SR, SPL, NE, and OSR on the same R2R setting, defining NE as mean final-goal distance, SR as ending within 3 m, SPL as success weighted by normalized inverse path length, and OSR as oracle stopping, though it reports no quantitative outcomes; and [(Zhuang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3aa5b4df53e5b5b6731dc0651c97239623789828) shows that SR, SPL, and NE can distinguish model variants, with a Recurrent VLN-Bert plus slot-attention model improving over baseline by 4 percentage points on SR and SPL on R2R validation seen, 1 percentage point on SR and 2 percentage points on SPL on test, and achieving the lowest NE, using ResNet-152 Place365 features, a 2080Ti GPU, AdamW, batch size 8, and 100K iterations. Additional support for SR and SPL as sensitive embodied metrics comes from [(Rosano et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3fe5993973ac5061e4703c21aa43c5912cb117), which uses 1,000 start-goal episodes, a 200-step limit, a 0.20 m success radius, and a geodesic-to-Euclidean ratio greater than 1.1, and reports SPL dropping from 0.8001 without noise to 0.6553, 0.4041, and 0.1467 under small, medium, and large sensor/actuator noise after 1.2M virtual and 1.2M real fine-tuning frames, indicating that SR/SPL can capture real-world degradation. The weakest support is [(Savva et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a35e548de27b6924e5f2ee41d37238a5c4a1d5), which motivates point-goal embodied navigation benchmarks and comparisons between learning-based agents and SLAM/hand-coded baselines in Habitat, but the supplied excerpt provides no SR, SPL, NE values, dataset sizes, hardware, or metric definitions, so it only weakly supports the proposed evaluation protocol.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is not that SR, SPL, and NE are invalid, but that their aggregate use can be misleading in a heterogeneous city benchmark. [(Gao, and Huang, 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cb7fd11450540ca82efcb8cda3029cbe9669f95) cautions that SPL-like metrics are useful only within the same setting because optimal path lengths differ across environments, and that aggregate efficiency metrics can obscure fine-grained trajectory quality; since the proposed EmbodiedCity plan samples varied start points, target locations, distances, and environmental complexity across a 2.8 km by 2.4 km district with 100 streets totaling 50 km, a single global SPL may conflate easy short routes with difficult long routes unless tasks are stratified or normalized. The next strongest contradiction is that city-scale navigation may require route-level correctness and orientation, not only endpoint success and path length. [(Paz-Argaman, and Tsarfaty, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/caf39cb40e29587c59204fa486d30fc07d864593) evaluates dense urban map navigation on the RUN dataset with 2,515 instructions from 389 paragraphs over three 0.5 km2 Manhattan areas, using 11.132 m tiles, exact route following, endpoint within five tiles, and correct facing for single-sentence instructions; its NO-MOVE baseline achieved 30.3% on single-sentence instructions and 0.3% on paragraphs while humans exceeded 80%, and models still failed on abstract entities such as blocks, intersections, and traffic lights, suggesting that SR/SPL/NE alone may miss route exactness, map compliance, and goal orientation. A further contradiction is that SR and SPL are highly sensitive to success radius, episode filtering, and sensor/actuator noise. [(Rosano et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3fe5993973ac5061e4703c21aa43c5912cb117) uses a 0.20 m success radius, 1,000 episodes, 200 steps, and geodesic-to-Euclidean ratio greater than 1.1, and reports SPL falling from 0.8001 without noise to 0.6553, 0.4041, and 0.1467 under small, medium, and large noise, implying that the proposed city metrics must specify thresholds, noise, and episode validity or results may not be comparable. Older indoor work also contradicts direct transfer of thresholds: [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) uses a 3 m success threshold in building-scale R2R paths with average length 10 m and minimum start distance 5 m, and does not report SPL or NE values, so the same 3 m threshold may be too strict or too lenient for urban goals such as building entrances, crosswalks, or street-level targets. The weakest contradiction is evidential rather than methodological: [(Savva et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4a35e548de27b6924e5f2ee41d37238a5c4a1d5) supports point-goal embodied navigation but provides no quantitative SR, SPL, NE, dataset size, hardware, or metric definitions, leaving the proposed metric protocol under-supported by that source.

### Suggested Action

Most important, define and report SPL in a way that satisfies the comparability condition highlighted by [(Gao, and Huang, 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cb7fd11450540ca82efcb8cda3029cbe9669f95): for each EmbodiedCity navigation episode, store the manually annotated optimal trajectory length under the same traffic, time-of-day, and map constraints used by the agent, compute SPL per episode as success times optimal length divided by actual path length, and report global SPL only alongside stratified SPL by distance bucket, street complexity, start/goal type, and agent embodiment, with confidence intervals or bootstrap significance tests. Next, add OSR and route-level diagnostics because [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e) uses OSR and [(Paz-Argaman, and Tsarfaty, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/caf39cb40e29587c59204fa486d30fc07d864593) shows city navigation can depend on exact route and facing; report OSR to separate stopping errors from navigation errors, and add route exactness or map-compliance metrics, final orientation error, collision/traffic-violation counts, and time-to-goal, especially for ground vehicles and drones in the AirSim-based platform. Third, specify a city-scale success threshold and episode validity protocol informed by [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) and [(Rosano et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b3fe5993973ac5061e4703c21aa43c5912cb117): choose a goal radius appropriate to the target granularity, for example 3 m for precise door/entrance goals, 5-10 m for street-level or block-level goals, and report sensitivity to radius; filter or report separately episodes with trivial geodesic-to-Euclidean ratios, maximum step limits, and sensor/actuator noise levels, because SPL can drop from 0.8001 to 0.1467 under large noise. Fourth, include strong baselines and human references: random, shortest-path, map-only, and human performance, following the random/human comparison in [(Anderson et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c37c23b12e00168833eccff8025a830ce27c5abc) and the human 87-90% urban aerial reference in [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e), and evaluate Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo with multiple seeds and paired significance tests. Finally, incorporate geographic semantic map context as an optional input or ablation, since [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/103a79f7413e4b13eb54ca82c817d8aec491d72e) found that a five-layer convolutional geographic semantic map improves Seq2Seq, CMA, and AerialVLN, and ensure that the benchmark's RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, and LiDAR interfaces are logged so that metric differences can be attributed to perception, planning, stopping, or map use.

## Context: This enables comparison of model behavior under varying urban challenges. Method: Our experimental setup will systematically test each model's capabilities across different task complexities and environmental conditions.

### Support

The related work forms an iterative sequence: [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) establishes urban task tiers, [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) adds multi-modal dynamic urban simulation, and [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) adds embodied seen/unseen evaluation. The strongest support for the proposed method is that systematic multi-task urban evaluation across modalities and dynamic conditions is already shown to reveal model strengths and weaknesses: [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) builds CityBench from OSM lane topology, street-view images accessed by location and facing direction, satellite imagery, and CitySimu dynamic partially observed decision-making, then evaluates eight tasks including street-view geolocalization, social indicator prediction, infrastructure inference, geospatial prediction, mobility prediction, urban exploration, and traffic signal control using local VLMEvalKit/vLLM or APIs, with baselines such as LLaMA3-8B/70B, Mistral-7B-v0.2, Mixtral-8x22B-v0.1, DeepSeekV2, GPT-3.5, GPT-4, LLaVA-NeXT, CogVLM-v2, MiniCPM-LLaMA3-V-2.5, Qwen-VL-plus, GPT-4o, GeoCLIP, RSVA, Remote-CLIP, LSTPM, and MaxPressure; its finding that no single model dominates, that GPT-4o leads only on the first two visual tasks while GLM-4v-9B, LLaVA-NeXT-34B, and InternVL2-8B lead on the remaining visual tasks, and that VLM performance tracks LLM backbone quality directly supports the plan to test Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo across varied urban task complexities and environmental conditions. A second, strongly relevant support is embodied environmental-condition stratification: [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) evaluates MuEP from ALFWorld and Ai2-THOR with 140 seen and 134 unseen tasks, max 30 steps, first-person images for LMMs, PDDL and TextWorld metadata, GPT-3 davinci-003 task generation with room-compatibility and state filtering, Q-LoRA LLM fine-tuning, frozen visual encoders with projection/adapter tuning, four Tesla V100 GPUs, and metrics including success rate, interaction steps, goal-condition success, language control, and reasoning disorientation; its quantitative results, such as OPT-6.7B achieving 66.43% seen and 79.10% unseen success, MiniGPT-4 achieving 21.43% seen and 25.37% unseen, and Llama-2-7B scoring 0% on the hardest Pick two and place category in seen tasks, support the proposed plan's need to separate task complexity, environmental novelty, and failure modes. A weaker but still useful support is the tiered urban complexity design in [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17), which uses Level 1 multiple-choice urban knowledge, Level 2 human-written urban reasoning, and Level 3 planning tasks such as next-location prediction, POI navigation without a map, daily schedule planning, and society simulation, with hundreds of samples per task and a shared agent architecture restricted to the simulator API; this supports the proposed five-task benchmark by showing that urban capabilities should be decomposed from knowledge to reasoning to planning, although it is less directly aligned with first-person 3D embodied observation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the cited works directly validates the proposed 3D Unreal Engine 5.3 first-person urban embodied setting with RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, and LiDAR observations: [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) uses street-view and satellite images plus OSM-derived simulation rather than a 2.8km x 2.4km UE city with AirSim-style first-person control, and [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) uses a simulated city API and datasets rather than embodied visual observations, so their evidence supports urban task evaluation but not the specific embodied sensor/action interface. A second contradiction is that the cited urban benchmarks do not provide the statistical and implementation detail needed to reproduce the proposed evaluation: [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) partially improves on the qualitative reporting in [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) by reporting model rankings and VLM degradation values such as 4.10% for LLaVA-NeXT-8B relative to LLaMA3-8B and a smaller degradation reported as more than 1.88% for MiniCPM-V2.5-8B, but the excerpt still does not specify exact dataset sizes, hardware specifications, prompt templates, metric formulas, or statistical significance, while [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) reports only qualitative conclusions and success rates over hundreds of samples without specific rates, baselines, or significance. A weaker contradiction is metric and task-domain mismatch: [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) is indoor household embodied interaction, not city-scale navigation, and its metrics of success rate, interaction steps, goal-condition success, language control, and reasoning disorientation are not the same as the proposed BLEU-1-4, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, Sentence-BERT, SR, SPL, and NE, so its support for the proposed method is indirect and its results cannot be transferred to urban outdoor tasks without additional validation.

### Suggested Action

Following the iterative progression from [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) qualitative urban tasks to [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) multi-modal dynamic urban benchmarking and [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) embodied seen/unseen evaluation, the most important action is to make the proposed systematic testing explicit and reproducible by adding seen/unseen environmental stratification and task-complexity tiers, as in [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5): define seen and unseen city districts, buildings, street layouts, object placements, and traffic/pedestrian conditions, report performance by task category, and impose a maximum action-step budget analogous to the 30-step MuEP limit. Next, adopt embodied and decision-making metrics beyond text-generation scores, including success rate, SPL, navigation error, interaction steps, goal-condition success, language control, and reasoning disorientation, and add statistical significance or confidence intervals for comparisons among Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo, because [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) shows model rankings are task-dependent and [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) shows failure modes such as 0% category success and high reasoning disorientation require more than BLEU or ROUGE. A further action is to report the implementation details that the related work leaves incomplete or that the proposed plan currently omits: dataset sizes for the 200 buildings, 100 streets, 50km road length, 6,000 urban elements, and sampled RGB/depth/segmentation/IMU/GPS/LiDAR observations; prompt templates; local versus API deployment; hardware; hyperparameters; and metric formulas, following the local VLMEvalKit/vLLM and API evaluation practice in [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231). Finally, include task-specific baselines and backbone ablations, such as GeoCLIP, RSVA, Remote-CLIP, LSTPM, MaxPressure in [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) or Q-LoRA and frozen-encoder adapter tuning in [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5), and use hundreds or more samples per task as in [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) to ensure that performance differences are not artifacts of small samples.

## Context: This positions the benchmark as a reference point for future urban embodied agent development. Method: The evaluation will provide insights into current limitations of large language models in urban embodied intelligence tasks and establish baseline performance metrics for future research.

### Support

The strongest support comes from recent urban LLM/VLM benchmark work, which shows that the plan's central claim—establishing baseline performance for LLMs in city-scale embodied tasks—is empirically feasible. [(Schumann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66d41e0f894dda2c37dd5bacbdd7bfd418e3350f) evaluates an LLaMA-7B urban VLN agent on Touchdown and Map2seq, each with about 10k navigation instances, using CLIP-ViT-bigG-14-laion2B-39B-b160k landmark scoring, two-shot in-context learning, LoRA fine-tuning, and response-based learning; it reports task completion, shortest-path distance, and key-point accuracy, and finds finetuned VELMA-FT improves over retrained ORAR by 10% relative task completion on Touchdown and 16% on Map2seq, with response-based learning adding 2.9 and 7.5 points. This directly supports the plan's embodied action task and the use of SR/SPL/NE-like navigation metrics, although it uses graph-based Street View rather than the proposed 2.8 km by 2.4 km UE5.3 Beijing environment. [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) further supports the plan by constructing CityBench with OSM topology, street-view and satellite imagery, and CitySimu for eight urban tasks, evaluating LLMs/VLMs including GPT-4o, Llama3-70B, LLaVA-NeXT, and task-specific baselines; its finding that no model consistently dominates across tasks supports the plan's multi-task baseline design but also warns against a single aggregate score. [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2f1a101eb1c54cab321c47ce4f3b4b416f4786a) supports the plan's metric suite because M3DBench evaluates embodied 3D LLM baselines on dense captioning, VQA, embodied QA, multi-region reasoning, and planning using BLEU-1 to BLEU-4, ROUGE-L, METEOR, CIDEr, GPT-4 scoring, and Acc@0.25IoU, with PointNet++/Transformer encoders and OPT-6.7B/LLaMA-2-7B/Vicuna-7B decoders trained on four A100 GPUs for about two days. This is the closest methodological precedent for the plan's five-task structure and text-generation metrics, although it is indoor and does not include traffic, GPS, LiDAR, or drone/vehicle action spaces.

A second tier of support is the validation of the automatic text metrics that the plan proposes. [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) shows CIDEr can predict human consensus on PASCAL-50S and ABSTRACT-50S with 82% accuracy, close to human 90% and 83%, and that CIDEr-D is more robust to gaming, supporting the plan's use of CIDEr for scene understanding and dialogue if multiple references are available. [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) supports METEOR with system-level Pearson correlations of 0.964 on Chinese TIDES 2003, higher than BLEU 0.817 and NIST 0.892, and shows normalized human scores improve correlation, supporting the plan's inclusion of METEOR for text generation. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) supports BLEU by reporting 0.99 and 0.96 correlations with monolingual and bilingual human ratings on Chinese-English MT, and [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008) provides the original ROUGE evaluation framework using DUC 2001-2003, Pearson/Spearman/Kendall correlations, and single/multiple references, although the supplied excerpt lacks numeric correlations. These older metric papers are weaker support for the plan than the urban LLM benchmarks because they validate metrics in MT, summarization, or image captioning, not in urban embodied LLM outputs.

A third, weaker tier supports the plan's benchmarking protocol rather than its urban setting. [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) provides a concrete LLM/LMM embodied evaluation protocol with 134 unseen and 140 seen ALFWorld tasks, max 30 steps, Q-LoRA fine-tuning of 7B LLMs, and metrics including success rate, interaction steps, goal-condition success, language control, and reasoning disorientation; this supports the plan's need for seen/unseen splits, step limits, and failure-mode metrics. [(Wang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee5661886abd37718edd5efa38b8bb660e2a7204) supports the plan's emphasis on reproducible benchmarking by reporting 32 RTX 2080 GPUs, 180M frames, 4,608 GPU hours, seen/unseen validation, and success-rate gains from multitask training, although it is indoor Matterport3D and not LLM-based. [(Zeng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d26c947406173145a4665dd7ab255e03494ea28) supports the plan's LLM evaluation design by showing contamination control via 13-gram overlap and held-out tasks, and by reporting hardware and FLOPs utilization, which the plan currently omits. [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) supports the plan's real-world urban scale by constructing a 29,641-panorama NYC Street View graph and evaluating navigation and spatial description resolution, but it is non-LLM and therefore weaker evidence for the plan's LLM baseline goal. [(Qiao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3925ef53f864e5c30189272f63801248ff1406f), [(Thomason et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b9ce27801c077433245ad0f9e43e3c38441cecd), [(Deitke et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7bca917b753b996074203cc77ba45935b7c62d7), [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17), and [(Xu et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9ddf09502c7ce0a87f4a9d35aba07882056d6c1) provide additional but weaker support for embodied task suites, urban API-based evaluation, and synthetic urban VLN data, respectively, though several lack quantitative outcomes or direct LLM urban embodied results. Taken together, the related work forms an iterative sequence: older metric papers establish automatic score validity, indoor embodied benchmarks establish task and baseline protocols, and newer urban LLM/VLM benchmarks extend those protocols to city-scale LLM evaluation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the plan's reliance on BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT as primary text-generation metrics is not sufficient as stated. [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) shows BLEU scores are sensitive to reference count, with a human translator scoring 0.3468 against four references but 0.2571 against two, and that small system differences require block-level paired t-tests; the plan does not specify multiple references, block size, or significance testing. [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008) shows ROUGE was validated against human content coverage using DUC summaries and correlation analysis, but the supplied excerpt lacks numeric correlations, indicating that ROUGE's suitability must be revalidated for the plan's urban scene descriptions and dialogues. [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5) warns that BLEU and NIST were designed for system-level scoring and that sentence-level application is not ideal, while METEOR's advantage depends on normalized human judgments; the plan does not specify whether text metrics will be computed per sentence, per task, or per system, nor how human judgments will be normalized. [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534) shows CIDEr's strength depends on 50 human references per image and triplet consensus annotations, a data burden not reflected in the plan's automated generation plus manual review; CIDEr-D is also recommended for robustness, but the plan only names CIDEr.

A second major contradiction is that current urban LLM/VLM evidence does not support a single general urban embodied intelligence score. This newer urban evidence contradicts the older metric-centric assumption that a single automatic score can rank embodied LLMs. [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) reports that no LLM or VLM, including GPT-4-series models, consistently performs best across CityBench tasks, and that VLM post-training can degrade textual tasks, with LLaVA-NeXT-8B worse than Llama3-8B by an average 4.10%; this contradicts any implicit assumption that evaluating Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo will produce a clean ranking. [(Schumann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66d41e0f894dda2c37dd5bacbdd7bfd418e3350f) shows urban LLM VLN performance is highly dependent on CLIP verbalization, LoRA fine-tuning, response-based learning, and a fixed CLIP score threshold of 3.5 chosen by inspection, so the plan's zero-shot or unspecified prompting of large models may not isolate model capability from prompt and adapter choices. [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) similarly reports only qualitative success for CityGPT and no numeric Level 1-3 results, limiting its ability to validate the plan's baseline metrics.

A third contradiction is that prior embodied benchmarks often show learned agents underperforming simple baselines or lacking statistical rigor, which the plan must avoid. [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) reports a combined urban navigation and spatial description pipeline with only 4.5% accuracy at an 80-pixel threshold versus estimated human performance of 92%, and non-learning baselines such as RANDOM and CENTER are weak but still necessary; the plan does not include non-learning baselines, human performance, or spatial description resolution. [(Thomason et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b9ce27801c077433245ad0f9e43e3c38441cecd) shows learned seq2seq navigation models achieved only 0.59 to 6.16 m goal progress on seen validation and 1.05 to 2.35 m on test, underperforming a shortest-path agent at 8.06 to 8.36 m, and uses Benjamini-Yekutieli FDR control; the plan's SR/SPL/NE metrics are stronger, but it lacks comparable non-learning baselines and significance testing. [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5) shows LMMs often have lower success than LLMs and that language-control failures can be severe, with Llama-2-7B at 4.17% language control on seen Pick two and place tasks, yet no statistical significance tests are reported; the plan should not assume that multimodal LLMs will outperform text-only or simple policies without ablations.

A fourth, weaker contradiction is that much of the related work is indoor, graph-based, or API-based, so it does not directly validate the plan's 3D multi-sensor urban simulator. [(Qiao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3925ef53f864e5c30189272f63801248ff1406f) evaluates REVERIE indoor navigation with SR/SPL/RGS/RGSPL but does not report absolute scores, hardware, or significance; [(Deitke et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7bca917b753b996074203cc77ba45935b7c62d7) describes indoor ObjectNav/ArmPointNav/Room Rearrangement without quantitative outcomes; [(Wang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee5661886abd37718edd5efa38b8bb660e2a7204) uses Matterport3D and reports compute but no statistical tests; and [(Xu et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9ddf09502c7ce0a87f4a9d35aba07882056d6c1) describes FLAME urban VLN without quantitative results, dataset size, simulator, or metrics. These works support the idea of urban/embodied benchmarks but contradict the plan's assumption that existing baselines can be directly transferred to a 2.8 km by 2.4 km UE5.3 Beijing environment with AirSim-based RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, LiDAR, drones, and ground vehicles.

Finally, the plan lacks contamination control and reproducibility details that related LLM benchmark work treats as essential. [(Zeng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d26c947406173145a4665dd7ab255e03494ea28) uses 13-gram overlap checks and held-out tasks, and reports 96 DGX-A100 servers, 400B tokens, and FLOPs utilization; the plan does not specify how test locations, instructions, or QA pairs will be protected from pretraining contamination, nor hardware, seeds, prompt templates, or inference cost. [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) also omits hardware, sample counts, seeds, and statistical tests, showing that even recent urban benchmarks may under-report reproducibility; the plan should not repeat this weakness.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a human-validated metric calibration layer before treating BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, CIDEr, and Sentence-BERT as primary scores. Following [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9), the plan should collect multiple human references per scene/question/dialogue, compute block-level variance and paired t-tests or bootstrap confidence intervals, and report correlations between automatic metrics and human ratings. Following [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008), it should test ROUGE variants with and without stemming/stopwords and report Pearson/Spearman/Kendall correlations to human content coverage. Following [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5), it should compute METEOR at system level, normalize human scores, and avoid interpreting sentence-level BLEU as a primary metric. Following [(Vedantam et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258986132bf17755fe8263e42429fe73218c1534), it should collect enough references per image, ideally multiple references and if feasible a 10-50 reference subset, and use CIDEr-D or at least report CIDEr-D robustness, plus human triplet consensus accuracy.

The second most important action is to adopt an explicit urban LLM baseline protocol from recent work. The plan should include seen/unseen geographic splits, non-learning baselines such as random, shortest-path, frequent, center, and average, human performance, and task-specific success definitions, as in [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962) and [(Thomason et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b9ce27801c077433245ad0f9e43e3c38441cecd). It should evaluate Fuyu-8B, Qwen-VL, Claude 3, and GPT-4 Turbo under identical prompts, shot settings, and API/local deployment conditions, and report contamination control using n-gram overlap or held-out locations, as in [(Zeng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d26c947406173145a4665dd7ab255e03494ea28). For navigation, it should follow [(Schumann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66d41e0f894dda2c37dd5bacbdd7bfd418e3350f) by testing zero-shot, few-shot, LoRA fine-tuning, response-based learning, and CLIP/visual verbalizer ablations, and by tuning CLIP thresholds systematically rather than by inspection. For multi-task urban evaluation, it should follow [(Feng et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d0cc75418eb129922f46c5201b76749d3b30231) and [(Xu et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34ad751c1e797975452c655e3912313044d1dd17) by reporting task-specific baselines rather than a single aggregate score, and by including task-specific non-LLM baselines where available.

The third action is to expand the metric suite beyond SR/SPL/NE and text metrics to capture grounding, language control, and failure modes. The plan should add spatial description resolution or key-point accuracy with pixel thresholds and mean distance error, as in [(Chen et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f75f0750a00f6f85107985c70eca9c275b5e0962); Acc@0.25IoU or similar localization metrics, as in [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2f1a101eb1c54cab321c47ce4f3b4b416f4786a); grounding metrics RGS/RGSPL, as in [(Qiao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3925ef53f864e5c30189272f63801248ff1406f); and language-control, reasoning-disorientation, interaction-step, and goal-condition success metrics, as in [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). It should also add GPT-4 or human scoring for open-ended scene understanding and dialogue, as in [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2f1a101eb1c54cab321c47ce4f3b4b416f4786a), and report both automatic and human scores.

The fourth action is to make data collection and synthetic data generation more rigorous. The plan should document how many references, QA pairs, dialogues, navigation routes, and planning scenarios are generated per task, how human refinement is sampled, and how irrelevant generated responses are filtered, following [(Li et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2f1a101eb1c54cab321c47ce4f3b4b416f4786a) and [(Li et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4bf1d4946ec440a00f2521f861d14a29e81712a5). It should include synthetic urban navigation data and three-phase tuning or response-based learning baselines, as suggested by [(Xu et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9ddf09502c7ce0a87f4a9d35aba07882056d6c1) and [(Schumann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66d41e0f894dda2c37dd5bacbdd7bfd418e3350f), and it should separate training, development, and test routes geographically to avoid leakage, as in [(Schumann et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66d41e0f894dda2c37dd5bacbdd7bfd418e3350f).

The fifth action is to report full reproducibility details: hardware, GPU hours, model sizes, prompt templates, random seeds, API versions, inference cost, and statistical tests. The plan should follow [(Wang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee5661886abd37718edd5efa38b8bb660e2a7204) in reporting compute, such as GPU type, frames, and GPU hours; [(Zeng et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1d26c947406173145a4665dd7ab255e03494ea28) in reporting hardware and FLOPs utilization; and [(Thomason et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b9ce27801c077433245ad0f9e43e3c38441cecd) in using FDR-controlled paired tests. It should also report seen/unseen validation selection, checkpoint selection, and ablations for observation modalities such as RGB, depth, segmentation, IMU, GPS, and LiDAR, and for action spaces such as drone versus ground vehicle, because related work shows that performance is sensitive to environment, supervision, and metric choice.
