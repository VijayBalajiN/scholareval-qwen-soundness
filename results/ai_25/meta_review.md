## Path selection: To address the training-inference decomposition mismatch in BERT-family generation, the plan proposes path selection, which expands the search space during inference by sampling multiple optional decoding paths from candidate spaces and selecting the best one based on highest total prediction probability, using k candidate selections for re-masked tokens with the lowest-k total prediction probabilities at each decoding step and a simplified version that limits search complexity.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Mao et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514627a1a81152f03de0c1e0e9e73525fb8da03c), which uses Seq2Path with T5-base on a single Nvidia 1080Ti, batch size 8, learning rate 1e-4, max input 128 and output 32, 5 epochs for beam-search negatives then 20 epochs, strict F1 over 5 seeds, and constrained beam search with k 4-10 plus a learned true/false validity token; it reports strict F1 gains of 4.74, 1.75, 3.91, and 3.67 points on AOPE Laptop14, Rest14, Rest15, and Rest16, and 7.17 and 13.80 points on ACOS Laptop14 and Rest16, directly supporting inference-time multi-path expansion and probability/validity-based selection for structured generation. Next, [(Suzgun et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4bdc300db297756f36bedee2859b62df8e268c2) shows that sampling 16 candidates with temperature sampling and selecting by BLEURT/BERTScore improves ROUGE/BLEU by 3-7 points over greedy, sample-once, random sampling, majority voting, and prompt-and-rerank on data-to-text, WMT'16, and style transfer, supporting the plan's claim that candidate-path selection can improve generation, although it is autoregressive and utility-based rather than BERT re-masking. [(Das et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3d4273db96381330a98b1277fddc3907af95fbd) supports the importance of path scoring and aggregation, with LogSumExp 70.11 %MAP versus Max 68.77, Top(k) 68.20, and Average 55.80, and a significant Max versus LogSumExp difference at p < 0.02, indicating that multiple candidate paths can be combined effectively. [(Ding et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2396e95d152fb212d2f18102bac5c5453519bc2) supports candidate expansion plus verification, with F1 gains of +1.0, +3.1, +3.0 on AE-48, AZ-15, AZ-33 and +1.3 F1 on AE-110K, and on AZ-15 with 745,216 training samples and 11,000 test samples verification reduces answerable false positives by about 20% on AE-48 and filters over 40% unanswerable cases on AZ-15/AZ-33, suggesting the proposed k-path selection should include a learned or calibrated verifier rather than relying only on total probability. [(Miao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0c31511134abdd23f990310e8a2f2eb3a629b62) supports k-width candidate expansion as a practical inference mechanism, using token-tree width k with expansion at the third token and reducing decoding steps by 1.2-1.5x for greedy and 1.3-1.4x for stochastic decoding, while [(Rammal et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c38bdb99e8593cff4ae608f832ad47a8b719436) provides only weak conceptual support by evaluating a UMI value function with epsilon 0.05 and alpha 0.1 on LLaMA2-7B outputs from greedy, beam, and top-k sampling without quantitative generation-quality results.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that hard or top-k probability selection may be suboptimal: [(Das et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3d4273db96381330a98b1277fddc3907af95fbd) reports Top(k) pooling at 68.20 %MAP below Max 68.77 and LogSumExp 70.11, with Max versus LogSumExp significant at p < 0.02, and notes LogSumExp provides non-zero gradients for all paths and faster training than Max, suggesting the proposed selection by highest total prediction probability over k re-masked paths may underperform soft aggregation or learned path scoring. A second major contradiction is computational overhead: [(Miao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0c31511134abdd23f990310e8a2f2eb3a629b62) shows token-tree width k improves latency only at batch sizes 1 and 2, while at batch sizes 4, 8, and 16 larger widths increase verification latency and widths 2 or 3 are best, warning that the plan's k-candidate search may not yield net speed or quality gains unless the simplified complexity limit is tightly tuned. Third, [(Suzgun et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4bdc300db297756f36bedee2859b62df8e268c2) contradicts the assumption that model-probability selection alone is sufficient, because its best gains come from BLEURT/BERTScore utility selection, and gains are modest 1-2 points on deterministic BIG-Bench tasks such as IPA transliteration and tense change, implying zero-shot BERT path selection may show limited benefits on some tasks. Fourth, [(Ding et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2396e95d152fb212d2f18102bac5c5453519bc2) shows verification can trade precision for recall, with removing verification increasing recall by 5.0 and 5.6 while reducing precision by about 47.1 and 41.6 on AZ-15/AZ-33, and replacing verification with PURE changes precision/recall substantially, so a probability-only selector may be unstable without a verifier. Finally, the evidence is indirect for BERT-family masked generation: [(Mao et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514627a1a81152f03de0c1e0e9e73525fb8da03c), [(Suzgun et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4bdc300db297756f36bedee2859b62df8e268c2), [(Miao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0c31511134abdd23f990310e8a2f2eb3a629b62), and [(Rammal et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c38bdb99e8593cff4ae608f832ad47a8b719436) are autoregressive or extraction/KB settings, and none directly tests re-masked BERT decoding, GeBERT, or DPO path-preference training, so support for the central training-inference decomposition mismatch remains unproven.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a path-selection ablation that compares highest-total-probability hard selection against Max, Top(k) average, LogSumExp, learned verifier, and BLEURT/BERTScore reranking, because [(Das et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3d4273db96381330a98b1277fddc3907af95fbd), [(Mao et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514627a1a81152f03de0c1e0e9e73525fb8da03c), [(Ding et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2396e95d152fb212d2f18102bac5c5453519bc2), and [(Suzgun et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4bdc300db297756f36bedee2859b62df8e268c2) show the selection signal materially affects F1, ROUGE/BLEU, and false-positive behavior. Second, measure and constrain computational cost by testing k values such as 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, and 16 across batch sizes 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16, reporting per-token latency, decoding steps, and generation speed, following [(Miao et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0c31511134abdd23f990310e8a2f2eb3a629b62) and the plan's simplified search version. Third, incorporate beam-search-derived negative paths and a validity or verifier signal into path selection* training, since [(Mao et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514627a1a81152f03de0c1e0e9e73525fb8da03c) found beam-search negatives had major effects while random replacement had smaller effects, and [(Ding et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2396e95d152fb212d2f18102bac5c5453519bc2) shows verification reduces false positives; use DPO-style positive/negative path pairs but also report a learned true/false token baseline. Fourth, report reproducibility details comparable to [(Mao et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/514627a1a81152f03de0c1e0e9e73525fb8da03c), including 5 random seeds, hardware, batch size, learning rate, sequence lengths, checkpoint selection, and statistical significance, and evaluate XSUM/MSQG with ROUGE F1, BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR plus speed. Fifth, validate the BERT-specific claim by comparing GeBERT path selection against standard Mask-Predict, token-aware beam search, AR baselines, and NAR baselines on both zero-shot and fine-tuned tasks, and include ablations of RoPE/SwiGLU and prefix/suffix masking to isolate the decomposition-mismatch effect.

## Path selection*: To enable BERT-family models to learn preferences for specific decoding paths, the plan proposes path selection*, which integrates path selection into training by randomly sampling two different decoding paths for masked tokens, generating corresponding outputs, and using exact match accuracy or BLEU score to identify positive and negative samples; it applies a modified DPO loss combined with penalty terms and traditional masked language modeling loss, initializes policy and reference models from fine-tuned checkpoints, freezes the reference model, and updates the policy model using the combined loss function with hyperparameters λ₁ and λ₂.

### Support

The strongest support for path selection* comes from [(Jiang, and Bansal, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7f010513bebc23116f03cacf69290878d491f61), which trains a policy network to predict the next masked position in a conditional masked language model, compared with uniform random, left-to-right, least-to-most, and Easy-First, on WMT'14 English-German with 4.5M sentence pairs and WMT'16 English-Romanian with 2.8M sentence pairs, using examples with maximum source and target length 30, average lengths 24.21 for De-En and 27.62 for Ro-En, and an entropy penalty with lambda 0.001 for De-En and 0.001 or 0.0005 for Ro-En; the learned order gives a statistically significant dev-set improvement over left-to-right for Ro->En with p<0.05 and achieves the highest dev BLEU in three of four tasks on dev subsets with maximum length 20 or 30, directly supporting the plan's premise that BERT-family generators can benefit from learned path or order preferences, although it uses reinforcement learning rather than the proposed DPO-style positive/negative path pairs. A second, older line of work supports the broader iterative decoding setting: [(Ghazvininejad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5efadc9019ce3378a0eb6c8f939cdde6c8918b1e) evaluates mask-predict conditional masked language models on WMT'14 EN-DE with 4.5M pairs, WMT'16 EN-RO with 610k pairs, and WMT'17 EN-ZH with 20M pairs, using a base 6-layer, 8-head, 512-dimensional transformer with 2048 feed-forward size, 128k-token batches, 0.3 dropout, 0.01 weight decay, 0.1 label smoothing, Adam with beta 0.9 and 0.999 and epsilon 1e-6, learning-rate warmup to 5e-4 over 10k steps, inverse square-root decay, 300k steps, averaging of five best validation checkpoints, mixed precision on two DGX-1 nodes with eight 16GB V100 GPUs each, five length candidates at decoding, and evaluation using BLEU and SacreBLEU for EN-ZH; this validates the plan's iterative masked decoding and candidate search, but it does not learn path preferences or use DPO. [(Gao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36a3cfa6ed48988e23cf68b3abcbe097b29e594a) further supports staged masked decoding on MSCOCO Karpathy with 113,287 training images, 5,000 validation images, and 5,000 test images, using a 6-layer, 8-head, 512-hidden, 2048-feed-forward decoder, Faster R-CNN features, cross-entropy losses from the 2nd, 4th, and 6th layers with weights 1.0, 1.1, and 1.2, and an early stage that predicts high-frequency tokens such as a and on before bidirectional filling, while comparing AIC, NAIC, and MNIC variants with different masking percentages, suggesting that path structure can matter, yet it uses greedy decoding and no preference optimization. Finally, [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12) supports the need for a strong masked sequence-to-sequence baseline because MASS pre-trains an encoder-decoder with masked reconstruction and fine-tunes on generation tasks, including conversational response generation on Cornell movie dialog with 140K pairs, 10K validation pairs, and 20K test pairs evaluated with perplexity, and it uses the same optimization hyperparameters from pre-training for fine-tuning, but it provides no evidence for path selection* beyond establishing that masked objectives can be adapted to generation. Taken iteratively, the 2019 masked decoding papers establish feasibility, and the 2021 learned-order work extends them by directly testing path preferences, which is the closest analogue to path selection*.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Jiang, and Bansal, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7f010513bebc23116f03cacf69290878d491f61): the learned order is not uniformly superior, because Easy-First achieves the highest BLEU on De->En, left-to-right is best on En->De, left-to-right and Easy-First marginally outperform the learned order on En->Ro, and the learned policy underperforms on 100 long examples with length 30 or more where errors concentrate at final steps when remaining space is tight; this contradicts the assumption that a learned path preference will automatically outperform simple heuristics or AR-style left-to-right decoding, and it is especially relevant because path selection* uses DPO-style pairs rather than the RL entropy penalty that helped the learned order, while the plan does not yet specify length-aware constraints. A newer and less direct contradiction comes from [(Kim et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/744cd4de6b581ebb21bd4f2dc341a05b9d08f162), where scrambled chunk-wise reconstruction in a 6-layer BERT with hidden size 768, maximum sequence length 128, batch size 512, learning rate 5e-4, random seed 42, 6GB of Korean Wikipedia and Namu-wiki pretraining, a separate low-resource BART experiment using only 100MB or 700K sentences, and all compared models trained for the same number of steps improves QPAIR to 87.2% and hate-speech detection to 63.7% but degrades KorNLI to 64.1%, NSMC to 86.14%, KorNER to 76.23%, and QS to 74.5% relative to the +PTP baseline, with the authors attributing some degradation to non-convergence under limited computation and no statistical significance tests reported; this warns that adding ordering or permutation objectives to BERT-family models can be unstable and compute-sensitive, so path selection* must demonstrate gains over simpler baselines under matched training budgets. An older but weaker contradiction is that [(Ghazvininejad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5efadc9019ce3378a0eb6c8f939cdde6c8918b1e) already obtains strong parallel decoding with only five length candidates, beam size five for autoregressive baselines, BERT-style initialization, distillation from larger left-to-right transformers for EN-DE and EN-ZH, and a base transformer trained for 300k steps on two DGX-1 nodes with eight 16GB V100 GPUs each, so the added DPO path-preference machinery must justify its overhead against a strong, simpler baseline; the supplied excerpt lacks numerical BLEU and speedups, making this a caution rather than a decisive refutation. The absence of path-preference evidence and reliable quantitative outcomes in [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12) and [(Gao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36a3cfa6ed48988e23cf68b3abcbe097b29e594a) is not a direct contradiction, but it means the related work does not yet validate the specific DPO loss, frozen reference model, or penalty terms proposed in the plan.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to benchmark path selection* against the strongest path/order baselines identified in the literature, especially left-to-right, uniform random, least-to-most, Easy-First, and the RL-trained order from [(Jiang, and Bansal, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7f010513bebc23116f03cacf69290878d491f61), as well as mask-predict with five length candidates and beam size five from [(Ghazvininejad et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5efadc9019ce3378a0eb6c8f939cdde6c8918b1e), because the evidence shows learned orders can be competitive but not consistently best and simple heuristics may already capture much of the benefit. Second, the method should add length-aware decoding and evaluation, including multiple length candidates, dynamic length prediction, or explicit length conditioning, and test long sequences separately, since [(Jiang, and Bansal, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7f010513bebc23116f03cacf69290878d491f61) found fixed-length bottlenecks and errors concentrated at final steps when space is tight. Third, initialize policy and reference models from fine-tuned checkpoints and ablate the DPO training systematically: compare DPO-only, DPO plus penalty terms for invalid paths, length mismatches, or low-confidence pairs, DPO plus masked language modeling, different lambda1 and lambda2 values, different positive/negative scoring functions such as exact match versus BLEU, a margin or confidence threshold for positive/negative pairs, different path sampling counts k, token-aware beam search variants, and frozen versus unfrozen reference models, with multiple seeds and significance tests, to isolate whether the preference loss itself helps rather than additional compute or decoding. Fourth, because [(Kim et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/744cd4de6b581ebb21bd4f2dc341a05b9d08f162) shows ordering objectives can degrade BERT performance under limited compute, the plan should report convergence diagnostics, training curves, matched step counts, hardware, batch size, learning rate, prefix and suffix masking ratios, and model sizes for the 124M and 352M GeBERT runs trained for 150k update steps on the Pile, and avoid claiming path-selection gains unless the baseline is fully converged. Fifth, the evaluation should include the iterative masked decoding and masked sequence-to-sequence baselines from [(Gao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36a3cfa6ed48988e23cf68b3abcbe097b29e594a) and [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12), with staged high-frequency token prediction, bidirectional fill, and MASS-style fine-tuning, so that GeBERT's RoPE and SwiGLU changes are not confounded with path-selection gains. Finally, report quantitative outcomes on the planned tasks with exact metrics: ROUGE F1 for XSUM, BLEU, ROUGE-L, and METEOR for MSQG, and LM-eval scores for ARC, BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, Race, SciQ, LogiQA, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA, including speed comparisons against AR baselines such as MASS, BART, ProphetNet, OPT, GPT-neo, Pythia, and RWKV at comparable 150M and 350M parameter scales, and report exact BLEU values, ROUGE scores, statistical significance, and multiple seeds.

## GeBERT: To support fair evaluation of BERT-family generative potential, the plan develops GeBERT, a new BERT-family variant with modified masking mechanisms during training, incorporating Rotary Positional Embedding and SwiGLU activation functions.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481), which directly tests corruption-based pre-training for generation and shows that masking/deletion choices materially affect ROUGE and human preference. BART pre-trains a 12+12-layer, hidden-1024 encoder-decoder on 160GB of news, books, stories, and web text for 500,000 steps with batch size 8000, GPT-2 BPE, 30% token masking, all-sentence permutation, label-smoothed cross-entropy with smoothing 0.1, and beam search with beam size 5, duplicate trigram removal, and length penalties; it improves XSum ROUGE by about 3.5 over the best prior RoBERTa-based system and outperforms CNN/DailyMail baselines, with human preference above prior models but below human references. This supports GeBERT's core premise that modified training-time corruption/masking can improve BERT-family generation and that generation-focused evaluation is necessary. It also supports the plan's path selection idea because BART's inference uses constrained beam search and length controls, indicating that decoding constraints matter. The next strongest support is [(Chang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c597874535c1537d7ddff3b3723015b4dc79d30), which shows a bidirectional masked transformer can generate by iterative decoding with a mask schedule and confidence-based finalization; MaskGIT uses a 24-layer, 768-dim transformer trained on 4x4 TPU devices with batch size 256, label smoothing 0.1, and dropout 0.1, and achieves FID 6.18 and IS 182.1 on ImageNet 256x256, 30 to 64 times faster decoding than VQGAN, with a cosine schedule outperforming linear, concave, and convex schedules. Although image-domain and using learnable positions rather than RoPE/SwiGLU, it supports the plan's modified masking and path-selection decoding by demonstrating that scheduled, confidence-guided unmasking can make masked models competitive. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) supports the evaluation design rather than the architecture: its zero/one/few-shot protocol, scale-matched 125M and 350M models, 2048-token context, K demonstrations typically 10 to 100, beam width 4, length penalty 0.6, and task-specific metrics such as PIQA 80.5 zero-shot, Winograd 88.3, and Winogrande 70.2 provide a template for fairly comparing GeBERT 124M/352M against AR baselines on ARC, PIQA, WinoGrande, and related tasks. Finally, [(Vries et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d5e425cac0bf84c86c0c9f720b6339d6288ffa) provides weak but relevant support that BERT-family objectives can be modified, using sentence-order prediction, word-piece grouping, 15% token masking with 80/10/10 replacement, 12GB/2.4B-token Dutch corpus, and 1M iterations, but it evaluates only Dutch NER, POS, SRL, and sentiment and reports no generation metrics, so its support for GeBERT's generative claim is limited.

### Contradictions

The most direct tension is from [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481): BART finds that token deletion outperforms masking on generation tasks, while objectives based only on rotation or sentence permutation perform poorly, and its gains come from a denoising seq2seq model rather than a pure BERT-style bidirectional model. If GeBERT's modified masking only changes prefix/suffix masking ratios without adding deletion, span corruption, or permutation, the evidence suggests it may underperform relative to BART-style denoising. BART also used 500,000 steps, batch size 8000, 160GB corpus, and a larger 12+12 hidden-1024 model, whereas the plan pre-trains 124M/352M GeBERT for 150k steps on the Pile; this compute gap means the plan's masking changes may be underpowered or not comparable. Second, [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows AR in-context learning is already strong on several zero-shot commonsense tasks, with PIQA 80.5 and Winograd 88.3, and that GPT-3's results are complicated by a filtering bug leaving benchmark overlap, so any GeBERT zero-shot advantage must be measured with contamination controls and statistical significance; the same paper also shows AR models remain weak on Winogrande, 70.2 zero-shot versus 79 fine-tuned RoBERTa, and on synthetic manipulation, which tempers but does not remove the risk that BERT-family models may not close the generation gap. Third, [(Chang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c597874535c1537d7ddff3b3723015b4dc79d30) supports iterative masked decoding only in a class-conditional image setting with calibrated confidence scores and a cosine mask schedule; it does not show that RoPE, SwiGLU, or DPO-style path preference training help, and its confidence-based finalization is not the same as GeBERT's lowest-k total-probability path selection, so the transfer to text is indirect. Finally, [(Vries et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d5e425cac0bf84c86c0c9f720b6339d6288ffa) provides no numeric generation evidence and no RoPE/SwiGLU, so it cannot support the claim that modified BERT masking plus modern components improves generation; its downstream understanding-only evaluation is a weak contradiction to the plan's generative focus.

### Suggested Action

Most important, revise GeBERT's pre-training corruption to include BART-style token deletion and sentence/span permutation in addition to prefix/suffix masking, and ablate deletion versus masking at 30% corruption, label smoothing 0.1, and beam search with duplicate trigram removal and length penalties, because [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) shows deletion can outperform masking for generation. Second, for path selection and path selection*, adopt MaskGIT-style iterative decoding controls: define a mask schedule, test cosine, linear, concave, and convex schedules, use calibrated confidence scores to finalize tokens, and compare this against the plan's lowest-k total-probability path selection; because no provided work validates DPO-style path preference, also ablate the DPO loss, lambda1/lambda2, and scoring functions such as exact match versus BLEU, as motivated by [(Chang et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7c597874535c1537d7ddff3b3723015b4dc79d30). Third, implement the GPT-3 zero/one/few-shot evaluation protocol for ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, PIQA, WinoGrande, and related tasks, with scale-matched AR baselines around 125M/350M, beam width 4, length penalty 0.6, likelihood scoring for multiple-choice tasks, explicit contamination checks, and significance tests, following [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0). Fourth, add discriminative-task ablations, including NER, POS, SRL, and sentiment, using fixed four-epoch fine-tuning and identical hyperparameters, following [(Vries et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d5e425cac0bf84c86c0c9f720b6339d6288ffa), to verify that generation-oriented masking does not degrade understanding. Fifth, report and align compute by documenting tokens seen, batch size, steps, hardware, and learning rate, and consider extending beyond 150k steps or matching BART's 500k-step/batch-8000 token budget, since BART's results were obtained at larger scale. Finally, because none of the provided works directly validates RoPE or SwiGLU for BERT generation, include ablations of RoPE versus absolute or learnable positions and SwiGLU versus GELU, and treat these components as secondary unless they show measurable gains on XSUM, MSQG, and zero-shot tasks.

## Pre-training GeBERT with generative masked language modeling: To create the base models for testing the decomposition mismatch hypothesis, the plan pre-trains 124M and 352M GeBERT models on the Pile dataset for 150k update steps using a generative masked language modeling objective and a novel masking strategy that decomposes instances into prefix and suffix parts with different masking ratios.

### Support

The strongest support for pre-training GeBERT with prefix/suffix-aware generative masked language modeling comes from [(Ketykó et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab226ac6347b07f45d02bd7dda2d4d7e23a4478e), which directly tests BERT-family PMLM for suffix prediction in event logs and reports that BERT is the only model not comparable to GPT, LSTM, and WaveNet, with worse average remaining-time MAE and sensitivity to skewed trace-length distributions; this provides empirical motivation for the decomposition-mismatch hypothesis and for replacing random masking with prefix/suffix decomposition, although the evidence is qualitative, uses 4-layer 128-dimensional models, 400 epochs, a single V100, and 9 process-mining datasets rather than 124M/352M Pile pre-training. A second strong line of support is [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161), which shows that conditional masked language modeling over source-target pairs with 15% target-token masking improves generation when used as a BERT teacher: IWSLT14 German-English dev BLEU rises from 35.27 to 36.93, IWSLT15 English-Vietnamese dev BLEU from 27.03 to 27.85, and Gigaword dev ROUGE-1/2/L from 46.64/24.37/43.17 to 47.35/25.11/44.04, supporting the value of masked target prediction and bidirectional context for generation, but it uses random target masking and requires distillation to an AR student rather than BERT-native generation. [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) provides large-scale support that denoising/masking pre-training can improve sequence generation: BART outperforms prior work on CNN/DailyMail and XSum, with roughly 3.5 ROUGE points over the best previous RoBERTa-based model on XSum, while remaining competitive on SQuAD/GLUE, but it uses an autoregressive decoder, 160GB corpus, 500k update steps, batch size 8000, 30% token masking, and sentence permutation, so it validates denoising pre-training more than BERT-style masked decoding. [(Dong et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c71771c701aadfd72c5866170a9f5d71464bb88) supports BERT-family generative pre-training by mixing bidirectional MLM with sequence-to-sequence and unidirectional objectives in a 340M BERT-large model trained for about 770k steps on Wikipedia/BookCorpus, and reports document-grounded dialog scores of 21.63 BLEU-4, 25.04 METEOR, and 51.09 ROUGE-L, but it does not isolate the generative objective or use prefix/suffix masking. Structured masking evidence from [(Joshi et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81f5810fbbab9b7203b9556f4ce3c741875407bc) and [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) supports modifying BERT’s random token masking: SpanBERT’s span masking plus span-boundary objective contributes about 1.8 F1 on MRQA and improves SQuAD 1.1 by 2.0 F1 over the authors’ BERT reimplementation, while ALBERT’s n-gram masking is associated with strong GLUE/SQuAD/RACE results, although neither paper evaluates generation. More indirect support comes from [(Zhou et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/abaadb4c6affc4d874c4f59bfac60686e851cb5e), where generative intermediate objectives for T5-base/large produce significant improvements (p<0.01), including 1.5 BLEU and 0.6 SPICE on CommonGEN, and from weaker, less quantified evidence in [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12) for encoder-decoder masked sequence-to-sequence pre-training and [(Liao et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f02bb4bc0b711fd90d32bd7dadd505465d6b45e1) for probabilistic masked inference, neither of which directly validates GeBERT’s prefix/suffix ratios, Pile pre-training, or 150k update steps.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan’s BERT-native generation goal is [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161), which explicitly notes that direct sequential generation from C-MLM is not feasible and instead distills BERT logits into an autoregressive Transformer or RNN student; this suggests that masked BERT-family models may not close the generation gap through inference-time path selection alone, and that the proposed GeBERT zero-shot and fine-tuned generation claims need careful validation. A second major concern is [(Ketykó et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab226ac6347b07f45d02bd7dda2d4d7e23a4478e), which finds that BERT’s non-AR masked objective is more sensitive to skewed sequence-length distributions and that BERT may require more training iterations for suffix prediction; this challenges the assumption that 150k update steps on the Pile will be sufficient for 124M/352M GeBERT models to learn robust prefix-to-suffix generation, especially when compared with longer training regimes in related work. [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) also raises a method-level contradiction: its small-scale comparisons indicate that deletion can outperform masking for generation, and its successful BART model relies on an autoregressive decoder plus 500k update steps and 160GB of data, implying that a purely masked BERT decoder may need deletion/span-deletion variants or more compute to match AR generation. Training-scale evidence further complicates the proposed 150k-step plan: [(Dong et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c71771c701aadfd72c5866170a9f5d71464bb88) uses about 770k steps for a 340M BERT-large model, [(Joshi et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81f5810fbbab9b7203b9556f4ce3c741875407bc) uses 2.4M update steps for BERT-large, and [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) uses 500k update steps for BART, so the proposed 150k steps may be undertrained for the 352M model unless Pile’s data volume and batch size compensate; [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) is closer at 125k steps but uses TPUv3 and a different ALBERT objective, so it does not directly justify 150k steps for GeBERT. Finally, the related work provides little direct evidence that Pile pre-training, RoPE/SwiGLU, or prefix/suffix masking ratios specifically improve BERT generation: [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12) and [(Liao et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f02bb4bc0b711fd90d32bd7dadd505465d6b45e1) lack quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpts, and [(Zhou et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/abaadb4c6affc4d874c4f59bfac60686e851cb5e) uses T5 rather than BERT, so the proposed combination remains plausible but under-evidenced.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a controlled ablation that directly tests the decomposition-mismatch hypothesis: compare GeBERT prefix/suffix generative MLM against random MLM, conditional C-MLM [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161), span/n-gram masking [(Joshi et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81f5810fbbab9b7203b9556f4ce3c741875407bc) and [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b), BART-style denoising with deletion/permutation [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481), and mixed BERT objectives [(Dong et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c71771c701aadfd72c5866170a9f5d71464bb88), reporting zero-shot LME scores and fine-tuned XSum/MSQG BLEU/ROUGE/METEOR with multiple seeds and statistical significance. Second, because BERT-native generation is the central risk, implement path selection and path selection* alongside standard Mask-Predict, token-aware beam search, and a C-MLM-style distillation baseline [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161), and explicitly evaluate zero-shot generation without fine-tuning; if direct BERT generation remains weak, consider hybrid decoding or distillation rather than relying only on inference-time search. Third, justify or extend the 150k update-step budget: include learning curves and longer runs (e.g., 300k/500k) for the 352M model, report Pile token count, batch size, hardware, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, and masking ratios, and compare compute-matched baselines, since [(Ketykó et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab226ac6347b07f45d02bd7dda2d4d7e23a4478e) suggests BERT suffix prediction may need more iterations and [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) uses 500k steps. Fourth, refine the masking strategy by ablating prefix/suffix ratios, deletion/span-deletion variants, end-of-target masking [(Dong et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c71771c701aadfd72c5866170a9f5d71464bb88), and span-boundary or n-gram sampling [(Joshi et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81f5810fbbab9b7203b9556f4ce3c741875407bc) / [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b), because deletion may be more effective for generation than pure masking. Fifth, strengthen evaluation and baselines: include M2/BART/GPT-BERT/C-MLM/u-PMLM comparisons where model sizes and data allow [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12), [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481), [(Dong et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c71771c701aadfd72c5866170a9f5d71464bb88), [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161), [(Liao et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f02bb4bc0b711fd90d32bd7dadd505465d6b45e1), report generation speed, and add human preference or quality checks if summarization results are close to prior work, as in [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481).

## Zero-shot evaluation: To assess the generative potential of BERT-family models without fine-tuning, the plan evaluates GeBERT on common sense reasoning and reading comprehension tasks including ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, Race, SciQ, LogiQA, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA using the Language Model Evaluation framework and compares against AR baselines including OPT, GPT-neo, Pythia, and RWKV models with comparable parameters.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed zero-shot evaluation comes from [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cf7de6c10f871ef9bc4cde15b4417299da18b00), which used a zero-shot, no-prompt lm-eval-harness protocol on PIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, and ARC-e with accuracy scoring for LLaMA and OPT on A800 80GB GPUs; although the excerpt lacks numeric results, it directly validates the harness, task overlap, and OPT baseline that the plan intends to use. The next strongest support is [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7310dac21abc6ba357bcd5e75fb2e6957a97303), which evaluates 125M and 350M autoregressive transformers zero-shot on ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, OBQA, WinoGrande, TQA, and RACE, closely matching the proposed ~150M and ~350M scale and benchmark suite; its training details, 480k iterations on 1T tokens with 32 A100 GPUs, Adam, weight decay 0.1, and initial learning rate 2e-3, make the sub-billion AR baseline comparison more concrete. [(Tuli et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46b8e95c97d04ac3ddb782cf9a6421a45470fd59) provides further support by evaluating Pythia and DynaMo multi-token prediction models zero-shot on ARC challenge, ARC easy, BoolQ, COPA, HellaSwag, OpenBookQA, PIQA, and WinoGrande without task-specific fine-tuning, which supports the inclusion of Pythia as an AR baseline and the use of no-fine-tuning common-sense evaluation. [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75) supports the broader AR evaluation protocol, especially zero-shot and few-shot scoring, multiple-choice likelihood normalization, exact match for open-ended tasks, and comparisons with OPT, GPT-Neo, and other AR models; its WinoGrande training trend also warns that benchmark performance may not track perplexity. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Zellers et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0f27bb594b1eaaf493eaf1e2ee723a2b0a19ad), which establishes HellaSwag as a four-way common-sense task with BERT-Large at 47.3%, human performance above 95%, and source-specific BERT versus GPT differences; [(Hendrycks et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/814a4f680b9ba6baba23b93499f4b48af1a27678) supports weighted multiple-choice accuracy and zero/few-shot AR evaluation, with GPT-3 X-Large at 43.9 and fine-tuned RoBERTa at 27.9; [(Mihaylov et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1536e8958697c5364f68b2e2448905dbbeb3a0ca) supports the difficulty of common-sense science QA, with human performance around 92% and knowledge-free baselines near 50%; and [(Sakaguchi et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92e121c6e114fe3cfb89370df03847c66a9b4e28) supports WinoGrande as a hard binary-choice task, with fine-tuned RoBERTa at 79.1% on a debiased set, human majority vote at 94.0%, and non-neural baselines at chance 50%. Finally, [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3a8f931ca353264a4737eb900a1e611f228ea2) provides weaker support by showing zero-shot common-sense evaluation of pruned LLaMA models and BERT-base GLUE/SQuAD experiments on a single A100 80G, indicating that BERT-scale evaluation is computationally feasible.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not provide direct evidence that BERT-family models can be fairly assessed as zero-shot generative models on these tasks; most BERT-family results are fine-tuned classifiers or category-level zero-shot after fine-tuning. [(Zellers et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0f27bb594b1eaaf493eaf1e2ee723a2b0a19ad) reports BERT-Large at only 47.3% on HellaSwag after fine-tuning, below 50% overall, with a roughly 5% drop on zero-shot category folds and human performance above 95%, suggesting that task-level zero-shot GeBERT may perform substantially worse than fine-tuned BERT baselines. [(Sakaguchi et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92e121c6e114fe3cfb89370df03847c66a9b4e28) shows fine-tuned RoBERTa at 79.1% on WinoGrande debiased, still far below 94.0% human majority vote, and emphasizes bias filtering and local-context baselines, implying that zero-shot BERT-family accuracy may be confounded by shortcut features. [(Hendrycks et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/814a4f680b9ba6baba23b93499f4b48af1a27678) further contradicts the assumption that BERT-family models are competitive in limited-tuning multiple-choice evaluation: fine-tuned RoBERTa-base achieved 27.9 weighted accuracy, ALBERT-xxlarge 27.1, and GPT-2 32.4, while GPT-3 X-Large reached 43.9, although GPT-3 Large was only 26.0, showing strong sensitivity to model scale and prompt format. A second contradiction is that the proposed path-selection mechanism is not evidenced by any of these related works; none of [(Zellers et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0f27bb594b1eaaf493eaf1e2ee723a2b0a19ad), [(Sakaguchi et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92e121c6e114fe3cfb89370df03847c66a9b4e28), [(Hendrycks et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/814a4f680b9ba6baba23b93499f4b48af1a27678), [(Tuli et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46b8e95c97d04ac3ddb782cf9a6421a45470fd59), or [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75) evaluates BERT-family candidate decoding paths, so zero-shot benchmark performance alone may not isolate the contribution of path selection or path selection*. A third contradiction is that AR baseline performance is not determined by parameter count alone, which threatens the proposed comparison of ~150M and ~350M OPT, GPT-Neo, Pythia, and RWKV models. [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75) reports LLaMA-65B behind Chinchilla-70B and PaLM-540B on MMLU 5-shot, partly attributed to smaller books and academic data, and notes WinoGrande does not correlate well with training perplexity; [(Yang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f78c3d44cd27a3ccbe23cc3efd6fd13a120476f7) shows 7B, 34B, and 67B AR checkpoints plateau on PIQA, HellaSwag, BoolQ, WinoGrande, and CommonsenseQA after about 300B tokens, while OpenLLaMA-7B can approach or exceed Yi-34B on some tasks, indicating that training tokens, data mix, and checkpoint selection matter more than nominal size. A fourth contradiction is that several target tasks contain biases or knowledge shortcuts that can make zero-shot accuracy misleading. [(Mihaylov et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1536e8958697c5364f68b2e2448905dbbeb3a0ca) reports knowledge-free baselines around 50% on OpenBookQA, oracle core fact improving readers by about 5%, and oracle fact plus author interpretation reaching about 76% versus 92% human performance, so common-sense QA may measure knowledge access rather than generation. [(Sakaguchi et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92e121c6e114fe3cfb89370df03847c66a9b4e28) and [(Zellers et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0f27bb594b1eaaf493eaf1e2ee723a2b0a19ad) similarly show that WinoGrande and HellaSwag require debiasing or source/category analysis because local context and category generalization can dominate. Weaker contradictions include the lack of statistical significance, confidence intervals, and detailed prompt or scoring specifications in many related works, as seen in [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cf7de6c10f871ef9bc4cde15b4417299da18b00), [(Tuli et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46b8e95c97d04ac3ddb782cf9a6421a45470fd59), and [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3a8f931ca353264a4737eb900a1e611f228ea2), which makes it difficult to know whether zero-shot differences between BERT-family and AR models are robust.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to lock the zero-shot protocol to a reproducible LM Eval or lm-eval-harness setup: use no-prompt zero-shot evaluation, accuracy for multiple-choice and boolean tasks, chance baselines, explicit prompt templates, and likelihood normalization for multiple-choice scoring where applicable, following [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cf7de6c10f871ef9bc4cde15b4417299da18b00), [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7310dac21abc6ba357bcd5e75fb2e6957a97303), and [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75); report per-task accuracy, aggregate accuracy, chance, and human or upper-bound references for ARC-easy, ARC-challenge, BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, WinoGrande, Race, SciQ, LogiQA, HellaSwag, and TruthfulQA. The next most important action is to make the AR baseline comparison fair by matching not only parameters but also training tokens, data mix, checkpoint, and evaluation harness: use ~150M and ~350M OPT, GPT-Neo, Pythia, and RWKV models, report training tokens and data composition, and include sub-billion AR references such as those in [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7310dac21abc6ba357bcd5e75fb2e6957a97303) and [(Tuli et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46b8e95c97d04ac3ddb782cf9a6421a45470fd59) rather than extrapolating from large-model results in [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75) or [(Yang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f78c3d44cd27a3ccbe23cc3efd6fd13a120476f7). Third, because the related work shows BERT-family underperformance and AR decoding advantage, the zero-shot evaluation should include path-selection ablations: compare GeBERT with path selection, standard Mask-Predict or single-path decoding, and token-aware beam search, and report both accuracy and decoding cost so that gains are not attributed to architecture alone. Fourth, mitigate task bias and knowledge shortcuts by evaluating WinoGrande with debiased or bias-controlled splits and local-context baselines as in [(Sakaguchi et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92e121c6e114fe3cfb89370df03847c66a9b4e28), reporting HellaSwag source-specific and category-zero-shot breakdowns as in [(Zellers et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b0f27bb594b1eaaf493eaf1e2ee723a2b0a19ad), and adding knowledge-free or oracle-knowledge baselines for knowledge-intensive tasks such as SciQ, LogiQA, and optionally OpenBookQA as motivated by [(Mihaylov et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1536e8958697c5364f68b2e2448905dbbeb3a0ca). Fifth, add statistical rigor: run multiple seeds or checkpoints, report confidence intervals or significance tests, and include zero-shot versus few-shot sensitivity analyses because prompt and shot sensitivity are evident in [(Hendrycks et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/814a4f680b9ba6baba23b93499f4b48af1a27678) and [(Touvron et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57e849d0de13ed5f91d086936296721d4ff75a75). Sixth, control contamination and leakage by verifying that the Pile pretraining mixture does not contain the evaluation tasks, using held-out categories or debiased test sets where available, and documenting any overlap with HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC, Race, SciQ, LogiQA, or TruthfulQA. Finally, report reproducibility details, including hardware, batch size, prompt length, decoding settings, path-selection overhead, and whether evaluation can be run on a single A100 or A800 GPU as suggested by [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6a3a8f931ca353264a4737eb900a1e611f228ea2) and [(Li et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cf7de6c10f871ef9bc4cde15b4417299da18b00).

## Task-specific generation evaluation: To measure the performance gap in generation after fine-tuning, the plan fine-tunes models on XSUM and MSQG, compares against AR baselines MASS, BART, and ProphetNet and NAR baselines BANG, ELMER, PreDAT, MIST, and DEER, and measures ROUGE F1 for XSUM and BLEU, ROUGE-L, and METEOR for MSQG while also evaluating generation speed compared to AR models.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed task-specific generation evaluation is that XSum is a validated, highly abstractive single-sentence summarization benchmark with an official 204,045/11,332/11,334 split, 36% novel unigrams, 83% novel bigrams, 96% novel trigrams, and weak LEAD and EXT-ORACLE extractive baselines, which makes ROUGE F1 a meaningful measure of abstractive generation gaps [(Narayan et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/305b2cf37e5dece81e95c92883d5a6e28ac93b22). A closely related strong support is that BART, one of the proposed AR baselines, has been directly evaluated on XSum with grid-search hyperparameter tuning and three-seed averaging, showing that BART is competitive on ROUGE-K and that ROUGE and keyword inclusion can diverge, which supports using BART as an XSum reference and reporting multiple ROUGE variants [(Takeshita et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c75dc12b3e82ddf585bbf234346e13eb12682d5). Another strong support is that BART has a published XSum protocol using beam size 5, duplicate trigram removal, length-penalty tuning, and ROUGE evaluation, and it outperforms prior RoBERTa-based summarization by about 3.5 ROUGE points, giving a concrete AR reference point for the planned comparison [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481). A further strong support is that AR sequence-to-sequence baselines including MASS, BART, and ProphetNet are comparable in recent generation benchmarks, and P3LM shows that order-aware AR pretraining and left-to-right inference improve ROUGE on summarization and question generation, with large P3LM improving over ProphetNet large by 0.9 GLGE points and +0.39/+0.18/+0.43 ROUGE-1/2/L on CNN/DM, which supports the planned AR baseline set and the importance of decoding order [(Bao et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ae0c69cac7bb2ba386faf6e9702db54b2ca594d). For MSQG metrics, METEOR is supported by machine-translation validation where full METEOR achieved Pearson correlation 0.964 with human system scores, higher than BLEU 0.817 and NIST 0.892, and recall-oriented matching improved correlation, although the evidence is not question-generation-specific [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5). BLEU is also supported as a generation-quality signal by its original validation on Chinese-to-English translation, where modified n-gram precision with brevity penalty tracked human rank order, yielded 0.99 and 0.96 linear-regression correlations with monolingual and bilingual judges, and showed significant system differences with t=6, but the test was only 500 sentences and reference count affected absolute scores [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9). Weaker but still relevant support comes from ROUGE validation on DUC summarization, where ROUGE was correlated with human content coverage using Pearson, Spearman, and Kendall correlations, although the excerpt does not provide numeric coefficients and the setting is not XSum [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008). Additional weaker support is that ROUGE and METEOR have been used to compare abstractive summarization systems against extractive baselines, with beam search improving over greedy decoding and pointer-generator/coverage improving ROUGE and METEOR, which supports the general evaluation design and provides a simple analogue of the plan's path-selection idea but not the specific BERT-family, NAR, or speed claims [(Rush et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ac30af5522c7a50ec4d1ee43fd2bd8652a9bd52) [(See et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/668db48c6a79826456341680ee1175dfc4cced71). The most task-specific but still indirect support is that BART and XSUM-pretrained models have been evaluated on SCITLDR with ROUGE-1/2/L and human nugget/correctness ratings, with correctness involving 29 authors covering 64 papers, and that BART can be tuned with dev-set ROUGE-1 and loss, which supports dev-based checkpoint selection and human-aware evaluation, although the task is not XSum fine-tuning or MSQG [(Cachola et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502a542b2e98d9094e1880a30f652d4170b9534). The weakest support is that BART-Large and other pretrained AR models outperform non-pretrained PTGen on interview summarization with ROUGE-L model selection, showing AR baselines are useful but in a different dataset and without MSQG, NAR, or speed evidence [(Zhu et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9623e9e461647a10a8f14419a9abe40482e9eb47).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that reference-based automatic metrics on XSum, including ROUGE, BLEU, and BERTScore, showed positive correlations with human annotations but worse overall evaluation performance than LLM-based evaluators on 100 XSum instances and 11 LLM outputs, which challenges the plan's reliance on ROUGE F1 for XSum and BLEU/ROUGE-L/METEOR for MSQG as primary quality signals [(Chu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a509397430eca1cd3970082ed57f9c490ef6e3ae). A strong related contradiction is that BERTScore outperforms n-gram exact-match metrics such as BLEU and SENTBLEU in segment-level human-judgment correlation and model selection, while n-gram metrics drop almost to chance on the 667-pair PAWS-QQP paraphrase robustness set, suggesting that BLEU and ROUGE-L may be unstable for abstractive or paraphrastic outputs [(Zhang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/295065d942abca0711300b2b4c39829551060578). A moderate contradiction is that METEOR parameters are language-specific and English-optimal parameters do not necessarily transfer, with tuned French, German, and Spanish parameters differing substantially and lacking unseen-test validation, so using METEOR for English MSQG without parameter validation or sensitivity analysis may be under-specified [(Lavie, and Agarwal, 2007-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34d7a07c493ca6336c92156806a2947e115caadc). A weaker contradiction is that XSum's high n-gram novelty and weak extractive baselines imply that ROUGE F1 may be low and sensitive to surface form, and that models optimized for ROUGE may not capture content if keyword inclusion and ROUGE diverge, as seen when BART outperformed TF-IDF methods on ROUGE-K while MTL/TF-IDF methods outperformed BART on traditional ROUGE, and zero-shot Llama 2 achieved ROUGE-1 21.8/22.0 while missing more than half keywords [(Narayan et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/305b2cf37e5dece81e95c92883d5a6e28ac93b22) [(Takeshita et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c75dc12b3e82ddf585bbf234346e13eb12682d5). Another weaker contradiction is that BART on XSum remains below human reference performance and that automatic ROUGE comparisons can miss factual or content issues, as human evaluation in BART and SCITLDR showed annotator preferences and correctness ratings that are not fully captured by ROUGE, so ROUGE F1 alone may overstate generation quality [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) [(Cachola et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502a542b2e98d9094e1880a30f652d4170b9534). The weakest contradiction is that BLEU's absolute values depend on reference count and test-set block variance, with human scores changing from 0.3468 with four references to 0.2571 with two references and block-wise variance estimated on only 20 blocks of 25 sentences, so BLEU-based path selection in the broader plan may be noisy if MSQG references are limited or test sets are small [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to supplement ROUGE F1, BLEU, ROUGE-L, and METEOR with a contextual or human-aligned metric such as BERTScore, and to audit the BLEU-based scoring used in path selection* by validating it on an MSQG dev set and, if possible, replacing or augmenting it with BERTScore, exact match, or answer-based scoring, because reference-based n-gram metrics are less robust to paraphrase and underperform LLM evaluators on XSum [(Zhang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/295065d942abca0711300b2b4c39829551060578) [(Chu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a509397430eca1cd3970082ed57f9c490ef6e3ae) [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9). The next most important action is to validate and report METEOR parameter settings for English MSQG, either by using English-optimal defaults with a sensitivity analysis over alpha, beta, and gamma or by tuning on a dev set with human judgments, because METEOR parameters are language- and task-sensitive and non-English tuning showed substantial parameter differences [(Lavie, and Agarwal, 2007-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/34d7a07c493ca6336c92156806a2947e115caadc) [(Banerjee, and Lavie, 2005-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7533d30329cfdbf04ee8ee82bfef792d08015ee5). A high-priority action is to standardize the XSum and MSQG evaluation protocol with the official XSum split, multiple random seeds, dev-set checkpoint selection, and explicit reporting of ROUGE-1, ROUGE-2, ROUGE-L F1, BLEU, METEOR, and generation speed, while reproducing AR baselines such as BART with beam size 5, length penalty, duplicate trigram removal, and target-length constraints where appropriate, and reproducing NAR baselines under identical decoding budgets because the related work does not directly validate BANG, ELMER, PreDAT, MIST, or DEER on these tasks [(Narayan et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/305b2cf37e5dece81e95c92883d5a6e28ac93b22) [(Lewis et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/395de0bd3837fdf4b4b5e5f04835bcc69c279481) [(Bao et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ae0c69cac7bb2ba386faf6e9702db54b2ca594d) [(Takeshita et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c75dc12b3e82ddf585bbf234346e13eb12682d5). Another important action is to report variance, reference-count sensitivity, and confidence intervals for BLEU and ROUGE, because BLEU absolute scores changed with reference count and block-wise variance was estimated on a small 500-sentence corpus, and ROUGE validation was originally on DUC rather than XSum [(Papineni et al., 2002-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d7da009f457917aa381619facfa5ffae9329a6e9) [(Lin, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60b05f32c32519a809f21642ef1eb3eaf3848008). A useful secondary action is to add content or keyword diagnostics for XSum, such as ROUGE-K or keyword inclusion, because BART and TF-IDF/MTL systems can diverge on surface ROUGE versus keyword coverage, which helps interpret whether BERT-family path selection improves content rather than only n-gram overlap [(Takeshita et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c75dc12b3e82ddf585bbf234346e13eb12682d5). A less urgent but still relevant action is to include human or LLM pairwise/pointwise evaluation for MSQG if budget allows, following SCITLDR and XSum LLM evaluation designs, to check whether automatic metrics align with question usefulness and correctness [(Cachola et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3502a542b2e98d9094e1880a30f652d4170b9534) [(Chu et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a509397430eca1cd3970082ed57f9c490ef6e3ae). The least urgent action is to document generation-speed comparisons with controlled hardware, batch size, beam/path width, and decoding steps, because the related work rarely reports speed and the plan's speed claim requires a reproducible computational protocol [(Rush et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ac30af5522c7a50ec4d1ee43fd2bd8652a9bd52) [(See et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/668db48c6a79826456341680ee1175dfc4cced71).

## Ablation studies: To isolate the contribution of the proposed inference and training mechanisms, the plan conducts ablation studies to analyze different decoding path selection methods, compare with token-aware beam search approaches, and examine the effects of various hyperparameter settings in path selection*.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Wan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4f116e63c30fb8ac11d90ed4d106cc6e9dc30cf), which directly ablates beam size and candidate selection in BART-large 406M summarization on XSum and CNN/DM, reporting oracle 'Max' versus decoder 'Top' beams and faithfulness-guided lookahead/ranking, with 200 human-evaluated examples per dataset. It shows that increasing beam size improves FactCC, DAE, QuestEval, and BERTScore precision, but the top beam improves only marginally relative to the oracle, and that guided selection improves faithfulness: on XSum, beam plus lookahead achieved 56.5% fully factual summaries versus 43.5% for beam, and reduced major factual errors from 44.5% to 31.5%, while ROUGE-L generally decreased. This supports the plan's ablation of decoding path selection and the need to compare path selection against token-aware beam search and candidate-selection heuristics on XSum, but it also implies that the ablation must report the oracle-to-top gap rather than only final ROUGE. Next, [(Lu et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c5bf29079cd958a2bef150077a02a1deb300652) provides a concrete token-aware constrained beam decoder over GPT-2, BART, and T5 for CommonGen, recipe, dialogue, and gender-constrained MT, pruning irreversible violations, filtering by top-alpha likelihood and top-beta clause satisfaction, and grouping candidates by satisfied clauses; it improves constraint coverage and quality over conventional beam search, with smaller models sometimes outperforming larger supervised models, supporting the planned comparison with token-aware beam search and the ablation of alpha/beta-like path-pruning hyperparameters. [(Post, and Vilar, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/823f335eee85b42502c8c6cb3ce38b4ae274ef89) supports beam-allocation and pruning ablations in WMT17 English-German with a four-layer attention RNN evaluated on newstest2014 with 2,737 sentences, showing dynamic beam allocation with k=10 achieved 26.7 BLEU versus 25.6 for grid beam search base beam 1, that threshold pruning at 20 was necessary to beat unpruned decoding, and that constrained decoding was about 3x slower on a Tesla V100, which motivates reporting candidate budget, pruning thresholds, and runtime for GeBERT path selection. [(Suzuki, and Nagata, 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/364f7f7bac907ce326dce84b26eb857f186d3dc2) supports token-level path pruning in K-best beam search for Gigaword summarization with about 3.8M training examples, where a word-frequency sub-model constrained decoding paths and outperformed a beam-size-5 OpenNMT baseline with ROUGE-1/2/L 33.65/16.12/31.37, although the frequency estimator itself was weak. [(Chen et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60acc34101dc0395ae6d2ab6abf26cbedb00e961) and [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161) support BERT-specific component ablations in generation: a full BERT teacher improved a base Transformer student by 1.66 BLEU on IWSLT14 German-English dev with 160k training samples and 0.82 BLEU on IWSLT15 English-Vietnamese tst2012 with 113k training samples, while the sm variant gave intermediate gains, showing that BERT supervision can help but must be ablated variant-by-variant. [(Sutskever et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cea967b59209c6be22829699f05b8b1ac4dc092d) supports candidate-selection and rescoring ablations in WMT14 English-French using a 12M-sentence training subset, where single forward/reversed LSTM beam-12 systems scored 26.17/30.59 cased BLEU versus an SMT baseline of 33.30, while ensembles and rescoring reached 34.81 and an oracle rescoring condition reached about 45 BLEU, indicating that path selection gains may require ensemble or rescoring-style candidate evaluation. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1) for decoding-hyperparameter ablations in 762M GPT-2 Large with 5,000 passages, [(Slobodkin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7160c71d032d09484919c69142421f660848309e) for greedy lookahead controlled decoding on Flan-T5/LED, [(Gan et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/778ce81457383bd5e3fdb11b145ded202ebb4970) for beam-size-5 captioning ablations on COCO/Flickr30k, [(Shang et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba49d3823d43515e447296ca4e1e55d3f1fd8c4d) for variant ablations with human judgment and Friedman p<0.05, [(Rajpurkar et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05dd7254b632376973f3a1b4d39485da17814df5) for feature-ablation design on SQuAD, [(Peters et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3febb2bed8865945e7fddc99efd791887bb7e14f) for component and sample-efficiency ablations, [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12) for masked sequence-to-sequence k studies, [(Zhang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4fb74b5f69e5c0f3822b82e1bd082d5d95fc8d0) for constrained beam decoding, and [(Zandie, and Mahoor, 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16282594e772f6fb97af9057020a2a7f62554953) for decoding-time gamma/threshold sensitivity.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is also from [(Wan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4f116e63c30fb8ac11d90ed4d106cc6e9dc30cf): although larger beams improve automatic faithfulness, the decoder's top beam is only marginally better than the oracle maximum, and faithfulness improvements generally reduce ROUGE-L. This contradicts a simple assumption that increasing k in GeBERT path selection will monotonically improve the plan's primary ROUGE F1/BLEU metrics; the ablation must separate path exploration from candidate selection and report overlap/faithfulness tradeoffs. [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1) contradicts the sufficiency of beam-like path selection for open-ended generation: in 762M GPT-2 Large, beam search produced repetitive loops, full sampling was least understandable, top-k drifted, and nucleus sampling received the highest HUSE score, implying that BERT path selection may need temperature, truncation, or diversity controls rather than only highest-probability path selection. [(Post, and Vilar, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/823f335eee85b42502c8c6cb3ce38b4ae274ef89) shows that decoding search hyperparameters can actively harm quality: low threshold pruning reduced BLEU, only threshold 20 beat the unpruned baseline, and constrained decoding runtime nearly doubled, so path selection* hyperparameters may have narrow safe ranges and high compute cost. [(Chen et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60acc34101dc0395ae6d2ab6abf26cbedb00e961) and [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161) contradict the assumption that all BERT-based generation components help: the l2r BERT teacher variant scored 35.20 versus 35.27 base on IWSLT14 German-English dev and 26.99 versus 27.03 on IWSLT15 English-Vietnamese tst2012, with no significance tests, so path selection* could degrade performance if the preference objective is misaligned. [(Sutskever et al., 2014-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cea967b59209c6be22829699f05b8b1ac4dc092d) contradicts the idea that a single beam path selector is enough: single forward and source-reversed LSTM beam-12 systems scored 26.17 and 30.59 BLEU, below the 33.30 SMT baseline, and only ensembles/rescoring approached state-of-the-art. [(Slobodkin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7160c71d032d09484919c69142421f660848309e) suggests that combining inference-time controlled decoding with RL training is not automatically additive: controlled decoding alone achieved precision 85.6, coverage 91.3, F-1 88.3, while RL plus controlled decoding achieved 83.4/92.3/87.7, and the manual evaluation sampled only 10 examples, limiting confidence. Weaker contradictions/limitations include [(Rajpurkar et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05dd7254b632376973f3a1b4d39485da17814df5), where lexicalized features overfit and L2 regularization hurt development performance, and constituency constraints created a 77.3% answer ceiling; [(Shang et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba49d3823d43515e447296ca4e1e55d3f1fd8c4d), where the human test set had only 110 posts and inter-rater agreement was fair; [(Gan et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/778ce81457383bd5e3fdb11b145ded202ebb4970), where quantitative ablation values were not provided; [(Song et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/145b8b5d99a2beba6029418ca043585b90138d12), where the MASS ablation/k study lacked quantitative detail; [(Zhang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4fb74b5f69e5c0f3822b82e1bd082d5d95fc8d0), where beam-size ablation outcomes were not quantified in the excerpt; and [(Zandie, and Mahoor, 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16282594e772f6fb97af9057020a2a7f62554953), where gamma/threshold effects were qualitative and no significance tests were reported.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the ablation should follow the oracle-to-top diagnostic from [(Wan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4f116e63c30fb8ac11d90ed4d106cc6e9dc30cf): for each GeBERT path-selection setting on XSum and MSQG, generate all k candidate decoding paths, report the oracle best path by ROUGE F1/BLEU/faithfulness, report the selected top path, and quantify the gap; this directly tests whether path selection is limited by search width or by the selection rule. The plan should compare token-aware beam search under an equal candidate budget and runtime, not only equal beam size, and include pruning/grouping/lookahead baselines inspired by [(Lu et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c5bf29079cd958a2bef150077a02a1deb300652) and [(Post, and Vilar, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/823f335eee85b42502c8c6cb3ce38b4ae274ef89), with explicit alpha/beta, threshold, beam allocation, and length-penalty sweeps. Second, path selection* should be ablated as a training mechanism with separate variants: no DPO, DPO only, DPO plus penalty terms, DPO plus MLM, and different lambda1/lambda2 values, scoring functions (exact match versus BLEU), positive-negative path sampling strategies, and frozen reference model choices; because related BERT distillation ablations such as [(Chen et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60acc34101dc0395ae6d2ab6abf26cbedb00e961) and [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75352cc69a29bd5fc411e0e79737cb96b6309161) report only dev BLEU without confidence intervals, the plan should use multiple seeds, report mean/std or confidence intervals, and use paired significance tests on XSum/MSQG. Third, evaluation should include faithfulness and human judgment, not only ROUGE/BLEU, because [(Wan et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4f116e63c30fb8ac11d90ed4d106cc6e9dc30cf) shows ROUGE-L can decrease when faithfulness improves; for XSum use FactCC, DAE, QuestEval, BERTScore precision, and a 3-star factual human rating, and for MSQG add human relevance/fluency checks. Fourth, the plan should report computational constraints and fair baselines: hardware, batch size, 150k update steps, candidate count, memory, and runtime per sentence for GeBERT 124M/352M versus MASS, BART, ProphetNet, BANG, ELMER, PreDAT, MIST, and DEER, following runtime reporting in [(Post, and Vilar, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/823f335eee85b42502c8c6cb3ce38b4ae274ef89) and decoding-budget controls in [(Holtzman et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cf4aa38ae31b43fd07abe13b4ffdb265babb7be1). Fifth, add BERT-specific component ablations for GeBERT's masking strategy, RoPE, SwiGLU, and path-selection inference, using the component-ablation templates in [(Peters et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3febb2bed8865945e7fddc99efd791887bb7e14f) and [(Rajpurkar et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/05dd7254b632376973f3a1b4d39485da17814df5), and validate the path-selection mechanism on smaller constrained or controlled generation tasks before full Pile pretraining, using diagnostics from [(Slobodkin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7160c71d032d09484919c69142421f660848309e), [(Zhang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4fb74b5f69e5c0f3822b82e1bd082d5d95fc8d0), and [(Zandie, and Mahoor, 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16282594e772f6fb97af9057020a2a7f62554953). For the zero-shot suite, run a smaller path-selection ablation on a subset of ARC-easy, BoolQ, and HellaSwag to check whether inference-time path selection changes exact match without fine-tuning, using the same candidate budget as task-specific runs.
