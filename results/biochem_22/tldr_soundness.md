## Strengths 
The strongest support is for explicit active/inactive curation and bias-aware benchmarking, as shown by [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) and [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8), and for fingerprint-based conventional ML baselines, supported by [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) and [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c). Multi-representation learning is supported by [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), and ratio-controlled paired evaluation is supported by [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) and [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern defining inactives as compounds with no known activity, because [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) and [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822) show absence of records is not a validated negative, and [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2) requires explicit assay labels. DUD-E decoys may introduce chemical-library and active-decoy similarity bias, as [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) and [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) indicate. Random 10-fold CV and accuracy, precision, and recall may miss ranking quality and scaffold leakage, as [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c) and [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) suggest. Five non-overlapping inactive sets can confound model comparison, as [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364) and [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) imply, and balanced training may not reflect high inactive-to-active screening conditions, as [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) and [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) show.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Replace no known activity with explicit inactive criteria: use assay-validated negatives, measured negative results at defined concentrations, or no activity above a predefined threshold; remove duplicates, salts, non-canonical SMILES, and contradictory records; apply physicochemical and similarity filters; treat DUD-E decoys as a separate decoy class and report true inactives, less-active IC50 > 10 uM compounds, and DUD-E decoys separately, following [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2), [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822), [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8), and [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3).

Add ranking and statistical rigor: report AUC-ROC, PR-AUC, enrichment, BEDROC, or top-k hit rates alongside accuracy, precision, and recall; use scaffold-aware or similarity-aware splits and nested cross-validation; use a common inactive set or block by the five inactive sets; use paired Wilcoxon tests, bootstrap confidence intervals, multiple-testing correction, and multiple stratified hold-out active sets, following [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a), [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129), and [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364).

Make the data-centric versus model-centric comparison fair: train a deep learning baseline on the exact BRAF dataset, same balanced training sets, same hold-out, same splits, and same fingerprints, or explicitly frame published deep learning results as external context only; compare conventional ML and deep learning on identical representations, and document random seeds, software versions, hyperparameter grids, hardware, runtime, and metrics, following [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a), [(Kim et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca7e8bc95864805c15e667ad016dfff98865fd51), [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), and [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c).
