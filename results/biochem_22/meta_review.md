## Context: To establish a high-quality benchmark for data-centric virtual screening, the plan will curate a BRAF active/inactive dataset with explicit activity definitions. Method: We will carefully curate a new dataset of BRAF actives and inactives for developing ligand-based virtual screening (LBVS) AI models. We will define actives as validated BRAF ligands with IC50 < 10 μM, while inactives will be carefully selected compounds with no known pharmacological activity against BRAF. We will manually curate approximately 4,100 BRAF actives and 24,000 inactive compounds.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), which evaluated DUD-E with 102 targets, 4,000 actives, 40,000 decoys, and 400 decoys per target, and showed that docking enrichment and BEDROC were strongly affected by chemical library bias and active-decoy similarity; GOLD achieved approximately 0.62 BEDROC and 3.0 1% enrichment, while Glide, Surflex, and FlexX were lower at approximately 0.58/2.6, 0.55/2.4, and 0.52/2.2, respectively. This directly supports the proposed BRAF plan because it shows that explicit active definitions, careful inactive selection, and bias-aware evaluation are necessary for a high-quality LBVS benchmark. The next strongest support is [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8), which builds on DUD-E-style decoy benchmarking and reports that stricter physicochemical and similarity constraints make decoys more challenging, while refining decoys toward true inactives improves LBVS validity; its database-level context includes DUD-E with 102 targets, 100 actives, and 1,000 decoys per target, and MUV/DEKOIS-style sets with 20 targets, 100-200 actives, and 1,000-2,000 decoys per target. This supports the proposed 4,100 actives, 24,000 inactives, balanced training sets, and DUD-E spike-in experiments as a controlled test of data composition. A further support is [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2), which used ChEMBL v23 with approximately 1.7M compounds, 13.5M bioactivity measurements, and about 11K targets, then retained 56 assays after MolVS normalization, removal of SMILES longer than 150 characters, explicit comment-based active/inactive labels, duplicate and contradiction filtering, and three-fold cross-validation; this supports the plan's emphasis on explicit activity definitions and data-quality filtering, although its assays were smaller and often unbalanced. Then [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822) supports the curation workflow by recommending duplicate removal, SMILES canonicalization, salt and metal removal, optional PAINS/ADMET filtering, and balanced active/inactive sets, and by reporting that ChEMBL V.29 was approximately 71% active and 31% inactive, which motivates deliberate construction of a large inactive pool. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Chen et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e66e075c1787824175966ce86488ca6c1e2cff2e), which found ligand-only CNN models equivalent to receptor-ligand models and evaluated ligand-fingerprint KNN baselines on PDBbind-derived data, supporting the feasibility of LBVS and KNN baselines, but it provides limited quantitative detail and is not BRAF-specific.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the plan's use of DUD-E decoys as inactives or spike-in inactives can conflict with evidence that DUD-E decoys are not experimentally validated inactives and can introduce chemical library and active-decoy similarity bias. [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) showed that targets with library bias and actives similar to the crystal ligand produced higher enrichment, and that removing such bias reduced apparent program differences, leaving only modest enrichment; [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) further showed that DUD-E-like decoys are more challenging and that benchmark validity improves when decoys are refined toward true inactives. Therefore, if DUD-E compounds are mixed into the 24,000 inactives without a separate decoy label, the benchmark may measure decoy chemistry rather than true BRAF inactivity. The next contradiction is that no known pharmacological activity is not equivalent to a validated inactive. [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822) notes that inactive status depends on assay conditions and predefined thresholds, and that public bioactivity data are skewed toward actives, with ChEMBL V.29 approximately 71% active and 31% inactive, making 24,000 true inactives difficult to obtain; [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2) used explicit comment strings such as inactive, not active, or inconclusive, rather than absence of records, and retained only 56 assays after removing contradictory measurements, indicating that absence of known activity can be noisy and assay-specific. A further contradiction is that the plan's primary metrics of accuracy, precision, and recall may understate virtual-screening performance because ranking is central; [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) evaluated enrichment and BEDROC and found target-specific ranking differences that classification metrics may miss. The weakest contradiction is that the high-quality benchmark claim is weakened by under-specified curation and split details: manual curation of 4,100 actives and 24,000 inactives is feasible only if normalization, duplicate/contradiction removal, filter choices, and the relationship between the 24,000 inactives and the five balanced training sets are documented, as emphasized by [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2) and [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822).

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace no known activity with explicit, assay-validated inactive criteria: include compounds with measured negative results at defined concentrations, or no activity above a predefined threshold, and exclude contradictory records, duplicates, salts, and non-canonical SMILES using MolVS or CDK, following [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2) and [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822). Second, treat DUD-E decoys as a separate decoy or spike-in class rather than true inactives; report performance on true inactives, less-active IC50 > 10 μM compounds, and DUD-E decoys separately, and choose decoys with physicochemical similarity but low topological similarity to avoid active-like scaffolds, following [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) and [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8). Third, add ranking metrics such as enrichment, BEDROC, AUPRC or AUC, and top-k hit rates, and use scaffold or temporal splits in addition to 10-fold cross-validation to avoid leakage, because [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) shows that ranking performance is target- and bias-dependent. Fourth, clarify the five balanced training sets: if 1:1 balance is intended, allocate 3,600 inactives per set for 18,000 total and report the remaining 6,000 as a composition-perturbation pool; if all 24,000 inactives are used, state the active:inactive ratio and test it as a composition variable, consistent with the balanced-set emphasis in [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) and [(Chávez-Hernández et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9d24516c346d5287f4826245bd0c688f4533822). Fifth, document curation provenance, source versions, IC50 units, assay types, PAINS/ADMET filters, and statistical significance, and compare against KNN/fingerprint baselines as in [(Chen et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e66e075c1787824175966ce86488ca6c1e2cff2e) and [(Galushka et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/175350d719829390942b516184ae5c229efabae2) to make the benchmark reproducible.

## Context: To control for training bias and enable reproducible evaluation, the plan will partition the curated compounds into balanced training and hold-out sets. Method: From this initial set, we will randomly select 3,600 BRAF actives for training datasets with the remaining 500 actives for hold-out testing. To avoid training bias, we will create 5 balanced training datasets, each containing 3,600 BRAF actives with equal numbers of unique inactives not shared across datasets.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a), which evaluated a ligand-only DUD-E baseline using 4096-bit ECFP fingerprints and logistic regression, partitioning 102 targets into 72 training and 30 held-out targets and reporting a mean AUC of 0.904 versus 0.700 for Smina, 0.855 for AtomNet, and 0.800 for DeepVS. Because the authors attributed the strong compound-only performance to decoy composition rather than target-specific learning, this directly supports the proposed plan to fix a 500-active hold-out set, curate BRAF inactives, and use five non-overlapping inactive training sets to reduce training bias and make data-composition effects visible. This newer ligand-based result extends the earlier DUD-E bias analysis in [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), which built 102 targets with 415,951 compounds, 102,494 actives, and 313,457 decoys and showed that apparent docking-program differences narrowed after accounting for target, ligand, and chemical-library biases. Next, [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) supports the same design by showing that random decoys can be too easy and inflate ligand-based virtual screening performance, while physicochemical filters, refined putative inactives, and true negatives produce more demanding benchmarks; its reviewed sets such as MUV with 10 targets and 250 actives and 250 decoys per target, DEKOIS/DUD-E with 100 actives and 100 decoys per target, NRLiSt BDB, and MUBD-HDACs align with the proposed BRAF-specific 4,100-active and 24,000-inactive construction. [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) supports repeated, ratio-controlled dataset construction by generating 27 E. coli protein-protein interaction datasets with 250 positives and 1,250 negatives, a fixed 5:1 negative-to-positive ratio, positive-to-total ratios from 10% to 90%, 10-fold cross-validation where applicable, LIBSVM C-SVC RBF kernel with c=1.0/128 and C=1000 for two-class SVMs, and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, which parallels the proposed use of five balanced training partitions to reduce training bias. [(Srinivas et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62313c5ed9300eee3d4465c0de915f19ef74b21b) further supports controlling validation-set variance and bias through ChEMBL23-based selection, multiple iterations, molecular fingerprinting, and K-nearest neighbors or random forest tuning by grid search, although the excerpt lacks numeric outcomes. Weakest but still relevant support is [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364), whose paired 10-fold cross-validation, identical training/testing partitions across BR, LP, and BR+, KNN k=3, default base learners, Wilcoxon signed-rank comparisons, and example-based metrics provide a template for reproducible paired evaluation, even though it is a medical multi-label text task rather than BRAF virtual screening and the excerpt lacks numeric table values.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the proposed inactives are defined as compounds with no known pharmacological activity against BRAF, whereas [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) recommends true negative compounds or highly refined putative inactives and warns that random or too-dissimilar decoys can make ligand-based models appear stronger than they are. This is empirically reinforced by [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a), where a simple ECFP plus logistic regression model achieved mean AUC 0.904 on DUD-E held-out targets, suggesting that inactive/decoy composition can dominate apparent performance; if the BRAF inactives are merely unannotated rather than validated negatives, the proposed 3,600-active training sets may learn easy chemical distinctions rather than true BRAF activity. Next, using five training datasets with unique inactives not shared across datasets can confound model comparison, because [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364) deliberately used the same training and testing partitions for compared algorithms, and [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) showed that chemical-library bias can drive apparent performance differences; if each of the 1,375 proposed models is evaluated on a different inactive chemical space, differences among fingerprints and ML algorithms may reflect inactive-set composition rather than model quality. A further contradiction is that balanced training may not represent real virtual screening conditions: [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) used a fixed 5:1 negative-to-positive ratio, while DUD-E in [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) contained 102,494 actives and 313,457 decoys and [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) used more than 1 million decoys against 22,886 actives, so a 1:1 balanced BRAF training set may overestimate performance relative to high inactive-to-active screening. The single 500-active hold-out also underrepresents validation variance relative to [(Srinivas et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62313c5ed9300eee3d4465c0de915f19ef74b21b), which emphasizes validation-set selection variance and multiple iterations, and relative to [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129), which used three random datasets per ratio; with only 500 of approximately 4,100 actives held out, random active selection without stratification by IC50, chemical similarity, assay quality, or target subtype can produce unstable estimates. Weakest contradiction is that the plan states 10-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter optimization and model evaluation but does not specify nested cross-validation, paired folds, or statistical tests, whereas [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364) and [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) used paired partitions and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, and [(Srinivas et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62313c5ed9300eee3d4465c0de915f19ef74b21b) used repeated evaluation to address variance.

### Suggested Action

Most important, validate the 24,000 inactives as true or highly refined putative inactives rather than merely unannotated compounds, following [(Réau et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b60797688a674adb89d868c8a3eea954454507d8) by applying physicochemical filters, removing close analogs of actives, checking similarity to known actives, and including counter-screened or assay-validated negatives where possible; also run the proposed spike-in DUD-E decoy experiments to quantify decoy bias as in [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) and [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3). Second, define the exact inactive count per training set and make model comparisons fair: if 24,000 inactives are divided into five non-overlapping sets, each set has 4,800 inactives, which is not 1:1 with 3,600 actives; if balanced 1:1 training is intended, use 3,600 inactives per set and reserve the remaining 6,000 for validation, spike-ins, or ratio studies. For ranking the 1,375 models, either use a common inactive set across all models or treat the five inactive sets as a blocking factor, report per-set results, and use paired comparisons within the same inactive set as in [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364). Third, add multiple hold-out active sets or repeated stratified splits, for example five or ten independent 500-active hold-outs, and use nested 10-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning, with Wilcoxon signed-rank or equivalent paired tests, following [(Srinivas et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62313c5ed9300eee3d4465c0de915f19ef74b21b), [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129), and [(Cherman et al., 2011-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/073d95a4ade6dd773160ac88d06f1aafcdcdc364). Fourth, test multiple active-to-inactive ratios rather than only balanced training, such as 1:1, 1:5, 1:10, and DUD-E-like ratios, because [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129) used 5:1 and DUD-E benchmarks used much larger decoy pools. Fifth, stratify the 3,600 training actives and 500 hold-out actives by IC50, chemical scaffold, similarity, assay source, and quality, and prevent close analog leakage between training and hold-out sets. Finally, report reproducibility details including random seeds, RDKit/CDK/scikit-learn versions, hardware, runtime, hyperparameter grids, and metrics beyond accuracy, precision, and recall, adding AUC-ROC, AUPRC, Matthews correlation coefficient, enrichment, and BEDROC as used or recommended in [(Kiliç, and Tan, 2012-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c1706d05cdc36519a2a7a4e3a1f25a39dca5129), [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), and [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a).

## Context: To test how data representation and algorithm choice affect virtual screening performance, the plan will train and evaluate a large grid of conventional ML models. Method: We will develop and assess 1,375 predictive models for LBVS of BRAF ligands using 5 ML algorithms with 55 different molecular representations (10 standalone + 45 paired combinations). We will systematically test different ML algorithms (k-nearest neighbors, Naïve Bayes, gradient-boosted decision tree, random forest, and support vector machine) with various molecular representations to evaluate how molecular representations affect ML algorithm performance. We will use 10-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter optimization and model evaluation, employing accuracy, precision, and recall as performance metrics.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), which directly evaluates binary active/inactive virtual screening on ChEMBL using ECFP12 fingerprints and conventional classifiers including SVM and logistic regression, closely matching the proposed BRAF LBVS focus on fingerprints and conventional ML. It uses 2,103,018 measurements across 5,069 targets and 743,336 compounds, 13,558,545 sparse ECFP12 features, and 1,230 targets after requiring at least 15 samples per class, validating thresholded binary labels and large fingerprint feature spaces. The next strongest support is [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), which shows that fingerprint, descriptor, and toxicophore representations can support strong cheminformatics prediction on about 12,000 Tox21 compounds, and it provides implementation details relevant to the proposed 1,375-model grid, including feature scaling, sparse storage, cross-validation hyperparameter selection, and multi-task/multi-view comparisons. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Cortes, and Vapnik, 1995-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52b7bf3ba59b31f362aa07f957f1543a29a4279e), which establishes SVM as a viable classifier and kernel feature-space method on USPS and NIST digit data, supporting inclusion of SVM among the five proposed algorithms, although it uses image pixels rather than molecular fingerprints.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), because it uses scaffold-aware 3-fold cross-validation over 400,000 single-linkage clusters and mean AUC, whereas the proposed plan uses 10-fold cross-validation and accuracy, precision, and recall; random 10-fold splits over 4,100 actives and 24,000 inactives may leak structurally similar compounds, and accuracy may not capture ranking quality in virtual screening. It also compares a deep neural network against SVM and logistic regression, but the excerpt lacks AUC values and p-values, so it does not prove that the proposed conventional ML grid will match or exceed deep learning. The next strongest contradiction is [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), which reports a deep multi-task Tox21 model with ECFP4, descriptors, and toxicophores achieving average AUC 0.846, task AUCs from 0.793 to 0.941, and significant multi-task improvements for several tasks, suggesting that model architecture, AUC, and multi-task learning may be important; it does not test the proposed 55 fingerprint combinations or the five conventional algorithms, so it weakens the assumption that a conventional ML fingerprint grid alone is sufficient. The weakest contradiction is [(Cortes, and Vapnik, 1995-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52b7bf3ba59b31f362aa07f957f1543a29a4279e), which uses fixed USPS and NIST digit splits, raw error, support-vector counts, and no cross-validation or statistical significance tests, so it provides limited direct evidence against the proposed molecular benchmark but highlights the need for rigorous CV and statistical comparison.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace or supplement the planned 10-fold random cross-validation with scaffold-aware or similarity-aware splits, applied within each of the 5 balanced training datasets and the 500 held-out actives, using ECFP, Tanimoto similarity, or single-linkage clustering, and to report AUC or PR-AUC alongside accuracy, precision, and recall because virtual screening is a ranking problem and related work uses AUC. The second most important action is to add statistical rigor for the 1,375 models, including paired Wilcoxon tests or bootstrap confidence intervals across folds, effect sizes, and multiple-testing correction, as suggested by the paired Wilcoxon comparisons in [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c). The third action is to include a deep learning baseline or explicitly compare the best conventional models against published deep learning LBVS/QSAR results on the same BRAF data, because [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9) shows strong deep multi-task performance and the research plan aims to test data-centric versus model-centric AI. The fourth action is to adopt implementation details from the Tox21 study, such as feature scaling, sparse fingerprint storage, thresholding, and nested cross-validation for hyperparameter tuning, and to report hardware, runtime, memory, and hyperparameters for the 55 representations. The least important but useful action is to use SVM-specific diagnostics from [(Cortes, and Vapnik, 1995-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52b7bf3ba59b31f362aa07f957f1543a29a4279e), such as support-vector counts, training error, and kernel-degree or kernel-type sensitivity, while keeping the primary focus on the proposed fingerprint and algorithm grid.

## Context: To evaluate the data representation pillar, the plan will compare standalone and merged molecular fingerprints and analyze multi-view representation learning. Method: We will systematically test molecular fingerprints including Estate, PubChem, Klekota-Roth, ECFP6, FCFP6, Extended, Topological Torsion, Atom Pairs, Daylight-like, and CATS2D fingerprints, along with their paired combinations. We will evaluate whether merged fingerprint combinations outperform standalone fingerprints. We will investigate multi-representation approaches as a form of multi-view learning, which constitutes an emerging direction in AI/ML with implications for improved generalization performance.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed data-representation pillar comes from [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), which provides earlier quantitative evidence that combining molecular feature views can improve predictive performance on Tox21. That work used a deep multi-task network with binary ECFP4 fingerprints, chemical descriptors, and toxicophore features, and reported that combined multi-task learning outperformed single-task learning in most tasks, with significant AUC gains such as NR.AR.LBD from 0.8799 to 0.9289 p=0.011, NR.Aromatase from 0.7523 to 0.7921 p=0.006, NR.ER from 0.6659 to 0.6949 p=0.006, NR.ER.LBD from 0.6532 to 0.7272 p=0.006, NR.PPAR.gamma from 0.6367 to 0.7102 p=0.006, SR.HSE from 0.7354 to 0.8101 p=0.006, and SR.p53 from 0.6955 to 0.7487 p=0.006. It also achieved the best average AUC of 0.846, best Nuclear Receptor panel average of 0.826, and best Stress Response panel average of 0.858 in the final challenge, supporting the plan hypothesis that merged or multi-representation molecular features can improve virtual screening performance. A weaker but still relevant support comes from the undated ChEMBL work, [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), which extends the representation-comparison line of work to a much larger multi-target benchmark with 2,103,018 measurements, 5,069 targets, and 743,336 compounds, and uses ECFP12 fingerprints with 13,558,545 sparse features, scaffold-aware three-fold cross-validation with 400,000 single-linkage clusters, AUC, and paired Wilcoxon tests. Although the excerpt does not provide numeric AUC values, it supports the plan emphasis on rigorous representation comparison and leakage-aware splits, especially for the BRAF benchmark with 4,100 actives, 24,000 inactives, 3,600 training actives, 500 hold-out actives, 5 balanced training datasets, 55 representations, and 1,375 models.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work directly validates the proposed specific design of 10 standalone fingerprints, 45 paired fingerprint combinations, and conventional ML algorithms on a single-target BRAF dataset. The 2015 Tox21 work, [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), used a deep multi-task network rather than k-nearest neighbors, Naive Bayes, gradient-boosted decision trees, random forests, or support vector machines, and its combined-feature gains may depend on nonlinear deep learning rather than simple fingerprint concatenation. It also used ECFP4, chemical descriptors, and toxicophore features rather than the proposed Estate, PubChem, Klekota-Roth, ECFP6, FCFP6, Extended, Topological Torsion, Atom Pairs, Daylight-like, and CATS2D fingerprints. A second important limitation is that the undated ChEMBL work, [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), tests a single ECFP12 fingerprint in a multi-target ChEMBL setting, has no venue or date in the supplied metadata, and does not provide numeric AUC, p-values, architecture, kernel, regularization, hardware, runtime, or hyperparameter details, so it cannot support the claim that merged fingerprint pairs outperform standalone fingerprints. A weaker but still relevant mismatch is metric and split design: both related works use AUC as the primary metric, whereas the plan uses accuracy, precision, and recall; both also use scaffold- or cluster-aware splits, whereas the plan specifies 10-fold cross-validation and 5 balanced training datasets without explicitly requiring scaffold-aware partitioning. Finally, the datasets differ in scale and task structure: Tox21 had approximately 12,000 compounds, 11,764 training compounds, 297 leaderboard compounds, and 643 private test compounds across 12 tasks, while the ChEMBL work had 2,103,018 measurements across 5,069 targets, so their evidence is not directly transferable to the plan single-target BRAF benchmark with IC50 < 10 μM actives and carefully selected inactives.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add AUC, preferably ROC-AUC and PR-AUC, alongside accuracy, precision, and recall, because both related works use AUC as the primary virtual screening metric and AUC is more informative for ranking actives against inactives than threshold-dependent accuracy. The plan should also make the 10-fold cross-validation and 5 balanced training datasets scaffold-aware or cluster-aware, following [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), which used single-linkage clustering into 400,000 clusters and three folds, and [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), which distributed structurally similar compound clusters across folds to avoid overly easy splits. Next, the plan should report paired statistical tests, such as Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, comparing each merged fingerprint pair against its standalone components and each algorithm, and should use multiple random seeds or initializations, as the Tox21 work used five random initializations and reported p-values. The method should also specify preprocessing and regularization for conventional ML: sparse fingerprint matrices, feature scaling or thresholding, class weighting, and hyperparameter search with nested cross-validation, because the Tox21 work used standard deviation scaling or tanh for descriptors, tanh or sqrt scaling for ECFP4, thresholding of sparse features, dropout, small L2 weight decay, early stopping, and per-task AUC-based hyperparameter optimization. A further improvement is to justify the fingerprint list by including or comparing ECFP4 and ECFP12, since the related works used ECFP4 and ECFP12 respectively, and to define multi-view learning explicitly as feature concatenation, late fusion, stacked generalization, or separate fingerprint-specific models with a meta-learner rather than assuming merged fingerprints alone constitute multi-view learning. Finally, the plan should report computational details for 1,375 models across 55 representations, including RDKit/CDK fingerprint generation time, scikit-learn training time, memory usage, and whether sparse or dense matrices are used, because the Tox21 work specified a single GPU with 12 GB RAM, mini-batches of 512, and dense conversion of sparse batches, while the ChEMBL work did not provide hardware or runtime details, making reproducibility harder.

## Context: To evaluate data quality, quantity, and composition, the plan will perturb dataset size and class composition using top-performing models. Method: Using four top-performing predictive models, we will investigate how dataset composition and size impact performance by testing scenarios where: (1) the number of inactives increases equally with actives, and (2) the ratio of inactives to actives increases while fixing one component. We will examine the impact of using "less active" compounds (IC50 > 10 μM) versus true inactives, and evaluate the effect of using DUD-E decoys as inactives through spike-in experiments.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed data-quality, quantity, and composition studies comes from [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), which explicitly used DUD-E decoys as inactive references in a large virtual-screening benchmark containing 102 targets, 102,000 decoys, and 102,000 actives, and evaluated four docking programs, Gold, Glide, Surflex, and FlexX, using docking enrichment, net balance, and BEDROC. This directly supports the proposed plan to evaluate DUD-E decoys as inactives through spike-in experiments, because it shows that DUD-E decoys can function as a structured inactive reference set for screening performance, even though the proposed plan applies this idea to ML classifiers rather than docking. A second, slightly weaker but still relevant form of support is the study’s analysis of chemical library bias, target families, binding-site properties, small-molecule descriptors, and similarity to the crystal-structure ligand; this supports the proposed BRAF benchmark by showing that dataset composition, ligand properties, and inactive-set quality can materially affect screening outcomes, which is consistent with the plan’s goal of perturbing dataset size and class composition using four top-performing predictive models. The weakest support is indirect: the study did not use the proposed ML algorithms, did not perturb class ratios, did not use IC50 > 10 μM less-active compounds, did not construct the proposed 4,100 BRAF actives and 24,000 inactives, and the supplied excerpt did not report numerical BEDROC, enrichment-factor, or net-balance values, so it cannot directly validate the proposed 1,375-model comparison, 55 molecular representations, or 10-fold cross-validation setup.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3) found docking performance remained target- and ligand-dependent even after removing identified biases, which cautions against assuming that simply increasing the number of inactives or changing the inactive-to-active ratio will cleanly isolate data-composition effects in the proposed BRAF benchmark. If the added inactives, DUD-E decoys, or less-active IC50 > 10 μM compounds are correlated with target-specific or ligand-specific properties, the proposed scenarios where inactives increase equally with actives or where the inactive-to-active ratio increases while fixing one component may confound data-composition effects with chemical-space, activity-threshold, or target-specific effects. A second contradiction is that the study treated DUD-E decoys as inactive references but also highlighted chemical library bias as a factor affecting Gold, Surflex, and FlexX; this suggests that DUD-E decoys are not neutral true inactives and may introduce bias if used as spike-in inactives for BRAF models, especially when compared with the proposed curated BRAF inactives selected to have no known pharmacological activity against BRAF. The weakest contradiction is methodological: the study evaluated docking programs rather than the proposed ML algorithms, k-nearest neighbors, Naïve Bayes, gradient-boosted decision tree, random forest, and support vector machine, used docking enrichment, net balance, and BEDROC rather than the proposed accuracy, precision, and recall metrics, and did not test the proposed 10-fold cross-validation, 55 molecular representations, or 1,375-model comparison, so its evidence cannot be taken as direct support for the proposed ML evaluation setup.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the DUD-E decoy spike-in experiment bias-controlled, following [(Chaput et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe7697844facb2a5106131b4d876513a2d8afba3), by reporting and matching decoy properties against BRAF actives and curated inactives, including molecular weight, logP, aromaticity, 2D similarity to actives, fingerprint composition, and similarity to any reference ligand if available; the plan should include multiple decoy ratios relative to the 3,600 training actives and a non-decoy inactive control to separate decoy-specific bias from general class-ratio effects. The next most important action is to align evaluation metrics with the imbalance and screening context: in addition to accuracy, precision, and recall, report AUC-ROC, AUC-PR, enrichment factor, BEDROC, or top-k hit rates, because the related work used enrichment-oriented metrics and the proposed class-ratio perturbations will make accuracy less informative. A further improvement is to treat IC50 > 10 μM less-active compounds as a separate graded or threshold-sensitive class rather than simply merging them with true inactives, and to test multiple IC50 cutoffs around 10 μM to determine whether the proposed active/inactive boundary is stable. Finally, to improve reproducibility and attribution, report random seeds, 10-fold CV splits, hyperparameter search spaces, computational runtime, and statistical significance across repeated runs, since the related work’s lack of numerical docking scores in the supplied excerpt highlights the need for transparent quantitative benchmarking.

## Context: To position the data-centric ML results against prior model-centric work, the plan will benchmark against published deep learning performance. Method: We will compare our conventional ML approaches against the performance levels achieved by sophisticated deep learning methods in previous studies to demonstrate whether data-centric approaches can achieve superior results without complex algorithms.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed comparative analysis is direct quantitative evidence that a simple fingerprint-based conventional ML model can outperform deep learning virtual-screening models: [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) trained logistic regression on 4096-bit ECFP fingerprints for DUD-E, using 72 training targets and 30 test targets from 102 targets, 22,886 actives and over 1 million decoys, and reported mean AUC 0.904, higher than AtomNet 0.855, DeepVS 0.800, and Smina 0.700, although no statistical significance or hardware details were provided. This supports the plan's hypothesis that data-centric conventional ML on curated BRAF actives/inactives and multiple fingerprints may achieve competitive or superior LBVS performance without complex algorithms, especially because the plan tests 55 representations with 5 conventional algorithms. A weaker but relevant support is the review by [(Kim et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca7e8bc95864805c15e667ad016dfff98865fd51), which reports mixed evidence: deep learning QSAR outperformed random forest on the Merck Molecular Activity Challenge, but random forest outperformed a one-shot deep virtual-screening model; this supports the need for a systematic data-centric benchmark rather than assuming DL superiority. The next weakest support is [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9), which provides concrete published deep-learning performance levels in a related cheminformatics task (Tox21: average AUC 0.846 on 643 private compounds, task AUCs 0.793 to 0.941, 12 tasks, 11,764 training compounds, 297 leaderboard compounds, single 12 GB GPU, batch size 512, up to 40,000 ECFP4/descriptor/toxicophore features), showing that published DL results can serve as external reference points, although the task is toxicity rather than BRAF LBVS. The weakest support is [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c), which benchmarked a DNN against SVM and logistic regression on ChEMBL multi-target activity using ECFP12 with 13,558,545 sparse features, 2,103,018 measurements, 5,069 targets, 743,336 compounds, 1,230 retained targets, scaffold-clustered 3-fold CV, mean AUC, and paired Wilcoxon tests; it supports the methodological template for comparing DL and conventional ML, but the excerpt lacks numeric AUC and p-values, so it cannot directly support the magnitude of DL superiority or inferiority.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that sophisticated deep learning achieved strong, statistically significant performance in a related cheminformatics benchmark: [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9) reported multi-task DNN Tox21 average AUC 0.846 on 643 private compounds, NR panel AUC 0.826, SR panel AUC 0.858, and significant multi-task over single-task improvements such as NR.AR.LBD 0.9289 versus 0.8799 with p=0.011, NR.Aromatase 0.7921 versus 0.7523 with p=0.006, NR.ER 0.6949 versus 0.6659 with p=0.006, NR.ER.LBD 0.7272 versus 0.6532 with p=0.006, NR.PPAR.gamma 0.7102 versus 0.6367 with p=0.006, SR.HSE 0.8101 versus 0.7354 with p=0.006, and SR.p53 0.7487 versus 0.6955 with p=0.006, using 11,764 training compounds, 297 leaderboard compounds, 643 private compounds, 12 tasks, a single 12 GB GPU, batch size 512, dropout/L2 regularization, early stopping, and 5-fold CV. This contradicts the plan's goal of demonstrating that data-centric conventional ML can achieve superior results without complex algorithms, because the DL baseline was already high-performing, even though it is not BRAF-specific. A second contradiction is that the simple-fingerprint advantage is not universal: [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) found that on PDBBind + DUD-E, the ECFP-based structure model performed worse than AutoDock Vina and Smina, while a learnable neural fingerprint model outperformed both docking baselines and reached AUC of 0.9 or greater on 11 test targets versus 4 targets for the reference methods; the paper also cautions that the DUD-E target-agnostic ECFP + logistic regression AUC of 0.904 may be inflated because decoys are too similar across targets. This contradicts overgeneralizing from one fingerprint/ML benchmark to the curated BRAF LBVS setting. A third contradiction is the mixed review evidence in [(Kim et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca7e8bc95864805c15e667ad016dfff98865fd51), which states that deep learning QSAR outperformed random forest on the Merck Molecular Activity Challenge, showing that complex DL can outperform conventional ML in QSAR, although random forest outperformed a one-shot deep VS model. The weakest contradiction is methodological: [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c) used scaffold-aware 3-fold CV, mean AUC, paired Wilcoxon tests, ChEMBL binary labels, ECFP12, and 1,230 targets, whereas the proposed BRAF plan uses 10-fold CV, accuracy/precision/recall, balanced training sets, 5 balanced training datasets of 3,600 actives and unique inactives, and a 500-active hold-out; therefore, comparing the plan's conventional ML results directly to published DL performance levels may be invalid unless metrics, splits, label definitions, and dataset composition are aligned.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the comparative analysis same-task and same-data: retrain or obtain DL baselines (e.g., AtomNet, DeepVS, or a DNN comparable to [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9) and [(Unterthiner et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95f7b2c0fe75f08e3ce0d2ac4315166f4239db5c)) on the exact curated BRAF dataset, using the same 5 balanced training sets of 3,600 actives and unique inactives, the same 500-active hold-out, the same 10-fold CV, and the same molecular representations, so that superiority can be attributed to data curation rather than dataset or metric mismatch. If same-task DL baselines are infeasible, the plan should explicitly frame published DL results from [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a), [(Kim et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca7e8bc95864805c15e667ad016dfff98865fd51), and [(Unterthiner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/65c10300c2094d86c47928ddf70eeda7d083dec9) as external context only, not evidence that data-centric ML is superior. The next most important action is to align evaluation metrics and statistical inference: report AUC and PR-AUC in addition to accuracy, precision, and recall, because related work uses AUC and scaffold-aware or structurally aware folds; add paired statistical tests across folds and models (Wilcoxon or Mann-Whitney), confidence intervals, multiple-testing correction, and scaffold-clustered or temporal splits to reduce leakage from similar BRAF ligands. Then, address dataset composition and inactives: because [(Gonczarek et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d3958d236d81f0b3c5e5ffef5cbf472744112a) shows DUD-E decoys can make target-agnostic compound classification look strong, the plan should report performance separately for curated inactives, less-active IC50 > 10 μM compounds, and DUD-E spike-ins, and include negative-control or scaffold-diverse inactives to avoid overestimating LBVS performance. A further action is to isolate the data-centric contribution by comparing conventional ML and DL on identical fingerprints: for example, compare ECFP6/ECFP12 logistic regression/SVM/RF/GBDT against a DNN with the same ECFP input, and compare merged 45-fingerprint representations against DL models that use the same merged features, rather than only comparing to DL models trained on different representations. Finally, document reproducibility details at the level reported in related work: software versions for Python, scikit-learn, RDKit, and CDK; hardware/CPU/GPU specs; random seeds; hyperparameter search spaces; batch size, dropout, L2, early stopping for any DL baseline; training time; and the exact 1,375-model evaluation pipeline, so that the 5-algorithm, 55-representation benchmark can be reproduced and compared fairly.
