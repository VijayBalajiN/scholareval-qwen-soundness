## Context: To distinguish optogenetic effects from sound adaptation, the plan uses the following method: 'include a control condition where we perform identical procedures but with 0 mW laser power.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 0 mW laser-power control comes from [(Aizenberg et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39a2ec89389ede9d0403c56ca9527571816afdb8), which used optogenetic photomodulation of PV interneurons and excitatory neurons in auditory cortex during tone-evoked response recording, frequency discrimination acuity testing, and auditory fear conditioning, and compared photomodulated with non-photomodulated or baseline conditions. This is directly analogous to the plan's 0 mW condition because it uses a no-light or reduced-light reference to separate optogenetic changes in tone-evoked response magnitude from repeated tone exposure, and it is especially relevant to the proposed 16 kHz and 54 kHz pure-tone paradigm in awake mouse AC. The paper reports that PV modulation changed behavioral frequency discrimination acuity and that this behavioral change correlated with tone-evoked response magnitude rather than frequency selectivity, supporting the plan's use of response amplitude as a primary readout while also showing that a 0 mW control is needed to attribute amplitude changes to rsChRmine activation rather than adaptation. The next strongest support is the newer [(Nocon et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e1794b37373003ba18544c465d2e12ce8c68ae7), which used optogenetic suppression of PV neurons in mouse ACx during competing auditory stimuli and auditory discrimination, and reported that suppression reduced discrimination performance at hotspots, degraded cortical temporal coding, and decreased performance across a wide range of timescales. Although the excerpt does not specify a 0 mW/no-laser control, laser power, holographic targeting, or statistical significance, it extends the iterative line from visual to auditory optogenetic control by showing that optogenetic effects in AC can alter temporal coding and discrimination, not only mean evoked amplitude, so a 0 mW control is relevant but must be paired with temporal and reliability metrics. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Zhu et al., 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aee9f349f9dbeb75033cb153e2ad9960c8e0d73e), which used optogenetic suppression of PV versus SOM interneurons during visual stimulation and found that PV but not SOM suppression reduced response reliability. This supports the general logic that a no-light or 0 mW condition is needed to separate opsin activation from stimulus adaptation, but it is less direct because it is visual, qualitative, and does not report quantitative effect sizes, laser power, stimulus parameters, animal population, or statistical tests.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the three works provides direct evidence that a 0 mW laser-power condition is sufficient to isolate optogenetic effects from sound adaptation. The most recent and closest work, [(Nocon et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e1794b37373003ba18544c465d2e12ce8c68ae7), does not describe a no-laser or 0 mW control, laser power, holographic targeting, or statistical significance, and it reports that PV suppression degraded cortical temporal coding and decreased performance across a wide range of timescales; this contradicts the assumption that a 0 mW control that only compares session-to-session ΔF/F amplitudes will capture all optogenetic effects, especially those affecting temporal coding or discrimination. The next strongest contradiction comes from [(Aizenberg et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39a2ec89389ede9d0403c56ca9527571816afdb8), which also does not explicitly use a 0 mW control and instead compares photomodulation with non-photomodulated or baseline conditions; its finding that behavioral frequency discrimination acuity changed with PV modulation but correlated with tone-evoked response magnitude rather than frequency selectivity contradicts the idea that a 0 mW amplitude control alone will reveal frequency-specific rebalancing among co-tuned ensembles. The weakest contradiction is from [(Zhu et al., 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aee9f349f9dbeb75033cb153e2ad9960c8e0d73e), which is visual and qualitative but shows that PV versus SOM suppression differentially affects response reliability without quantitative effect sizes or explicit 0 mW parameters, indicating that a 0 mW control may miss cell-type- or ensemble-specific reliability changes unless reliability metrics are included.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the 0 mW condition should be implemented as a true no-light control with identical optical path, shutter timing, hologram generation, two-photon imaging, tone sequence, ISI, trial order, and session length, and the order of 0 mW and 5 mW sessions should be counterbalanced or interleaved to separate laser effects from session order and repeated-tone adaptation; the plan should model adaptation in the 0 mW data as a function of trial number and session and use that model to correct or test the 5 mW data. Second, because [(Aizenberg et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39a2ec89389ede9d0403c56ca9527571816afdb8) and [(Nocon et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e1794b37373003ba18544c465d2e12ce8c68ae7) show optogenetic effects on response magnitude, temporal coding, and discrimination, the analysis should add trial-to-trial reliability, frequency tuning/selectivity, and temporal coding metrics such as ISI distance, RI-SPIKE-distance, or van Rossum decoding-time, rather than relying only on ΔF/F amplitude. Third, to test the co-tuned ensemble hypothesis, add non-co-tuned ensemble stimulation, spatially offset holograms, and distance-decay controls, plus light-alone or subthreshold low-power trials to verify that 5 mW rsChRmine activation is causal and not due to imaging light, heat, or opsin expression. Finally, report granular reproducibility details missing from the related work: animal and cell numbers, laser power calibration, opsin expression levels, temperature, statistical tests, effect sizes, confidence intervals, and mixed-effects models with session, trial, frequency, and co-tuned status as factors.

## Context: To assess whether stimulation-induced changes are persistent, the plan uses the following method: 'present the same tone series without stimulation to assess whether any observed changes persist after the manipulation period.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Session 4 is indirect but relevant: [(James et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a18feaf7a7bfd92f6159c891cca2567bcc4836) used awake head-fixed mice and compared optogenetic stimulation to a passive no-stimulation baseline, showing that optogenetic activation can produce a statistically significant acute network change, specifically a 10-100/1-10 Hz cortical desynchrony ratio increase relative to the immediately preceding 750-ms baseline (Wilcoxon signed-rank test, p=6.036e-6). This supports the proposed logic of using a no-light post-stimulation tone series to separate stimulation-induced changes from passive auditory responses, because the related work demonstrates that optogenetic effects can be detected against a passive baseline in awake mice. A weaker but still relevant support is that the study included Ai32 control mice, 6 of 38 total mice alongside 32 Chat-ChR2 mice, supporting the proposed 0 mW control condition for distinguishing optogenetic effects from procedural or adaptation artifacts. The weakest support is that the related work’s passive-sound dataset showed trial-to-trial cortical desynchrony during the inter-trial interval did not significantly predict PFC ERP magnitude (Wilcoxon rank-sum test, p=0.879), which indirectly supports the proposed plan’s emphasis on comparing pre-stimulation, stimulation, and post-stimulation sessions rather than relying on a single baseline measure to infer persistent network effects.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that [(James et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a18feaf7a7bfd92f6159c891cca2567bcc4836) did not include a delayed post-stimulation tone series; it only compared a 750-ms light window to the immediately preceding 750-ms baseline, so it provides no direct evidence that optogenetically induced changes persist after the manipulation period. This is the central claim of the proposed Session 4, and the absence of a no-light post-stimulation session in the related work means the persistence test is not empirically validated by this study. A moderate contradiction is that the same study reported optogenetic stimulation did not alter sound-evoked amplitude, although no effect size or p-value was provided; this is in tension with the proposed method’s expectation that stimulation may produce measurable changes in tone-evoked ΔF/F amplitude that could persist, especially because the proposed method uses 100-ms 16 or 54 kHz pure tones, 5.8-6.5-s inter-stimulus intervals, two-photon GCaMP8s imaging, and holographic rsChRmine stimulation of five co-tuned AC neurons, whereas the related work used 70 dB SPL acoustic white noise, 10-s inter-trial intervals, 500-ms sound windows, 1-150 Hz LFP filtering, 381 Hz sampling, and ChR2-based optogenetic stimulation. A weaker contradiction is that the related work’s acute optogenetic effect was a desynchrony/power-ratio change, not a persistent change in evoked amplitude, and its baseline desynchrony did not predict PFC ERP magnitude (p=0.879), suggesting that the proposed post-stimulation analysis should not assume that baseline network state or a single acute desynchrony change will translate into stable, frequency-specific amplitude rebalancing.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make Session 4 an explicit persistence test rather than a generic no-light session: present the identical 100-ms 16/54 kHz tone series, with the same 5.8-6.5-s inter-stimulus intervals, 30 trials, random order, and trial count as Session 2, and compare post-stimulation responses to Session 2 using within-subject paired statistics with effect sizes, while also including a no-stimulation control session that undergoes the same tone series without optogenetic stimulation to separate persistence from session-order or adaptation effects. The next most important action is to add a 0 mW control and, if feasible, a delayed no-light control after a sham stimulation session, because [(James et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a18feaf7a7bfd92f6159c891cca2567bcc4836) used passive baselines and Ai32 controls but did not test delayed persistence; this will help distinguish true aftereffects from natural adaptation to repeated tones. A further action is to measure multiple response metrics, including ΔF/F amplitude, latency, trial-to-trial variability, and frequency-specific co-tuned versus non-co-tuned effects, rather than relying only on amplitude, because the related work’s strongest optogenetic effect was a desynchrony/power-ratio change and its sound-evoked amplitude result was reported without statistical detail. Finally, the plan should pre-register the post-stimulation comparisons, account for multiple comparisons across sessions, frequencies, and cell populations, and include a power analysis or sample-size justification, since the related work used 38 mice for LFP-based optogenetic tests whereas the proposed two-photon/holographic single-cell design may require different statistical power to detect persistent ensemble-level changes.

## Context: To infer the spatial and temporal mechanism of rebalancing, the plan uses the following method: 'examine the spatial distribution of affected neurons and the temporal dynamics of any observed changes to understand the underlying mechanisms.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from the direct use of two-photon imaging in mouse primary auditory cortex to construct frequency preference maps and quantify local spatial tuning heterogeneity in [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2). This is highly relevant because the research plan also relies on in vivo two-photon calcium imaging in awake mouse A1 to identify frequency-tuned neurons, select small co-tuned ensembles at 16 kHz or 54 kHz, and examine the spatial distribution of affected neurons. The related work demonstrates that neighboring A1 neurons can differ in preferred frequency and that this local heterogeneity is present at multiple spatial length scales, which supports the plan’s assumption that spatial analysis of affected neurons is meaningful and should not be reduced to simple nearest-neighbor comparisons. A weaker but still useful point of support is that [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2) also measured signal correlations and found them higher in C57BL/6 mice than in F1 CBAxC57 mice, suggesting that population-level spatial organization and correlated activity are measurable and potentially important. This supports the plan’s need to distinguish co-tuned from non-co-tuned responses and to consider baseline network structure when interpreting stimulation-induced changes, although the related work does not itself test optogenetic rebalancing.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction, or more precisely the strongest limitation, is that [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2) does not use holographic optogenetic stimulation and does not test the temporal dynamics of stimulation-induced rebalancing. Therefore, it provides no direct evidence that increasing activity in a small co-tuned ensemble will suppress other co-tuned neurons, that this effect will be frequency-specific, or that it will occur rapidly and persist across sessions as the research plan predicts. A second, more indirect contradiction is that the related work shows genetic background can alter the spatial organization of frequency tuning: F1 CBAxC57 mice had a broader range of frequency preference and higher local heterogeneity than C57BL/6 mice, while signal correlations were lower in F1 mice. This means that the spatial distribution of affected neurons and the strength of co-tuned ensemble interactions may depend on strain, so the proposed method should not assume that spatial tuning structure or rebalancing effects will be identical across mouse lines. A weaker limitation is that the supplied excerpt from [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2) does not provide numeric effect sizes, sample sizes, or statistical significance values, which limits how strongly its findings can be used to benchmark the expected magnitude or reliability of spatial heterogeneity in the proposed experiment.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to operationalize the spatial analysis using the kind of local heterogeneity and length-scale framework demonstrated in [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2). The plan should explicitly quantify frequency preference maps, local spatial tuning heterogeneity, and distance-dependent tuning similarity in the imaged field of view, then compare the spatial distribution of affected neurons to co-tuned and non-co-tuned baselines rather than only reporting whether affected neurons are near the stimulated five cells. The second most important action is to specify and control for genetic background, because [(Bowen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2e23eb3d78060040112339530fabb33b24815d2) shows that F1 CBAxC57 and C57BL/6 mice differ in frequency preference range, local heterogeneity, and signal correlations. If the plan uses C57BL/6 mice, it should report that baseline spatial tuning structure is expected to be less heterogeneous and more correlated than in F1 mice; if it uses F1 mice, it should account for broader tuning and higher local heterogeneity when selecting the top 30% responsive 16 kHz or 54 kHz ensembles. The next improvement is to strengthen the temporal dynamics analysis by measuring trial-by-trial ΔF/F time courses, latency, decay, and persistence across the four-session paradigm, and by using the 0 mW control to separate true optogenetic rebalancing from adaptation to repeated 100 ms tones. Finally, the plan should report sample sizes, effect sizes, statistical significance, imaging depth, frame rate, laser power, opsin expression, and stimulation validation metrics such as response decay with distance and the proportion of successfully activated target neurons, because the related work’s lack of detailed quantitative reporting in the excerpt highlights the need for transparent statistical and implementation details.

## Context: To verify that holographic stimulation is localized to intended cells, the plan uses the following method: 'conduct control experiments varying stimulation position relative to target cells and measuring response decay with distance.'

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Triplett et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d1fdb72be2328293d106365d9f20374230ad1a3), which directly operationalizes the proposed control by delivering holographic stimulation on a spatial grid around each simulated neuron, varying x/y offsets from -20 to 20 μm in 10 μm steps and, in 3D, depths from -60 to 60 μm in 30 μm steps, while fitting a GP-Bernoulli model to activation probability as a function of distance, direction, depth, and power; this shows that position-response decay can be quantified and used to reduce off-target activation, with 2D optimization reducing average write-in error by 85% and 3D optimization reducing it by 89% from 0.7 to 0.08, with average stimulus displacements of 4.9 μm and 8.3 μm. This supports the plan's use of shifted 15 μm spiral holograms relative to five auditory-cortex targets, although the evidence is synthetic/slice-derived. Additional physical support comes from [(Ronzitti et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b6db4822512484b3fda84d998d801e21196604), which demonstrated that two-photon CGH using phase-only liquid-crystal SLMs and temporal focusing can produce spatially restricted 15 μm-diameter spots and extended patterns in acute mouse slices and zebrafish at low average powers of 0.05-0.6 mW/μm^2, with ChR2-expressing interneurons activated at 201 ± 9 μm depth and ~120 pA currents during 10 ms illumination; the plan's 5 mW per cell over a 15 μm-diameter spot corresponds to about 0.03 mW/μm^2 if uniformly delivered, within that low-power regime. Weaker but relevant support is the same paper's multi-plane CGH, which projected patterns in a 240 × 240 × 260 μm^3 volume and displaced a plane over ~300 μm, and temporal focusing, which maintained activation at ≥500 μm, supporting axial position controls if the plan extends beyond a single focal plane.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction or limitation is that [(Ronzitti et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b6db4822512484b3fda84d998d801e21196604) reports two-photon CGH speckle inhomogeneity of about 50%, underfilled-pupil NA loss, and SLM tiling limits, meaning the delivered 15 μm spiral may not be uniform or perfectly localized; a measured decay with distance could therefore reflect illumination nonuniformity, speckle, or axial defocus rather than true biological off-target activation, especially in awake tissue where motion and scattering are greater. Next, [(Triplett et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d1fdb72be2328293d106365d9f20374230ad1a3) provides no direct in vivo auditory-cortex validation, no rsChRmine/GCaMP8s co-expression, no 5-cell co-tuned ensemble, and no statistical significance tests or confidence intervals; its 116 simulated neurons were built from only four cell-attached L2/3 interneuron recordings in mouse V1 slice and assigned one of four ground-truth ORFs, so its 85-89% error reductions may not transfer to the plan's 5 mW per cell, 100 ms, 15 μm spiral, awake AC conditions. Weaker contradictions include that [(Ronzitti et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b6db4822512484b3fda84d998d801e21196604) used ChR2, C1V1, Chronos, CoChR, and ReaChR in slices, cultures, and zebrafish with 1-10 ms pulses rather than rsChRmine in awake AC with 100 ms stimulation, and that [(Triplett et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d1fdb72be2328293d106365d9f20374230ad1a3) used power ranges of 10-70 mW and 10 μm grid steps that may be coarser or higher than the plan's 5 mW per cell and 15 μm spiral, limiting direct extrapolation.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace a simple one-dimensional distance-decay control with a spatially explicit ORF mapping protocol modeled on [(Triplett et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d1fdb72be2328293d106365d9f20374230ad1a3): for each of the five target cells, shift the 15 μm spiral hologram on an x/y grid, for example -20 to +20 μm in 5-10 μm steps, and if feasible add z offsets of -30 to +30 μm, at 5 mW per cell and at lower powers, record GCaMP8s ΔF/F, fit a GP-Bernoulli or equivalent activation-probability model, and use the fitted field to reposition holograms and adjust power to minimize off-target activation while preserving target activation. Second, calibrate and control the illumination field as emphasized by [(Ronzitti et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b6db4822512484b3fda84d998d801e21196604): measure the actual PSF and speckle statistics of the 15 μm spiral at the target plane, use speckle-free CGH or time averaging, fill the pupil to preserve NA, and include 0 mW and shifted-pattern controls to separate biological response decay from illumination artifacts. Third, quantify multi-cell stimulation reliability by reporting per-cell activation probability, write-in error, and trial-to-trial variability across the five-cell ensemble, with confidence intervals and significance tests, rather than only mean response amplitude. Fourth, if axial control is available, test depth-dependent decay using multi-plane CGH or defocused holograms, because both [(Triplett et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d1fdb72be2328293d106365d9f20374230ad1a3) and [(Ronzitti et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20b6db4822512484b3fda84d998d801e21196604) show that depth is a major determinant of off-target activation. Finally, match stimulation parameters to rsChRmine kinetics by testing shorter durations and power ramps around the planned 100 ms, 5 mW per cell condition to avoid saturation and ensure that the distance-decay measurement is not limited by opsin kinetics or calcium indicator dynamics.

## Context: To verify that the intended five-cell ensembles are reliably activated, the plan uses the following method: 'test the reliability of multi-cell stimulation by assessing the proportion of successfully activated target neurons across trials.'

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Schmieder et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac0ac520022ceeeb270a260aa9e68fc209d54716) demonstrated that holographic SLM-based optogenetic stimulation can selectively evoke activity in a targeted opsin-expressing neuron in hiPSC-derived neuronal networks on MEA chips, using AP frequency and burst frequency relative to a pre-stimulation baseline as the activation criterion. This directly parallels the proposed plan's need to verify that five-cell ensembles are reliably activated by comparing evoked responses to baseline and by measuring the proportion of target neurons activated across trials. A second, weaker support is the paper's distance-dependent decay of evoked AP or burst frequency in neighboring electrodes, which supports the proposed control of varying stimulation position and measuring response decay with distance to assess holographic precision. However, this support is indirect because the related work was single-neuron, in vitro, MEA-based, and did not report numerical success rates, fold changes, distance-bin values, or statistical significance, so it cannot establish that the same validation strategy will work for simultaneous five-cell, in vivo, awake mouse auditory cortex stimulation with GCaMP8s and rsChRmine at 5 mW per cell.

### Contradictions

There is no direct contradiction, but the strongest limitation is that the related work does not test the core proposed method: multi-cell ensemble activation. It validated single-neuron holographic targeting, not simultaneous activation of five co-tuned neurons, and therefore cannot support the assumption that the proportion of successfully activated target neurons across trials will be high or stable for a five-cell ensemble. A weaker contradiction to the plan's implicit generalizability is that the related work used in vitro hiPSC-derived neuronal cultures on MEA chips with AP and burst frequency readout, whereas the proposed experiment uses awake head-fixed mice, primary auditory cortex layer 2/3, two-photon calcium imaging, ΔF/F, 100 ms tones, 5 mW per cell, a 15 μm spiral pattern, and 30 revolutions; these differences in species, preparation, readout, optical access, and ensemble size mean the single-neuron in vitro success rate and distance decay cannot be extrapolated to the proposed in vivo setting. The weakest but important gap is the absence of quantitative outcomes, such as activation success rates, effect sizes, distance-bin values, or statistical significance, which prevents the related work from providing a benchmark for the proposed reliability metric.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define an explicit, quantitative activation criterion for each of the five target neurons before scoring trial success, analogous to the related work's baseline-relative AP and burst frequency criterion but adapted to ΔF/F; for example, require a tone-evoked or stimulation-evoked ΔF/F peak or integrated response to exceed a pre-stimulation baseline threshold such as a z-score, percentile, or minimum fold change, and then report the proportion of target cells activated per trial and per ensemble with confidence intervals. The next priority is to add a distance-decay control that shifts the holographic pattern by known offsets relative to the target cells and quantifies response decay across neighboring cells or electrodes, mirroring the related work's neighboring-electrode analysis, to establish the spatial precision of the 15 μm spiral pattern and 5 mW per cell in vivo. A further action is to compare activation rates and response amplitudes against the 0 mW control using appropriate statistical tests, such as binomial tests for activation proportions and mixed-effects models for ΔF/F across cells, trials, and sessions, to distinguish true optogenetic activation from tone-evoked or adaptation effects. Finally, because the related work provides no multi-cell in vivo benchmark, the plan should include a pilot validation or ex vivo or in vitro calibration to estimate expected success rates, spatial spread, and power limits before committing to the full four-session awake mouse paradigm.
