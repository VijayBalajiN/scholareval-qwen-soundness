## Core loss design: We propose to develop a novel loss function called "zephyr loss" with the mathematical formulation:
L(a) = α(√(a² + ε) - √ε)
where ε is a small positive regularization parameter and α is a scaling factor. This is the central proposed method because it defines the new GAN objective and is intended to provide smooth, robust, and controllable gradients.

### Support

The strongest support for the zephyr loss is quantitative evidence that controlling discriminator/critic gradients improves GAN sample quality and stability. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) showed WGAN-GP with a gradient penalty reached 7.86 +/- 0.07 Inception score on CIFAR-10 ResNet, above DCGAN 5.62, Improved GAN 6.16 +/- 0.07, EGAN-Ent-VI 6.86 +/- 0.06, and DFM 7.72 +/- 0.13, while providing more stable convergence than weight clipping; although later work complicates the universality of WGAN-GP, this result directly supports the plan's goal of a smooth, controllable discriminator objective and the inclusion of WGAN as a baseline. A second strong line of support is that existing modified losses are not automatically stable under unfavorable hyperparameters, motivating a new loss. [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf) reported that under deliberately unstable Adam settings, LSGAN and WGAN-gp underperformed baseline DCGAN and data-space NSS-GAN on FID and IS across CelebA, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Conference, and Oxford-Flowers, e.g. CelebA LSGAN 33.38 +/- 5.10 FID and WGAN-gp 42.16 +/- 2.77 FID versus DCGAN 21.36 +/- 1.71 FID and NSS-GAN 19.45 +/- 1.15 FID, which supports the hypothesis that a smoother, epsilon-regularized loss may reduce sensitivity to outliers and optimizer settings. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) further supports the need for robust gradient control by showing spectral normalization remained stable under aggressive Adam settings while WGAN-GP failed under high learning-rate and momentum settings on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, suggesting that the zephyr loss's bounded gradient alpha*a/sqrt(a^2+epsilon) and epsilon smoothing should be tested under the same stress conditions. [(Zhang, and Khoreva, 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/971b5a5b9945f1b027dc24e9330ad938976f4163) supports the planned evaluation and regularization framing by showing PA-GAN produced consistent FID reductions of about 3 points over SN DCGAN and SA GAN baselines, improved MS-SSIM diversity from 0.283 to 0.266 on CELEBA-HQ, and used KID-based progression scheduling with medians over five runs, aligning with the plan's FID, KID, and ablation emphasis. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) supports the generator-side feature matching objective and the use of Inception score, but also specifies that IS should be computed on at least 50,000 samples to capture diversity, which the plan should adopt. [(Odena et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecc0edd450ae7e52f65ddf61405b30ad6dbabdd7) supports the IS protocol and stability concerns by reporting 8.25 +/- 0.07 IS on 32x32 CIFAR-10 versus 8.09 +/- 0.07 for a prior baseline, using 50,000 samples for model selection and 25,000 for final reporting, and showing that class splitting reduced collapse in ImageNet class-conditional training, which is relevant to CIFAR-100. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Petzka et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f64233585c793b56d61f95c1cf25282ad28ceb4b), where WGAN-LP was less sensitive to penalty weight than WGAN-GP, supporting the plan's desire for epsilon and alpha robustness; [(Roth et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/698d3b667a7f3073eed8368d9daf84f990c24a65), where a regularized JS-GAN achieved cross-testing false positive rate 0.0 versus 1.0 for unregularized GAN on CIFAR-10, supporting discriminator-side regularization and generalization metrics; and [(Yahiro et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/056c860e5cd87f3cebd3cea5319a99fb229075f8), where flooding with a tunable loss floor b prevented collapse when combined with spectral normalization or gradient penalty, analogous to the zephyr loss's epsilon floor and alpha scaling.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan's central assumption is that changing the discriminator loss alone may not be sufficient for stable high-quality GAN training. [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf) showed that LSGAN and WGAN-gp, both established modified discriminator objectives, underperformed baseline DCGAN and data-space NSS-GAN under unstable hyperparameters on FID and IS across CelebA, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Conference, and Oxford-Flowers, e.g. LSUN-Conference WGAN-gp 121.05 +/- 7.60 FID versus DCGAN 77.96 +/- 5.49 FID and NSS-GAN 64.61 +/- 3.20 FID, indicating that a new smooth loss such as zephyr must be validated against both loss-only and data-space stabilization baselines before claiming superiority. A second major contradiction, and a newer complication of the earlier positive WGAN-GP result in [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), is that WGAN-style objectives are not uniformly stable, which challenges the plan's reliance on WGAN as a stable baseline and on a fixed Adam configuration. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) found WGAN-GP failed to train good GANs under high learning-rate and high-momentum settings D, E, and F on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, while spectral normalization remained stable, so the zephyr loss should not be evaluated only at batch size 64, Adam learning rate 0.0002, and beta1 0.5. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) contradicts the assumption that feature matching alone will yield strong visual quality: feature matching alone produced samples that were not visually appealing, and minibatch discrimination improved visual quality but reduced classifier performance, so the plan's generator objective may need additional terms or careful tuning. [(Odena et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecc0edd450ae7e52f65ddf61405b30ad6dbabdd7) contradicts the idea that a single DCGAN-style unsupervised model will reliably converge on more complex class spaces: increasing class count harmed performance and was associated with generator collapse, and even a split size of one class did not guarantee convergence, which is directly relevant to CIFAR-100 and STL-10. A weaker but important contradiction is that the plan's primary metric, Inception score, is less reliable than FID/KID in newer work and requires large sample sizes; [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) and [(Odena et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecc0edd450ae7e52f65ddf61405b30ad6dbabdd7) both use 50,000-sample IS protocols, while later work such as [(Zhang, and Khoreva, 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/971b5a5b9945f1b027dc24e9330ad938976f4163) and [(Zhao et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b31fdc82691b36e22b2ba15846a7a757061da4fa) treats FID as the primary metric, so making IS primary may obscure distribution-matching gains. Finally, the loss formulation is under-specified relative to related work: if a is the raw discriminator output with true labels 1 and false labels 0, L(a) is minimized at a=0, which would push real outputs toward 0 unless a is defined as a signed residual D(x)-y or an absolute residual; this ambiguity is not present in the standard, LSGAN, WGAN, or hinge objectives used in [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc), [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), and [(Lee, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cb811229b10bf4548cf4a1fb89a1e5564d46938).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize the zephyr loss before experiments: define a as the signed residual a = D(x) - y or an explicitly stated absolute residual, derive the gradient alpha*a/sqrt(a^2+epsilon), prove smoothness, convexity, bounded gradient, and Lipschitz gradient with constant alpha/sqrt(epsilon), and analyze how epsilon=0.1 to 0.0001 changes curvature when discriminator outputs are near 0 or 1. This should include the planned theoretical validation of convergence, uniqueness of the optimal discriminator, and support-mismatch cases, using the zero-centered penalty and manifold analysis in [(Mescheder et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b4ca249b3b28d3fee65f69714440c08d42cee64) as a template. The second most important action is to strengthen the empirical protocol: use FID and KID as co-primary metrics with IS computed on at least 50,000 samples as in [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) and [(Odena et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecc0edd450ae7e52f65ddf61405b30ad6dbabdd7), report medians or means over at least five independent runs and preferably 20 trials as in [(Zhang, and Khoreva, 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/971b5a5b9945f1b027dc24e9330ad938976f4163) and [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf), and add baselines including WGAN-GP, WGAN-LP, HingeGAN, spectral-normalized GAN, R1-regularized GAN as in [(Choi et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5474ddca920f59c4ec3c243345a5b9248e64065b) and [(Zheng et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b21c344b4f212c8ea88926d18d70007a5ac0711), flooding as in [(Yahiro et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/056c860e5cd87f3cebd3cea5319a99fb229075f8), PA-GAN, LSGAN, ComGAN/RaGAN as in [(Lee, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cb811229b10bf4548cf4a1fb89a1e5564d46938), data-space NSS-GAN, and the planned Diffusion-GAN baseline, all with the same DCGAN architecture, batch size 64, Adam learning rate 0.0002 and beta1 0.5 where applicable, RMSProp for WGAN as planned, plus stress tests with aggressive learning-rate and momentum settings motivated by [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf). The third action is to make the alpha and epsilon ablations diagnostic rather than merely grid-based: plot FID, IS, KID, gradient norm, discriminator confidence, and training stability against alpha in 0.3 to 1.5 and epsilon in 0.1 to 0.0001, compare sensitivity to WGAN-GP lambda and WGAN-LP lambda in [(Petzka et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f64233585c793b56d61f95c1cf25282ad28ceb4b), and use KID-based progression or early stopping as in [(Zhang, and Khoreva, 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/971b5a5b9945f1b027dc24e9330ad938976f4163). The fourth action is to add optimization-dynamics diagnostics, including path-angle and Jacobian eigenvalue analysis around locally stable stationary points as in [(Berard et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec09db517aa4399ebbb33fb3acc342178f2aca90), generator path-length or Jacobian monitoring as in [(Karras et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/14fdc18d9c164e5b0d6d946b3238c04e81921358), and mode-coverage or MS-SSIM diversity metrics as in [(Che et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/024d30897e0a2b036bc122163a954b7f1a1d0679) and [(Zhang, and Khoreva, 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/971b5a5b9945f1b027dc24e9330ad938976f4163). The fifth action is to adapt the dataset plan: for CIFAR-100, use class-conditional or class-split training as in [(Odena et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecc0edd450ae7e52f65ddf61405b30ad6dbabdd7) to reduce collapse, and for SVHN and STL-10 report FID/KID with 10,000-sample evaluations as common in [(Zhao et al., 2020-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b31fdc82691b36e22b2ba15846a7a757061da4fa) and [(Lee, 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2cb811229b10bf4548cf4a1fb89a1e5564d46938). The least urgent but still useful action is to document reproducibility details missing from many related works, including hardware, random seeds, training iterations, FID/KID sample counts, and whether Diffusion-ZGAN uses paired reconstruction or perceptual terms as in [(Isola et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8acbe90d5b852dadea7810345451a99608ee54c7) and [(Kupyn et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81c1dc00b32d12edfab00656593f5db56cfa79e0).

## Loss property design: Our methodology centers on leveraging the theoretical properties of this loss function:
- Smoothness and convexity: The function will be designed to be smooth, convex, and Lipschitz continuous
- Gradient stabilization: The ε parameter will provide regularization and prevent abrupt gradient changes near a = 0
- Flexible control: The α parameter will allow modulation of loss magnitude and gradient updates. This specifies the intended mathematical behavior of the zephyr loss and connects the formulation to the claimed training-stability benefits.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Qin et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2ae5e23afbcce774fbfdf2d2a57fcd061ae573e), which directly tests a loss-domain scaling factor alpha under gradient penalty and spectral normalization on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA with batch size 64, RMSProp learning rate 5e-5, 100000 iterations, and FID, IS, and Neural Divergence; it reports that FID generally improves as alpha decreases, that alpha at most 1e-9 can match strong Lipschitz settings, and that alpha at most 1e-11 becomes WGAN-like and slightly worse, supporting the proposed alpha as a flexible control of loss magnitude and gradient updates. This newer loss-domain result refines the older LSGAN and WGAN-GP line by showing that scaling the loss itself can change the effective training regime, not only the network or penalty term. The next strongest support is [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa), which supports the Lipschitz and gradient-stabilization motivation by showing SN-GAN on CIFAR-10, STL-10, and ImageNet with convolutional GANs, Adam settings, 100K generator updates, IS and FID, and singular-value analysis is more robust to aggressive learning rates and momentum than WGAN-GP, and that hinge loss improves performance, tying directly to the plan's DCGAN, Adam, IS, and FID setup. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) provides older but strong evidence that gradient-level regularization improves training: WGAN-GP outperforms weight-clipping WGAN on CIFAR-10 with the same optimizer and learning rate, and a ResNet WGAN-GP reaches IS 7.86 plus or minus 0.07 versus DCGAN 5.62, Improved GAN 6.16 plus or minus 0.07, EGAN-Ent-VI 7.07 plus or minus 0.10, and DFM 7.72 plus or minus 0.13, supporting the plan's claim that stabilizing discriminator gradients can improve sample quality. [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) supports the baseline and loss-design framing by introducing LSGAN with DCGAN-style TensorFlow models on LSUN scenes and HWDB1.0, using Adam beta1=0.5 and learning rates 0.001 or 0.0002, and explicitly comparing stability against regular GANs, although the excerpt lacks numerical IS, FID, or KID results. [(Zhou et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfb9ea4f70aef592c59817d70f69b832e605c773) supports gradient-scale control by showing maximum gradient penalty converges more stably than gradient penalty in two-dimensional and ten-sample CIFAR-10 toy settings, while full CIFAR-10 FID curves for GP, MGP, and augmented Lagrangian are similar, indicating that gradient behavior is a real training variable. [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf) supports the evaluation design and baseline context by reporting 20-trial FID and IS for DCGAN-based NSS-GAN on CelebA, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Conference, and Oxford-Flowers, with large FID reductions over LSGAN and WGAN-gp, while using non-saturating loss and Adam. [(Roth et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/698d3b667a7f3073eed8368d9daf84f990c24a65) supports tunable discriminator regularization with gamma=0.1 on CIFAR-10, using 10,000 real and 10,000 generated samples for cross-testing and reporting false-positive rates, though not IS, FID, or KID. [(Mescheder et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b4ca249b3b28d3fee65f69714440c08d42cee64) supports the theoretical-validation component by extending convergence analysis to zero-centered gradient penalties and lower-dimensional supports, but provides no empirical metrics. Finally, [(Zamorski et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e158a155a4374f733e0527e14f254902c5ab3426) weakly supports the plan's qualitative and perceptual evaluation by showing PSNR and SSIM can be lower while MOS improves by 6.2%, 24.8%, and 55.5% in a 4x superresolution GAN with VGG19 perceptual loss.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Qin et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2ae5e23afbcce774fbfdf2d2a57fcd061ae573e): its alpha is a loss-domain scaling factor, and the reported regime where FID improves is much smaller than the proposed alpha grid 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and 1.5; at alpha at most 1e-11 performance becomes WGAN-like and slightly worse, while alpha at least 1e5 makes 1-Lipschitz regularization fail, so the proposed grid may test only a narrow, possibly non-decisive range and may not demonstrate the claimed flexible gradient control. It also reports that LSGAN behaves abnormally under zero-centered scaling because of its 0.5-centered domain, implying that zephyr's zero-centered form must be carefully validated against target labels 1 and 0 and against non-saturating generator losses. A second major contradiction is that [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) show that Lipschitz or gradient stabilization can be achieved without replacing the scalar loss: SN-GAN controls discriminator spectral norm and is robust to aggressive Adam settings, while WGAN-GP adds a gradient penalty and improves IS over weight clipping; this challenges the necessity of a new zephyr loss unless it outperforms or composes with these baselines. [(Zhou et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfb9ea4f70aef592c59817d70f69b832e605c773) further weakens the assumption that better gradient behavior automatically yields better final FID, because full CIFAR-10 GP, MGP, and augmented Lagrangian variants produced similar FID curves and final sample quality with little apparent sensitivity to regularization weight, and spectral normalization diverged under Wasserstein, showing objective-regularizer interactions. [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf) contradicts a loss-only sufficiency claim by showing that a data-space noisy scale-space DCGAN method outperforms LSGAN and WGAN-gp by large FID margins, for example reducing FID by 13.93, 17.02, 17.29, and 35.53 over LSGAN and by 22.71, 43.75, 56.44, and 42.77 over WGAN-gp on CelebA, LSUN-Church, LSUN-Conference, and Oxford-Flowers, suggesting that instability may require data-space or architectural interventions in addition to loss design. [(Roth et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/698d3b667a7f3073eed8368d9daf84f990c24a65) contradicts indiscriminate application of discriminator regularization to the generator path because the regularizer was not applied directly to the generator near convergence due to destabilization, which is relevant if the plan later uses zephyr in generator-related objectives. [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) provides weaker contradiction because it motivates LSGAN as a stable alternative to cross-entropy but the excerpt lacks quantitative IS, FID, or KID evidence, so it cannot confirm that least-squares or zephyr-like losses improve the planned metrics. [(Mescheder et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b4ca249b3b28d3fee65f69714440c08d42cee64) is not a direct empirical contradiction but limits the theoretical claim by focusing on gradient penalties rather than a smooth convex scalar loss, so convergence proofs for zephyr cannot be borrowed without new analysis.

### Suggested Action

Most important, redesign the alpha and epsilon ablation around the evidence in [(Qin et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2ae5e23afbcce774fbfdf2d2a57fcd061ae573e): use a log-spaced alpha range that includes values below 1 and above 1, report the induced loss interval, gradient norm, effective Lipschitz constant, and whether the objective becomes WGAN-like at small alpha, and explicitly test the proposed 0.3 to 1.5 values as a subset rather than the full search. Second, add the strongest stability baselines and combinations: LSGAN, WGAN-GP, SN-GAN, hinge or vanilla GAN, and zephyr plus SN or GP, using the plan's DCGAN, batch size 64, Adam learning rate 0.0002 and beta1 0.5, but also include the aggressive Adam settings from [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) to test whether zephyr improves robustness beyond spectral normalization or gradient penalty. Third, make the theoretical validation concrete by proving the optimal discriminator, convergence, and distribution-matching relationship for L(a)=alpha(sqrt(a^2+epsilon)-sqrt(epsilon)), including the bounded gradient alpha*a/sqrt(a^2+epsilon), the gradient Lipschitz constant alpha/epsilon, and the effect of epsilon near a=0, while contrasting the result with the convergence analyses in [(Mescheder et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b4ca249b3b28d3fee65f69714440c08d42cee64) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4). Fourth, align evaluation with the strongest empirical papers: report IS, FID, and KID on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN with multiple seeds, mean and standard deviation, and significance tests, using at least the 20-trial reporting style of [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf) and the IS and FID protocols of [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4). Fifth, because [(Zamorski et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e158a155a4374f733e0527e14f254902c5ab3426) shows numerical image-quality metrics can diverge from human perception, include qualitative visual assessment and, if feasible, a small perceptual or MOS study or FID over a fixed 50k sample set. Sixth, test generator-objective variants carefully: keep feature matching as planned, but also compare a non-saturating generator loss and, if zephyr is applied to the generator, follow the caution in [(Roth et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/698d3b667a7f3073eed8368d9daf84f990c24a65) by annealing or disabling generator-side regularization near convergence. Finally, document reproducibility details missing from several baselines, including hardware, iteration count, dataset splits, random seeds, and exact LSGAN and WGAN-GP hyperparameters from [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc), [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), and [(Nakamura et al., 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d6a95b59a6f3ccb235025e39bd06a683c69cabf).

## GAN integration: We will integrate this zephyr loss into the GAN framework to create ZGAN, where:
- The discriminator objective will minimize zephyr loss to measure closeness between discriminator outputs and target values
- The generator objective will use feature matching principles to align generated samples with real data distribution. This turns the proposed loss into a trainable GAN architecture and specifies how both discriminator and generator are optimized.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed GAN integration is direct evidence that feature matching can be used as a GAN generator objective and that Inception Score is a relevant quality and diversity metric. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) trained semi-supervised GANs on MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, and ImageNet with 20 to 200 labeled examples averaged over 10 balanced subsets, 5 hidden layers, weight normalization, and Gaussian noise, and showed that feature matching improved the semi-supervised classifier over minibatch discrimination while also recommending 50k generated images for Inception Score because the metric measures diversity. This supports ZGAN's plan to use feature matching for the generator and IS as a primary metric, especially on CIFAR-10 and SVHN. The next strongest support is that modifying the discriminator objective can improve stability and sample quality, which motivates replacing the standard discriminator loss with zephyr loss. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) showed WGAN-GP with a gradient penalty converged faster and reached higher CIFAR-10 Inception Score than DCGAN, reporting 7.86 ± 0.07 for a ResNet WGAN-GP versus 5.62 for DCGAN, 6.16 ± 0.07 for Improved GAN, 7.07 ± 0.10 for EGAN-Ent-VI, and 7.72 ± 0.13 for DFM, and it also supports using WGAN as a baseline. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) further supports gradient and Lipschitz control by showing spectral normalization keeps discriminator layer norms near 1 to 1.05, is more robust than WGAN-GP across six optimizer settings on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, and improves IS/FID, which aligns with the zephyr loss goal of smooth gradient flow. The iterative progression is important: Salimans 2016 introduced feature matching and IS, while later Gulrajani 2017 and Miyato 2018 show that discriminator regularization can further improve stability and quality, so ZGAN should combine feature matching with a controlled discriminator objective. [(Miyato et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b1c6f6521da545892f3f5dc39461584d4a27ec0) supports the alpha and epsilon design because virtual adversarial training uses alpha and epsilon as regularization controls on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, finds alpha=1 useful, examines epsilon values 1.0, 2.0, and 3.0, and shows K=1 virtual adversarial perturbations reduce normalized gradient standard deviation relative to random perturbations, although the performance gain is not explained by variance reduction alone. [(Toutouh et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85a38c605b92827d3620135b237d8f2bcd404e2c) supports the planned systematic evaluation by showing that GAN loss-function choice materially affects FID and total variation distance under a fixed 9-hour cloud budget, with 30 independent MNIST runs, a 5 by 9 asynchronous grid evaluating 45 GANs per epoch, and Holm-corrected rank-sum tests at alpha=0.01 and 0.001. [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) supports including LSGAN as a baseline and using Adam with beta1=0.5 in a DCGAN-style TensorFlow implementation, although its LSUN and HWDB1.0 experiments use learning rates 0.001 and 0.0002 rather than the planned 0.0002 for all non-WGAN models. [(Ulyanov et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9350f43816f3866ead2f1b54ad782d15eeed8f8), [(Tran et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bffc86a2c24b5054d09a9f2b4b0ca4720b6ecda5), and [(Srivastava et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81d5740cac256489978c2751e867128e97620eae) support adding explicit generator-side objectives or auxiliary losses because AGE uses Adam lr 0.0002, two generator updates per encoder update, and latent Gaussianity/reconstruction losses on CIFAR-10 and SVHN; Dist-GAN uses two generator updates per iteration on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 and reports FID convergence; and VEEGAN uses a reconstructor to improve mode capture on synthetic mixtures and qualitative CIFAR-10 samples. Weaker support comes from [(Yeh et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a3bf4d403a39ed33f0fa8cf78dc906d6130595f), which validates a DCGAN-style 64x64 architecture with Adam on CelebA, SVHN, and Stanford Cars; [(Liang et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb911796464b1e01e98c2b3d007a4ef9310272e2), which uses adversarial dual objectives for video prediction but provides no quantitative excerpt; [(Acharya et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc4e0bec299f3d9bcb83b9613db914385597766e), which uses IS/FID for progressive video generation; [(Gan et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6172eeb71a33d41dcbbe35cda56c0f041ee68bb8), which supports theoretical analysis of GAN objectives with CelebA and COCO P@10/nDCG evaluations; and [(Cohen, and Giryes, 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6296aa7cab06eaf058f7291040b320b5a83c0091), which supports label conditioning if ZGAN is made class-conditional.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that feature matching alone may not produce visually appealing samples. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) found that feature matching improved the semi-supervised classifier over minibatch discrimination but the generated samples were not visually appealing, whereas minibatch discrimination improved visual quality, with MTurk annotators distinguishing generated from real images in 52.4% of 2000 votes, only slightly above chance, while yielding a worse classifier. This directly challenges a ZGAN generator objective that relies primarily on feature matching unless it is combined with an adversarial or other visual-quality objective. The next strongest contradiction is that existing discriminator regularizers already address the stability and gradient-control motivation, so zephyr loss may be redundant unless compared against them. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) showed spectral normalization is more robust than WGAN-GP across six optimizer settings, especially with aggressive learning rates and momentum where WGAN-GP failed on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, and it achieved strong IS/FID with 100K generator updates, latent dimension 128, and weight clipping constant 0.01 or gradient penalty lambda 10 in comparisons. This newer result partially contradicts the sufficiency of the earlier gradient-penalty approach in [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), which reported WGAN-GP reaching 7.86 ± 0.07 IS on CIFAR-10 and found that Adam with a higher learning rate can improve performance, conflicting with a fixed Adam lr 0.0002 configuration except for WGAN RMSProp. A further contradiction is that the plan's epsilon sweep down to 0.0001 may undermine the claimed gradient stabilization because the zephyr gradient is alpha*a/sqrt(a^2+epsilon) and the maximum curvature or Lipschitz constant of that gradient near a=0 is alpha/sqrt(epsilon), so smaller epsilon makes the gradient change more rapidly; this is relevant because [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) show that Lipschitz or gradient-penalty control is central to stable GAN training. [(Miyato et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b1c6f6521da545892f3f5dc39461584d4a27ec0) also cautions that regularization with alpha and epsilon can reduce gradient variance but does not automatically explain performance gains, since random perturbation training with nine perturbations and optimal alpha still underperformed virtual adversarial training, implying that zephyr's smoothness must be validated by IS/FID/KID rather than gradient statistics alone. Mode coverage is another contradiction: [(Srivastava et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81d5740cac256489978c2751e867128e97620eae) showed vanilla GAN can have high sample quality but collapse to one mode on a 2D ring, while VEEGAN's reconstructor improved mode capture; [(Tran et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bffc86a2c24b5054d09a9f2b4b0ca4720b6ecda5) used two generator updates and reconstruction to improve convergence and variation on CIFAR-10 and STL-10; and [(Toutouh et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85a38c605b92827d3620135b237d8f2bcd404e2c) showed methods without genome diversity could produce mode collapse, with about half of generated MNIST samples in class 3 and no samples in classes 4 and 7, indicating that feature matching alone may not guarantee diversity. The plan's reliance on IS as the primary metric is also weakened by later work emphasizing FID and total variation distance: [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) uses IS and FID, and [(Toutouh et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85a38c605b92827d3620135b237d8f2bcd404e2c) uses FID and TVD with significance tests, suggesting IS alone may miss distributional failures. Finally, the provided related work contains no direct validation of zephyr loss, Diffusion-ZGAN, or the claimed uniqueness of the optimal discriminator, and a regression-style zephyr objective to 1/0 targets is not shown to preserve the adversarial minimax structure that LSGAN and WGAN-GP retain; [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) show that loss-function claims require quantitative comparisons against LSGAN and WGAN-GP, and [(Yeh et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8a3bf4d403a39ed33f0fa8cf78dc906d6130595f), [(Liang et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb911796464b1e01e98c2b3d007a4ef9310272e2), and [(Cohen, and Giryes, 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6296aa7cab06eaf058f7291040b320b5a83c0091) provide only weak or qualitative support for DCGAN-style or conditional GAN components.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add WGAN-GP and spectral normalization as first-class baselines and ablations, and to test zephyr loss both with and without spectral normalization, because [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) show these methods are strong stability controls on CIFAR-10 and STL-10; report IS, FID, training curves, spectral norms, and optimizer sensitivity, including RMSProp for WGAN, Adam at 0.0002, and higher Adam learning rates. The second most important action is to avoid relying on feature matching alone for the generator: combine it with the adversarial objective and, if needed, minibatch discrimination or auxiliary reconstruction/Gaussianity losses, following [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), [(Srivastava et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81d5740cac256489978c2751e867128e97620eae), [(Tran et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bffc86a2c24b5054d09a9f2b4b0ca4720b6ecda5), and [(Ulyanov et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9350f43816f3866ead2f1b54ad782d15eeed8f8), and evaluate visual quality with FID, KID, total variation distance, mode count, class-distribution histograms, and 50k-sample IS as recommended by [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) and [(Toutouh et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85a38c605b92827d3620135b237d8f2bcd404e2c). Third, run systematic alpha and epsilon ablations on CIFAR-10 with at least five seeds, fixed compute budget, batch size 64, Adam lr 0.0002 and beta1=0.5, and report mean and standard deviation plus Holm-corrected rank-sum tests as in [(Toutouh et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85a38c605b92827d3620135b237d8f2bcd404e2c); include gradient norm, gradient variance, and the gradient curvature or Lipschitz constant alpha/sqrt(epsilon) because [(Miyato et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b1c6f6521da545892f3f5dc39461584d4a27ec0) shows epsilon/alpha tuning and gradient variance are important but insufficient alone. Fourth, clarify the exact zephyr discriminator loss for true/fake labels 1 and 0, for example whether the loss is applied to D(x)-1 for real samples and D(G(z))-0 for generated samples, and test label encodings 1/0 versus 1/-1, and verify that the resulting discriminator-generator dynamics preserve adversarial pressure rather than becoming a pure regression task, because related losses such as LSGAN, WGAN-GP, and hinge loss in [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc), [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), and [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) depend on target and penalty design. Fifth, include LSGAN, WGAN, DCGAN, and optionally Dist-GAN or VEEGAN-style auxiliary objectives as baselines, and if class labels are used, condition both generator and discriminator as in [(Cohen, and Giryes, 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6296aa7cab06eaf058f7291040b320b5a83c0091). Sixth, report full reproducibility details: hardware, wall-clock time, number of iterations, sample count for IS/FID/KID, random seeds, and dataset splits, and compare STL-10 at 48x48 for parity with [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) before scaling to 96x96. Finally, defer Diffusion-ZGAN until the image-domain ZGAN is validated against WGAN-GP, spectral normalization, LSGAN, the planned Diffusion-GAN baseline, and feature-matching baselines, and provide theoretical proofs of the optimal discriminator, Lipschitz constant, and distribution-matching behavior in comparison with WGAN and LSGAN.

## Diffusion extension: We will also develop Diffusion-ZGAN by incorporating diffusion processes into the ZGAN framework. This extends the core ZGAN method to a diffusion-based generative model and tests whether the zephyr loss can improve diffusion-GAN training.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Diffusion-ZGAN extension comes from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3ceae3cf605f934cc5f04a44feae23b5252faa), which directly studies a diffusion-GAN by injecting instance-level forward-diffusion noise into both real and generated samples, training the discriminator on noisy samples, and backpropagating through the differentiable diffusion chain. This is the closest existing analogue to the plan's goal of incorporating diffusion processes into a GAN framework, and its theoretical claim that diffusion-noised f-divergence objectives become smoother supports the plan's motivation for improving gradient stability. The paper also reports that Diffusion-GAN built on InsGen improves InsGen on FFHQ 1024x1024 limited splits of 200, 500, 1k, 2k, and 5k images and on AFHQ-Cat, AFHQ-Dog, and AFHQ-Wild at 512x512 with about 5k images, indicating that diffusion-based GAN training can be useful in low-data regimes, although the excerpt lacks numeric FID, IS, KID, confidence intervals, and significance tests. A second strong support is [(Dhariwal, and Nichol, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64ea8f180d0682e6c18d1eb688afdb2027c02794), which provides a concrete diffusion reverse process, classifier-guided stochastic sampling, and DDIM deterministic sampling with 25 steps, and shows state-of-the-art generation on LSUN and ImageNet 64x64/128x128/256x256/512x512. This supports the feasibility of using a diffusion component inside Diffusion-ZGAN and validates the plan's planned FID, sFID, Precision/Recall, and IS evaluation protocol, although the paper does not use a GAN objective, feature matching, or zephyr loss. A third support is [(Xiao et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f73a26f7c2c942d7f1b8267e1307d6f5207613b), which supplies an architectural template for a denoising diffusion GAN using an NCSN++ U-Net, timestep conditioning, and adaptive group normalization controlled by the latent variable; however, the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes, so it supports architecture only. A fourth support is [(Li et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb429a9fa6b40d974688d586eb6468ae2231908), which surveys augmentation for GANs and reports that a denoising diffusion GAN can improve over strong GAN baselines and that augmenting all GAN training processes is more effective than augmenting only one side, supporting the idea that diffusion-based regularization can help GAN training if applied consistently. A fifth support is [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258cc11138aa857186db3d5ff0d202f21e056ebb), which shows that a DDPM-like forward noise process applied to the discriminator input can reduce expected calibration error by about half on DIV2K validation and Urban100 and improve PSNR by 0.4 to 0.7 dB and SSIM by 0.01 to 0.03 in 4x super-resolution, suggesting that diffusion-style discriminator noise can improve calibration and reduce artifacts. Older GAN work provides weaker but still relevant support: [(Hu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e476be61d650be8243d7a55f70e6dbc4bd3a5fa) supports the plan's CIFAR-10 32x32 setting, Adam lr 0.0002 beta1 0.5 beta2 0.999, class-conditional training, and IS/FID evaluation, with VCGAN achieving IS 8.90 +/- 0.16 and FID 16.9 on CIFAR-10; [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) supports the plan's use of feature matching as a generator objective and its adoption of Inception Score computed on 50k generated samples; and [(Prabhu et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f951065c4e66ef949bfe6e58dfd93feea36f3523) provides weak support that alternative GAN objectives can train generators to produce useful synthetic digits, but it does not use diffusion or zephyr loss. Taken iteratively, the line moves from Salimans' feature matching and 50k-sample IS, through Hu's CIFAR-10 FID/IS conditional GAN baselines, to Dhariwal's diffusion models and FID-centric evaluation, and finally to Wang's diffusion-GAN, which is the most direct support for the proposed extension. No related work directly tests the zephyr loss, so the support is for the diffusion-GAN extension and evaluation practices rather than for the specific loss function.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3ceae3cf605f934cc5f04a44feae23b5252faa) already provides a diffusion-based mechanism that smooths f-divergence GAN objectives and improves limited-data GAN training, so the plan's claim that a zephyr loss is needed to improve diffusion-GAN training is not yet differentiated from existing diffusion-GAN smoothing. The paper also warns that individual diffusion steps can produce nearly flat regions and motivates mixing over multiple diffusion steps, whereas the proposed plan does not specify a diffusion schedule, timestep mixing, noise scale, or how zephyr loss interacts with those choices. A second important contradiction is that [(Dhariwal, and Nichol, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64ea8f180d0682e6c18d1eb688afdb2027c02794) treats FID as the default overall metric and uses sFID, Precision/Recall, and IS, which contradicts the plan's choice of IS as the primary metric for image quality and diversity; diffusion models also outperform strong GANs on higher-resolution ImageNet, suggesting that a GAN-centric framing may be less compelling unless Diffusion-ZGAN is explicitly compared against pure diffusion baselines. A third contradiction comes from [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), where feature matching produced generated samples that were not visually appealing even though it improved semi-supervised classification, while minibatch discrimination improved visual realism to the point that MTurk annotators only reached 52.4% accuracy over 2000 votes; this contradicts the sufficiency of the plan's feature-matching generator objective and IS-primary evaluation for visual quality. A fourth contradiction is from [(Li et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb429a9fa6b40d974688d586eb6468ae2231908), which reports that augmenting only real images, only generated images, or only the discriminator does not help, that InstanceNoise alone does not help, and that spatial augmentations are more effective than visual augmentations; if Diffusion-ZGAN merely adds diffusion noise to one side of the GAN or relies on a single noise mechanism, it may not obtain the reported benefits. A fifth contradiction is from [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258cc11138aa857186db3d5ff0d202f21e056ebb), where diffusion-style discriminator augmentation improved calibration and distortion metrics but slightly worsened perceptual metrics, with LPIPS increasing by 0.0003 to 0.001 and DISTS increasing by 0.0004 to 0.006, indicating that diffusion noise can remove GAN-generated details and may harm perceptual quality if not carefully controlled. A weaker contradiction is that [(Prabhu et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f951065c4e66ef949bfe6e58dfd93feea36f3523) shows that a CNN trained only on 140,000 synthetic BGAN samples achieved only 60% to 75% real-test accuracy across five scripts, and that adding about 20% real data substantially improved accuracy, including Gujarati digit 6 from 0.7% to 95%; this suggests that GAN-generated data alone may not fully replace real data, although the paper is not a direct test of Diffusion-ZGAN or zephyr loss. Taken iteratively, newer diffusion-GAN and diffusion-model work weakens the plan's reliance on an IS-primary, feature-matching-only, single-noise-mechanism design, but none of the papers directly tests zephyr loss, so the contradiction is to the sufficiency and novelty of the proposed design rather than to a proven failure.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define Diffusion-ZGAN as a concrete algorithm that explicitly compares zephyr loss against the diffusion-GAN baseline of [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3ceae3cf605f934cc5f04a44feae23b5252faa): specify the forward diffusion schedule, timestep selection or mixture, noise injection on both real and generated samples, backpropagation through the diffusion chain, discriminator zephyr loss L(a)=alpha(sqrt(a^2+epsilon)-sqrt epsilon), feature-matching generator loss, and whether zephyr replaces or augments the diffusion-GAN objective. The theoretical validation should explicitly compare zephyr's smoothness, convexity, and Lipschitz properties with the f-divergence smoothing result in Wang. The second most important action is to align evaluation with the strongest modern evidence: use FID as a primary or co-primary metric, report KID, sFID, Precision/Recall, and IS on 50k generated samples, following [(Dhariwal, and Nichol, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64ea8f180d0682e6c18d1eb688afdb2027c02794) and [(Hu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e476be61d650be8243d7a55f70e6dbc4bd3a5fa), and add human or MTurk evaluation because [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) showed IS can miss visual realism. The third action is to adopt an architecture and training recipe that is known to support diffusion-GAN training: use an NCSN++ U-Net or equivalent, timestep embedding, and adaptive group normalization from the latent variable as in [(Xiao et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f73a26f7c2c942d7f1b8267e1307d6f5207613b), while keeping DCGAN-based baselines for LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN comparisons as planned; also consider auxiliary class-conditional losses or truncation from [(Hu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e476be61d650be8243d7a55f70e6dbc4bd3a5fa) and minibatch discrimination from [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) if visual quality lags. The fourth action is to include augmentation and noise controls: test spatial augmentations such as translation, zoom, and cutout, and ensure augmentation is applied to all GAN training processes rather than only one side, because [(Li et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eb429a9fa6b40d974688d586eb6468ae2231908) reports that InstanceNoise alone and one-sided augmentation do not help. The fifth action is to add limited-data and calibration experiments: evaluate CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN with full and reduced training sets, and consider high-resolution face splits such as FFHQ 200/500/1k/2k/5k or AFHQ 5k if resources allow, because [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c3ceae3cf605f934cc5f04a44feae23b5252faa) shows benefits in limited-data regimes; also monitor expected calibration error and perceptual metrics such as LPIPS or DISTS, following [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258cc11138aa857186db3d5ff0d202f21e056ebb), to detect over-smoothing. The sixth action is to expand hyperparameter ablations beyond alpha and epsilon to include diffusion T, beta schedule, noise scale eta, timestep mixing probabilities, batch size, and generator/discriminator update ratios, using Adam lr 0.0002 beta1 0.5 as a starting point from [(Hu et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e476be61d650be8243d7a55f70e6dbc4bd3a5fa) and [(Prabhu et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f951065c4e66ef949bfe6e58dfd93feea36f3523), and reporting hardware, iterations, and statistical significance; for resource context, [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/258cc11138aa857186db3d5ff0d202f21e056ebb) used 4 NVIDIA GTX A100 GPUs, batch size 16 per GPU for synthetic experiments, Adam lr 1e-4, and 300K iterations. The least important but still useful action is to add a downstream utility check, such as training a classifier on generated samples, because [(Prabhu et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f951065c4e66ef949bfe6e58dfd93feea36f3523) shows synthetic data can support downstream learning but may require real data.

## Theoretical validation: We will provide mathematical proofs demonstrating the convergence properties, uniqueness of optimal discriminator, and relationship to distribution matching for our proposed method. This grounds the empirical plan in theory and supports the claim that zephyr loss addresses GAN optimization limitations.

### Support

The strongest support for the theoretical validation method comes from [(Franceschi et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60f7e41e70f498c49f5c848eaa3f6dd93e88b5e4), which proves that under an infinite-width NTK discriminator, finite empirical training distribution, smooth loss, and alternating optimization, the discriminator problem that is ill-posed in unconstrained L2 becomes well-posed with a unique finite-time L2 solution, while the generator follows a gradient flow of a true loss and the true generator loss is non-increasing, with asymptotic optimality under additional concavity assumptions. This is directly relevant to the plan because it supplies a proof template for the three requested properties: convergence, uniqueness of the optimal discriminator, and relationship to distribution matching, and it already analyzes smooth losses such as IPM and LSGAN that are closer in spirit to the proposed smooth root form L(a)=α(√(a²+ε)-√ε) than vanilla cross-entropy. The next strongest support is the newer [(Birrell, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53dae7b4af66074490adcc15f27a9444673faad4), which extends the theoretical line by decomposing (f,Γ)-GAN error into optimization, discriminator approximation, and statistical estimation terms, deriving high-probability concentration inequalities, mean-error bounds, and almost-sure convergence of the divergence to the population optimum when complexities vanish; this supports the plan's claim that ZGAN can be tied to distribution matching on finite CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN training sets, provided the zephyr loss can be embedded in an (f,Γ) or IPM divergence and the discriminator class Γ has controlled Rademacher complexity. [(Yang, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2de9bf11395067f7de622c416ffc671dbf00d65a) further supports the plan by giving a GAN-as-distribution-estimator framework with Rademacher complexity, covering numbers, chaining, pseudo-dimension, and neural approximation bounds, showing how empirical GAN objectives can converge to population objectives and how source-sample size affects rates, which is useful for justifying the plan's finite-sample training configuration and ablations over α and ε. Earlier work provides weaker but still relevant support: [(Farnia, and Tse, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36209356eee0dbcba8cc253b0991d060875177a1) connects Lipschitz-constrained discriminators to hybrid Jensen-Shannon/Wasserstein divergences and reports that spectral normalization or gradient penalty produced smoother monotonic divergence estimates on CelebA and LSUN-bedroom, using Adam for 200,000 generator iterations with five discriminator updates per generator update and 2,000 validation samples, supporting the plan's emphasis on smoothness, Lipschitz control, and distribution matching, while [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f) supports the use of a Dirac-GAN toy model to test convergence to an analytically known equilibrium, and [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456) is the weakest support because it only shows that a kernel-smoothed GAN objective can be trained on MOG, MNIST, CIFAR-10, and CelebA with kernel bandwidth selection and autoencoder-based feature generation, without quantitative metrics, hardware details, or convergence/uniqueness proofs. Read iteratively, the literature moves from smoothed objectives in 2017, to Lipschitz duality and empirical stability in 2018, to schedule-dependent convergence in 2020, to NTK-based uniqueness and gradient-flow analysis in 2021, and finally to finite-sample and (f,Γ) error bounds in 2022 and 2024, which collectively make the proposed theoretical validation plausible but only if the zephyr loss is placed inside these constrained, finite-sample, alternating-optimization frameworks.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not show that the zephyr loss alone makes the discriminator problem well-posed or unique. [(Franceschi et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60f7e41e70f498c49f5c848eaa3f6dd93e88b5e4) explicitly states that the unconstrained L2 discriminator problem is ill-posed and that uniqueness is obtained only after imposing an NTK/infinite-width discriminator, finite empirical distribution, smooth loss regularity, and alternating optimization; therefore, if the plan's proof assumes that L(a)=α(√(a²+ε)-√ε) by itself guarantees a unique optimal discriminator, that assumption is contradicted by the closest theoretical evidence. A second strong contradiction concerns convergence under the plan's training setup: [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f) shows in a Dirac-GAN WGAN toy model that a fixed five discriminator updates per generator update schedule does not converge, whereas an adaptive update rule with λ=1 reaches the unique equilibrium θ=1 and ψ=0; the proposed plan specifies Adam with learning rate 0.0002, β1=0.5, batch size 64, and WGAN with RMSProp, but it does not specify discriminator-to-generator update ratios, adaptive schedules, or optimizer-specific convergence conditions, so a proof of convergence for ZGAN cannot be inferred from the loss form alone. A third contradiction is that stability and distribution matching in GANs often require explicit Lipschitz control of the discriminator, not merely a smooth loss on the scalar output: [(Farnia, and Tse, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36209356eee0dbcba8cc253b0991d060875177a1) found that a DCGAN with batch normalization kept the Jensen-Shannon estimate near 1 and exhibited mode collapse around the 110,000th of 200,000 generator iterations on LSUN-bedroom, while spectral normalization or gradient penalty gave smoother hybrid Jensen-Shannon/Wasserstein-1 estimates and better samples on CelebA, using Adam for 200,000 generator iterations with five discriminator updates per generator update; this suggests that zephyr loss with α controlling only the scalar loss Lipschitz constant may still fail unless the discriminator architecture is constrained by spectral normalization, gradient penalty, or an equivalent Lipschitz bound. A fourth contradiction comes from finite-sample theory: [(Yang, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2de9bf11395067f7de622c416ffc671dbf00d65a) and [(Birrell, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53dae7b4af66074490adcc15f27a9444673faad4) show that GAN convergence depends on approximation error, statistical estimation error, optimization error, Rademacher complexity, sample sizes n and m, and the richness of the generator and discriminator classes; the plan's α values 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and 1.5 and ε values 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, and 0.0001 may change curvature, Lipschitz constants, and concentration behavior, but the plan does not yet bound these quantities, so the claimed convergence and distribution matching are not established. The weakest contradiction is that [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456) provides no quantitative outcomes, no FID/IS/KID, no statistical significance, and no proof that kernel smoothing yields the same theoretical guarantees as zephyr loss, so it cannot be used as evidence that the proposed loss solves GAN optimization limitations. Additionally, the related work's emphasis on gradient flow and Lipschitz regularization makes the scalar zephyr gradient α a / √(a²+ε) a concern: it is zero at a=0, so if fake samples are trained toward target 0, the loss may still exhibit exact vanishing gradient at the target, partially contradicting the plan's claim that ε prevents vanishing-gradient problems.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize ZGAN in a constrained discriminator class before claiming uniqueness or convergence, using the NTK/infinite-width or Lipschitz-constrained frameworks from [(Franceschi et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60f7e41e70f498c49f5c848eaa3f6dd93e88b5e4) and [(Farnia, and Tse, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36209356eee0dbcba8cc253b0991d060875177a1); specifically, prove that the zephyr loss is smooth, convex, and α-Lipschitz in the scalar argument, then show that the optimal discriminator is unique under spectral normalization, gradient penalty, NTK regularization, or a bounded Lipschitz class, and state the exact assumptions under which the unconstrained problem is ill-posed. The second most important action is to embed the zephyr loss in an (f,Γ) or IPM divergence framework following [(Birrell, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/53dae7b4af66074490adcc15f27a9444673faad4) and [(Yang, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2de9bf11395067f7de622c416ffc671dbf00d65a), deriving an error decomposition into optimization error, discriminator approximation error, generator approximation error, and statistical estimation error, with Rademacher or covering-number bounds that explicitly include α, ε, discriminator width/depth, Lipschitz constant, and sample sizes for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and SVHN. The third action is to make the convergence proof optimizer- and schedule-aware, because [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f) shows that fixed update ratios can fail; the plan should specify the discriminator-to-generator update ratio, whether Adam or RMSProp is used for each baseline, the number of discriminator steps per generator step, and either prove convergence for the chosen schedule or include an adaptive update rule and test it in a Dirac-GAN or two-dimensional toy experiment. The fourth action is to align the theory with the empirical evaluation by proving that the population minimizer corresponds to a meaningful distribution-matching objective, then validate the theory with divergence-estimate curves, FID, KID, IS, and ablations over α and ε, using the same DCGAN architecture, batch size 64, Adam lr 0.0002 and β1=0.5, and WGAN RMSProp baseline described in the plan; this would address the gap in [(Farnia, and Tse, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36209356eee0dbcba8cc253b0991d060875177a1), which relied on qualitative samples and divergence estimates rather than standard metrics. The least important but still useful action is to compare the zephyr loss against smoothed kernel or MMD-style objectives from [(Sinn, and Rawat, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/564f084106ac6d662bf7ca5c5343a3c5997ad456) in a small CIFAR-10 ablation, and to report statistical significance across seeds, because that earlier work shows the CIFAR-10 benchmark is appropriate but does not provide the quantitative evidence needed to distinguish zephyr loss from other smoothing strategies.

## Benchmark evaluation: We will evaluate on CIFAR-10 (32×32), CIFAR-100 (32×32), STL-10 (96×96), and SVHN (32×32) to assess performance across varying image sizes and complexities. This provides the empirical testbeds for validating ZGAN and Diffusion-ZGAN.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000), which standardizes GAN benchmarking on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, and other datasets using a shared ResNet backbone, identical per-dataset training configurations, generator-update steps, IS/FID/KID, three random seeds, and released checkpoints; this directly validates the proposed dataset suite and the inclusion of KID to reduce FID sample-size bias. This is the culmination of an iterative line from [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) introducing IS on CIFAR-10 and SVHN with 50k samples, through [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) using CIFAR-10 IS to compare WGAN-GP and DCGAN, to [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) adding FID, FID-5K, Sliced Wasserstein Distance, GAN-train/GAN-test, and human evaluation on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100. A close second is [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), which evaluates CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 at 32x32 with IS averaged over 10 5k-image splits, FID using 10k real and at least 10k generated images, FID-5K, Sliced Wasserstein Distance, and GAN-train/GAN-test classifier metrics; it reports real-data classifier accuracy of 92.8 percent on CIFAR-10 and 69.4 percent on CIFAR-100, and shows that SNGAN and WGAN-GP with 10M parameters can have similar IS/FID but different classifier-based rankings. [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) supports the proposed CIFAR-10, STL-10, and SVHN coverage by reporting FID on 50,000 generated samples, FID-5ep, and best FID over five runs on a single RTX 2080 Ti for CIFAR-10/SVHN and Quadro RTX 6000 for STL-10, with Adam, batch size 64/128, gradient penalty, and qualitative label-grouped inspection. [(Winter, and Weinshall, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c60a3c8f380d748d22df484bd25bdd6d571e09) supports the exact STL-10 96x96 resolution and CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 32x32 suite, using FID, CAS, and GAN-Train accuracy under fixed InfoGAN-style architectures and equal hyperparameter budgets, although it omits IS and KID. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) supports CIFAR-10 and STL-10 GAN benchmarking with IS and FID, 128-d latent, Adam, 100K generator updates, spectral normalization, and comparisons to WGAN-GP, weight clipping, and normalization baselines, but its STL-10 is 48x48. [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29) supports CIFAR-10 32x32 and STL-10 48x48 with IS/FID, hinge loss, spectral normalization, Adam lr 2e-4, discriminator batch 64, generator batch 128, 50K generator iterations, and reports FID 12.42 on CIFAR-10. [(Tran et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aef8d07d3c229ff60b4bb257f69df47b829eb17b) supports CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 48x48 FID benchmarking with 10K real and 5K generated samples, 300K iterations, and multiple architectures. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) supports the use of IS on CIFAR-10 and SVHN, evaluates 50k samples, and adds MTurk human evaluation with 2000 votes, although it predates FID/KID. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) supports CIFAR-10 IS benchmarking and WGAN-GP versus DCGAN, reporting WGAN-GP ResNet IS 7.86 +/- 0.07 versus DCGAN 5.62. [(Pasini, and Yin, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd8b0558c9d72fa09f849768879777f04599f7d4) supports CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100 IS/FID with fixed architectures, Adam lr 2e-4, 1000 epochs, and V100 scaling, but omits STL-10/SVHN. Older classification work [(Coates et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be9a17321537d9289875fe475b71f4821457b435) supports CIFAR-10 and STL-10 as empirical testbeds, including STL-10 downsampled to 32x32 with 100 labeled examples per class and accuracy 51.5% +/-1.73%, but it does not use generative metrics. [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) supports resolution-controlled benchmarking by comparing 79x79, 151x151, and 299x299 Inception models at near-constant compute, with top-1 75.2/76.4/76.6%, but on ImageNet classification. [(Wang, and Liu, 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fa88943665de1176b0fc6de4ed7469b40cdb08c) supports CIFAR-10 generative comparison against DCGAN qualitatively. [(Ni et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/204857ccc30dc338bbf96769712bf49dfd7c2592) supports CIFAR-10 and SVHN as GAN testbeds in semi-supervised classification with 4,000 CIFAR-10 and 1,000 SVHN labeled images, but not generation metrics. [(Brock et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22aab110058ebbd198edb1f1e7b4f69fb13c0613) supports IS/FID and stability monitoring at large scale, but on JFT-300M with batch 2048 and Inception v2, so less direct.

### Contradictions

The most direct contradiction is that the plan makes IS primary, while newer benchmarking evidence contradicts the older IS-only emphasis introduced by [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea). [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) found that SNGAN and WGAN-GP with 10M parameters had similar IS and FID on CIFAR-100 but different GAN-train/GAN-test scores, and human preference for SNGAN over WGAN-GP was not significant (230/500, p=0.37), indicating IS/FID alone may not rank quality/diversity. [(Winter, and Weinshall, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c60a3c8f380d748d22df484bd25bdd6d571e09) explicitly excludes IS because it does not capture intra-class diversity and uses FID, CAS, and GAN-Train on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and STL-10 96x96. Therefore, using IS as the primary metric for ZGAN/Diffusion-ZGAN is not well supported. Second, the proposed STL-10 96x96 DCGAN benchmark is less established than 48x48 GAN benchmarks. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa), [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29), and [(Tran et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aef8d07d3c229ff60b4bb257f69df47b829eb17b) all use STL-10 at 48x48, with 5K labeled and 100K unlabeled images in Gong, 100K generator updates in Miyato, and 300K iterations in Tran. Only [(Winter, and Weinshall, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c60a3c8f380d748d22df484bd25bdd6d571e09) and possibly [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) align with 96x96, but Watanabe does not provide STL-10 numeric FID in the excerpt. Running DCGAN at 96x96 with batch 64 may be computationally expensive and not comparable to prior SOTA unless compute is matched. Third, the proposed baselines LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN are weaker than the methods used in later benchmarking. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) shows WGAN-GP outperforms DCGAN and Improved GAN on CIFAR-10 IS (7.86 +/-0.07 vs 5.62 and 6.16 +/-0.07), and [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) shows SN-GAN with hinge loss outperforms WGAN-GP under aggressive settings, while [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29) reports AutoGAN FID 12.42 on CIFAR-10 and [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) benchmarks SNGAN, cGAN-PD, SSGAN, and InfoMax-GAN. Comparing ZGAN only against LSGAN/WGAN may overstate gains. Fourth, the fixed DCGAN architecture and Adam beta1=0.5 may be insufficient for harder datasets. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) reports WGAN-GP failed at high learning rates and momentum on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, while SN-GAN remained robust; [(Brock et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22aab110058ebbd198edb1f1e7b4f69fb13c0613) documents spectral explosion and collapse even with regularization at large scale; [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29) uses spectral normalization, hinge loss, and separate generator/discriminator batch sizes. The plan does not include these stabilizers or report singular-value monitoring. Fifth, the plan lacks the statistical protocol now common in benchmarking. [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) averages three seeds; [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) averages IS over 10 5k splits and reports human p-values; [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) uses five evaluation runs and FID-5ep; [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) uses 2000 MTurk votes; [(Coates et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be9a17321537d9289875fe475b71f4821457b435) reports STL-10 accuracy with error margin. The proposed method does not specify seeds, confidence intervals, sample counts, or significance tests. Sixth, FID/KID are sensitive to sample size and Inception features. [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) compares FID with 10k real/10k generated and FID-5K; [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) adds KID to reduce sample-size bias; [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) uses 50k generated samples. The plan does not specify generated/real sample counts, Inception model, or KID sample size. Seventh, SVHN is less directly supported for generative FID/KID benchmarking than CIFAR/STL. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) and [(Ni et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/204857ccc30dc338bbf96769712bf49dfd7c2592) use SVHN for GAN/semi-supervised classification, and [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) names SVHN but does not provide numeric FID in the excerpt. The proposed SVHN evaluation is plausible but under-evidenced for the exact metric suite. Eighth, many strong benchmarks are class-conditional, while the plan does not specify conditional generation. [(Miyato, and Koyama, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab559473a01836e72b9fb9393d6e07c5745528f3) evaluates class-conditional ImageNet with intra-class FID and 5,000 generated samples per class; [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc) uses artificial labels and conditional GAN; [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) finds cGAN-PD generally best because labels help. Unconditional IS/FID on CIFAR-100 may be less informative. Ninth, none of the provided related work directly benchmarks Diffusion-ZGAN or diffusion-GAN hybrids on these datasets, so the diffusion component lacks direct empirical precedent in the supplied literature. Tenth, the plan omits hardware and training budget, whereas related work reports RTX 2080 Ti/Quadro RTX 6000 [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc), V100 [(Pasini, and Yin, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd8b0558c9d72fa09f849768879777f04599f7d4), 50 replicas [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58), and batch 2048 [(Brock et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22aab110058ebbd198edb1f1e7b4f69fb13c0613), making reproducibility and STL-96 feasibility uncertain.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace IS-primary evaluation with a standardized multi-metric protocol: report FID and KID as primary distribution metrics, IS as secondary, and add GAN-train/GAN-test or per-class FID/human evaluation. Use at least 50k generated samples for FID/KID where feasible, 10k real samples, 10 splits for IS, 3+ random seeds, confidence intervals, and report the Inception model. This follows [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000), [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc), and [(Winter, and Weinshall, 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/88c60a3c8f380d748d22df484bd25bdd6d571e09). Second, align STL-10 with prior work by evaluating both 48x48 and 96x96, using the standard 5K labeled/100K unlabeled/8K test split, and reporting compute; if 96x96 is retained, match training iterations and architecture capacity to baselines as in [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29), [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa), and [(Tran et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aef8d07d3c229ff60b4bb257f69df47b829eb17b). Third, strengthen baselines to include WGAN-GP, SN-GAN, SSGAN, InfoMax-GAN, cGAN-PD, and a recent diffusion model, using the same DCGAN/ResNet architecture and training budget, because [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa), [(Gong et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3810f2c401874651afce74fb0a54b309491cc29), and [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) show these are more competitive than LSGAN/WGAN. Fourth, add stability controls: spectral normalization or orthogonal regularization, hinge loss, gradient penalty where appropriate, singular-value monitoring, and training curves, motivated by [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Brock et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22aab110058ebbd198edb1f1e7b4f69fb13c0613). Fifth, specify hardware, batch size, generator updates, wall-clock, and FLOPs, especially for STL-96, using reported resources such as RTX 2080 Ti/Quadro RTX 6000 [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc), V100 [(Pasini, and Yin, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd8b0558c9d72fa09f849768879777f04599f7d4), and 50 replicas [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58). Sixth, clarify conditional generation: evaluate unconditional and class-conditional variants, with per-class FID and label alignment, following [(Miyato, and Koyama, 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab559473a01836e72b9fb9393d6e07c5745528f3) and [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc). Seventh, include SVHN with explicit train/test splits and FID/KID, and if possible human evaluation, supported by [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), [(Ni et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/204857ccc30dc338bbf96769712bf49dfd7c2592), and [(Watanabe, and Favaro, 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3676a4e91bfc6be256320c46785edd705fe379fc). Eighth, for Diffusion-ZGAN, add diffusion-specific baselines and report NFE/sample count/compute, because the supplied related work does not directly cover it. Ninth, on CIFAR-10 ablations for alpha and epsilon, report gradient norms, discriminator confidence, loss curves, and stability metrics, not only final IS/FID/KID, to test the zephyr loss hypothesis.

## Baseline comparison: We will compare ZGAN and Diffusion-ZGAN against established methods including LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN using consistent DCGAN architectures. This isolates the effect of the zephyr loss and diffusion extension against standard GAN baselines.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed baseline comparison comes from [(Mao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d0c0ca6445af0dc2e513c2e3ec5b852a0b5afa), which directly compares LSGAN(-110), LSGAN(011), NS-GAN, and WGAN-GP under one DCGAN-style architecture with four convolutional layers, 100-dimensional noise, batch size 64, Adam learning rate 0.0002 except LSUN-scenes at 0.001, beta1 0.5, N(0,0.02) initialization, pixel scaling to [-1,1], and Tanh generator output, and computes FID from 50,000 generated images every 4,000 iterations; its CIFAR-10 FID values of 35.19 for LSGAN(-110), 35.25 for NS-GAN, 40.83 for WGAN-GP, and 36.46 for LSGAN(011), together with wall-clock times of about 1,100 minutes for LSGAN versus about 4,600 minutes for WGAN-GP, strongly validate the planned use of consistent DCGAN architectures, batch size 64, Adam learning rate 0.0002, beta1 0.5, and FID-based evaluation. A close second is [(Qi, 2017-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b71ab38d3ac6eee0da5e68ef666d33d1028bad8), which also holds the DCGAN architecture fixed while comparing DCGAN, LS-GAN, WGAN, WGAN-GP, and GLS-GAN on CIFAR-10 and tiny ImageNet, reporting test MRE of 0.1506 for DCGAN, 0.1109 for WGAN, and 0.1089 for GLS-GAN on CIFAR-10, thereby supporting the idea that loss or regularization effects can be isolated under a shared convolutional backbone, although it uses MRE rather than the planned IS, FID, or KID. Newer work further supports the fixed-backbone design: [(Wei et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7564221c59886c6411b6fa474852d8012908cbfa) compares DuelGAN against DCGAN, D2GAN, WGAN, DRAGAN, LSGAN, MicroBatchGAN, Dist-GAN, PresGAN, and QSNGAN using the same DCGAN-style generator and discriminator backbone on each dataset and evaluates FID and IS, while [(Bang, and Shim, 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/72a6044a0108e0f8f1e68cd70ada46c81a416324) compares DCGAN, LSGAN, DRAGAN, and WGAN-GP under a common DCGAN-style architecture and reports CIFAR-10 IS values such as 6.5050 for DCGAN, 5.9843 for LSGAN, and 5.7951 for WGAN-GP at gradient penalty 0.1. The DCGAN backbone itself is supported by [(Radford et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8388f1be26329fa45e5807e968a641ce170ea078), which established strided convolutions in the discriminator, fractional-strided convolutions in the generator, batch normalization except at the generator output and discriminator input, ReLU with Tanh output in the generator, and LeakyReLU in the discriminator as a stable all-convolutional design. Evaluation protocol support is provided by [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), which introduced Inception-based scoring and human assessment, recommending 50k samples for IS, and by [(Mordido et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879b14e5f06042e595429955198696d0b239f387), which uses the same architecture for a GAN variant, LSGAN, and DRAGAN in a CIFAR-10 FID comparison with adversarial dropout 0.5 and reports baseline FID values of 79.58, 83.66, and 80.57. [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) also supports the LSGAN baseline by deriving LSGAN objectives and using a DCGAN-style TensorFlow implementation with Adam beta1 0.5 and learning rates 0.001 for scenes and 0.0002 for Chinese characters, although it does not report IS, FID, or KID. Additional methodological support comes from [(Nguyen et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46bc2a73ff36d00357eda4e8051f0b015b63e246), which uses DCGAN-style convolutional models, Adam learning rate 0.0002, beta1 0.5, minibatch size 64, LeakyReLU slope 0.2, and N(0,0.01) initialization for controlled GAN comparisons, and from [(Li et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff83aade985b981fbf2233efbbd749600e97454c), which argues for reporting distributions across 576 grid-search runs rather than single best runs when evaluating adversarial modifications. Finally, [(Tran et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bffc86a2c24b5054d09a9f2b4b0ca4720b6ecda5) and [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) support the planned metric set and reproducibility emphasis, with Tran using FID and qualitative grids on CIFAR-10 and STL-10 and Lee using IS, FID, and KID across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, CelebA, ImageNet, and LSUN-Bedroom with three random seeds, although Lee uses a ResNet backbone rather than DCGAN. Although none of these works directly tests the zephyr loss or Diffusion-ZGAN, they collectively support the planned controlled baseline-comparison design.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the plan uses plain WGAN with RMSProp as the Wasserstein baseline, whereas later work shows that WGAN-GP is often more stable, faster, and more representative of the Wasserstein family. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) shows that WGAN-GP converges faster and reaches a better Inception score than weight-clipping WGAN, and reports a CIFAR-10 ResNet WGAN-GP IS of 7.86 +/- 0.07 versus DCGAN 5.62, Improved GAN 6.16 +/- 0.07, EGAN-Ent-VI 6.86 +/- 0.06, and DFM 7.72 +/- 0.13, suggesting that a plain WGAN baseline may be less stable and less competitive. [(Mao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d0c0ca6445af0dc2e513c2e3ec5b852a0b5afa) further contradicts the assumption that WGAN-family baselines will be strong under the planned DCGAN/FID setup: WGAN-GP obtains CIFAR-10 FID 40.83, worse than LSGAN(-110) 35.19, NS-GAN 35.25, and LSGAN(011) 36.46, and takes about 4,600 minutes versus about 1,100 minutes for LSGAN. [(Qi, 2017-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4b71ab38d3ac6eee0da5e68ef666d33d1028bad8) provides a partial counterpoint that WGAN improves MRE over DCGAN, from 0.1506 to 0.1109 on CIFAR-10, but GLS-GAN is still better at 0.1089, and the metric is MRE rather than the planned IS, FID, or KID. A second major contradiction is that the plan makes IS the primary metric while the literature increasingly treats IS as insufficient or biased and favors FID and KID. [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) explicitly includes KID to reduce bias from small sample sizes and reports IS, FID, and KID under standardized conditions; [(Mordido et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879b14e5f06042e595429955198696d0b239f387) uses FID because it is sensitive to image quality and mode collapse, and reports CIFAR-10 FID baselines of 79.58 for a GAN variant, 83.66 for LSGAN, and 80.57 for DRAGAN; [(Bang, and Shim, 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/72a6044a0108e0f8f1e68cd70ada46c81a416324) notes that IS alone does not capture diversity and adds MS-SSIM on CelebA; and [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) shows that IS should be evaluated on 50k samples and correlates with human judgment, but also reports MTurk distinguishability of 52.4% over 2000 votes, indicating that automatic IS and human perception can diverge. A third contradiction is that a consistent DCGAN architecture may not be the best or fairest backbone for all planned datasets, especially CIFAR-100, STL-10 at 96x96, and SVHN. [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000) uses a Residual Network backbone and standardized hyperparameters per dataset across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, CelebA, ImageNet, and LSUN-Bedroom, indicating that modern controlled comparisons often move beyond DCGAN; [(Wei et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7564221c59886c6411b6fa474852d8012908cbfa) uses shallow DCGAN-style versions for MNIST and FashionMNIST and deeper versions for CelebA and VGGFace2, suggesting dataset-dependent depth is common; and [(Radford et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8388f1be26329fa45e5807e968a641ce170ea078) warns that batch normalization placement is critical, excluding it from the generator output and discriminator input, which may interact with the zephyr loss in ways not tested by the original DCGAN. A fourth contradiction is the absence of a well-established Diffusion-GAN baseline in the related work. None of the cited GAN baseline studies directly evaluates Diffusion-GAN under the same DCGAN architecture, and the closest diffusion-related comparisons are indirect, such as DFM in [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) and [(Mordido et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879b14e5f06042e595429955198696d0b239f387), so the planned Diffusion-ZGAN comparison risks confounding the diffusion extension with architecture, training budget, or metric sensitivity. A further limitation is that none of the cited works directly tests the zephyr loss, so the planned isolation of the zephyr effect is not directly validated by prior evidence. Finally, several studies report limited reproducibility details that the proposed plan must avoid: [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) gives LSGAN hyperparameters such as Adam beta1 0.5 and learning rates 0.001 for scenes and 0.0002 for Chinese characters but no FID or IS values; [(Tran et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bffc86a2c24b5054d09a9f2b4b0ca4720b6ecda5) reports FID convergence but not exact baseline values in the excerpt; [(Wei et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7564221c59886c6411b6fa474852d8012908cbfa) does not specify batch size, optimizer, learning rate, training steps, hardware, or random seeds; and [(Nguyen et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/46bc2a73ff36d00357eda4e8051f0b015b63e246) reports synthetic stability but no numerical real-image outcomes in the excerpt.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make FID and KID co-primary metrics rather than treating IS as the primary metric, because the strongest recent evidence favors FID for quality and mode collapse and KID for reduced small-sample bias; the plan should compute IS and FID on 50k generated samples as recommended by [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), use KID with multiple seeds and confidence intervals as in [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000), and report mean, standard deviation, and significance tests across at least three random seeds. The next most important action is to add WGAN-GP as a baseline alongside plain WGAN, and if plain WGAN is retained, implement it with the same DCGAN architecture, RMSProp, weight clipping, and identical training budget while reporting training curves, because [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) shows WGAN-GP is more stable and stronger than weight-clipping WGAN and [(Mao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d0c0ca6445af0dc2e513c2e3ec5b852a0b5afa) shows WGAN-GP can be slower and weaker than LSGAN under a DCGAN/FID protocol. Third, define the Diffusion-GAN baseline explicitly as the same DCGAN architecture with the same data splits, batch size, optimizer, learning rate, training iterations, and evaluation metrics, and add an ablation that separates the diffusion extension from the zephyr loss, because no cited work directly validates Diffusion-GAN under the planned controlled DCGAN comparison. Fourth, use a dataset-appropriate but consistent backbone: keep DCGAN for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and SVHN if feasible, but for STL-10 at 96x96 use a deeper DCGAN or ResNet backbone applied identically to all methods, following the dataset-dependent depth practice in [(Wei et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7564221c59886c6411b6fa474852d8012908cbfa) and the standardized ResNet protocol in [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000). Fifth, conduct the alpha and epsilon ablations on CIFAR-10 using validation FID or KID rather than IS alone, report the full distribution of results across many seeds and hyperparameter settings as advocated by [(Li et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff83aade985b981fbf2233efbbd749600e97454c), and include training curves, mode-coverage checks, and qualitative grids to detect instability or mode collapse. Finally, report full reproducibility details, including hardware, wall-clock training time, batch size, optimizer, learning rate, beta1, weight initialization, pixel scaling, checkpoint selection, code, and pretrained checkpoints, following the detailed protocol in [(Mao et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4d0c0ca6445af0dc2e513c2e3ec5b852a0b5afa) and the standardized model-zoo approach in [(Lee, and Town, 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5c3be4be479dcbbb1d28e75c862afe46a8ce000).

## Hyperparameter optimization: We will systematically explore:
- α values: 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and 1.5 for both discriminator and generator
- ε values: 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, and 0.0001 to determine optimal regularization. This tests the flexible gradient-control parameters that are central to the proposed loss.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed alpha/epsilon hyperparameter optimization comes from [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), which shows that adding a gradient-penalty regularization to the WGAN discriminator improves convergence and Inception score relative to weight clipping, with WGAN-GP achieving 7.86 +/- 0.07 unsupervised CIFAR-10 IS versus DFM 7.72 +/- 0.13, EGAN-Ent-VI 7.07 +/- 0.10, Improved GAN 6.16 +/- 0.07, and DCGAN 5.62, and that changing optimizer and learning rate further improves performance. This directly supports the plan's premise that gradient-control terms in a GAN objective can stabilize training and that systematic tuning of such terms is worthwhile, even though the paper tunes a gradient-penalty coefficient and optimizer rather than zephyr alpha/epsilon. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d), which performs a small-scale search over discriminator objective coefficients lambda_h, lambda_c, and lambda_s and finds lambda_h=4, lambda_c=3, and lambda_s=5 to produce strong linear-probing and clustering features on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-10, using AdamW with learning rate 2e-4, weight decay 0.1 on the discriminator, batch size 500 for CIFAR-10/CIFAR-100, 320 for ImageNet-10, one discriminator update per generator update, and 500 to 1000 epochs. This supports the proposed method's use of a bounded grid search over regularization/scaling coefficients in a GAN objective, but the evidence is indirect because the paper evaluates representation quality with linear SVM and K-Means rather than the plan's IS, FID, and KID generation metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work directly validates the zephyr loss L(a)=alpha*(sqrt(a^2+epsilon)-sqrt(epsilon)) or the specific alpha grid 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and 1.5 and epsilon grid 0.1, 0.01, 0.001, and 0.0001, so the proposed hyperparameter optimization lacks direct empirical support for its central loss-specific parameters. A second, more concrete concern is that [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d) found effective objective coefficients lambda_h=4, lambda_c=3, and lambda_s=5, which are larger than the proposed alpha range, suggesting that if alpha acts as a loss or gradient scaling factor, the planned maximum alpha=1.5 may be too restrictive and that a wider or log-spaced alpha search is needed. A third concern is configuration dependence: [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d) uses AdamW with constant learning rate 2e-4, discriminator weight decay 0.1, batch sizes 500 or 320, one discriminator update per generator update, and 500 to 1000 epochs, whereas the plan uses Adam with learning rate 0.0002 and beta1=0.5, batch size 64, no stated weight decay, and unspecified discriminator-to-generator update ratio, so the optimal alpha/epsilon values may not transfer across these settings. A weaker concern is that [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) shows WGAN-GP performance depends on optimizer, learning rate, architecture, and gradient-penalty details, and that DCGAN can be faster in wall-clock time but less stable, which contradicts any assumption that a single fixed DCGAN/Adam configuration will isolate the benefit of zephyr alpha/epsilon without additional controls. The weakest contradiction is metric mismatch: [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) reports Inception score but not FID or KID, and [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d) reports linear SVM and K-Means accuracy rather than generation metrics, so neither directly supports the plan's reliance on IS as primary and FID/KID as distributional checks.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to broaden and restructure the alpha/epsilon search around the evidence from [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4): include larger alpha values such as 2, 3, 5, and 10, test alpha_D and alpha_G separately, and use a log-spaced epsilon grid that may extend below 0.0001 if gradients near a=0 remain unstable. The next most important action is to run a CIFAR-10 ablation with a fixed DCGAN architecture, multiple seeds, and explicit reporting of IS, FID, KID, discriminator and generator loss variance, gradient norms, and training stability, comparing zephyr against LSGAN, WGAN, WGAN-GP, and DCGAN under the same optimizer and batch-size protocol. Then, because the related work shows that optimal coefficients depend on optimizer and batch size, the plan should test at least two training configurations: the proposed Adam lr 0.0002, beta1=0.5, batch size 64, and a configuration closer to [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d) using AdamW lr 2e-4, discriminator weight decay 0.1, batch size 320 or 500, one discriminator update per generator update, and 500 to 1000 epochs. A further useful action is to add auxiliary discriminator-feature evaluations such as linear probing and K-Means clustering, as in [(Zhang, and Maire, 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d2861d6c3946cbdc2493a2a17100e3779c6f774d), to verify that epsilon and alpha improve gradient informativeness even when IS is noisy. Finally, the paper should report exact hardware, batch size, learning rates, beta values, weight decay, discriminator-to-generator update ratios, gradient-penalty or zephyr coefficients, seeds, and statistical significance, because the provided related-work excerpts lack some of these details and reproducibility is a key limitation.

## Evaluation metrics: We will assess model performance using:
- Inception Score (IS) as the primary metric for image quality and diversity
- Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) for measuring similarity between generated and real image distributions
- Kernel Inception Distance (KID) for additional distribution comparison
- Qualitative visual assessment of generated samples. This defines how the generated image quality and distributional fidelity will be measured.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253be933f46f98f486472d7a9207f95985153112), which directly benchmarks GAN discriminator losses and regularizers on DCGAN, SNGAN, and StyleGAN2 using FID as the main limited-data metric, KID for very small datasets, IS in an SNGAN ablation, and qualitative inspection; its SNGAN ablation on FFHQ-2.5K reports FID 45.31 and IS 4.32 versus FID 46.14 and IS 4.37 with a regularizer, and its RTX 4090 measurement of negligible overhead with lambda 10 and coarse-graining 2 shows that this metric suite can be used to compare loss-function choices. [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea) provides the foundational IS definition and the 50k-sample recommendation, with MTurk discrimination of 52.4% versus 50% random, supporting IS as a quality and diversity metric. 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d609fd41790fc7940228f193d783ba780b12d4b) with IS/FID on CIFAR/FFHQ/AFHQ, [(Han et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d181656b2e40f17c34b04d6ee69a3015e2e56479) with 30k real/10k fake rarity, and [(Carvalho, and Ponti, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a05adb57262cd0e3dfb580b3911c3baf6cb67b22) with dendrogram distance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) shows IS and FID can fail to separate quality from diversity: on CIFAR-10 corruption, FID was 27.1 between training and sigma=5 noisy training versus 29.6 between training and a random 5,000-image noisy subset, while GAN-test remained 95% and GAN-train dropped from 91% to 80%; on CIFAR-100, SNGAN and WGAN-GP 10M had similar IS and FID but different GAN-train/GAN-test accuracies, and the human study found SNGAN preferred over DCGAN in 368/500 trials with p < 10^-7 but not over WGAN-GP 2.5M or 10M at 274/500 p=0.28 and 230/500 p=0.37. This directly contradicts treating IS as the primary metric and FID/KID/visual inspection as sufficient for comparing ZGAN, LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN. Next, [(Tibebu et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b6c0a431cb0ec2fb6477bade5dfcd4efc63719fc) reports IS/FID disagreement on CUB, with IS 1.94 versus 2.88 but FID 74.12 versus 68.79, and notes that IS and FID can be affected by mode collapse and limited caption diversity, undermining IS as a sole primary metric. [(Li et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff83aade985b981fbf2233efbbd749600e97454c) shows an inception-score-like ICP metric can be unstable across 576 architecture/hyperparameter runs, with ground-truth GMM ICP 4.977 ± 0.016, ALICE above 4.5 in 77% of runs, and ALI showing large run-to-run variation, so single-run IS can mislead. [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253be933f46f98f486472d7a9207f95985153112) shows metric trade-offs, with a regularizer worsening SNGAN FID from 45.31 to 46.14 while improving IS from 4.32 to 4.37, and it favors KID only for very small datasets, so the proposed fixed metric hierarchy may not transfer across losses and data scales. [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) warns that Inception-style feature extraction is resolution-sensitive, with constant-compute top-1 accuracy of 75.2 at 79x79, 76.4 at 151x151, and 76.6 at 299x299, so resizing 32x32 CIFAR/SVHN and 96x96 STL-10 to Inception inputs may bias IS/FID/KID. Weaker contradictions or limitations include [(Isola et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8acbe90d5b852dadea7810345451a99608ee54c7), where human real/fake fooled rates were task-dependent and not always significant, with 18.9% for map-to-aerial, 6.1% for photo-to-map not significantly different from L1, and 22.5% for colorization; [(Tulyakov et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e76edb86f270c3a77ed9f5a1e1b305461f36f96f), which reports IS 12.42 versus 11.85 and user-study preferences without confidence intervals; [(Radford et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8388f1be26329fa45e5807e968a641ce170ea078), which evaluates distribution fidelity with a nearest-neighbor classifier rather than IS/FID/KID; and [(Choi et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/302207c149bdf7beb6e46e4d4afbd2fa9ac02c64), [(Kim et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d57622db429112152ee726bbee80ddd567c342bc), [(Zhang et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2d600c5df637c4f1b1460802d78144d028566a), [(Kim et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d609fd41790fc7940228f193d783ba780b12d4b), [(Han et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d181656b2e40f17c34b04d6ee69a3015e2e56479), and [(Carvalho, and Ponti, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a05adb57262cd0e3dfb580b3911c3baf6cb67b22), which do not validate the full IS/FID/KID suite or provide sufficient quantitative evidence for the proposed CIFAR/STL/SVHN benchmarks.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add complementary metrics beyond IS/FID/KID/visual, especially GAN-train/GAN-test classifier accuracy, precision/recall, sliced Wasserstein distance, rarity or dendrogram distance, and a blinded human pairwise study, because [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) shows IS/FID can miss quality-diversity trade-offs and mode dropping, and [(Han et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d181656b2e40f17c34b04d6ee69a3015e2e56479) and [(Carvalho, and Ponti, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a05adb57262cd0e3dfb580b3911c3baf6cb67b22) provide out-of-manifold and hierarchical-structure diagnostics. Second, specify the exact evaluation protocol: generate at least 50k samples for IS per [(Salimans et al., 2016-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/571b0750085ae3d939525e62af510ee2cee9d5ea), use 10 splits of 5k for IS and at least 10k generated images or 5k/5k for FID/KID per [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), report mean and standard deviation over multiple random seeds, bootstrap confidence intervals, and significance tests, as in [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) and [(Isola et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8acbe90d5b852dadea7810345451a99608ee54c7). Third, define Inception preprocessing for 32x32 and 96x96 images, test sensitivity to resizing and center-cropping, and consider fine-tuned or dataset-specific Inception features, motivated by [(Szegedy et al., 2015-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23ffaa0fe06eae05817f527a47ac3291077f9e58) and [(Kim, and Ha, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d79e7bc943c1e0bed999fbb249e4529a2fb1de9). Fourth, do not rank ZGAN solely by IS; use FID/KID for distribution fidelity, treat IS as secondary, and add corruption/noise, sample-size, and alpha/epsilon ablation runs to detect metric insensitivity and training instability, following [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115), [(Tibebu et al., 2022-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b6c0a431cb0ec2fb6477bade5dfcd4efc63719fc), and [(Li et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff83aade985b981fbf2233efbbd749600e97454c). Fifth, standardize qualitative assessment with blinded pairwise comparisons, a rubric, sample size, and significance, informed by [(Isola et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8acbe90d5b852dadea7810345451a99608ee54c7), [(Tulyakov et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e76edb86f270c3a77ed9f5a1e1b305461f36f96f), and [(Choi et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/302207c149bdf7beb6e46e4d4afbd2fa9ac02c64). Finally, report training details such as hardware, epochs, EMA, gradient penalty, and WGAN/RMSProp settings, align with [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253be933f46f98f486472d7a9207f95985153112) by testing loss/regularization interactions, and for Diffusion-ZGAN compare against Diffusion-GAN with identical sample counts while reporting FID/KID/IS plus qualitative samples.

## Ablation studies: We will conduct detailed ablation studies on CIFAR-10 to understand the individual impact of ε and α parameters on model performance. This isolates the contribution of the regularization and scaling parameters within the zephyr loss.

### Support

The strongest support is that the field has iterated from generic component ablations to controlled CIFAR-10 GAN regularization ablations, and the most directly relevant evidence is that coefficient sweeps on CIFAR-10 can isolate the effect of a regularization or scaling term. [(Zhang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63470afe06145e08c3b851491450f68c83cc938f) swept λ over 0.1, 1, 10, and 100 in SNDCGAN and ResNet GANs on CIFAR-10 with Adam, batch size 64, 200K generator updates, three random seeds, and FID distributions, reporting best FID 14.56 and showing that regularization coefficient effects can be measured with the same batch-size regime as the proposed plan. [(Adler, and Lunz, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143b0fb106d11265f2af62a29d06ef74d2935124) directly studied a regularization constant λ and scaling factor γ in a Banach Wasserstein GAN on CIFAR-10 64x64 with batch size 64, Adam learning rate decaying from 2e-4 to 0 over 100K iterations, and 5 discriminator updates per generator update, reporting Inception Score axis around 16 to 20 and heuristic values λ≈27 and γ≈27, which supports the relevance of scaling factors in GAN losses. [(Zhou et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4e657bf884097a2b88d56aef1a8733379bfb63) supports the one-factor ablation strategy on CIFAR-10 by holding the GAN architecture fixed while changing a loss term or conditioning strategy, with Inception Score dropping from 7.41 to 6.48 and AM Score increasing from 0.17 to 0.43 when an entropy loss was added. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) supports the need to compare a new discriminator loss under fixed architecture and optimizer, showing WGAN-GP with gradient penalty reached Inception Score 7.86±0.07 on CIFAR-10 versus DCGAN 5.62 and that optimizer choice affected performance. Later work strengthens this line by showing that regularization ablations must account for optimizer and batch-size sensitivity: [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) found spectral normalization more robust than WGAN-GP to aggressive Adam learning rates and momentum on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, and [(Salimans et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69902406e7d08f8865f02185699978db499d25e7) found batch size 200 unstable while batch size 8000 gave Inception Score 8.47±0.12 on CIFAR-10, with optimal-transport matching much better than random matching. Weaker but still relevant precedents include [(Le, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/384ce792cf2b2afbe001f2168bfe7d5e7804c736) for CNN component ablations on TinyImageNet, [(Radford et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8388f1be26329fa45e5807e968a641ce170ea078) for a MNIST GAN batch-normalization ablation, [(Brock et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/22aab110058ebbd198edb1f1e7b4f69fb13c0613) for large-scale GAN component ablation, [(Osahor et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/006cc434de566be21fe39a26d85e1efa6866f93c) for loss-weight ablation in a face sketch GAN, and [(Keswani et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4ce6ec7ab9073bfcacb9ef96219a9603149603a) for CIFAR-10 GAN training with an epsilon stationarity threshold, although none directly tests the zephyr loss parameters.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method as written is that related CIFAR-10 GAN ablations show regularization and scaling effects are coupled to optimizer, batch size, and architecture, so a single fixed DCGAN/Adam setup may not isolate ε and α. [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) found that WGAN-GP failed under high learning-rate and high-momentum settings while spectral normalization remained more robust on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, implying that the proposed Adam learning rate 0.0002 and β1=0.5 could change the apparent effect of ε or α. [(Zhang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63470afe06145e08c3b851491450f68c83cc938f) similarly found regularization coefficient effects depended on loss function and architecture, with some methods producing FID above 100 while consistency regularization achieved FID 14.56 on CIFAR-10, and the study used only three seeds, indicating that single-run ablations can be misleading. [(Salimans et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69902406e7d08f8865f02185699978db499d25e7) contradicts the assumption that the planned batch size 64 is automatically stable, because batch size 200 was unstable and batch size 8000 was needed for stable high Inception Score on CIFAR-10. A second contradiction is that the planned metric suite may be insufficient: [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) showed FID alone can fail to separate diversity from quality on CIFAR-10, with FID 27.1 for Gaussian noise sigma=5 and 29.6 for a random 5k subset while GAN-test remained 95% but GAN-train dropped from 91% to 80%, so Inception Score as primary and FID/KID as secondary may miss mode collapse or diversity loss caused by ε or α. A weaker but important contradiction to the planned grid is that scaling factors in related CIFAR-10 GAN losses can be much larger than the proposed α range: [(Adler, and Lunz, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143b0fb106d11265f2af62a29d06ef74d2935124) used γ≈27, suggesting that α values above 1.5 might be needed, although the loss is different. Older ablations also contradict the sufficiency of simple point estimates: [(Zhou et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4e657bf884097a2b88d56aef1a8733379bfb63) reported Inception Score and AM Score changes without statistical significance tests, [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) reported Inception Score 7.86±0.07 but omitted exact gradient-penalty coefficient and hardware details, and [(Le, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/384ce792cf2b2afbe001f2168bfe7d5e7804c736) showed that architectural ablations can be confounded by activation and dataset screening without confidence intervals. Finally, parameter-role ambiguity weakens direct transfer: [(Keswani et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4ce6ec7ab9073bfcacb9ef96219a9603149603a) uses ε as a discriminator stationarity threshold rather than a zephyr-loss regularizer and reports no quantitative CIFAR-10 metrics, while [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) uses alpha for Adam learning rate, so neither result directly validates the proposed ε/α interpretation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the ablation a factorial CIFAR-10 experiment that treats ε, discriminator α, and generator α as separate factors, with at least three to five random seeds per combination and reported mean, standard deviation, confidence intervals, or bootstrap intervals for FID, KID, and Inception Score, following the multi-seed FID-distribution practice in [(Zhang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63470afe06145e08c3b851491450f68c83cc938f) and addressing the missing significance in [(Zhou et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4e657bf884097a2b88d56aef1a8733379bfb63) and [(Le, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/384ce792cf2b2afbe001f2168bfe7d5e7804c736). Second, because regularization and scaling effects are optimizer-sensitive, the plan should keep the DCGAN architecture fixed but test the ε/α grid under the stated Adam learning rate 0.0002 and β1=0.5 plus at least one alternative optimizer setting, such as β1=0.9 or a small learning-rate sweep, inspired by [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4); this will show whether the zephyr loss provides flexible gradient control rather than merely benefiting from a particular optimizer. Third, the evaluation should not rely on Inception Score as the primary metric; report FID and KID with fixed sample sizes, and add diversity/quality diagnostics such as GAN-train/GAN-test, nearest-neighbor coverage, or mode-count checks, because [(Shmelkov et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8b1a1949789d12997b0a5ade953309308a81e115) showed FID alone can conflate diversity and quality on CIFAR-10. Fourth, monitor training stability and mode collapse by logging discriminator/generator losses, gradient norms, singular values or spectral norms, and sample grids over training, as in [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and [(Salimans et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69902406e7d08f8865f02185699978db499d25e7); if batch size 64 is unstable, include a batch-size sensitivity check or use larger batches/multi-GPU training, noting that [(Salimans et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69902406e7d08f8865f02185699978db499d25e7) found batch size 200 unstable and batch size 8000 stable on CIFAR-10. Fifth, broaden the α grid beyond 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 1, and 1.5 if compute permits, because [(Adler, and Lunz, 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/143b0fb106d11265f2af62a29d06ef74d2935124) found scaling factors around 27 relevant in a different CIFAR-10 GAN loss, while keeping ε on a log scale from 0.1 to 0.0001 and including a near-zero ε control to test the regularization hypothesis. Finally, document all implementation details needed for reproducibility: random seeds, number of generator/discriminator updates, checkpoint selection, FID/KID sample counts, hardware, exact loss values, and baseline comparisons to LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN under the same DCGAN architecture, following the gaps noted in [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), [(Zhou et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d4e657bf884097a2b88d56aef1a8733379bfb63), and [(Keswani et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f4ce6ec7ab9073bfcacb9ef96219a9603149603a), and, if Diffusion-ZGAN is in scope, repeat the same ablation on the diffusion variant to ensure the ε/α effects are not specific to the plain GAN.

## Training configuration: All models will use consistent training parameters (batch size 64, Adam optimizer with learning rate 0.0002 and β₁ = 0.5, except WGAN which will use RMSProp), with true/fake labels set to 1 and 0 respectively. This standardizes the experimental setup so that performance differences can be attributed to the proposed loss and model variants.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed standardized training configuration comes from [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f), which explicitly uses CIFAR-10 WGAN/WGAN-GP with batch size 64, 32x32 images, Adam beta1=0.5 for WGAN-GP, and RMSProp for WGAN, matching the proposed batch size 64, Adam beta1=0.5, and WGAN RMSProp exception. This is directly relevant because the research plan seeks to compare ZGAN and Diffusion-ZGAN against DCGAN/WGAN baselines on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 while attributing differences to the zephyr loss rather than optimizer drift. A second strong support is [(Saeed, and Hashim, 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cee10c47803ce47e881f75cb3ff40a6df027e65d), which evaluates a DCGAN on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 with a hard-label baseline using true/fake labels 1 and 0, Adam beta1=0.5 and beta2=0.999, and FID/IS evaluation, supporting the proposed hard-label convention and the use of FID/IS on the same datasets. Next, [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) supports the LSGAN baseline and the Adam beta1=0.5 choice, and it uses learning rate 0.0002 for a handwritten Chinese character DCGAN-style setup, although it does not confirm batch size 64 or label values. [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) supports the WGAN/RMSProp exception and the use of Inception Score on CIFAR-10, and it shows WGAN-GP can be trained with RMSProp or Adam, but it does not specify the proposed batch size, beta values, or hard labels. The foundational support is [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8), which validates Adam's bias-corrected exponential moving averages and shows that beta values and bias correction affect convergence, making the proposed Adam beta1=0.5 choice plausible, although the paper does not test GAN training or the exact proposed learning rate. Weaker support comes from [(Barua et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b14d69543ba8fc1f68070b84197886af9366762), which supports DCGAN/WGAN baselines and IS/FID evaluation on MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, and ImageNet, but does not specify the optimizer, batch size, learning rate, beta1, or label convention. The weakest support is [(Drygala et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff202d33d1037a8fbd5c09a7a101bb4e94c2aeb4), which shows GAN training can be standardized in a domain-specific turbulence setting but uses batch size 10, Adam learning rate 2e-4, beta1=0.9, and physics-based metrics rather than the proposed IS/FID/KID protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Saeed, and Hashim, 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cee10c47803ce47e881f75cb3ff40a6df027e65d), which reports that a modified DCGAN with label smoothing and discriminator noise outperforms a hard-label DCGAN benchmark on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, with best FID 95.52 and IS 64.27 on CIFAR-10, suggesting that fixed true/fake labels 1 and 0 may be less stable or less effective than smoothed labels for DCGAN-style training. This directly challenges the proposed method's decision to set true/fake labels to 1 and 0 for all models, because the research plan wants performance differences to be attributed to zephyr loss, but label convention itself may confound results. The next contradiction is from [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f), which uses lower learning rates than the proposed fixed Adam learning rate 0.0002, specifically Adam 0.0001 for WGAN-GP and RMSProp 0.00005 for WGAN, and shows that update-frequency ratio is critical, with the best CIFAR-10 adaptive setting updating the generator three to four times more often than the discriminator rather than the fixed discriminator-heavy schedule. This contradicts the assumption that a single standardized learning rate and unspecified update ratio are sufficient to isolate the zephyr loss. A weaker contradiction is from [(Drygala et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff202d33d1037a8fbd5c09a7a101bb4e94c2aeb4), which uses Adam beta1=0.9, learning rate 2e-4, and batch size 10 for a conditional GAN, indicating that beta1=0.5 and batch size 64 are not universally optimal and that task-specific tuning may be necessary, although the domain is turbulence images rather than CIFAR/STL/SVHN. Another weaker contradiction is from [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8), which shows Adam's performance depends on beta2, bias correction, and learning-rate schedule, and that Adam only marginally improved over SGD with momentum on CIFAR-10 CNNs, so the proposed fixed Adam setting may be suboptimal if beta2 is not specified or if a non-Adam optimizer would be better for some baselines. The weakest apparent contradiction is from [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4), which reports that WGAN-GP with Adam and a higher learning rate improves performance, implying that the proposed fixed Adam learning rate 0.0002 may be too low for WGAN-GP-like variants, but this is less direct because the proposed plan uses RMSProp for WGAN and does not explicitly include WGAN-GP.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to complete the optimizer and update schedule so that the standardized configuration is actually reproducible: specify Adam beta2 (for example 0.999 or 0.9999) and confirm bias correction, specify the RMSProp learning rate for WGAN, and define the discriminator-to-generator update ratio or test 1:1, 5:1, and adaptive ratios, because [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f) shows update frequency can dominate WGAN/WGAN-GP performance and [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8) shows beta2 and bias correction affect Adam stability. The next most important action is to treat the hard-label choice as an ablation rather than a fixed assumption, because [(Saeed, and Hashim, 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cee10c47803ce47e881f75cb3ff40a6df027e65d) finds label smoothing/noise improves DCGAN on CIFAR-10/100; the plan should either justify labels 1 and 0 for zephyr loss or include a 0.9/0.1 smoothing variant while keeping all other parameters fixed. A third action is to validate the fixed learning rate 0.0002 against the lower rates used in WGAN/WGAN-GP work, such as Adam 0.0001 and RMSProp 0.00005 in [(Ouyang, and Agam, 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0195f44de098e1bfba8a490cc4005b7f15fcc0f), and to test separate discriminator/generator learning rates such as 0.0003/0.0001 in [(Saeed, and Hashim, 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cee10c47803ce47e881f75cb3ff40a6df027e65d), especially because the plan compares LSGAN, WGAN, and Diffusion-GAN with different loss sensitivities. A fourth action is to add WGAN-GP or a gradient-penalty variant as a baseline or ablation, since [(Gulrajani et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edf73ab12595c6709f646f542a0d2b33eb20a3f4) shows WGAN-GP is more stable than weight clipping and can outperform DCGAN on CIFAR-10 Inception Score, which would strengthen the claim that ZGAN improves over the strongest WGAN variant rather than a weaker clipped WGAN. A fifth action is to implement LSGAN using the objective and Adam beta1=0.5 practice from [(Mao et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74ff6d48f9c62e937023106629d27ef2d2ddf8bc) while forcing the proposed batch size 64, learning rate 0.0002, and hard labels, so that the LSGAN baseline is comparable to ZGAN and Diffusion-ZGAN. A sixth action is to report full reproducibility details, including epochs or iterations, random seeds, initialization, batch normalization settings, hardware, and metric sample sizes for IS, FID, and KID, because several related studies omit these details and the plan needs to attribute differences to zephyr loss. A seventh action is to include mode-collapse and discriminator-confidence diagnostics, such as loss curves, generator/discriminator loss oscillation, and sample diversity, because [(Barua et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b14d69543ba8fc1f68070b84197886af9366762) shows DCGAN can collapse when batch normalization is removed and [(Saeed, and Hashim, 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cee10c47803ce47e881f75cb3ff40a6df027e65d) targets discriminator overconfidence. The least important but still useful action is to test whether the standardized batch size 64 and beta1=0.5 remain optimal on higher-resolution or more complex datasets such as STL-10 and SVHN, since [(Drygala et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff202d33d1037a8fbd5c09a7a101bb4e94c2aeb4) shows task-specific hyperparameters can differ, but this should be a sensitivity analysis rather than a departure from the main standardized comparison.
