## We will develop Ukiyo-e-Seq, an integrated approach to generate paired optical and transcriptomic data from individual, uncultured eukaryotic plankton: central proposed system that directly addresses the optical-genomics dichotomy by linking organismal appearance to transcriptomic function.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c), which demonstrates an adapted MASC-seq workflow for single-cell transcriptomic analysis of microbial eukaryotic plankton by immobilizing individual cells on microarray slides, fixing and permeabilizing them, performing in situ fluorescent cDNA synthesis, imaging spots, removing cells, and preparing libraries. This directly supports the central Ukiyo-e-Seq premise that optical/spatial identity and transcriptomic data can be paired for individual eukaryotic plankton, and it validates the need to assign sequencing reads to a physical location, analogous to the proposed 384-well plate. It also supports the proposed emphasis on imaging before transcriptomic processing, although it uses in situ cDNA spot imaging rather than only brightfield and epifluorescence. As an iterative progression, this newer single-cell work builds on the older community-level evidence in [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f), which showed that environmental eukaryotic marine plankton transcriptomes can be reconstructed as MGTs with taxonomic assignment, abundance, completeness/contamination assessment, functional annotation, and pathway-level capabilities such as DMSP biosynthesis and degradation. That older work supports Ukiyo-e-Seq's reference-free transcriptomic strategy, the use of metagenomic/metatranscriptomic databases for Kraken2, and the biological value of linking plankton transcriptomes to function without cultured isolates, but it does so at bulk community resolution rather than single-organism optical resolution.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that neither related work validates the proposed uncultured, capillary-picked, 384-well Smart-seq2 merge-split workflow at the stated scale. [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) uses cultured plankton on microarray slides with in situ cDNA and spot imaging, not environmental samples, glass-capillary transfer, 384-well lysis, Smart-seq2, 26-cycle cDNA amplification, Nextera XT 15-cycle library amplification, or approximately 250,000 2x300 bp read pairs per well; therefore it does not establish that the proposed optical identity will survive transfer into wells or that transcriptomes can be confidently assigned to the imaged organism. It also highlights a methodological gap: MASC-seq images fluorescent cDNA spots in situ, providing a molecular spatial marker, whereas Ukiyo-e-Seq relies mainly on pre-capture brightfield/epifluorescence and post-capture brightfield, which is weaker evidence that the RNA in a well originated from the selected cell. A second contradiction is that [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f) shows environmental eukaryotic transcriptome reconstruction depends on high-throughput community metagenomic/metatranscriptomic data, MGT binning, completeness/contamination assessment, and functional pathway analysis, but provides no evidence that a single-well transcriptome with the proposed NextSeq 500 depth is sufficient for de novo rnaSpades assembly, ORF extraction, KEGG photosystem I/II annotation, or AlphaFold 3 modeling, especially for large eukaryotic genomes. The absence of quantitative read-depth, assembly, binning, or statistical significance in both excerpts means the proposed 66-well plus 4-negative design, public/private contig analysis, and taxonomic/functional conclusions remain unvalidated. Additionally, the proposed 25 μm to 1 mm size selection may capture colonies, fragments, or multiple organisms, and neither related work demonstrates reliable single-organism capture across that size range.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a molecular spatial validation step analogous to MASC-seq, such as in situ cDNA fluorescence or well-specific barcoding before lysis, and require post-capture imaging plus negative controls to confirm one organism per well. Next, pilot the workflow on cultured plankton with a bulk RNA-seq reference, such as Heterocapsa sp. used in [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c), to benchmark Smart-seq2, 26-cycle PCR, Nextera XT 15-cycle amplification, 250,000 read pairs per well, and merge-split rnaSpades assembly; increase sequencing depth or pool wells if coverage is insufficient. Then incorporate [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f)-style completeness/contamination assessment and MGT/metatranscriptomic reference checks for Kraken2 and functional annotation, using community MGTs as a baseline for DMSP and photosynthesis pathways. Also optimize capture for uncultured environmental cells by comparing capillary picking to microarray immobilization, testing fixation/permeabilization/lysis conditions, and adding single-cell confirmation for the 25 μm to 1 mm size class. Finally, strengthen statistical evaluation by replicating the two collection days, normalizing read depth, reporting taxonomic confidence and contamination rates, and applying AlphaFold 3 only to high-confidence ORFs.

## We will prepare libraries for single-well RNA sequencing using Smart-seq2 adapted to 384-well plates: core transcriptomic method enabling single-well RNA-seq from captured plankton.

### Support

The strongest support for preparing single-well Smart-seq2 libraries in 384-well plates comes from [(Kumasaka et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f710ddcce2fa27cd172fbd6937b11af636ab8ab9), which explicitly used a plate-based single-cell Smart-seq2 workflow in which individual cells were sorted or captured into 384-well plates, lysed in single wells, and cDNA was amplified to generate single-cell RNA-seq libraries. This directly matches the proposed Ukiyo-e-Seq step of transferring selected plankton into microtiter wells and performing Smart-seq2 in a 384-well format, and it shows that single-well full-length transcriptomic capture can be coupled to downstream alignment, gene counting, and quality-control filtering. Although the study used stimulated human dermal fibroblasts and PBMCs rather than environmentally collected plankton, its plate-based single-cell design supports the feasibility of the proposed 66 sample wells plus 4 negative control wells and the use of single-well libraries for expression or cell-state analysis. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Hagai et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d254a730b326b2deefe42a2bc14e0ddd5aab8d4), which used single-cell full-length RNA-seq from well-based formats to quantify differential gene expression and cell-to-cell variability in an immune-response experiment. This supports the broader premise that single-well full-length RNA-seq can resolve transcriptomic heterogeneity, but it is less direct because the supplied excerpt does not specify Smart-seq2, 384-well plates, cDNA synthesis chemistry, PCR cycling, sequencing platform, or quantitative outcomes.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work demonstrates the proposed adaptation to uncultured eukaryotic plankton, where organismal size variation, low RNA input, environmental contaminants, and absence of reference genomes are central constraints. [(Kumasaka et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f710ddcce2fa27cd172fbd6937b11af636ab8ab9) used sorted or captured human cells in a controlled stimulation setting, not plankton picked from ocean water, and the supplied excerpt does not report numeric library-preparation or sequencing outcomes such as cells passing QC, median read counts, gene counts, duplicate rates, donor numbers, stimulation time points, sequencing platform, read depth, or QC thresholds. Therefore, it does not provide evidence that the proposed 250,000 paired-end reads per well on an Illumina NextSeq 500, 26-cycle cDNA PCR, 15-cycle Nextera XT library PCR, or 66-well environmental capture will yield sufficient transcriptomic coverage for plankton. A weaker contradiction is that [(Hagai et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d254a730b326b2deefe42a2bc14e0ddd5aab8d4) provides no quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, fold changes, p-values, plate format, sequencing depth, or instrument specifications, so it cannot support the proposed method's specific computational or wet-lab parameters. Its immune-response context also differs from plankton community sampling, and the lack of protocol detail limits its usefulness for judging whether the proposed Smart-seq2 adaptation is reproducible.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a pilot validation using cultured plankton or other low-RNA eukaryotic cells before environmental sampling, with explicit QC metrics such as number of wells passing QC, median read pairs, contig or gene counts, duplicate rate, contamination rate, empty-well rate, and comparison of the 4 negative controls to sample wells. This would directly test whether the proposed 384-well Smart-seq2 protocol, including reverse transcription at 42°C for 90 minutes, 70°C for 5 minutes, 26 PCR cycles, 1.4 ng/μl normalization, Nextera XT 15-cycle PCR, and approximately 250,000 paired-end reads per well, is sufficient for plankton. Second, adopt the plate-based single-cell Smart-seq2 design principles from [(Kumasaka et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f710ddcce2fa27cd172fbd6937b11af636ab8ab9) by documenting organism numbers per well, lysis conditions, storage at -80°C, plate layout, sequencing platform, read depth, and filtering thresholds, and by including no-template, no-cell, and lysis-only controls to distinguish biological signal from environmental or reagent contamination. Third, because the proposed merge-split rnaSpades strategy depends on pooled reads from all wells, the method should report how many wells contribute to each contig, how private versus public contigs are defined, and how low-abundance plankton transcripts are handled relative to the 250,000-read-per-well target. A less urgent but useful action is to follow the broader single-cell full-length RNA-seq rationale in [(Hagai et al., 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9d254a730b326b2deefe42a2bc14e0ddd5aab8d4) by planning differential expression or variability analyses that can demonstrate whether single-well transcriptomes capture meaningful biological heterogeneity rather than only technical noise.

## Libraries will be sequenced on Illumina NextSeq 500 using reagent v3 kit (2×300 bases) at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well: sequencing method that generates the transcriptomic reads for each captured organism.

### Support

The strongest support for sequencing each captured plankton well on an Illumina NextSeq 500 with 2x300 bp reads and approximately 250,000 read pairs per well is conceptual rather than quantitative: [(Janiszewski et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6209637ace22229f4f54cca00c8aa66bdfe4e7db) shows that single-cell RNA-seq can resolve transcriptomic states from individual biological units and connect expression dynamics to biological processes, specifically X-chromosome reactivation and Xist silencing during somatic cell reprogramming to induced pluripotent stem cells. This supports the Ukiyo-e-Seq premise that per-well transcriptomic reads from individual captured organisms can be used to infer organismal identity and functional capabilities, such as photosystem I and II components or developmental proteins in fish egg/larva samples. A weaker but relevant support is that the paper used RNA-seq and single-cell RNA-seq in a controlled reprogramming system to detect gradual, allele-specific transcriptional changes over several days, which is analogous to using per-well expression matrices to distinguish public contigs spanning many wells from private contigs expressed in single wells. The weakest support is that the study is not an environmental plankton workflow: it does not demonstrate robot-assisted capture, brightfield/epifluorescence imaging, Smart-seq2 in 384-well plates, merge-split de novo assembly with rnaSpades, Kraken 2 classification, ORF annotation, or AlphaFold 3 structural prediction, so its relevance to the proposed 66-well plus 4-negative-control experiment is limited to the general feasibility of single-unit transcriptomics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no direct evidence that approximately 250,000 read pairs per well on an Illumina NextSeq 500 reagent v3 kit with 2x300 bp reads is sufficient for the proposed downstream analyses in uncultured eukaryotic plankton, including rnaSpades de novo assembly, Kraken 2 taxonomic classification, NCBI ORF finder annotation, and AlphaFold 3 complex modeling. [(Janiszewski et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6209637ace22229f4f54cca00c8aa66bdfe4e7db) does not report the sequencing platform, read length, library kit, per-sample read depth, or quantitative sequencing metrics, so the proposed depth cannot be benchmarked against its results. A weaker contradiction is the absence of statistical comparisons, sample size, library preparation protocol, computational parameters, or reproducibility details, which means the related work cannot support the claim that 250,000 read pairs per well will yield reliable public/private contig patterns, taxonomic clarity, or pathway-level functional characterization across 66 wells and 4 negative controls. The weakest contradiction is biological mismatch: the study examines mammalian somatic cell reprogramming, not marine plankton, and therefore does not address the larger genome and organismal size variation, rRNA burden, environmental contamination, or optical-to-transcriptomic pairing that are central to the proposed method.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a depth-of-sequencing benchmark before committing to 250,000 read pairs per well: sequence known plankton standards or cultured eukaryotes of comparable size and transcriptomic complexity at 250,000 read pairs per well and at lower and higher depths on the same Illumina NextSeq 500 reagent v3 2x300 setup, then evaluate rnaSpades contiguity, Kraken 2 taxonomic assignment, ORF recovery, photosystem I/II annotation, and AlphaFold 3 model confidence to determine whether the proposed depth is sufficient. The next most important action is to report quantitative controls that would make the method reproducible, including per-well raw and filtered read counts, library complexity, duplicate rates, rRNA depletion efficiency, negative-control contamination, spike-in transcript recovery, and statistical comparisons of public versus private contigs across the 66 wells and 4 negative controls. A further action is to specify implementation details that the related work lacks, such as Smart-seq2 adaptation parameters, Nextera XT 15-cycle PCR, Agencourt Ampure XP bead purification, Trimmomatic settings, rnaSpades parameters, Kraken 2 database versions, and ORF finder settings, so that the 250,000-read-pair target can be independently assessed. The least critical action is to use the related work's time-course and allele-specific design as an inspiration for validating developmental expression patterns in fish egg/larva samples, but this should not be treated as evidence that the plankton sequencing depth is adequate.

## We will develop a merge-split strategy to address the challenge of reference genome unavailability for most oceanic species: key computational strategy that makes single-well transcriptomes analyzable without species-specific reference genomes.

### Support

The strongest support comes from newer reference-free eukaryotic metatranscriptome work closest to the proposed oceanic plankton setting: eukrhythmic extends the older 2011-12 principle that genome-independent assembly is useful without reference genomes to eukaryotic marine transcriptomes, benchmarking simulated MMETSP communities and Tara Oceans/Narragansett Bay data and reporting that assemblies can recapitulate taxonomic diversity and functional annotations [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58). This directly supports the premise that a pooled de novo contig reference can be used for uncultured eukaryotic marine transcriptomes. The next strongest support is the foundational 2011-12 methodological principle that genome-independent de Bruijn graph assembly is the reasonable strategy when no high-quality reference genome exists, which is exactly the merge-split motivation [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c4baeb20f8868b1f134683cec53b213bbef88a6). The split step is supported by newer 2013 work, where reference-guided alignment/assembly improved completeness and contiguity on shallow human brain data and read-back usage was used to evaluate transfrag support, analogous to aligning each well to a pooled contig reference [(Lu et al., 2013-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1187edee50091dff9da539dfdc34bbf1746a642). Xie et al., 2013 supports the use of paired-end reads, contig filtering, and quality metrics because SOAPdenovo-Trans achieved high correctness (91.8%-98.0%) and low chimerism (1.0%-2.6%) on rice and mouse paired-end RNA-Seq data after filtering to >300 bp [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4). The 2011-12 assembly benchmark supports careful k-mer and contig-length filtering because assembly strategy changed N50 from 244 to 479 bp in brain and 249 to 513 bp in cell lines, and total assembled length from 18.4 to 25.2 Mb [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7436408467bb48fcc1ea4fbb657f50b4177ddae). Weaker but relevant support includes contig-based expression analysis and RT-PCR validation in bulk brain/cell-line transcriptomes [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc0a14e21f8f790b728d13715b344e341eaf482), and de novo metatranscriptome assembly followed by taxonomic/functional composition analysis [(Lau et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/604e878c17bb1e551e021b25989a428a23b4a6e7).

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is newer 2013 benchmark evidence that de novo transcriptome assembly is less complete and less contiguous than reference-guided methods under shallow coverage, and produces redundant, fragmented, polyA-tailed, and sometimes chimeric transfrags; Lu et al., 2013 found genome-guided assemblers had higher completeness and contiguity on 22x human brain data, while de novo assemblers improved only with depth and still generated more transfrags, often shorter and more redundant [(Lu et al., 2013-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1187edee50091dff9da539dfdc34bbf1746a642). This is a direct risk for the proposed 66 single-well transcriptomes at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well, where effective coverage per organism may be lower than bulk 22x and the pooled assembly may be dominated by low-abundance, fragmented, or cross-contaminated contigs. The next strongest contradiction is the general limitation that genome-independent assembly mainly recovers moderate- to high-abundance transcripts, while low-abundance transcripts require high sequencing depth, and repetitive/homologous sequences cause mapping ambiguity and assembly collapses [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c4baeb20f8868b1f134683cec53b213bbef88a6). This matters because plankton wells may contain rare transcripts, multiple taxa, host/contaminant RNA, and homologous genes. Newer Xie et al., 2013 contradicts the assumption that all assembled contigs are biologically meaningful isoforms: many putative alternative splice forms were redundant or fragmented assemblies with little new exon content, and subspecies mismatch caused close to 10% of rice assemblies to fail alignment until combined genomes were used [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4). This warns that private contigs and ORFs may be assembly artifacts or taxonomically mismatched sequences. The 2011-12 benchmark also shows that de novo assembly choices can produce many short contigs and variable total assembled length, so an unoptimized rnaSpades run could generate a contig matrix with poor continuity and high redundancy [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7436408467bb48fcc1ea4fbb657f50b4177ddae). Newer Krinos et al., 2022 adds a weaker but important contradiction that multiple assemblers improved metatranscriptome assembly and less stringent clustering decreased annotations, implying that relying on a single rnaSpades assembly and a single clustering/annotation threshold may underperform [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58). The weakest limitations are the lack of direct quantitative validation for single-well merge-split, the absence of direct rnaSpades benchmarking, and the lack of reported statistical significance tests in the older contig-expression and metatranscriptome studies [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ebc0a14e21f8f790b728d13715b344e341eaf482), [(Lau et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/604e878c17bb1e551e021b25989a428a23b4a6e7).

### Suggested Action

Most important, validate the merge-split workflow before biological interpretation by simulating 66 low-coverage eukaryotic single-well transcriptomes at approximately 250,000 paired-end reads per well, pooling them, assembling with rnaSpades and alternative assemblers such as SOAPdenovo-Trans, Trinity, Oases, trans-ABySS, or eukrhythmic, then aligning each simulated well back to the pooled reference and measuring read usage, contig completeness, N50, chimerism, redundancy, taxonomic recovery, and functional annotation recovery using the metrics used by Lu et al., 2013 and Xie et al., 2013 [(Lu et al., 2013-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1187edee50091dff9da539dfdc34bbf1746a642), [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4). Second, optimize and quality-control rnaSpades using paired-end 2x300 reads, k-mer selection, minimum contig length filtering (test 100 bp and 300 bp), polyA-tail trimming, redundancy clustering, chimera detection, and negative-control filtering, because assembly parameter choice strongly affected contig length and total assembled length in the 2011-12 benchmark [(Chen et al., 2011-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7436408467bb48fcc1ea4fbb657f50b4177ddae) and high correctness/low chimerism required filtering in Xie et al., 2013 [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4). Third, make the well-by-contig matrix statistically defensible by aligning each well separately with unique/multi-mapping handling, minimum mapping quality, read-back usage, normalization for Smart-seq2 amplification/library PCR bias, and thresholds for public versus private contigs, while using the 4 negative controls to estimate contamination and cross-well artifacts, following the read-usage logic in Lu et al., 2013 [(Lu et al., 2013-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1187edee50091dff9da539dfdc34bbf1746a642). Fourth, add taxonomic and functional annotation safeguards: run Kraken 2 against multiple metagenomic/metatranscriptomic databases, complement with BLAST/OrthoFinder/fastANI/protein clustering, filter ORFs by length, coverage, and negative controls, and validate KEGG photosystem I/II and developmental ORFs before AlphaFold 3 modeling, consistent with eukrhythmic and Lau et al., 2018 [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58), [(Lau et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/604e878c17bb1e551e021b25989a428a23b4a6e7). Fifth, test sensitivity to assembler choice and clustering thresholds because multiple assemblers improved metatranscriptome assembly and clustering stringency affected annotation [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58). Finally, report full reproducibility details: rnaSpades version, k-mer, hardware (prior benchmarks used a 512 GB NUMA server with 48 cores [(Lu et al., 2013-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1187edee50091dff9da539dfdc34bbf1746a642) and 10 threads on two quad-core CPUs with 70 GB memory [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4)), read depth per well, alignment tool, filtering thresholds, and statistical tests for public/private contigs and taxonomic/functional comparisons.

## We will filter reads using Trimmomatic and remove template switch oligo sequences and sequencing artifacts using custom scripts: bioinformatic quality-control method that prepares raw reads for assembly and expression analysis.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Trimmomatic and custom template-switch oligo removal step is that the related Drop-seq workflow performed read-level quality control, alignment, and gene quantification before downstream single-cell clustering and spatial mapping, and the processed transcriptomes successfully resolved spatial identities and supported computational spatial mapping or vISH-based prediction of gene expression in Drosophila embryos [(Karaiskos et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac2a8e0e0860bed66336c6cfa163fbc5e43e3b27). This supports the general principle in the research plan that raw Smart-seq2 reads from 66 wells plus 4 negative control wells, sequenced on Illumina NextSeq 500 with 2x300 bp reads and approximately 250,000 read pairs per well, should be cleaned before pooling for rnaSpades assembly, per-well alignment, Kraken 2 classification, ORF annotation, and expression visualization. A weaker but still relevant support is that the related work treated read filtering as a prerequisite for reliable expression matrices, which is analogous to the proposed merge-split strategy where read quality can affect contig assembly, read-to-contig counts, public versus private contig detection, and downstream taxonomic or functional interpretation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no direct evidence that Trimmomatic, a custom TSO-removal script, or specific artifact-removal parameters improve Smart-seq2 single-well RNA-seq data from uncultured eukaryotic plankton; the excerpt does not report quantitative read-filtering outcomes, read retention rates, TSO-removal statistics, adapter or artifact contamination levels, Trimmomatic-specific metrics, statistical significance, or comparisons to unfiltered baselines [(Karaiskos et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac2a8e0e0860bed66336c6cfa163fbc5e43e3b27). This is a gap because the proposed method depends on precise read cleaning before assembly and expression analysis, and without such metrics it is unclear whether filtering will preserve enough reads per well, reduce false private contigs, or avoid removing biologically relevant sequences. A weaker contradiction is that the related work is a Drosophila embryo Drop-seq pipeline rather than a Smart-seq2, 384-well, environmental plankton workflow, and the excerpt does not specify sequencing platform, read length, trimming parameters, TSO sequence definitions, alignment tool, gene-counting tool, cell-filtering thresholds, normalization parameters, or computational resources, so its read-level QC details cannot be directly transferred to the proposed NextSeq 500 2x300, approximately 250,000 read pairs per well, merge-split, rnaSpades, Kraken 2, and ORF-finder analysis.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to specify the exact Trimmomatic version and parameters, the exact template-switch oligo and sequencing artifact sequences, and the custom script logic, including whether trimming is performed before pooling, before rnaSpades assembly, before per-well alignment, or both, and to report pre- and post-filtering read counts, retained read pairs, adapter/TSO contamination, per-well read depth, and negative-control contamination for the 66 wells plus 4 negative control wells [(Karaiskos et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac2a8e0e0860bed66336c6cfa163fbc5e43e3b27). The next priority is to validate the filtering step by comparing downstream outcomes with and without Trimmomatic and TSO removal, including assembly contig number and N50, read-to-contig alignment rates, Kraken 2 taxonomic assignment confidence, ORF annotation completeness, and the number of public versus private contigs, so that the QC step is shown to improve rather than bias the merge-split analysis. A final action is to document reproducibility details such as input file formats, command-line calls, versioned scripts, and thresholds for artifact removal, because the related work demonstrates the value of read-level QC but does not provide enough implementation detail to reproduce the proposed plankton-specific pipeline.

## We will use rnaSpades for de novo assembly and compile read numbers from each sample that align to each contig into a single matrix: implements the merge-split analysis and produces a contig-by-well expression matrix.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Tulin et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb536d81303161b510839ee8f9d2f1e3eda5e821), which used Trinity and Oases to build a pooled de novo transcriptome and then quantified sample reads against assembled contigs, validating contig-based expression estimation with known RNA spike-ins; this is the closest analogue to Ukiyo-e-Seq's merge-split step of pooling single-well reads, assembling a shared reference, and compiling a contig-by-well read-count matrix, although it used bulk developmental RNA-seq rather than 384-well plankton Smart-seq2 libraries. The next strongest support is [(Lau et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/604e878c17bb1e551e021b25989a428a23b4a6e7), which assembled metatranscriptomic reads into a shared contig reference and then characterized transcript abundance, length, homology, taxonomy, and function, directly mirroring the proposed use of a pooled contig reference for expression, taxonomic, and functional profiling of eukaryotic plankton, though the excerpt does not name rnaSpades or provide numerical benchmarks. Newer eukaryotic metatranscriptomic work by [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58) supports the broader logic by showing that pooled eukaryotic metatranscriptome reads can be assembled, clustered, translated, and annotated for taxonomy and function, and that assemblies can recover simulated taxonomic diversity and Tara Oceans coding sequences absent from reference atlases, but it focuses on bulk communities rather than per-well counts. Quantitative support for de Bruijn-graph assembly and read-to-contig mapping is provided by [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4), whose SOAPdenovo-Trans used paired-end read linkages and read counts to build contigs and scaffolds and was computationally competitive, with peak memory of 10.7 to 29.3 GB and runtimes of 0.2 to 1.0 h on rice and mouse bulk datasets, demonstrating that contig-level read mapping is feasible at scale, although its mapping was for scaffolding rather than sample-wise expression. More indirect support comes from [(Westrin et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa1656233ace990aaac9caef380b277f8b829fac), which shows that short-read RNA-seq assemblies can be improved by clustering guiding contigs and merging primary and clusterwise assemblies, and from [(Carruthers et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8606af11b245da4ef05633ae72f91210a9fd2a0), which supports the need for assembly optimization, redundancy removal, full-length reconstruction, BUSCO, and OrthoFinder before downstream functional interpretation; these are weaker for the proposed method because they do not directly validate a contig-by-well matrix or rnaSpades. Finally, [(Mittal, and McDonald, 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13b2147173f363b5549ec451a2debdaf99f28b25) supports the assembly-and-quantification logic and the importance of filtering chimeric or fusion-like transcripts before expression comparison, though it is bulk cancer transcriptomics and lacks single-well detail.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is from newer assembly-quality evidence in [(Westrin et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa1656233ace990aaac9caef380b277f8b829fac), which reports that SQANTI and CRBB true-positive transcript sets differ and that recall varies over expression levels and number of exons, with only a 95% coverage threshold explicitly stated; this implies that a single rnaSpades contig reference may miss or misrepresent lowly expressed, isoform-rich, or private contigs in individual wells, directly threatening the contig-by-well matrix and the plan's reliance on top five highly expressed contigs. The next strongest concern is [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58), which found that multiple assemblers improved metatranscriptome assembly of Narragansett Bay phytoplankton and that less stringent clustering slightly decreased identified annotations, suggesting that relying solely on rnaSpades without consensus assembly or clustering may underperform in eukaryotic plankton metatranscriptomes. Older quantitative evidence from [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4) also cautions that de Bruijn transcript assemblers can produce fewer long transcripts than alternatives: SOAPdenovo-Trans produced fewer transcripts longer than 300 bp than Trinity and Oases in rice and mouse bulk data, for example 25,800 versus 35,690 and 36,145 for rice small and 25,037 versus 43,892 and 49,624 for mouse large, and although its correct assembly rates were high at 91.8% to 98.0% with low chimeric rates of 1.0% to 2.6%, it did not validate sample-wise expression quantification. [(Mittal, and McDonald, 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13b2147173f363b5549ec451a2debdaf99f28b25) contradicts the proposed method's omission of explicit chimeric filtering by showing that transcriptome assembly can generate 1,393 fusion transcripts, some cancer-specific and protein-coding, requiring detection and filtering before expression comparison; without analogous filtering, Ukiyo-e-Seq contigs could include chimeric or fusion-like artifacts that distort well-specific counts and ORF, KEGG, and AlphaFold interpretation. Weaker but still relevant, [(Carruthers et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8606af11b245da4ef05633ae72f91210a9fd2a0) indicates that high-quality transcriptome resources require redundancy removal, full-length reconstruction, BUSCO completeness, OrthoFinder orthology, and GO annotation, none of which are specified in the proposed rnaSpades merge-split workflow; the absence of these QC steps does not prove the method will fail, but it leaves the contig-by-well matrix vulnerable to redundant, fragmented, or incomplete contigs.

### Suggested Action

Most important, benchmark rnaSpades against at least two alternative short-read RNA-seq assemblers, such as Trinity, SOAPdenovo-Trans or Oases, ClusTrast, or an eukrhythmic-style multi-assembler consensus, on simulated single-well plankton metatranscriptomes and on the actual pooled 66-well dataset, using metrics from [(Westrin et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa1656233ace990aaac9caef380b277f8b829fac) such as SQANTI and CRBB true-positive sets, 95% reference coverage, recall by expression level and exon count, and from [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58) such as read fidelity, taxonomic diversity recovery, and functional annotation completeness; this directly addresses the risk that a single assembler misses private or lowly expressed contigs. Second, add explicit contig QC before matrix construction: remove redundant contigs, reconstruct full-length transcripts, filter chimeric or fusion-like contigs as in [(Mittal, and McDonald, 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13b2147173f363b5549ec451a2debdaf99f28b25), and assess completeness and orthology with BUSCO and OrthoFinder as in [(Carruthers et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d8606af11b245da4ef05633ae72f91210a9fd2a0), because the proposed plan currently jumps from rnaSpades contigs to expression, Kraken2 taxonomy, ORF annotation, KEGG photosystem components, and AlphaFold 3 without these controls. Third, validate the contig-by-well count matrix using spike-ins or known expression controls analogous to [(Tulin et al., 2013-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fb536d81303161b510839ee8f9d2f1e3eda5e821), and use the four negative control wells to estimate background, contamination, and multi-mapping artifacts; report alignment identity and coverage thresholds, unique versus multi-mapping read handling, and whether counts are raw, normalized, or log-transformed, since the plan only says to compile read numbers. Fourth, because the experiment uses 2x300 bp Nextera XT libraries at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well and Smart-seq2 cDNA, specify rnaSpades hyperparameters, including k-mer sizes, paired-end insert-size handling, and memory or CPU allocation, and pilot the computational load, using [(Xie et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d67a93295497578e114125a2e5e1041a1ec91fb4) as a resource benchmark: SOAPdenovo-Trans required 10.7 to 29.3 GB and 0.2 to 1.0 h for bulk rice and mouse datasets, so the pooled 66-well dataset should be tested for memory, runtime, and contig fragmentation before full-scale analysis. Finally, integrate the metatranscriptome-specific lessons from [(Lau et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/604e878c17bb1e551e021b25989a428a23b4a6e7) and [(Krinos et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00396f9385b8ae6a2b478469b1579f12098b8e58) by reporting transcript length and continuity, homology-supported quality, taxonomic composition, and functional composition alongside the expression matrix, and consider consensus assembly or clusterwise merging as in [(Westrin et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa1656233ace990aaac9caef380b277f8b829fac) to improve recovery of eukaryotic isoforms before using contigs for taxonomic classification, ORF annotation, KEGG photosystem I and II extraction, and AlphaFold 3 structural prediction.

## We will perform taxonomic classification using Kraken 2 against large databases of metagenomic and metatranscriptomic data to understand taxonomic identity despite lack of reference genomes: taxonomic method for assigning identities to single-well transcriptomes.

### Support

The strongest support for using Kraken2 in Ukiyo-e-Seq comes from [(Wood et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1553161499f48202696cb1fac9f6ca0f006dc6b0), which directly validated the classifier's core algorithm, including spaced-seed/minimizer hashing, exact lookup, lowest-common-ancestor assignment, and translated search for protein-coding sequences, on large metagenomic benchmarks. The translated-search mode is especially relevant because the proposed plan sequences cDNA from Smart-seq2 single-well transcriptomes rather than genomic DNA, and the reported speed, about 100 million 100 bp reads in roughly 47 minutes, approximately 50-fold faster than Kraken and approximately 100-fold faster than BLAST, makes classification of the planned 66 wells at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well computationally feasible. The paper also reported smaller databases, about 1.4 GB versus 2.5 GB for Kraken1, near-linear scaling to 8 threads on a multi-core Intel Xeon system, and strong performance on strain-exclusion and FDA-ARGOS data, with approximately 96% concordance at species level on FDA-ARGOS reads. A second, more contextual support is [(Smith et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82805ec212e6bbaab9a86c9a498c6c0fba6f4609), which showed that Kraken2 classification rate and accuracy are strongly affected by reference database composition and that adding environment-specific cultured genomes greatly improved performance. This supports the proposal's intention to use large metagenomic and metatranscriptomic databases, but it does so by emphasizing database curation rather than proving that a generic large database will work for uncultured eukaryotic plankton.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work directly validates Kraken2 on the target data type: single-well eukaryotic plankton transcriptomes from uncultured organisms. [(Wood et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1553161499f48202696cb1fac9f6ca0f006dc6b0) evaluated standard genomic reference libraries, strain-exclusion datasets, and FDA-ARGOS metagenomic reads, but not 384-well Smart-seq2 cDNA, large eukaryotic genomes, low-coverage single-organism transcriptomes, or metatranscriptomic databases dominated by expressed sequences. Therefore, the reported accuracy may not transfer to the plan's approximately 250,000 read-pair-per-well NextSeq 500 2x300 data, where expression bias, rRNA contamination, and limited genome coverage can reduce taxonomic resolution. A second, newer contradiction is [(Smith et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82805ec212e6bbaab9a86c9a498c6c0fba6f4609), which found that database composition strongly affected both classification accuracy and inferred abundance, and that standard Kraken2 libraries alone were not sufficient; environment-specific cultured isolates improved performance, while MAGs could help only when taxonomy was well resolved. This contradicts a naive interpretation of the proposed method that any large metagenomic or metatranscriptomic database will assign identities despite lack of reference genomes, especially for Wiley's Baths/Coogee NSW plankton if marine eukaryotic references are absent. A third limitation is resource and reproducibility: Wood's benchmark used up to 8 threads and multi-gigabyte hash tables, Smith's database builds used 16 threads, and the proposal does not specify database size, RAM, disk, build time, or query thread count, so large metatranscriptomic databases may be impractical or non-reproducible without those details.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to construct and benchmark multiple Kraken2 databases tailored to the study system, following [(Smith et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82805ec212e6bbaab9a86c9a498c6c0fba6f4609): include standard bacterial, archaeal, viral, and human libraries, UniVec_Core, marine eukaryotic and prokaryotic references, Australian coastal or Wiley's Baths/Coogee NSW-specific sequences where available, cultured isolates, and MAGs only when taxonomy is sufficiently resolved. These databases should be evaluated on simulated known-truth reads and the four negative-control wells to quantify classification rate, precision, and false-positive contamination. The second priority is to validate Kraken2 on single-well transcriptome-like data, addressing the gap in [(Wood et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1553161499f48202696cb1fac9f6ca0f006dc6b0): subsample reads from available eukaryotic plankton genomes to approximately 250,000 paired 300 bp reads per well, test with and without translated search, compare species- and genus-level sensitivity and specificity against BLAST or Bracken, and report unclassified fractions. The third action is to specify computational resources and implementation details, including database size, RAM, disk, build threads, query threads, minimizer or k-mer parameters, and read filtering, so that the 384-well workflow is reproducible. Finally, integrate Kraken2 with the merge-split strategy by classifying both raw reads and rnaSpades contigs, reconciling read-level lowest-common-ancestor assignments with contig-level taxonomy, applying confidence thresholds, and using the top-five highly expressed contigs and negative controls to assess taxonomic clarity and coexistence patterns.

## We will identify open reading frames (ORFs) in contigs using NCBI ORF finder, functionally annotate ORFs, and extract specific pathway components of interest: functional annotation method connecting transcriptomes to biochemical capabilities.

### Support

The strongest support is that the two related studies form an iterative line of evidence showing that transcriptome-based functional assessment can reveal loss or degradation of photosynthetic pathway components in a eukaryote, which aligns with the proposed plan's goal of using transcriptome contigs, ORF identification, functional annotation, and extraction of photosystem I/II components to connect transcriptomes to biochemical capabilities. [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5bcde5b1559c2baee15b8ffaad3458108b47fe5) used Illumina transcriptome sequencing, plastid genome annotation, and expression analysis of photosynthesis pathway components in A. indica, while [(Chen et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1050c8a95ae1c379b98af365c6d5e3cba54141eb) extended this by explicitly connecting transcriptomic data to photosynthetic biochemical capability and adding qRT-PCR validation of selected genes. This supports the proposed method's central assumption that pathway-level biochemical capability can be inferred from transcriptomic sequence data, especially when focusing on conserved photosynthesis pathway components such as KEGG photosystem I and II subunits. A weaker but still relevant support is that the related work combined sequence annotation with expression evidence and experimental validation, suggesting that the proposed NCBI ORF finder and functional annotation pipeline would be more credible if it similarly distinguishes ORF presence from expression of pathway components and validates key hits in the single-well plankton data.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work tests the proposed single-cell, optical-plus-transcriptomic plankton workflow; both are bulk or tissue-level analyses of a single plant species, A. indica, and do not demonstrate that NCBI ORF finder on de novo transcriptome contigs, KEGG photosystem I/II extraction, or AlphaFold 3 modeling can recover organism-specific photosynthetic capability from individual uncultured eukaryotic plankton. [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5bcde5b1559c2baee15b8ffaad3458108b47fe5) and [(Chen et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1050c8a95ae1c379b98af365c6d5e3cba54141eb) therefore provide only indirect support for pathway inference, not for the proposed merge-split single-well transcriptomics, rnaSpades assembly, Kraken 2 classification, 66-well plus 4 negative-control design, or Illumina NextSeq 500 sequencing at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well. A second contradiction is the absence of quantitative performance evidence: the excerpts report qualitative degradation of the photosynthesis pathway but do not provide read counts, fold changes, p-values, contig counts, sequencing depth, qPCR Ct values, or statistical thresholds, so they cannot demonstrate that ORF annotation and KEGG extraction will be sensitive or specific enough for low-coverage single-cell transcriptomes, where photosystem subunits may be low abundance, fragmented, or confounded by contamination. A third, weaker contradiction is that transcript presence or ORF annotation alone may not prove biochemical capability; the related studies relied on plastid genome shrinkage, loss of plastid genes, relaxed purifying selection, and qRT-PCR validation to support pathway loss, whereas the proposed method as written relies primarily on ORF identification and functional annotation without explicit expression quantification, validation, or statistical confidence for extracted pathway components.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a validation and quantification layer to the ORF/annotation method: for each of the 66 wells and 4 negative controls, quantify expression of extracted photosystem I/II ORFs using read counts, TPM/FPKM, or equivalent normalized metrics, set minimum expression, contig-length, and ORF-length thresholds, and validate a subset of predicted photosynthetic and non-photosynthetic hits by RT-qPCR or targeted sequencing, mirroring the qRT-PCR validation used in [(Chen et al., 2019-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5bcde5b1559c2baee15b8ffaad3458108b47fe5) and [(Chen et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1050c8a95ae1c379b98af365c6d5e3cba54141eb). The next priority is to benchmark NCBI ORF finder against transcriptome-appropriate ORF prediction and annotation tools, such as TransDecoder, Prokka or GeneMark for prokaryotic contaminants, InterProScan/HMMER, and KEGG Orthology, because the related work does not specify NCBI ORF finder and the proposed method should not assume that ORF finder alone provides reliable functional annotation. The method should also report reproducibility metrics: number of contigs, ORFs, KEGG hits, public/private contig overlap, negative-control contamination rates, and statistical comparisons between photosynthetic and non-photosynthetic wells, including effect sizes and significance tests where replicates allow. Finally, because the related work supports pathway-level inference but not single-cell optical linkage, the plan should explicitly test whether the extracted photosystem components co-occur with brightfield and epifluorescence phenotypes and Kraken 2 taxonomic assignments, and include known photosynthetic and non-photosynthetic plankton controls to establish precision and recall for pathway extraction before using AlphaFold 3 to model protein complexes.

A secondary support is that the study used triplicate simultaneous filtrations and shook 20 L carboys before each filtration to maintain homogeneity, which supports adding replicate 1 L collections, split samples, or homogenization steps to the proposed two-occasion Wiley's Baths design. A third contradiction is that the study removed metazoans and focused on microbial 16S/18S markers, whereas the proposed plan explicitly targets eukaryotic plankton, photosynthetic organisms, and fish egg/larva developmental contigs, so its community metrics do not validate metazoan or whole-transcriptome recovery. A weaker contradiction is that it tested DNA preservation and extraction from filters at -80°C, not RNA integrity, lysis, reverse transcription, cDNA yield, or Smart-seq2 library quality, and it used one field campaign in November 2016 rather than the proposed two separate occasions, so it does not demonstrate temporal reproducibility or RNA-specific sampling bias.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a bulk community validation track that mirrors [(Ribeiro et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e336c7fc56d5368a5ef93a9aec8babb067f3578c): collect the same 1 L Wiley's Baths water in at least triplicate per occasion, split the sample before net/sieve processing, and profile the size-selected fraction by 18S/ITS or 16S/18S metabarcoding with Illumina sequencing, rarefaction, Bray-Curtis, ANOVA/Tukey, and Simprof to test whether net/sieve/centrifugation biases alter community composition. The next priority is to validate the size-selection and flow conditions directly, because the related work showed filtration time and flow are pressure/volume dependent; record mesh size, flow rate, pressure or pump settings, filtration time, clogging, and particle-size distribution, and compare manual net collection with a controlled filtration or automated sampling method if possible. A further action is to add RNA-specific controls, since the related work only validated DNA storage at -80°C: include immediate lysis or RNAlater, RNA integrity/RIN or RQ measurements, qPCR/eDNA yield checks, no-template and no-cell negative controls, and spike-in standards to distinguish sampling loss from sequencing loss. Finally, because the related work used bulk marker-gene libraries rather than single-cell transcriptomes, the proposed 250,000 read pairs per well should be benchmarked against pilot Smart-seq2 libraries and, if necessary, increased or pooled to ensure enough coverage for merge-split assembly, Kraken 2 classification, ORF annotation, and KEGG photosystem I/II identification.

The strongest contradiction is that the related work provides no direct evidence for the proposed sample preparation step of processing samples through tea sieves and filter paper, with centrifugation and sedimentation, to isolate the 1 mm to 25 um target size range; [(Gall et al., 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/48f5b02cea2812d55fa3eba7a859d1437c200364) instead relies on enriched or flow-sorted primary cultures, on-board incubation, transport, and shore-lab purification, so it cannot support the assumption that sieve/filter/centrifugation will yield pickable, intact, and uncontaminated cells. ## Using the microscope-mounted cell picker, we will image and sequence 66 wells plus 4 negative control wells: experimental scale and control design for validating single-organism capture and sequencing.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 66-well plus 4-negative-control microscope-mounted cell picker experiment comes from [(Vincent et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89f6279d5c6a88788ed65c84be2fbb5e6c285856), which used advanced micromanipulation to isolate individual diatom-tintinnid consortia from environmental coastal and Tara Oceans samples and then identified partners by targeted PCR of small-subunit rDNA, ITS1, ITS2, 5.8S, and 28S regions with phylogenetic placement. The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that the related work does not provide quantitative evidence that 66 experimental wells plus 4 negative controls is sufficient to validate single-organism capture and sequencing. [(Vincent et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89f6279d5c6a88788ed65c84be2fbb5e6c285856) reports no well-level capture success rates, read depths, negative-control contamination values, or statistical comparisons, and [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) provides only section headings without capture success, read counts, contamination metrics, or control comparisons. Therefore, the proposed 70-well design, with only about 5.7% negative controls, lacks an empirical benchmark for distinguishing true single-cell transcriptomes from ambient contamination, failed picks, or low-input RNA noise. A second, important contradiction is that [(Vincent et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89f6279d5c6a88788ed65c84be2fbb5e6c285856) isolated diatom-tintinnid consortia rather than strictly single organisms, showing that optical picking can capture interacting partners or epibionts; this challenges the assumption that each of the 66 wells will contain one organism, and the proposed 4 negative controls would not detect such co-picking. A weaker but relevant tension is that [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) uses a microarray-based MASC-seq workflow with fixation, permeabilization, and fluorescent cDNA synthesis, whereas the proposed method relies on direct capillary transfer into 384-well Smart-seq2 lysis, NextSeq 500 sequencing at approximately 250,000 read pairs per well, and 26-cycle cDNA PCR; the related work does not demonstrate that this picker-to-Smart-seq2 route will achieve comparable single-cell RNA recovery or purity for uncultured plankton.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to strengthen the 66+4 control design into a quantitative validation matrix: add multiple negative controls beyond 4, including no-cell lysis blanks, picking-only blanks, reagent-only blanks, and empty-well controls, plus positive controls using cultured single cells or known diatom-tintinnid consortia, and predefine acceptance thresholds for contamination, capture success, and taxonomic purity. The next most important action is to add explicit single-organism purity checks because [(Vincent et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89f6279d5c6a88788ed65c84be2fbb5e6c285856) shows consortia can be picked together; use pre- and post-capture brightfield and epifluorescence images at 515, 595, and 681 nm, high-magnification confirmation, and taxonomic classification of each well to flag mixed or contaminated wells rather than treating all 66 wells as single-organism samples. Third, incorporate technical RNA controls and optimization informed by [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c), such as spike-in RNAs, fixation/permeabilization tests, or MASC-seq-like lysis conditions, to assess whether Smart-seq2 cDNA synthesis at 42°C for 90 minutes and 26 PCR cycles recovers sufficient RNA from fragile plankton. Finally, increase the experimental scale or perform a power analysis: 66 wells is a small pilot and may not robustly estimate background contamination, capture efficiency, or community diversity, so consider expanding to 96-384 wells, including replicate picks from the same sample, and reporting read counts, unique molecular identifiers, negative-control overlap, and statistical significance for contamination and taxonomic purity.

## We will confirm successful picking with post-capture brightfield images: quality-control method ensuring that the sequenced well corresponds to the intended organism.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed post-capture brightfield QC is that [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) demonstrates a single-cell transcriptomics workflow in which imaging is used to verify that the physical location being processed corresponds to the intended organism. In their MASC-seq plankton workflow, cells are fixed to microarray spots, fluorescently imaged, aligned, and spot coordinates are extracted to link the visualized cell-containing spot to downstream permeabilization, fluorescent cDNA synthesis, cell removal, and library preparation. This is directly analogous to Ukiyo-e-Seq's need to confirm that a 384-well Smart-seq2 sample corresponds to the organism picked from the microscope field. A weaker but still relevant support is that the prior workflow shows spatial verification can be embedded in an uncultured single-cell transcriptomics pipeline, which aligns with the research plan's goal of pairing optical and genetic data from individual plankton without relying on culture. The support is nevertheless limited because the prior work uses fluorescent spot imaging and coordinate extraction rather than brightfield post-capture well imaging, and it does not provide quantitative evidence that such QC improves downstream transcriptomic accuracy.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) does not report quantitative QC metrics for the imaging or coordinate verification step, including success rates, false-positive or false-negative rates, error rates, or effects on downstream transcriptomic quality. This leaves the central claim of the proposed method—that post-capture brightfield images ensure the sequenced well corresponds to the intended organism—unvalidated by the cited prior work. A second, important contradiction is a modality and geometry mismatch: the related workflow uses fluorescent imaging of microarray spots and coordinate extraction, whereas the proposed method uses brightfield images of 384-well plate wells after capillary transfer. Brightfield imaging may be less specific for transparent plankton, debris, empty wells, multiple cells, or partial captures, and the related work does not show that brightfield post-capture well imaging can resolve these failure modes. A third contradiction is population and reproducibility mismatch: the related work is described for cultured plankton cells on microarray slides, while the proposed experiment targets environmental samples from Wiley's Baths with organisms size-selected between 25 μm and 1 mm, variable morphology, and 66 plus 4 negative-control wells. The available related work also omits stain, magnification, microscope settings, coordinate extraction parameters, and statistical comparisons to baselines, so it cannot be taken as direct evidence that the proposed QC will be reproducible or sufficient for the planned merge-split, Kraken 2, ORF, and AlphaFold 3 analyses.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to convert the proposed post-capture brightfield QC from a qualitative check into a quantitative, statistically evaluated validation step. For each of the 66 target wells and 4 negative controls, capture pre-capture and post-capture brightfield images, and for the fluorescence subset also record 515, 595, and 681 nm channels; then use automated image analysis to measure organism presence/absence, area, perimeter, circularity, intensity, and well-to-well coordinate alignment, and report the rate of empty wells, debris-only wells, multiple-cell wells, and misassigned wells. The second priority is to link image QC to transcriptomic outcomes, because the related work [(Grujčić et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3783015c02117e2c696d790a76c9f03ff5ef4c5c) does not provide such evidence: compare QC-passed and QC-failed wells for read pairs per well, rRNA/mitochondrial content, host or contaminant reads, private/public contig patterns, Kraken 2 taxonomic clarity, and ORF/KEGG photosystem recovery, with appropriate statistical tests and confidence intervals. The third priority is to define explicit acceptance thresholds and a sensitivity analysis, for example excluding wells below a minimum brightfield area or above a maximum debris score, and showing how the merge-split assembly and downstream functional annotations change when QC criteria are relaxed. Finally, report implementation details needed for reproducibility, including microscope magnification, exposure, illumination, image alignment method, coordinate extraction parameters, and how the 384-well plate layout maps to the cell picker coordinates, so that the QC step can be independently assessed rather than assumed from the MASC-seq precedent.

## We will visualize expression patterns of the top five most highly expressed contigs from each well to identify both "public" contigs (spanning most samples from single collection days) and "private" contigs (highly expressed in single wells only): analysis method for distinguishing shared community transcripts from organism-specific transcripts.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f), which used contig-level MGT abundance and expression profiles across environmental samples to group sequences with similar abundance or expression patterns and thereby separate broadly shared community transcripts from sample-specific or ecotype-specific transcripts. This directly parallels the Ukiyo-e-Seq goal of distinguishing public contigs spanning most wells from private contigs restricted to single wells, although the related work operated at community scale with metagenomic and metatranscriptomic MGTs rather than single-well Smart-seq2 libraries. A second, weaker support is that the related work performed taxonomic assignment, completeness and contamination assessment, and functional annotation of ORFs and pathways, which supports the broader Ukiyo-e-Seq plan to use Kraken 2, NCBI ORF finder, KEGG photosystem I and II components, and pathway extraction on contigs. The weakest support is conceptual: the paper shows that eukaryotic plankton transcriptomes can be reconstructed and functionally interpreted from environmental sequencing, but it provides no direct evidence for the specific top-five visualization, 384-well Smart-seq2 capture, 250,000 read pairs per well, 66-well design, four negative controls, or optical pairing. Because only one related work is provided, it cannot be contrasted with newer iterative studies; it functions as an earlier community-scale iteration of expression-profile separation that supports the general strategy but does not yet validate the single-organism iteration.

### Contradictions

The main contradiction is methodological rather than a direct negative result: [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f) relied on canopy clustering of many MGTs and multivariate abundance or expression profiles across samples, whereas the proposed method reduces each well to only the top five most highly expressed contigs. This reduction risks missing private contigs with moderate expression, overrepresenting highly expressed housekeeping, ribosomal, or contaminant transcripts, and failing to capture the multivariate abundance patterns that the related work used to define shared versus specific sequences. A further contradiction is that the related work included completeness and contamination assessment, taxonomic assignment, and functional annotation, but the proposed top-five visualization does not specify normalization, clustering, statistical thresholds, or negative-control filtering, making it less rigorous than the related work. The related work also reports no numeric completeness or contamination scores, read counts, cluster sizes, statistical tests, or baseline comparisons, so it cannot demonstrate that expression-profile separation is quantitatively reliable; this absence weakens support for using top-five expression alone as the primary analysis method. Finally, the related work is community-scale metatranscriptomics and does not address single-well capture, Smart-seq2 dropout, low coverage, the 66-well and four-negative-control design, or optical pairing, so it cannot validate the single-organism resolution claimed by Ukiyo-e-Seq.

### Suggested Action

Most important, replace the top-five-only visualization with a full contig-by-well expression matrix and apply normalization and clustering, ideally canopy or hierarchical clustering analogous to [(Vorobev et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/657fd1a6d80d911100025694e6575a2de8234d5f), to define public and private contigs using prevalence, fold-change, and statistical significance across the 66 wells and two collection days. Next, incorporate completeness and contamination assessment and taxonomic assignment before classifying contigs as public or private, using Kraken 2 plus eukaryotic completeness and contamination tools and the four negative control wells to remove contaminants and low-confidence sequences. Then, add quantitative validation by reporting read counts, TPM or CPM values, cluster sizes, prevalence thresholds, and statistical tests such as negative-binomial or Fisher's exact comparisons to distinguish shared community transcripts from organism-specific transcripts, and compare against baselines such as pooled metatranscriptomic MGTs or reference transcriptomes where available. Finally, document reproducibility details missing from the related work and needed for Ukiyo-e-Seq, including rnaSpades assembly parameters, Kraken 2 database composition, ORF annotation databases, KEGG pathway extraction criteria, Smart-seq2 PCR cycles, 250,000 read-pair depth, and clustering hyperparameters, so that the top-five visualization can be interpreted as a diagnostic summary rather than the sole analytical method.

## We will use AlphaFold 3 to predict three-dimensional structures of protein complexes from individual organisms: structural prediction method that extends functional annotation to protein-complex architecture.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815), which established that sequence and MSA-driven deep learning can convert protein sequence information into accurate three-dimensional models, reporting a median GDT_TS of 92.4 on 43 CASP14 free-modeling targets, 90 percent of targets above GDT_TS 90, and a median GDT_TS of 90.4 on a recent-PDB test set of 500 structures. This directly supports the research plan step of extending KEGG-based photosystem I and II ORF annotation into structural architecture, provided the plankton ORFs have sufficient homologous sequence coverage. The next strongest support is [(Mirdita et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20411effeaac9aa457e528090dc274cb46c3412), which operationalizes AlphaFold2 and AlphaFold2-Multimer through ColabFold, using MMseqs2 against reduced BFD, MGnify, UniRef30, UniRef100, and PDB-derived templates, and shows monomer median GDT_TS of 92.0 with median pLDDT around 88.5, complex median GDT_TS of 86.9 with about 72 percent of complexes above GDT_TS 80, and median runtimes of about 1.5 hours per target versus about 3.5 hours for the original AlphaFold2 pipeline, reduced to about 0.5 hours with early stopping. Its taxonomic MSA pairing for multimer inputs is conceptually aligned with the plan's single-organism transcriptomic contigs and organism-specific photosystem subunit grouping. The next support is [(Gao et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/811af1dbe260cf0f7cf49b58e56c54f67aa885fc), which shows that an AlphaFold2-based complex workflow, AF2Complex, can predict multimeric CASP14 targets such as CcmEFH and CcmFGH and outperform docking-centric approaches with statistical tests and an interface clash indicator, supporting the idea that sequence-based complex prediction can infer interface architecture. The weakest support is [(Ryck et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60d572e3569ea40fc0f7724a6721eed0526583a0), which provides only conceptual precedent for using AlphaFold and AlphaFold-Multimer to extend functional annotation to structures and complexes, without reporting AlphaFold outcomes, but it does frame the same annotation-to-structure logic used in the proposed plankton workflow.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the related works directly benchmark AlphaFold 3 on the proposed input type, namely single-well Smart-seq2 transcriptomic contigs from 66 picked uncultured eukaryotic plankton wells plus 4 negative controls, assembled with rnaSpades, classified with Kraken 2, and annotated with NCBI ORF finder and KEGG photosystem I and II components. The closest quantitative evidence is still AlphaFold2-based, and [(Jumper et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc32a984b651256a8ec282be52310e6bd33d9815) explicitly ties accuracy to MSA depth, using UniRef30 and BFD truncated to 512 sequences and trained on 20,000 PDB structures with 8 recycles; uncultured plankton ORFs may have shallow homologous coverage, fragmented contigs, and low single-cell transcriptomic depth, which could reduce AlphaFold 3 reliability. The next strongest contradiction is that complex prediction is less accurate than monomer prediction and requires careful chain and taxonomic inputs: [(Mirdita et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20411effeaac9aa457e528090dc274cb46c3412) reports ColabFold complex median GDT_TS of 86.9 and about 72 percent above GDT_TS 80, below the monomer median GDT_TS of 92.0, and below the AlphaFold2-Multimer baseline of 88.9 and about 75 percent above 80, while also requiring taxonomic labels to pair per-chain MSAs; if plankton contigs lack reliable organism-specific taxonomic labels or correct subunit grouping, complex architecture may be mispredicted. The next contradiction is that [(Gao et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/811af1dbe260cf0f7cf49b58e56c54f67aa885fc) provides only limited quantitative detail, with no numeric accuracy values, sample sizes, p-values, or specific metric names in the supplied excerpt, so its claim of significantly higher accuracy than docking-centric approaches cannot be directly mapped to photosystem I or II complex prediction. The weakest contradiction is [(Ryck et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60d572e3569ea40fc0f7724a6721eed0526583a0), which reports no AlphaFold or AlphaFold-Multimer experimental outcomes, no confidence scores, no complex accuracy metrics, and no comparison to experimentally determined structures; its structure-guided functional annotation results are for Phyre2 analysis of RipAK and RipAY, not for the proposed AlphaFold 3 complex-prediction step.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define an explicit AlphaFold 3 input and confidence-filtering protocol for the plankton transcriptomic workflow: group KEGG-assigned photosystem I and II ORFs into organism-specific chain sets per well, filter by contig coverage, expression, ORF length, taxonomic assignment, and absence of negative-control contamination, provide organism-specific taxonomic labels for MSA pairing, generate MSAs from the de novo contigs plus UniRef30, BFD, MGnify, or equivalent databases, and report MSA depth, pLDDT, predicted aligned error, interface confidence, and clash metrics before interpreting complex architecture. The next most important action is to benchmark AlphaFold 3 against known photosystem I and II structures from related algae, plants, or cyanobacteria and against AlphaFold2, AlphaFold2-Multimer, ColabFold, or AF2Complex baselines using GDT_TS, TM-score, interface contact metrics, and statistical significance, including negative controls with shuffled or non-photosystem chains to estimate false-positive complex formation. The next action is to specify computational reproducibility: state GPU type, number of recycles, sampling, early stopping, batch size, wall-clock time per complex, and a fallback route using ColabFold or AlphaFold2-Multimer if AlphaFold 3 is unavailable or fails on low-coverage contigs, because [(Mirdita et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a20411effeaac9aa457e528090dc274cb46c3412) shows runtime can vary from about 0.5 to 3.5 hours per target and complex prediction requires careful MSA and template handling. The final action is to document limitations and validation boundaries: acknowledge that single-cell transcriptomes may miss lowly expressed subunits, that fragmented rnaSpades contigs may produce incomplete ORFs, that AlphaFold 3 has not been directly validated in the supplied related work for uncultured eukaryotic plankton, and that structural comparisons should be made against PDB or species-specific AlphaFold database subsets using tools such as DALI as suggested by [(Ryck et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60d572e3569ea40fc0f7724a6721eed0526583a0).

## For developmental analysis, we will examine highly expressed contigs from fish egg/larva samples to identify novel proteins related to cell cycle and embryonic development pathways: developmental application method testing the workflow on non-photosynthetic eukaryotic plankton.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed developmental analysis comes from [(Chou et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f0ee4ba02c7a14108972eefb6e7239cfa245034), which used bulk cDNA sequencing, de novo assembly, read mapping, expression estimation, functional annotation, and whole-mount in situ hybridization validation on Platynereis dumerilii embryos collected at 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, and 14 h post-fertilization. This directly supports the proposed use of de novo contigs, highly expressed contigs, and pathway annotation to infer cell cycle and embryonic development processes in a non-model developmental system, and it shows that stage-resolved transcriptomes can reveal maternal-to-zygotic transition and cleavage-stage biology analogous to fish egg/larva samples. The next strongest support is [(Bonnet et al., 2007-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cae47e96a680994ac657a32f0022a198311e1d9), which used fish egg/gamete RNA, cDNA microarrays, and real-time PCR to identify genes with differential, quality-dependent, and developmental-potential-correlated mRNA abundance; this supports the biological premise that fish egg transcriptomes contain actionable developmental markers and that qPCR validation is appropriate, although it does not support single-well RNA sequencing or de novo assembly. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Mazurais et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce4714fa6b5cb57a2e6187aa6386727ee8b6bd4e), a review of fish larval EST, suppression subtractive hybridization, and microarray approaches, which supports the broader use of fish egg/larva transcriptomes for developmental gene discovery but provides only qualitative evidence and no single-cell, optical capture, or quantitative validation. Viewed iteratively, Bonnet 2007 established probe-based fish egg expression, Chou 2016 extended the logic to sequencing and de novo assembly in a non-model embryo, and Mazurais contextualizes fish larval transcriptomics as a developmental application.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most methodologically relevant study, [(Chou et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f0ee4ba02c7a14108972eefb6e7239cfa245034), obtained developmental conclusions from bulk pooled embryos across defined time points and validated candidates by cloning and whole-mount in situ hybridization, whereas the proposed plan relies on single-well Smart-seq2 libraries, approximately 250,000 read pairs per well, and inspection of the top five highly expressed contigs without temporal staging, biological replicates, differential-expression statistics, or spatial validation. This undermines the assumption that highly expressed single-organism contigs alone are sufficient to identify novel cell-cycle or embryonic-development proteins, especially because early developmental transcripts can be stage-specific, low abundance, or masked by maternal transcripts. The next strongest limitation is from [(Bonnet et al., 2007-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cae47e96a680994ac657a32f0022a198311e1d9), which showed that fish egg transcriptomic inference required experimental grouping, quality-dependent abundance analysis, and qPCR validation; the proposed method does not specify sample size, normalization, fold-change thresholds, false-discovery-rate control, or validation, so its claim to identify novel proteins is not yet supported by the evidence base. The weakest contradiction is from [(Mazurais et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce4714fa6b5cb57a2e6187aa6386727ee8b6bd4e), which is qualitative and does not provide evidence that single-organism optical capture, merge-split de novo assembly, ORF prediction, or AlphaFold-based structural inference will outperform bulk EST/microarray approaches for fish developmental gene discovery.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to convert the developmental application into a staged, replicated, and statistically controlled experiment: define the fish species, egg/larva stage, synchronization method, number of individuals per stage, biological and technical replicates, and negative controls; use the brightfield and epifluorescence images to assign developmental stage and exclude damaged or contaminated wells; allocate the planned 66 wells plus 4 negative controls to stages and replicates rather than treating them as an unstructured set; and compare single-well expression against bulk pooled RNA-seq or microarray baselines using TPM/FPKM normalization, differential-expression testing with fold-change and FDR thresholds, and validation of candidate cell-cycle and embryonic-development genes by qPCR as in [(Bonnet et al., 2007-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4cae47e96a680994ac657a32f0022a198311e1d9) and by whole-mount in situ hybridization or immunostaging as in [(Chou et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f0ee4ba02c7a14108972eefb6e7239cfa245034). The next most important improvement is to strengthen the computational workflow: use available fish reference genomes or transcriptomes where possible in addition to rnaSpades de novo assembly, report assembly and contig metrics such as N50, BUSCO completeness, coverage, and ORF length, annotate ORFs with KEGG, InterPro, Pfam, and cell-cycle pathway databases, evaluate merge-split cross-contamination and public/private contig assignment, and determine whether 250,000 read pairs per well provides sufficient depth for private contigs and novel ORF discovery, increasing sequencing depth or pooling low-quality wells if needed. The least critical but useful improvement is to incorporate the candidate-marker logic from [(Mazurais et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce4714fa6b5cb57a2e6187aa6386727ee8b6bd4e) by using known fish developmental and cell-cycle genes as positive controls, filtering for stage-specific expression, and treating AlphaFold 3 structural predictions as downstream validation rather than primary evidence for novel developmental proteins.
