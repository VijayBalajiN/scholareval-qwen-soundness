## Context: This is the central proposed system for testing whether foundation models can improve Boolean logic learning; Method: We will develop a multi-task foundation model for Boolean logic using graph neural networks.

### Support

Strongest support is direct evidence that GNNs can learn Boolean SAT structure from CNF graphs: [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) trained GNNs on uniformly random 3-SAT CNF graphs at clause-to-atom ratios 4.4, 6.6, and 10, balanced 50 percent satisfiable, and beat a random baseline with about 70 percent near the phase shift and about 65 percent elsewhere, supporting the plan's 100-variable uniform-random 3SAT phase-transition dataset and GNN input. Next, [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175) shows GNNs on symbolic graph-structured logic can be extremely sample efficient, reaching 100 percent on bAbI tasks 4, 15, 16, and 18 with 50 examples and fewer than 600 parameters while RNN and LSTM baselines with 50-dimensional embeddings and about 5,000 or 30,000 parameters needed more data or failed, supporting the plan's data-efficiency hypothesis although not SAT. [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) provides the closest architectural precedent: a bipartite CNF graph with variable-assignment and clause nodes, satisfying/dissatisfying edge types, d=64, T=10, three hidden layers of 64 ReLU units, and a single A100, targeting SAT and model counting, supporting GPS++-style message passing, 64-dimensional embeddings, and task heads, although it does not report multi-task pretraining or fine-tuning. [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) supports the planned component ablations, especially sum versus mean pooling, because SUM-MLP GIN-0 outperformed mean variants on structure-only social graphs, reaching 92.4±2.5 on REDDIT-BINARY and 57.5±1.5 on REDDIT-MULTI5K while Mean-MLP collapsed to 50.0±0.0 and 20.0±0.0, and GIN variants outperformed weaker GNNs on all nine datasets. [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) supports benchmarking and dataset construction by building G4SATBench with SR, 3-SAT, CA, PS, k-Clique, k-Dominating Set, and k-Vertex Cover, using 3-SAT near the phase transition with m=4.258n+58.26n^(-2/3), 80k training/10k validation/10k test easy-medium pairs, 10k hard test pairs, RTX8000 GPUs, 16 CPU cores, and about 8000 GPU hours, validating the need for strong GNN baselines and phase-transition instances. [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e) supports the planned RL branching task: a GNN clause-literal graph with 32-dimensional embeddings, two message-passing iterations, policy MLP, evolution strategies, 1,800 training and 200 test instances, outperformed SharpSAT by a factor of 3.0 on grid world and 1.8 on cellular automata and generalized to larger instances, showing GNN policies can learn Boolean solver behavior. [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Chan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/169de5c7d9ad2c40239f34f14948ad037b525554) support unsatisfiable-core detection as a useful learned signal: NeuroCore used 150,000 DRAT-trim core labels from Z3, MiniSat, and Glucose 4.1 and improved SATCOMP-2018 solves from 187 to 205 with MiniSat and from 186 to 206 with Glucose, while IB-Net uses Weighted Literal-Incidence Graphs and Kissat/Drat-trim to guide CDCL, though both are single-task and the IB-Net excerpt lacks numbers. [(Shi et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06547a615390ba14d37c684136c30a4ac559d610) supports multi-task Boolean learning by training SATformer with satisfiability and assignment/probability objectives and a hybrid solver, but uses transformers rather than GNNs. Finally, [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) supports generating near-boundary paired SAT/UNSAT examples with Minisat, although its SR distribution is not the plan's uniform 3SAT.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the most direct prior GNN SAT result is modest: [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) achieved only about 70 percent near the phase shift and about 65 percent elsewhere, with no statistical significance, suggesting a GNN may rely on the clause-to-atom ratio and not learn robust formula-level structure; this challenges the plan's expectation that a 1M-instance foundation model will automatically yield strong SAT, model counting, backbone, core, and branching performance. Next, [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) contradicts any assumption that mean pooling or shallow message passing is sufficient for structure-only graphs: Mean-MLP collapsed to chance on REDDIT-BINARY 50.0±0.0 and REDDIT-MULTI5K 20.0±0.0, while SUM-MLP GIN-0 reached 92.4±2.5 and 57.5±1.5, and training accuracy did not exceed the WL subtree kernel, implying GPS++ with eight layers and 64-dimensional embeddings may be expressively limited for 100-variable CNF graphs unless sum aggregation, MLPs, and possibly non-WL features are used. [(Shi et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06547a615390ba14d37c684136c30a4ac559d610) weakly contradicts the GNN-centric choice by reporting that SATformer, a hierarchical transformer with a multi-task satisfiability/assignment objective, outperformed NeuroSAT and improved hybrid solving, but the excerpt lacks exact numbers, so this is not strong evidence that transformers are better. [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e) partially contradicts the data-hungry premise for all tasks: with only 1,800 training instances per structured dataset, 1,000 evolution-strategy iterations, 96 mirror-sampled perturbations and 768 episodes per update, a GNN branching policy outperformed SharpSAT and generalized upward, suggesting some Boolean tasks may not require 1M uniform 3SAT examples, although the plan's multi-task foundation may still benefit. [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Chan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/169de5c7d9ad2c40239f34f14948ad037b525554) contradict the novelty/need for a foundation model for core extraction because single-task core-variable predictors already improve CDCL solvers, with NeuroCore solving 205 versus 187 SATCOMP-2018 problems and IB-Net targeting Kissat-guided core prediction, yet neither demonstrates that multi-task pretraining or fine-tuning is necessary; IB-Net also reports no quantitative results in the excerpt. [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) suggests the plan's compute budget may be underpowered: G4SATBench's limited GNN SAT benchmarking cost about 8,000 GPU hours on RTX8000 GPUs for SR and 3-SAT tasks, whereas the plan allocates 24 A100 hours for 1M instances and 16 tasks, although the comparison is not exact. Finally, [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) and [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175) limit transferability rather than directly contradict: SR(n) uses variable count n, clause length mean slightly above 4, Minisat stopping at first UNSAT, and paired single-literal flips, while bAbI uses symbolic stories with fewer than 600-parameter GNNs, so neither validates the plan's 100-variable uniform 3SAT, 16-task, fine-tuning protocol.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add rigorous baselines and statistics: compare the foundation model against from-scratch GPS++/GIN, the Bünz ratio classifier, NeuroSAT, G4SATBench GNN baselines, CDCL/LS, and solver baselines such as Kissat/MiniSat, using multiple seeds, confidence intervals, paired significance tests, and task-specific metrics such as accuracy, R2, model-counting error, core F1, branching steps, solver runtime, and cactus plots. Second, adjust the architecture based on [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9): default to sum aggregation with MLPs, test GIN epsilon, avoid mean pooling unless ablated, and consider adding edge-type features, clause/variable degree or solver-derived features, an attention or transformer baseline inspired by [(Shi et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06547a615390ba14d37c684136c30a4ac559d610), and possibly more than eight layers or 64 dimensions if WL expressivity limits are observed. Third, refine dataset construction: for 100-variable 3SAT use the phase-transition clause count implied by [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), m=4.258n+58.26n^(-2/3) about 428 to 429 clauses, verify labels with a modern solver, balance satisfiable and unsatisfiable examples, and include SR-style paired near-boundary examples from [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) plus structured distributions to prevent ratio shortcuts and support OOD. Fourth, align core and branching supervision with proven pipelines: use DRAT-trim or Drat-trim with Kissat, MiniSat, or Z3 as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Chan et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/169de5c7d9ad2c40239f34f14948ad037b525554), and for RL branching use episode-based rewards, step caps, and solver metrics as in [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e). Fifth, re-budget compute: 24 A100 hours may be insufficient for 1M instances, 16 tasks, and ablations given [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) 8000 GPU hours and [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) A100 message-passing setup, so use subsampled pretraining, mixed precision, early stopping, checkpoint selection, and report GPU-hours. Sixth, improve multi-task training: replace simple mean loss with task-weighted or uncertainty-weighted losses, normalize regression targets, and evaluate leave-one-task-out, frozen versus full fine-tuning, and single-task pretraining controls. Finally, expand OOD evaluation to 4SAT, 5SAT, controlled backbones, small-world coloring, quasi-group, spectrum repacking, and size generalization to 150 to 600 variables, using the upward generalization evidence from [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e) as a target while reporting distribution-shift metrics.

## Context: The plan requires a large, shared training corpus to support multi-task pretraining; Method: We will compile a dataset of one million uniform-random 3SAT instances at the solubility phase transition, each with 100 variables.

### Support

The strongest support for compiling a one-million uniform-random 3SAT corpus at the solubility phase transition with 100 variables comes from [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), the newest direct phase-transition 3SAT benchmark, which extends the older near-threshold SAT pair construction in [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) and the solver-generated core corpus in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) to a GNN benchmark. It builds G4SATBench from synthetic CNF generators and explicitly generates random 3-SAT at the phase-transition region using m = 4.258n + 58.26n^{-2/3}, with medium instances spanning 40 to 200 variables and 80k satisfiable/unsatisfiable training pairs, 10k validation pairs, and 10k test pairs. This directly validates phase-transition 3SAT graphs as a GNN benchmark for satisfiability, satisfying assignment, and unsat-core variable prediction, and it supplies a concrete clause-count formula that can be instantiated at n=100, approximately 429 clauses, for the proposed corpus. It also provides an evaluation protocol with three random seeds, validation checkpoint selection, and a single RTX8000 GPU plus 16 AMD EPYC 7502 cores with about 8,000 GPU hours total, which makes the proposed 1M-instance, 16-task, 24-hour A100 pretraining plan more plausible while showing that prior phase-transition 3SAT work is much smaller. The next strongest support is [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6), which constructs near-threshold SAT pairs by adding clauses until Minisat declares unsatisfiability and then flipping one literal in the critical clause, showing that minimally different satisfiable and unsatisfiable pairs near the threshold are a high-value training signal for satisfiability classification, although it uses variable clause length with mean slightly above 4 rather than fixed 3SAT. [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) supports large generated SAT corpora by creating 150,000 unsatisfiable-core examples from SATCOMP and scheduling seeds with Z3 60-second timeouts, DRAT-trim core extraction, and March branching, and by improving neuro-minisat from 187 to 205 solved SATCOMP-2018 instances and neuro-glucose from 186 to 206 under a 5,000-second timeout on 400 parallel processes across 8 identical 64-core machines and 20 GPUs, demonstrating that generated SAT data can transfer to solver performance, though the corpus is not uniform 3SAT and is smaller than one million. [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2) provides further support that generated SAT instances can be useful for solver-related learning, with HardSATGEN reducing relative error to benchmark graph statistics by 38.5%, reducing VIG clustering error from 47.37% to 1.75% on SP4, recovering exact SAT solver ranking with 100% accuracy on LEC-CORP, and improving Glucose on MP1 by 22.19% and CaDiCaL on LEC-CORP from -7.02% to +15.03%, although its instances are real-world and dataset-specific rather than uniform 3SAT. [(Zhang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9f0145a34d9903123e9c87f1524de27698f63a) supports the broader pretraining idea by using tens of millions of generated small SAT instances to pretrain NLocalSAT before fine-tuning on SAT Competition random-track instances, but the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, so this support is weaker. [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) supports the proposed graph representation and embedding scale by using a clause-variable bipartite graph, d=64 features, 10 message-passing iterations, MLPs with 3 hidden layers of 64 units, ReLU activations, and an A100 GPU for SAT and model counting, although it reports no results in the excerpt. [(Mandl et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1965cfb79c7f957ad053c24da6d81d8504d6df62) supports random 3SAT phase-transition generation by using n=10, m=15 to 80, alpha=1.5 to 8, 400 instances per m, and PySAT ground truth, but the scale is tiny. [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175) offers indirect support for GNN sample efficiency on symbolic reasoning, reaching perfect test accuracy on bAbI tasks 4, 15, and 16 with 50 examples and fewer than 600 parameters, but it is not SAT and does not address 100-variable 3SAT. [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34) provides the weakest support, showing that a GNN can be trained on existing LEC and SAT Competition corpora for solver selection with a single Tesla V100, learning rate 1e-3, up to 100 epochs, and a 500-second cutoff, but it does not construct a one-million uniform-random 3SAT multi-task corpus.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction comes from newer structure-aware generation work, [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), which contradicts the older assumption that generic generated SAT data is sufficient by showing that HardSATGEN with GCN or GraphSAGE, node splitting, core scrambling, and tie-in post-processing reduced relative error to benchmark graph statistics by 38.5%, reduced VIG clustering error from 47.37% to 1.75% on SP4, recovered exact SAT solver ranking with 100% accuracy on LEC-CORP, and improved Glucose on MP1 by 22.19% and CaDiCaL on LEC-CORP from -7.02% to +15.03%, suggesting that a corpus of purely uniform-random 100-variable 3SAT may underrepresent the structural and hardness features needed for solver transfer, even though the plan includes OOD non-random tasks. The next strongest contradiction is that large generated SAT data that demonstrably improves solvers can be much smaller and solver-derived rather than one million uniform instances: [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) used only 150,000 unsatisfiable-core examples generated from SATCOMP and scheduling instances with Z3, DRAT-trim, and March, yet improved neuro-minisat from 187 to 205 solved SATCOMP-2018 instances and neuro-glucose from 186 to 206, and no statistical significance tests are reported, implying that instance hardness and task-specific labels may matter more than raw uniform scale. [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) also contradicts the fixed uniform-3SAT assumption by constructing near-threshold pairs with variable clause length sampled around mean slightly above 4 and minimal literal flips, showing that the most informative threshold data may not be fixed 3SAT. [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) provides a weaker contradiction to the necessity of one million instances because its phase-transition 3SAT benchmark uses 80k training pairs, 10k validation pairs, and 10k test pairs, with medium instances spanning 40 to 200 variables rather than a fixed 100-variable corpus, and it does not show that 1M instances are required for strong GNN performance. [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175) weakly contradicts the need for massive data by achieving perfect bAbI test accuracy with 50 examples and fewer than 600 parameters, although the domain is symbolic QA rather than SAT. [(Mandl et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1965cfb79c7f957ad053c24da6d81d8504d6df62) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34) provide the next weakest contradictions: the former uses only n=10 and 400 instances per clause count, and the latter trains on existing LEC and SAT Competition corpora rather than a 1M uniform 3SAT corpus, so they do not directly refute the proposed scale but show that related GNN SAT work has succeeded without it. The weakest contradiction is that [(Zhang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9f0145a34d9903123e9c87f1524de27698f63a) and [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) do not provide quantitative evidence that large pretraining or 10 message-passing iterations will make the proposed 1M-instance, 8-layer plan superior, leaving the scale and depth assumptions under-supported.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define and validate the exact phase-transition generation protocol for n=100 before compiling one million instances. Use the G4SATBench formula m = 4.258n + 58.26n^{-2/3} from [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), which gives approximately 429 clauses at n=100, and sample a band around alpha 4.2 to 4.3, for example 420 to 440 clauses, rather than a single m value. Specify that each clause uses three distinct variables, literal negation probability 0.5, duplicate clauses are removed, and ground truth is computed with PySAT or Minisat as in [(Mandl et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1965cfb79c7f957ad053c24da6d81d8504d6df62) and [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6), then report the satisfiable/unsatisfiable ratio, clause-count distribution, and a small validation set before full generation. The second most important action is to add paired, minimally flipped, and solver-derived hard instances or labels to the corpus, because [(Selsam et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe257027193ea4a74fdab99d7509ce4002ad7de6) shows near-threshold pairs created by flipping one literal in a critical clause are useful, and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) shows DRAT-trim unsat cores, solver branching, and NeuroCore-style schedules can generate task-relevant labels and improve solvers. This would directly support the plan's sixteen tasks, especially unsatisfiable core detection, backbone prediction, and RL-based branching. The third most important action is to include a structure-aware or real-world augmentation ablation, motivated by [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), by reserving a small fraction of pretraining for SATCOMP random-track, LEC, or HardSATGEN-style generated instances, or by adding node splitting, core scrambling, and tie-in features to the OOD evaluation, so the model is not overfit to purely uniform 3SAT. The fourth most important action is to align the architecture and compute budget with the strongest related evidence: use the clause-variable bipartite graph, 64-dimensional embeddings, and test 8 versus 10 message-passing layers as in [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a), while using three random seeds, validation checkpoint selection, and grid search as in [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576); because that work used about 8,000 GPU hours for a much smaller 80k-pair benchmark, the plan's 24-hour A100 pretraining for one million 100-variable instances and sixteen tasks should be stress-tested with mixed precision, graph batching, subsampling, or a longer budget. The fifth most important action is to strengthen evaluation by comparing against NeuroSAT and GGNN with UNS2 loss from [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), MiniSat, Glucose, Kissat, and CaDiCaL from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), and SATzilla12 from [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34), while reporting accuracy, R2, F1, solver runtime, cactus plots, and statistical significance across seeds. The least important but still useful action is to document reproducibility details that are missing in several related works, including random seeds, clause-sampling with or without repetition, duplicate-clause handling, storage format, feature-computation overhead as in [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34), hardware, optimizer, batch size 20, learning rate 1e-4, and the exact 16-task loss weighting.

## Context: The dataset must support many Boolean logic objectives, so labels are generated for a broad task suite; Method: We will generate ground truth labels for sixteen prediction tasks spanning ten different categories, including satisfiability prediction, model counting, backbone prediction, unsatisfiable core detection, RL-based branching, and various instance-level and variable/clause-level properties derived from existing SAT solver features.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed sixteen-task label-generation method comes from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which extends the older multi-task principle in [(Caruana, 1997-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161ffb54a3fdf0715b198bb57bd22f910242eb49) to SAT-specific solver-derived labels: it generated 150,000 binary variable-in-core labels by running Z3 for 60 seconds, extracting DRAT proofs with DRAT-trim, and training NeuroCore, after which neuro-minisat solved 205 of 400 SATCOMP-2018 main-track problems within 5,000 seconds versus 187 for MiniSat, a 10 percent increase, with satisfiable instances improving from 109 to 125 and unsatisfiable instances from 78 to 80, while neuro-glucose solved 206 versus 186, an 11 percent increase, although no statistical significance tests were reported. This newer SAT-specific result supports the proposed inclusion of unsatisfiable-core detection and variable-level solver-feature labels, and it shows that solver-derived supervision can transfer into practical SAT performance. The next strongest support is [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), which iterates on earlier graph-based SAT generation such as [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212) by constructing G4SATBench, a synthetic CNF benchmark near the 3-SAT phase transition that evaluates multiple SAT-related prediction tasks, including satisfiability prediction, satisfying assignment prediction, and unsat-core variable prediction, using 80k training pairs for easy and medium datasets, 10k validation pairs, 10k test pairs, three random seeds, and validation-based checkpoint selection; although the excerpt lacks numeric accuracies, its task taxonomy and phase-transition 3-SAT setting closely align with the proposed 100-variable uniform-random 3SAT label suite. The older foundational evidence is [(Caruana, 1997-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161ffb54a3fdf0715b198bb57bd22f910242eb49), which found that adding a related function as an extra output improved the primary task more than adding it as an extra input in a backprop multi-task experiment with 20 inputs, 16 hidden units, 50 training patterns, 1000 test cases, early stopping, and 50 trials, supporting the proposed use of multiple auxiliary task heads rather than feeding auxiliary labels as inputs. Additional support comes from [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), which is newer than [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212) and extends graph generation from structural fitting to solver-derived hardness, reducing average relative structural error by 38.5%, improving SP4 VIG clustering relative error from 47.37% to 1.75%, recovering exact solver ranking on LEC-CORP with 100% accuracy, and improving MP1 runtime by 22.19% using Kissat, CaDiCaL, and Glucose, indicating that solver-related Boolean structure is learnable and useful. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Mishchenko et al., 2006-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/534ab7bb74a222a19f20ab7443db1f6f225696d9), an earlier algorithmic flow that uses SAT solver conflict counts, activity, and J-frontier variable ordering in ABC on a 1.6 GHz laptop with 1 GB RAM and a 1800-second timeout, reports that all reported miters were solved within four iterations, and finds the integrated checker more robust than direct MiniSat and CSAT on industrial unsatisfiable benchmarks, showing that instance- and variable-level SAT signals are operationally meaningful; [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34), a newer GNN solver-selection method trained on LEC and SAT Competition data with a Tesla V100, learning rate 1e-3, and 100 epochs that reports better average runtime and solved-within-500-second performance than competitors, supporting GNN use over SAT instances; and [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212) and [(Li et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c4bb681fe5cb159b0d577b784fa52c952871e17), earlier and intermediate evidence that graph representations and solver-derived supervision can be used for SAT formulas, with You et al. fitting graph statistics on ten small SATLIB formulas of 82 to 1122 variables and 327 to 4555 clauses and Li et al. evaluating DeepSAT against NeuroSAT on in-sample, random k-SAT, and novel distributions, though the excerpts lack the numeric SAT solver outcomes needed to strongly support the proposed scale.

### Contradictions

The most direct contradiction to the proposed method's assumption that a GNN foundation model trained on uniform-random 3SAT labels will generalize broadly is [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f), which is newer than earlier GNN SAT solver work such as NeuroSAT used in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Li et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c4bb681fe5cb159b0d577b784fa52c952871e17), and reports that NeuroSAT and a reimplemented DG-DAGRNN predicted zero correct SAT assignments on a deliberately small set of 10 symmetric AIGs with at most 3 inputs, using two RTX 3090 GPUs, Adam, and an R-layer outcome dimension of 10; this shows that existing GNN SAT solvers can fail on structured symmetry even when they work on other random or circuit datasets, challenging the plan's out-of-distribution and size-generalization claims. A second important contradiction is that the proposed one-million uniform-random 100-variable 3SAT corpus may be too narrow for a foundation model, because [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), which is newer than [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212), demonstrates that solver hardness and solver ranking are strongly tied to non-random industrial and generated structure, with exact 100% solver-ranking recovery on LEC-CORP, a 22.19% MP1 runtime reduction, and positive LEC-CORP tuning gains only when generated instances preserve solver-derived hardness, suggesting that labels from uniform random 3SAT alone may not capture the variable/clause-level properties needed for real Boolean logic tasks. A third contradiction concerns label reliability and coverage: [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), while supporting SAT-specific label mining, also contradicts the assumption of clean uniform labels because it generated core labels only from unsatisfiable subproblems, used a 60-second Z3 timeout, stopped after 150,000 datapoints, and explicitly noted that the process was not guaranteed to produce diverse cores, so the proposed sixteen-task label suite must avoid a similar bias toward unsat-only, timeout-dependent, or solver-specific labels. A fourth contradiction is computational feasibility: [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) reports approximately 8,000 GPU hours for G4SATBench experiments on a single RTX8000 GPU and 16 AMD EPYC 7502 CPU cores, and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) used 400 parallel processes on 8 identical 64-core machines plus 20 GPUs for solver-integrated evaluation, suggesting that generating and training on one million 100-variable instances with sixteen labels may exceed the proposed 24-hour A100 pretraining budget if label generation, proof extraction, and multi-task training are not carefully costed. Weaker contradictions include [(Caruana, 1997-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161ffb54a3fdf0715b198bb57bd22f910242eb49), whose older multi-task benefit was demonstrated on small synthetic functions with 50 training patterns and 50 trials but without reported numeric values, effect sizes, or statistical significance, so it does not establish the magnitude of benefit for the proposed large Boolean setting; [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34), a newer GNN solver-selection method that was not the most accurate at selecting the optimal solver despite better average runtime, indicating that GNN SAT-feature models can optimize the wrong objective; and [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212) and [(Li et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5c4bb681fe5cb159b0d577b784fa52c952871e17), earlier and intermediate work with limited quantitative SAT solver evidence, with You et al. using only ten small SATLIB formulas and Li et al. reporting no numeric DeepSAT versus NeuroSAT outcomes, making them weak grounds for the proposed scale.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a pilot label-generation study before committing to one million instances: generate 10k to 100k 100-variable 3SAT instances near the phase transition, compute all sixteen labels using fixed solver versions, DRAT or proof-based core extraction, and explicit sat and unsat coverage, then measure label diversity, class balance, timeout rates, solver-specific variance, storage, CPU time, and GPU training cost, with confidence intervals and significance tests, because [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) shows proof-based core labels are useful but also warns about unsat-only, timeout-dependent, and non-diverse label generation, while [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) indicate that large SAT-GNN experiments can require substantial GPU and CPU resources. Second, broaden the training and evaluation distribution beyond uniform random 3SAT by adding non-random, industrial, symmetry-controlled, and generated instances, including the plan's small-world graph coloring, quasi-group completion, spectrum repacking, 4SAT, 5SAT, controlled backbone instances, and possibly LEC-style or G2SAT-style generated formulas, because [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f) shows newer evidence that GNN SAT solvers can fail on symmetric circuits and [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2) shows that newer solver-hardness-aware generation outperforms older structural-only generation. Third, revise the compute plan: the proposed 24-hour A100 pretraining budget should be treated as a lower bound, with a separate budget for label generation, proof trimming, caching, and multi-task training, and the experiment should report GPU hours, CPU hours, memory, and wall-clock time, since [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) used about 8,000 GPU hours for benchmarking and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) used 20 GPUs for solver-integrated evaluation. Fourth, implement the sixteen tasks as auxiliary output heads rather than auxiliary inputs, following the older but directly relevant multi-task evidence in [(Caruana, 1997-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161ffb54a3fdf0715b198bb57bd22f910242eb49), and monitor whether noisy auxiliary outputs degrade primary tasks. Fifth, derive variable/clause-level labels from multiple solver artifacts, such as DRAT cores, conflict counts, activity, and frontier-based ordering, as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Mishchenko et al., 2006-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/534ab7bb74a222a19f20ab7443db1f6f225696d9), but validate them against independent solvers to avoid encoding a single solver's heuristics. Sixth, add symmetry-aware evaluation and, if needed, canonicalization or equivariant GNN components, because of the zero-solution failure in [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f). Seventh, strengthen statistical evaluation by using multiple seeds, leave-one-task-out, data subsampling at 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances, frozen versus full fine-tuning, cactus/scatter plots, solved counts, accuracy/R2, and comparisons against MiniSat, Glucose, CaDiCaL, Kissat, NeuroSAT, and single-task baselines, as suggested by the evaluation practices in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), and [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34). Finally, consider adding structural graph-statistics labels or solver-selection tasks only as auxiliary objectives if they improve shared representations, based on the weaker but relevant evidence in [(You et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d6388c4df36ac53bc614dc33312551f6edca6212), [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3a6288b25b19678874e588cdc52f7357d17f34b2), and [(Zhang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d481f54186caadc7b041d8cc4d447117f4209b34).

## Context: The model needs a graph representation suited to CNF formulas; Method: We will adapt the GPS++ architecture, representing CNF formulae as clause-variable bipartite graphs with one-hot encoded inputs.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed clause-variable bipartite GNN representation comes from [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a), which encodes CNF formulas as an undirected bipartite graph with variable-assignment and clause nodes, typed satisfaction edges, 64-dimensional embeddings, 10 message-passing iterations, three-hidden-layer 64-unit ReLU MLPs, and an A100 GPU, closely matching the plan's 64-dimensional message-passing clause-variable graph and A100 training setting, although it does not report quantitative outcomes or use one-hot inputs. This newer NSNet work extends the earlier clause-literal GNN evidence of [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e) and the earlier random-3SAT GNN prediction of [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) by making the CNF graph structure and message-passing depth more explicit. The next strongest support is [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e), which builds on earlier GNN SAT work by using a clause-literal bipartite graph with 32-dimensional trainable embeddings, two message-passing iterations, ReLU MLPs, and a policy head, and quantitatively outperforms SharpSAT on i.i.d. test sets, including a 3.0x reduction in branching steps on grid world and 1.8x on cell, supporting the plan's clause-variable/literal graph and branching/model-counting tasks, but with smaller embeddings, fewer layers, and single-task RL rather than a multi-task foundation model. Strong support for the foundation-model premise comes from the newer [(Duan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c654d4bf5f3f3f1af40cc3bf2f8e87bdf80a43f), which extends supervised GNN SAT work by showing that self-supervised representations trained with label-preserving augmentations improve low-label linear probing by 9.42 percent on SR, 14.07 percent on PR, 30.81 percent on DP, and 22.18 percent on PS with 10 labels, and that 100 labels match or exceed supervised models trained with 10,000 labels, supporting the plan's data-efficiency goal, although the experiments use 40-variable instances and a NeuroSAT-style encoder rather than GPS++ one-hot clause-variable graphs. Support for the plan's uniform-random 3SAT phase-transition dataset comes from the earlier [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c), which trains and tests GNNs on balanced random 3SAT at clause-to-atom ratios 4.4, 6.6, and 10, achieving about 70 percent test accuracy at 6.6 and about 65 percent at 4.4 and 10, showing that GNNs can beat random baselines near the phase transition, but without GPS++, one-hot inputs, or significance tests. Support for learned CNF representations and core-related tasks comes from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which generate 150,000 unsat-core labels from Z3 and DRAT-trim, integrate a neural core predictor into MiniSat and Glucose, and improve SATCOMP-2018 main-track solves from 187 to 205 with neuro-minisat and from 186 to 206 with neuro-glucose under a 5,000-second timeout, supporting the plan's core-detection task and solver-derived labels, but as a single-task predictor rather than a multi-task foundation model. Weaker support comes from the newer [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062), which shows a learned diffusion SAT sampler can process 100-variable 3SAT instances at clause/variable ratio 4.3 in about 0.06 seconds per instance with 32 steps and can solve 100 percent of 3-Clique instances with 128 steps, supporting the feasibility of learned models on 100-variable 3SAT and GPU-batched inference, but not the proposed GPS++ classifier. The weakest support is the earliest work, [(Domshlak, and Hoffmann, 2007-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ac5865c619fb1432ec387ef7ffd9089f548a2f4), which uses an implication graph over a weighted CNF belief state for probabilistic planning, showing that graph-structured Boolean/CNF representations can encode dependencies, but it is not a GNN, not a clause-variable bipartite graph, and reports no quantitative planner performance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the closest quantitative clause-variable/literal GNN evidence does not validate one-hot encoded GPS++ inputs; [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e) uses trainable 32-dimensional literal and clause embeddings, two message-passing iterations, and a policy head, and outperforms SharpSAT, suggesting that learned embeddings may be more effective than fixed one-hot inputs for clause-variable/literal graphs. A related structural contradiction is that the newer [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) represents CNF with variable-assignment nodes and satisfaction-typed edges rather than simple one-hot variable nodes, implying that edge polarity and assignment-level features may be important for message passing and that the proposed one-hot clause-variable graph may omit useful structure. A direct contradiction to broad GNN SAT generalization comes from the newer [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f), where NeuRoSAT and DG-DAGRNN failed to predict any SAT solutions on 10 small symmetric AIGs, indicating that GNN SAT methods can fail on symmetric or circuit-like instances and that the plan's OOD and size-generalization claims need explicit symmetry tests; this newer failure mode tempers the earlier optimistic GNN SAT results of [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) and [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e). Contradictory evidence for the foundation-model pretraining strategy comes from [(Duan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c654d4bf5f3f3f1af40cc3bf2f8e87bdf80a43f), where label-altering augmentations were not much better than random initialization under linear evaluation and hurt supervised performance, reducing accuracy by 20.21 percent on DP(40) with 10,000 labels, so multi-task or augmentation-based pretraining that changes labels could degrade the plan's shared representations. Contradictory or limiting evidence for the solver-integration and evaluation design comes from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which are single-task core predictors rather than multi-task foundation models, do not report statistical significance or confidence intervals, and coarsely tuned the hyperparameter kappa using SATCOMP-2018 runs, raising evaluation-hygiene concerns for the plan's fine-tuning comparisons. Weaker contradictory evidence is that the earlier [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) achieves only about 70 percent accuracy on balanced random 3SAT near the phase transition and provides no significance tests, suggesting that satisfiability prediction remains difficult and that the plan's 16-task 100-variable model may not achieve high accuracy without strong baselines and careful evaluation. Further, the newer [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062) reports that QuerySat is better than DiffusionSat on 3-SAT, and the method is a single-task sampler rather than a multi-task GNN foundation model, so it does not support the proposed architecture. The weakest contradiction is the earliest [(Domshlak, and Hoffmann, 2007-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ac5865c619fb1432ec387ef7ffd9089f548a2f4), which provides no quantitative planner performance and uses an exact probabilistic implication/chance graph rather than a GNN, so it neither validates nor directly refutes the proposed method.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add an architecture ablation for the proposed GPS++ clause-variable bipartite graph, comparing one-hot variable/clause inputs against learned embeddings, edge polarity or satisfaction-typed edges, and an assignment-node variant inspired by the newer [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) and the earlier quantitative clause-literal GNN of [(Vaezipoor et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8411a6d999fbb2e422d954f2fbd0489d78c65a7e), while keeping the plan's 64-dimensional embeddings, eight message-passing layers, two-layer MLP encoder, Adam learning rate 0.0001, and batch size 20 as the baseline. The next most important action is to design the pretraining and multi-task loss to avoid label-altering corruption, using label-preserving augmentations and task heads with clear label semantics, and to evaluate linear probing, full fine-tuning, and frozen-head fine-tuning in the low-label regime as in [(Duan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c654d4bf5f3f3f1af40cc3bf2f8e87bdf80a43f). It should also compare multi-task pretraining against single-task pretrained models, because [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) show that single-task learned SAT components can already improve solvers, so the foundation-model benefit must be demonstrated over strong single-task baselines. Third, the dataset construction should follow the strongest data-generation evidence from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) by using solver-derived labels, DRAT-trimmed unsat cores, diverse core generation, and strict separation between hyperparameter tuning and evaluation sets, while retaining the plan's one-million 100-variable uniform-random 3SAT phase-transition instances and balanced satisfiability as in [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c). Fourth, the evaluation should explicitly test symmetry and circuit-like failure modes from [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f), including small symmetric AIGs, medium SR(n) circuit instances, and large AIGEN instances, in addition to the plan's non-random OOD distributions and 150-600-variable size generalization. Fifth, the plan should report statistical significance, confidence intervals, cactus or scatter plots, runtime, memory, and convergence curves, because several related works lack significance tests or report only aggregate solve counts, as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c). Finally, the compute plan should be profiled before committing to 24 hours of A100 pretraining on one million 100-variable graphs, using evidence that 64-dimensional message-passing CNF models can run on a single A100 [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a), that neural SAT solvers required 20 GPUs and 8 64-core machines for evaluation [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), and that 100-variable 3SAT inference can be batched efficiently [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062), with fallbacks such as mixed precision, graph batching, subsampling, or distributed training if the 24-hour budget is exceeded.

## Context: The architecture specifies how graph information is encoded, propagated, pooled, and mapped to tasks; Method: Our model will consist of: (1) pre-GNN node and edge encoders, (2) a sequence of message-passing layers that iteratively update node and edge embeddings, (3) a shared-embedding layer that pools and concatenates graph and node level embeddings, and (4) task-specific heads for each of the sixteen tasks.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9), which directly validates the proposed message-passing core: GIN uses sum aggregation over neighbor multisets followed by an MLP, and GIN-0 nearly fits training sets while mean, max, and one-layer variants underfit. On structure-dependent social graphs with uninformative node features, SUM-MLP achieved 92.4 +/- 2.5 on REDDIT-BINARY and 57.5 +/- 1.5 on REDDIT-MULTI5K, versus 50.0 +/- 0.0 and 20.0 +/- 0.0 for MEAN-MLP without informative features, and even 71.2 +/- 4.6 and 41.3 +/- 2.1 with degree features; this supports the plan's one-hot clause-variable inputs, its mean-versus-sum pooling ablation, and the use of sum-like graph readout in the shared-embedding layer. It also supports task-specific heads because GIN maps pooled graph structure to classification, although it does not test 16 Boolean tasks. As an iterative line, the 2022 CNF works extend this message-passing idea to Boolean inference. Weaker but relevant support is from [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a), which uses a 64-dimensional CNF GNN with 10 message-passing iterations, 3-hidden-layer 64-unit MLPs, ReLU activations, and an A100 GPU, closely matching the proposed 64-dimensional embeddings, 8 message-passing layers, MLP encoders, and A100 training budget; although no numerical SAT or model-counting results are provided, this makes the proposed scale plausible. The weakest support is from [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f), which uses clause-variable bipartite graphs for 100-variable 3-SAT at the phase transition and attention-based message passing, confirming the domain-relevant graph representation and the value of attention, but it reports only qualitative attention visualizations, no accuracy, F1, runtime, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance, and uses 128-dimensional embeddings, 26 iterations, and 8-head attention, so it cannot strongly validate the proposed 8-layer/64-dimensional multi-task foundation model.

### Contradictions

The strongest quantitative tension is with any mean-pooling variant in the proposed shared-embedding layer: [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) found MEAN-MLP collapsed to 50.0 +/- 0.0 on REDDIT-BINARY and 20.0 +/- 0.0 on REDDIT-MULTI5K when node features were uninformative, versus 92.4 +/- 2.5 and 57.5 +/- 1.5 for SUM-MLP, and even with degree features it reached only 71.2 +/- 4.6 and 41.3 +/- 2.1; this directly warns against treating mean pooling as equivalent to sum pooling in a structure-dependent Boolean graph with one-hot inputs. A weaker, non-quantitative tension is that [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f) uses 26 message-passing iterations, 128-dimensional embeddings, and two 8-head attention layers on 100-variable phase-transition 3-SAT, whereas the proposed plan uses 8 message-passing layers and 64-dimensional embeddings; although the paper reports no accuracy, F1, runtime, or baseline comparisons, its architecture suggests that 100-variable SAT may require more propagation depth or attention capacity than the proposed compact GPS++ configuration. The weakest tension is representational: [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) encodes CNF formulas with two assignment nodes per variable and typed satisfying/dissatisfying edges to approximate belief propagation, while the proposed method uses a clause-variable bipartite graph with one-hot inputs; this does not prove the proposed representation is inferior, but it indicates that the proposed graph may not capture assignment-level probabilistic messages as directly as a BP-inspired model, especially for model counting and unsatisfiable-core tasks.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should make sum aggregation the default in the message-passing readout and shared-embedding layer, and explicitly test GIN-style variants such as sum+MLP, sum+linear, mean+MLP, and max+MLP on the 100-variable 3-SAT data, reporting training loss/accuracy as an expressivity check and test accuracy/R2 as generalization, because [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) shows sum+MLP is materially better when features are weak. Second, add an attention and depth ablation: include a Chang-style clause-variable attention model with 128-dimensional embeddings, 26 iterations, and 8-head attention, or at least a smaller 16-layer/128-dimensional variant, to test whether the proposed 8-layer/64-dimensional GPS++ is underpowered for phase-transition SAT, and report convergence curves, runtime, and A100 cost. Third, because the supplied related work does not demonstrate a 16-task Boolean foundation model, the experiment should report leave-one-task-out fine-tuning versus from-scratch with 100/1k/10k/100k subsamples, frozen versus full fine-tuning, single-task pretraining, and statistical significance, so that the foundation-model claim is not inferred solely from architectural plausibility. Fourth, incorporate [(Li, and Si, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/179fa640e76f5cf4bc2020653e1eb8cd08bdd68a) assignment-node/typed-edge design as an alternative encoder or edge-type feature, using two assignment nodes per variable and satisfying/dissatisfying edge types, with 64-dimensional 3-hidden-layer MLPs and 10 iterations, to improve model counting and unsatisfiable-core extraction. Fifth, adopt the more conservative SAT training controls from [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f) as baselines: balanced satisfying/unsatisfying instances, 8:1:1 splits, Adam with 2e-5, gradient clipping at 0.65, L2 regularization, and parameter scaling at 1e-10, while still testing the proposed 1e-4 learning rate and batch size 20.

## Context: Multi-task pretraining is implemented through a joint objective over the task suite; Method: We will optimize multiple tasks simultaneously using a mean loss across all tasks, with mean squared error for regression tasks and cross-entropy for classification tasks.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed mean-loss multi-task pretraining method comes from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which shows that a learned Boolean-logic predictor can be integrated into CDCL solvers and improve solving performance, validating the practical value of one of the plan's sixteen tasks, unsatisfiable core detection. That work generated 150,000 binary variable-core datapoints by running Z3 on SATCOMP instances through 2017 and hard scheduling problems, using DRAT-trim on DRAT proofs to label variables in unsat cores, and then trained neuro-minisat and neuro-glucose; neuro-minisat solved 205 of 400 SATCOMP-2018 problems versus 187 for MiniSat under a 5,000-second timeout, with satisfiable instances improving from 109 to 125 and unsatisfiable instances from 78 to 80, while neuro-glucose solved 206 versus 186. This supports the plan's focus on unsat-core detection and suggests that learned graph/solver features can yield downstream solver gains, although it does not test the proposed joint 16-task objective. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367), which demonstrates that a shared message-passing neural network with task-specific readout can be trained on multiple property targets and evaluated with target-specific normalized error ratios, mirroring the proposed GPS++ GNN with shared embeddings and task-specific heads. That work's use of explicit hydrogens, edge features, set2set readout, and normalized MAE also supports the plan's need for task-specific evaluation and careful architecture ablations, even though its multi-task result is negative.

### Contradictions

The strongest available contradiction is direct: [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367) found that joint training on all 13 QM9 targets with a single message-passing network was consistently worse than training separate models for each target, with individual-target models achieving lower normalized MAE and improvements of up to 40% for some targets; the best individual model was further improved by ensembling five low-validation-error models. This directly challenges the proposed method's use of a simple mean loss across 16 heterogeneous Boolean-logic tasks, because it shows that a shared multi-task objective can underperform task-specific training when tasks are combined without task-specific weighting or normalization. A second, more indirect contradiction comes from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which provides a strong single-task baseline for unsat-core prediction rather than evidence for multi-task pretraining. Its neuro-minisat and neuro-glucose gains, while positive, were modest on unsatisfiable instances, improving from 78 to 80 solved problems, about 3%, and required substantial resources: 150,000 datapoints generated at roughly one datapoint per 60 seconds per worker, 400 parallel processes on eight 64-core machines, and 20 GPUs across five machines for inference, with runtime including GPU query time. This implies that the proposed foundation model must beat not only training from scratch but also well-tuned single-task learned solvers, and that practical evaluation must account for inference overhead. Finally, the proposed mean loss combines MSE for regression and cross-entropy for classification without reported task weighting, loss scaling, or per-task normalization; [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367) used target-specific chemical-accuracy normalization to make errors comparable, suggesting that the plan's unweighted mean loss may be dominated by high-variance regression tasks such as model counting or solver-feature regressions and may harm classification tasks such as satisfiability or backbone prediction.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace or augment the unweighted mean loss with task-specific loss scaling and ablations, including per-task validation-based weighting, uncertainty weighting, GradNorm or similar gradient balancing, and target-specific normalization for regression tasks analogous to the chemical-accuracy normalization in [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367). The plan should report per-task validation curves and final metrics, and compare the proposed 16-task mean loss against single-task pretraining, from-scratch training, and ensembles of single-task models, because [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367) showed ensembling five individual models improved the best individual model. The second priority is to adopt the unsat-core data-generation and solver-integrated evaluation protocol from [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500): generate core labels with Z3 and DRAT-trim where possible, evaluate prediction accuracy and R2/accuracy as planned, but also measure downstream SAT solver runtime, cactus plots, scatter plots, and solved-instance counts on SATCOMP-style and hard scheduling instances, including the wall-clock cost of querying the model. Third, the plan should explicitly budget and pilot-test the data-generation and training cost, because [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) required about 60 seconds per worker per datapoint and 20 GPUs for inference, while the proposed plan uses one million 100-variable 3SAT instances, sixteen tasks, eight message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, batch size 20, and 24 hours of A100 pretraining; a smaller pilot with 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances should verify that the compute budget is sufficient before full-scale training. Fourth, the plan should strengthen statistical and reproducibility reporting by using multiple seeds, confidence intervals, paired significance tests for fine-tuning versus from-scratch, and full hardware/hyperparameter details, since neither related work provides enough hardware or hyperparameter detail for full reproduction and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) reports solver counts without formal significance tests.

## Context: The main within-distribution evaluation tests whether pretraining on other tasks helps a held-out task; Method: For each task category, we will train a foundation model on all other tasks, then fine-tune on the held-out task.

### Support

The strongest support comes from the later transfer study by [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), which extends GNN work on Boolean logic to a reinforcement-learning solver and shows that a model trained on SATLIB random 3-SAT SAT-50-218 near the clause-to-variable phase transition can transfer zero-shot to the flat graph coloring benchmark from SATLIB, where all problems are satisfiable, with MRIR, computed as MiniSat iterations divided by Graph-Q-SAT iterations, above 1 in five of eight cases. This is directly relevant to the proposed leave-one-task-out foundation-model evaluation because the plan also uses random 3SAT near the phase transition and asks whether training on other task categories helps a held-out category. A second, weaker support is Kurin's data-efficiency result: training on one to 800 SAT-50-218 instances generalized across problem sizes and to unSAT instances, and increasing data helped in most cases, which supports the plan's subsampling at 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances and the hypothesis that Boolean-logic GNNs can be sample efficient. The weakest support is from the earlier [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), which showed that BPNN-D, a GNN on factor graphs implemented in PyTorch Geometric, could learn from tens of real-world SAT instances and generalize to held-out SAT instances harder for an exact model counter, while being compared against a standard GNN, belief propagation, an exact model counter, and handcrafted approximate model counters. This supports the plan's use of GNNs for model counting and the plausibility of data-efficient training, but it is indirect because it does not test multi-task pretraining or fine-tuning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction comes from the same later Kurin study: direct training on flat-75-180 and selecting on flat-100-239 produced higher MRIR than the model transferred from SAT-50-218, indicating that target-task training can outperform cross-task transfer. This directly challenges the proposed method's assumption that a foundation model pretrained on all other task categories will beat training from scratch on the held-out task. A second contradiction is that Kurin's transferred model had lower MRIR than same-distribution evaluation and only positive transfer in five of eight cases, with only average, maximum, and minimum MRIR reported over five runs, and no exact numeric values or statistical significance, so the evidence for transfer is not robust. The weakest contradiction is that the earlier [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49) provides only qualitative outcomes for SAT model counting, without numerical accuracy, R2, confidence intervals, statistical significance, instance sizes, hardware, hyperparameters, or training duration, and it does not compare multi-task pretraining against from-scratch training. Therefore it cannot substantiate the plan's central foundation-model claim, even though it supports GNN feasibility for model counting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the leave-one-task-out comparison compute- and data-matched by training a from-scratch baseline on the held-out task using the same number of instances, same architecture, same A100 time budget, and same validation schedule, then reporting final accuracy/R2 and convergence curves for fine-tuning versus scratch; this is necessary because [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) showed direct target-task training can outperform transfer. The next most important action is to add zero-shot and same-distribution controls, plus a solver-based baseline such as MiniSat with and without restarts, and report MRIR or task-specific metrics with confidence intervals over multiple seeds, because Kurin's MRIR protocol and 5/8 positive transfer result show that transfer gains can be modest and task-dependent. A third action is to include a BPNN-D or standard GNN/belief-propagation baseline for model counting, as in [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), and report quantitative accuracy, R2, speedup, and hardware, because the plan's model-counting task needs a strong from-scratch GNN baseline. A fourth action is to extend the data-efficiency curve to very small regimes, e.g., 1, 10, 100, 800, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances, and report per-task variance, because Kurin found generalization from a single instance and improvement from 1 to 800 instances. Finally, document hyperparameters, batch size, message-passing depth, embedding dimension, optimizer, learning rate, GPU hours, and code release to address reproducibility gaps present in both related works.

## Context: The evaluation defines the metrics used to compare fine-tuned foundation models with scratch-trained baselines; Method: Our evaluation will focus on comparing fine-tuning performance metrics (accuracy for classification, R² for regression) against training from scratch baselines, measuring both final performance and convergence efficiency across all sixteen tasks.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation comes from [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), which evaluates GNN-based SAT solvers on G4SATBench across satisfiability prediction, satisfying assignment prediction, and unsat-core variable prediction, using grid search, validation-set checkpoint selection, three random seeds, and a single RTX8000 GPU with 16 AMD EPYC 7502 cores, with about 8,000 GPU hours reported. This directly supports the plan's need to evaluate GNN Boolean-logic models on multiple tasks with validation-based final performance and resource accounting, although it does not test fine-tuned foundation models or convergence curves. A second strong support is [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), whose BPNN learns SAT model counts from tens of training instances, generalizes to harder instances, and is hundreds of times faster than handcrafted approximate counters, supporting the plan's data-efficiency and efficiency comparisons for model counting, but it does not compare fine-tuning versus scratch training or report R²/accuracy. [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367) supports the plan's multi-task and data-efficiency framing by comparing single-target versus jointly trained GNNs on 13 QM9 targets and showing that a strong architecture can match a 110k-sample baseline with 11k samples, although it uses MAE ratios rather than accuracy/R² and does not fine-tune a foundation model. [(Lederman et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0173606a3db75d3c2309d72203b705fb5af993) supports evaluating learned GNN Boolean models against strong heuristics and testing generalization beyond the training distribution, using a small 8353-parameter GNN policy on 1835 Reductions QBF training formulas and cactus plots over decisions and runtime, but it is single-task RL and uses solver success counts rather than the proposed accuracy/R². [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90) supports regression evaluation for #SAT by training BPGAT with MSE on log model counts and proposing RMSE/MRE against ApproxMC, aligning with the plan's regression tasks, although the excerpt lacks quantitative results. [(Kroc et al., 2008-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3504a757319242a3c771278ebecb2e292ee47dc4) supports using exact or probabilistic model counts and runtime bounds as references for model-counting evaluation, with BPCount/MiniCount producing 99% bounds under 12-hour and 2 GB limits, but it predates GNN foundation models. [(Devlin, and O'Sullivan, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f9e85e2c6e90410a6a5dcb369b0b6ac81c678) and [(Danisovszky et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50915372bd3862871f2606643986778cab9e2b4a) provide older SAT classification baselines using 10-fold cross-validation and accuracy, with Devlin reporting above 90% and sometimes above 99% accuracy and Danisovszky finding a one-hidden-layer 250-neuron MLP trained for 4000 epochs sufficient, supporting the use of accuracy and convergence/training-time measurements but not fine-tuned multi-task foundation models.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367), where training one model per target consistently outperformed jointly training all 13 targets, with up to 40% improvements on some targets, directly challenging the assumption that multi-task pretraining will automatically improve fine-tuned performance; the plan's single-task versus multi-task pretraining experiment is therefore essential. A second important contradiction is methodological: [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90) and [(Kroc et al., 2008-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3504a757319242a3c771278ebecb2e292ee47dc4) show that model-counting evaluation is best framed on log counts, relative error, and confidence bounds, because counts can span orders of magnitude and exact counters may exceed 12-hour/2 GB limits; this contradicts relying only on raw R² for regression, which can be dominated by large-count instances and obscure useful approximate counts. [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) also contradicts the feasibility of a low-cost 24-hour pretraining budget by reporting roughly 8,000 GPU hours for GNN SAT solver experiments on G4SATBench with grid search and three seeds, suggesting that the plan's 24-hour A100 pretraining, 6-hour fine-tuning, and 2-hour frozen fine-tuning may be underpowered unless the task set, model size, or data scale is reduced. [(Lederman et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0173606a3db75d3c2309d72203b705fb5af993) contradicts the expectation that a learned GNN will uniformly beat strong Boolean heuristics: on QBFEVAL the learned policy solved 111 formulas versus 125 for VSIDS and 67 for Random, and VSIDS solved more formulas up to 10-second runtime limits, so the plan's RL-based branching task needs cactus-plot success/runtime baselines rather than only accuracy/R². Finally, older feature-based SAT classification work, [(Devlin, and O'Sullivan, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f9e85e2c6e90410a6a5dcb369b0b6ac81c678) and [(Danisovszky et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50915372bd3862871f2606643986778cab9e2b4a), contradicts the necessity of a foundation model for some classification tasks by showing that hand-crafted SATzilla/CNFStats features with WEKA or a small MLP can reach high accuracy using 10-fold cross-validation, although they do not measure convergence efficiency or multi-task transfer.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to revise the regression evaluation for model-counting and other count-like tasks: report R² only on log-transformed counts or alongside log-RMSE, mean relative error, and calibration/bounds, using exact counters or ApproxMC/BPCount-style bounds as references, because [(Kroc et al., 2008-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3504a757319242a3c771278ebecb2e292ee47dc4) and [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90) show raw counts and log-scale errors are more meaningful. Second, add strong baselines that the proposed fine-tuned foundation model must beat: hand-crafted SATzilla/CNFStats feature classifiers from [(Devlin, and O'Sullivan, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b8f9e85e2c6e90410a6a5dcb369b0b6ac81c678) and [(Danisovszky et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50915372bd3862871f2606643986778cab9e2b4a), heuristic/CDCL/LS/VSIDS solvers from [(Lederman et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0173606a3db75d3c2309d72203b705fb5af993) and [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), and ApproxMC or other approximate counters from [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90). Third, make convergence efficiency explicit by logging validation accuracy, log-R²/RMSE, and loss at fixed wall-clock intervals and GPU-hour checkpoints for fine-tuning, frozen fine-tuning, and scratch training, with at least three seeds and confidence intervals, following the validation-checkpoint and multi-seed protocol in [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) and the epoch/training-time observation in [(Danisovszky et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50915372bd3862871f2606643986778cab9e2b4a). Fourth, re-baseline the compute budget: report A100 hours, batch size 20, 8 message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, Adam 1e-4, and ablate the 1M-instance 100-variable 3SAT corpus using the planned 100/1k/10k/100k subsampling, because [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576) reports about 8,000 GPU hours for GNN SAT experiments and [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49) shows data efficiency can be tested from tens to thousands of examples. Fifth, explicitly test single-task versus multi-task pretraining and frozen versus full fine-tuning, because [(Gilmer et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e24cdf73b3e7e590c2fe5ecac9ae8aa983801367) found single-task training can outperform joint multi-task training by up to 40%, and the plan's leave-one-task-out design should include task-specific loss weighting or per-task head ablations. Sixth, for RL-based branching and solver-related tasks, use cactus plots of solved formulas versus decisions and wall-clock runtime against VSIDS/CDCL/LS, as in [(Lederman et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b0173606a3db75d3c2309d72203b705fb5af993) and [(Li et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/490446ac0419c2f232b481e4c73117bcffe45576), rather than relying solely on classification accuracy. Finally, include per-class accuracy, F1/AUC where labels are imbalanced, and statistical significance tests, because older SAT classification studies used 10-fold cross-validation and accuracy but did not provide significance, and the proposed sixteen-task comparison requires reproducible uncertainty estimates.

## Context: Data efficiency is a primary claim, so training sets are reduced to compare sample requirements; Method: We will subsample training sets at 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances to measure how much training data each approach requires to achieve comparable performance.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000-instance subsampling method comes from [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), which directly trains a Graph-Q-SAT reinforcement-learning GNN on random 3-SAT instances and varies the number of training problems from 1 to 800, evaluating every 1000 batch updates on validation instances, selecting the best validation checkpoint, and reporting Median Relative Iteration Reduction relative to MiniSat over five training runs. This is the closest analogue to the plan's data-efficiency experiment because it measures performance as a function of training-set size for a Boolean-logic GNN, uses validation-based model selection, and uses a median solver-relative metric that is robust to outliers; it supports the plan's choice to subsample and to compare convergence rather than only final accuracy. This SAT-specific data-efficiency evidence extends the earlier graph-based sample-efficiency result of [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175), where GG-NN graph models reach 100% test accuracy on symbolic bAbI tasks 4, 15, 16, and 18 with only 50 training examples, while RNN and LSTM baselines with 50-dimensional embeddings and hidden layers fail on tasks 15, 16, and 18; the GG-NNs have fewer than 600 parameters compared with about 5,000 for RNN and about 30,000 for LSTM, supporting the plan's graph-based GPS++ representation and the hypothesis that structured reasoning models can be sample-efficient, although the task is not SAT and does not involve foundation-model fine-tuning. The later large-scale SAT work of [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) supports the feasibility of the plan's 100,000-instance scale and unsat-core task by generating 150,000 labeled CNF instances from SATCOMP through 2017 and hard scheduling problems using Z3 with a 60-second timeout, DRAT-trim on DRAT proofs, and March branching for unknowns, then evaluating NeuroCore-assisted MiniSat on 400 SATCOMP-2018 main-track problems with a 5,000-second timeout on 400 parallel processes across 8 identical 64-core machines and 20 GPUs on 5 machines; this shows that solver-derived Boolean labels at the 100,000 scale can produce a 10 percent increase in solved problems, from 187 to 205, with a 15 percent increase on satisfiable instances, but it does not test subsampling or foundation-model fine-tuning. [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49) provides further support for one of the plan's sixteen tasks, model counting, because its BPNN on factor-graph SAT instances learns from 10's of training problems, generalizes to instances harder for an exact model counter, and estimates model counts 100's of times faster than handcrafted approximate counters, but the excerpt lacks exact training-set sizes, error bars, and hardware, so the support is weaker. [(Azar et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00722d2776dc076704102c7dbe1417dc84b06f85) provides weaker but relevant support for a fixed 20,000-instance training regime and transfer to larger structures: NNgSAT is trained on 20,000 small obfuscated circuit samples with multipliers up to 8x8, crossbars up to 16x16, LUTs up to 8 inputs, and AND trees, then evaluated on ISCAS-85 and ITC-99 circuits with larger-than-trained structures, using MiniSAT, 8 parallel solver runs, confidence ratios 0.7-0.9, ADAM with learning rate 1e-4, and a Dell PowerEdge R620 with a 28-core Intel Xeon E5-2670 and 64 GB RAM; this supports the plan's use of solver labels and transfer evaluation, but not the proposed 100/1,000/10,000/100,000 grid. [(Hutter et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3e7d210ffcda4caedbb6719750a7ddfa125d365) supports the 10,000-instance checkpoint because it evaluates runtime-prediction models at exactly 10,000 training points and finds random forests with uncertainty estimation and linear-interpolation split selection achieve the lowest or tied lowest RMSE Domain on CPLEX and SPEAR datasets, for example CPLEX-BIGMIX 0.55, CPLEX-CORLAT 0.49, CPLEX-REG 0.17, CPLEX-RCW 0.09, CPLEX-CR 0.38, CPLEX-CRR 0.32, SPEAR-IBM 0.44, SPEAR-SWV 0.44, and SPEAR-SWV-IBM 0.45; however, it is a fixed-size comparison for solver runtime prediction, not a data-efficiency curve or foundation-model fine-tuning study. The weakest support is [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f), which constructs a reduced SR 5000 (3,10) random SAT dataset with 5,000 training, 3,000 validation, and 3,000 test paired instances and trains a clause-variable bipartite GNN with 26 message-passing iterations, 128-dimensional embeddings, two 8-head attention layers, binary cross-entropy, Adam learning rate 2e-5, and gradient clipping 0.65; this is conceptually aligned with few-sample SAT learning, but the provided excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, so it cannot substantively support the proposed method.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or tension for the proposed method is that the closest large-scale Boolean-logic solver-guided study, [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), uses 150,000 labeled instances and still reports only a modest 10 percent increase in solved SATCOMP-2018 problems, from 187 to 205 within 5,000 seconds, with no statistical significance and no subsampling curve; this contradicts the assumption that merely training at 100,000 instances will automatically yield a clear data-efficiency advantage, and it implies that the plan must demonstrate statistically reliable gains over from-scratch training and solver baselines rather than relying on dataset scale alone. The next strongest contradiction is [(Hutter et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3e7d210ffcda4caedbb6719750a7ddfa125d365), which evaluates ridge regression with quadratic basis and forward selection, SPORE-FoBa, neural networks, regression trees, approximate Gaussian processes, and random forests at a single 10,000-instance training size and finds random forests best or tied best on all listed CPLEX and SPEAR datasets, with severe ridge outliers such as RR greater than 10 and SP 100 on CPLEX-BIGMIX and SP 11 on SPEAR-IBM; this challenges the plan's emphasis on a GNN foundation model as the data-efficient approach because simpler tree-based models can be competitive at the plan's 10,000-instance checkpoint, and the absence of a sample-size curve means the paper cannot show how much data is required for comparable performance. A further contradiction is that [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f) proposes a 5,000-instance few-sample random SAT experiment on 3 to 10 variable paired instances but provides no accuracy, loss, learning curve, baseline comparison, or statistical significance, which contradicts the feasibility of making strong data-efficiency claims from small training sets without explicit learning curves and robust evaluation; its small variable range and paired single-literal-flip instances also do not match the plan's 100-variable phase-transition 3SAT setting, limiting transferability. [(Azar et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00722d2776dc076704102c7dbe1417dc84b06f85) provides a weaker contradiction because it uses a fixed 20,000-instance training set and reports that some obfuscated circuits have identical runtimes with and without network guidance, while confidence-ratio analysis shows that lower average confidence ratios produce more misguiding predictions and that the best range is 0.7-0.9; this suggests that sample size alone may not determine performance and that solver-guidance hyperparameters, parallel solver runs, and timeout behavior must be tightly controlled in the plan's 100/1,000/10,000/100,000 comparison. [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49) is a weaker contradiction because it claims BPNN can learn from 10's of training problems and generalize to harder model-counting instances, but the excerpt does not provide exact training-set sizes, quantitative accuracy, error, confidence intervals, hardware, or statistical significance, so it cannot validate the plan's specific subsampling grid. The weakest contradiction is that [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) and [(Li et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/492f57ee9ceb61fb5a47ad7aebfec1121887a175) demonstrate extreme data efficiency at much smaller scales, Kurin with 1 to 800 training problems on 50-variable random 3-SAT and Li with 50 examples on symbolic bAbI, which may imply that the plan's 100,000-instance upper bound is overkill for some narrow tasks but also means their results cannot be extrapolated to a 100-variable, sixteen-task, foundation-model fine-tuning setting without direct evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the proposed 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000-instance subsampling grid as a full learning-curve experiment for both foundation-model fine-tuning and from-scratch training, including full and frozen fine-tuning, with at least five random seeds, validation-based checkpoint selection, and reported confidence intervals or statistical tests, because [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) uses validation selection and five training runs, while [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) and [(Hutter et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3e7d210ffcda4caedbb6719750a7ddfa125d365) show that fixed-size or single-size results are insufficient to establish data efficiency; the plan should also define comparable performance operationally, for example within 1-2 percentage points for classification accuracy, within 0.05 R2 for regression, or with overlapping 95% confidence intervals. The second most important action is to add strong non-foundation baselines at every subsample size, including single-task from-scratch GNNs, single-task pretrained models, and simple supervised models such as random forests or ridge regression where task-appropriate, because [(Hutter et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3e7d210ffcda4caedbb6719750a7ddfa125d365) shows random forests can be best at 10,000 instances and the plan must prove that multi-task fine-tuning is not merely outperforming weak from-scratch GNNs. The third most important action is to use solver-grounded, task-specific metrics rather than only accuracy and R2: for RL branching report Median Relative Iteration Reduction relative to MiniSat as in [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), for unsat-core detection use DRAT-trim-derived labels and SATCOMP-style cactus plots as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), for model counting compare against exact counters and handcrafted approximate counters as in [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), and for solver-guided attacks report parallel solver runs and confidence-ratio controls as in [(Azar et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00722d2776dc076704102c7dbe1417dc84b06f85); because the plan uses a mean loss across sixteen tasks, per-task metrics should be reported to avoid masking task-specific data requirements. The fourth most important action is to explicitly test transfer and out-of-distribution generalization at each subsample size, including larger-than-trained structures, 150-600 variable instances, and non-random distributions, because [(Azar et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00722d2776dc076704102c7dbe1417dc84b06f85) evaluates transfer to larger obfuscated circuits and [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) evaluates generalization across problem sizes and to unSAT instances, while the plan's OOD section should be coupled to the data-efficiency grid rather than evaluated only at full data. The fifth most important action is to report full reproducibility details for every subsample condition, including A100 GPU count and memory, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, number of epochs or validation checkpoints, random seeds, dataset split construction, label generation timeouts, and hyperparameter search ranges, because [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f), [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), and [(Hutter et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d3e7d210ffcda4caedbb6719750a7ddfa125d365) all leave implementation details or quantitative outcomes insufficient for independent verification; the plan should also report GPU-hours for the 24-hour pretraining, 6-hour fine-tuning, and 2-hour frozen fine-tuning budgets so that data efficiency is interpreted alongside compute efficiency. The least important but still useful action is to consider adding 10 and 1,000,000 training instances if compute permits, or to justify why 100 is the lower bound and 100,000 is the upper bound, because [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) shows useful generalization from a single 50-variable instance, while the plan's one-million-instance dataset and 100-variable phase-transition setting may require a larger upper bound to detect saturation.

## Context: Training-time efficiency is a primary claim, so convergence is tracked during training; Method: We will measure validation performance at regular intervals during training to compare convergence speed between fine-tuning and training from scratch.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Langendonck et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5636a05497764439e718fe65c186a50a7c74fcce), which directly implements the proposed interval-based convergence comparison by training both from-scratch and fine-tuned spatio-temporal GNNs for each downstream task and sample size, recording training and validation loss curves, normalizing them, and averaging per epoch, per dataset, and per training strategy. In that few-shot Netflow setting, with 50 epochs, pretraining on unlabeled UNSW-NB15 and ToN-IoT data, and downstream BoT-IoT, UPC-COMNET14, and CTU13 tasks, fine-tuning reached comparable or better performance faster, with an average 6.87 percent performance-loss advantage relative to a 200-epoch reference model, and training loss decreased faster for all three datasets; final evaluation used accuracy, weighted F1, and macro F1. This supports the Boolean-logic plan's use of validation performance at regular intervals to compare fine-tuning and scratch training, especially because the proposed plan also uses GNNs, small downstream budgets of 6 hours full fine-tuning and 2 hours frozen fine-tuning on A100 GPUs, and multiple downstream tasks. The next strongest support is [(Yeh et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f1d35d56ee04dbf6ba2ac6d42e8e85f4424ca6), which compares convergence trajectories of TimeCLR-pretrained and non-pretrained time-series backbones on 128 UCR tasks, using 50 percent of each dataset as unlabeled pretraining data and 3:1:1 task splits, and selects models by validation accuracy. It found smoother fine-tuning convergence for the pretrained model on the harder Arrow-Head task and similar behavior on the easier CBF task, and pretrained foundation models were beneficial in 14 of 20 method-architecture configurations, directly validating the proposed idea that convergence curves can reveal when foundation-model fine-tuning is more efficient, although the evidence is qualitative and limited to two representative datasets. A further supporting precedent is [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b), which compares a 12-layer encoder-decoder Transformer pre-trained on C4 for 2^35 tokens and fine-tuned for 2^18 steps against a no-pretraining model trained for the same 2^18 steps, reporting validation scores such as 83.28 versus 66.22 on GLUE, 80.88 versus 50.31 on SQuAD, and 71.36 versus 53.04 on SuperGLUE, with 10-run standard deviations such as 0.343 for SQuAD and 0.416 for SuperGLUE. This supports the plausibility that fine-tuning from a pretrained model can outperform scratch training under a fixed downstream step budget, and it supports repeated-run variance reporting, but it uses endpoint validation scores rather than regular-interval curves. Weaker support comes from [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8), which demonstrates that Adam-based training-time convergence tracking is feasible by plotting training cost against iterations for logistic regression, fully connected networks, convolutional networks, and a VAE, using minibatch size 128, a c64-c64-c128-1000 CIFAR-10 network, and a VAE with 500 softplus units and a 50-dimensional latent variable while varying beta1 from 0 to 0.9, beta2 from 0.99 to 0.9999, and log10 alpha from -5 to -1. This is relevant because the proposed plan uses Adam with learning rate 0.0001 and batch size 20, but the paper tracks training cost rather than validation performance and does not evaluate fine-tuning. Additional weak support is provided by [(Liu et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/191fff8a3a4a9f87b1aded88c9ddb625dfca3c1a), which pre-trains and fine-tunes a relational GNN code encoder on Java methods from 27,581 packages totaling 29,024,142 methods and Python methods from ogbg-code totaling 452,741 methods, using two-layer or five-layer RGCNs with 300 hidden units, Adam learning rates from 0.01 to 0.0001, dropout 0.2, L2 regularization 0.0001, up to 10 pre-training epochs and 100 fine-tuning epochs, and 4 Tesla V100 GPUs with 32 CPUs and 244GB memory; it shows validation-based training is feasible for GNN fine-tuning but does not report convergence curves or scratch comparisons. Similarly, [(Huix et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d970b5e773aaa317d3b522eefae77943c6847d0) uses validation-set performance to select checkpoints for frozen and unfrozen fine-tuning of vision foundation models on APTOS2019, CBIS-DDSM, ISIC2019, and CHEXPERT with AdamW, learning rates searched from 1e-5 to 1e-3, weight decay 1e-5, and five repeated runs, with unfrozen fine-tuning and a linear head showing DINOv2 outperforming IMNET-1K-SUP by up to 3.2 percent, but it does not report interval-based convergence curves or scratch comparisons. The weakest support is [(Sanford et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7f0f0c3d2b1dcdac04e9801c9bae40c95567023), which compares final accuracy of a 60M transformer, an 11B fine-tuned transformer, and GNN baselines at 1K and 100K graph-reasoning samples, such as the 60M transformer improving from 31.6 percent to 91.7 percent on node degree and MPNN reaching 99.4 percent at 1K; it supports the broader data-efficiency question but does not track validation performance over training intervals.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the closest convergence-curve evidence is not statistically robust and uses a proxy metric: [(Langendonck et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5636a05497764439e718fe65c186a50a7c74fcce) reports normalized training and validation loss curves and an average 6.87 percent advantage for fine-tuning, but provides no statistical significance tests, confidence intervals, or task-specific accuracy or R-squared metrics, and uses only 50 epochs with pretraining and downstream datasets from different network settings, while also omitting hardware, optimizer, learning rate, and batch-size details. For the Boolean plan, where the claimed benefit is training-time efficiency across 16 tasks with accuracy and R-squared as final metrics, relying on unvalidated loss curves or a single run could overstate convergence speed. The next strongest caution is that convergence benefits may be task-dependent and qualitative: [(Yeh et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f1d35d56ee04dbf6ba2ac6d42e8e85f4424ca6) found smoother fine-tuning convergence for TimeCLR-pretrained models on the harder Arrow-Head dataset but similar behavior on the easier CBF dataset, and did not report numeric speedup, validation intervals, hardware, or exact epochs. This suggests that the proposed regular-interval validation comparison must be applied per task and must quantify speedup, because an aggregate claim that fine-tuning converges faster may fail on easier Boolean tasks. A further contradiction to the assumption that fine-tuning always yields large gains is [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b), where pre-training produced only a 0.05-point gain on WMT English-to-French despite large gains elsewhere, and the comparison used a fixed 2^18-step fine-tuning budget with best-checkpoint early stopping, making endpoint performance sensitive to the chosen step count. The Boolean plan should therefore not assume uniform convergence advantages across all 16 tasks. Weaker contradictions include optimizer and protocol confounds: [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8) shows that Adam convergence can be strongly affected by bias correction, beta1, beta2, stepsize decay, and hyperparameter grid search, and it evaluates training cost rather than validation performance, so the proposed Adam learning rate 0.0001 and batch size 20 comparison could be confounded if scratch and fine-tuning models are not tuned equivalently. [(Liu et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/191fff8a3a4a9f87b1aded88c9ddb625dfca3c1a) provides little direct contradiction but shows that a GNN pre-training and fine-tuning protocol can omit convergence-speed results entirely, with only partial downstream F1 evidence and low F1 for short method names, indicating that validation-based training alone does not guarantee a meaningful efficiency claim. [(Huix et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d970b5e773aaa317d3b522eefae77943c6847d0) contradicts the sufficiency of a single fine-tuning mode by showing frozen foundation models are generally inferior to unfrozen fine-tuning and that freezing depth matters, but it does not report convergence curves, so it warns that the proposed frozen versus full fine-tuning comparison must be measured separately. The weakest contradiction is [(Sanford et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7f0f0c3d2b1dcdac04e9801c9bae40c95567023), which reports only final accuracy at 1K and 100K samples and shows that GNN baselines can be very strong on local graph tasks at low sample sizes, such as MPNN reaching 99.4 percent node degree at 1K, suggesting that a foundation-model convergence advantage may be task- and architecture-specific and cannot be inferred from final accuracy alone.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to convert the proposed measure validation performance at regular intervals into a quantitative, statistically valid convergence-speed protocol. For each of the 16 Boolean tasks, log validation accuracy for classification and R-squared for regression at fixed optimizer steps or epochs, and compute explicit speed metrics such as steps or wall-clock time to reach a predefined performance threshold, area under the validation-performance curve, or normalized curve difference between fine-tuning and scratch training. This is necessary because [(Langendonck et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5636a05497764439e718fe65c186a50a7c74fcce) and [(Yeh et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f1d35d56ee04dbf6ba2ac6d42e8e85f4424ca6) show that convergence curves can be informative but are weak when they are qualitative, loss-based, or unsupported by significance tests. The second most important action is to make the comparison step-matched and compute-matched, following [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b), by training from-scratch models for the same number of downstream steps, data samples, and wall-clock budget as fine-tuning, while also reporting total compute including the 24-hour pretraining budget. The proposed 6-hour full fine-tuning and 2-hour frozen fine-tuning budgets on A100 GPUs should be mirrored for scratch baselines, and the batch size of 20, Adam learning rate 0.0001, and architecture should be held identical except for initialization and pretraining. Third, run multiple random seeds and data shuffles, at least for a representative subset of the 16 tasks and ideally for all tasks, and report mean and standard deviation or confidence intervals using paired comparisons such as bootstrap confidence intervals or paired tests, because [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b) reports 10-run standard deviations and [(Langendonck et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5636a05497764439e718fe65c186a50a7c74fcce) lacks significance. Fourth, define the validation interval and model-selection rule before training: evaluate at a fixed cadence such as every 1,000 steps or every 1 percent of the training budget, report both best-checkpoint and fixed-endpoint results, and avoid using the same validation set to both select the final checkpoint and claim convergence speed without accounting for selection bias, as in [(Yeh et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f1d35d56ee04dbf6ba2ac6d42e8e85f4424ca6) and [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b). Fifth, separately report convergence for full fine-tuning and frozen shared architecture, because [(Huix et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d970b5e773aaa317d3b522eefae77943c6847d0) shows frozen models can be substantially worse and freezing depth matters; the proposed 2-hour frozen experiment should include the same interval tracking and should not be collapsed into the full fine-tuning result. Sixth, control optimizer hyperparameters more explicitly, because [(Kingma, and Ba, 2014-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6cb366736791bcccc5c8639de5a8f9636bf87e8) shows Adam convergence depends on bias correction, beta1, beta2, stepsize decay, and grid search; the plan should state Adam defaults or perform a small matched hyperparameter search for scratch and fine-tuning, and report learning-rate schedules, weight decay, and warmup if used. Seventh, stratify results by task difficulty and data size, because [(Yeh et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f1d35d56ee04dbf6ba2ac6d42e8e85f4424ca6) found convergence benefits mainly on harder tasks and [(Sanford et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7f0f0c3d2b1dcdac04e9801c9bae40c95567023) shows low-sample performance can favor GNNs on local graph tasks; the 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000-instance subsampling should be paired with convergence curves, not only final accuracy or R-squared. Finally, log implementation and resource details for reproducibility, including A100 model, number of GPUs, wall-clock time per step, memory, software stack, validation set sizes, and random seeds, because several related works omit hardware or optimizer details and the proposed 24, 6, and 2-hour budgets make resource accounting central to the training-time-efficiency claim.

## Context: The plan distinguishes between full fine-tuning and cheaper head-only adaptation; Method: We will test both full parameter fine-tuning and frozen shared architecture (training only task heads) to assess computational requirements.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed full-versus-frozen comparison comes from [(Houlsby et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29ddc1f43f28af7c846515e32cc167bc66886d0c), which extends earlier shared-trunk multi-task work by directly contrasting full fine-tuning of BERT with frozen-backbone adaptation on GLUE and 17 classification tasks, using Adam with linear warmup and decay, batch size 32, 4 TPUs, and validation-selected learning rates; adapter tuning was within 0.4% of full fine-tuning while training only 1.14% new parameters per task and 1.19x total task-specific parameters versus 17x for full fine-tuning, and variable fine-tuning of 52% of layers gave 9.9x total parameters, showing that frozen shared architectures with small task-specific modules can be a meaningful compute-saving alternative to the plan's 6-hour full fine-tuning of a Boolean GNN. The next strongest support is [(Liu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/619cf9d39abb93fe1ab17921c163fc5734ac1e70), whose Dense baseline is a shared network with task-specific heads without attention, structurally close to the plan's shared GPS++ trunk plus 16 task heads; on CityScapes 7-class segmentation plus depth it achieved mIoU 51.91, pixel accuracy 90.89, absolute depth error 0.0138, and reported relative-error values 27.21 and 3.63 with equal weights, demonstrating that a shared-trunk/head-style architecture can be trained from scratch with ADAM lr 1e-4, batch size 8, 80k iterations, and controlled parameter counts, although MTAN outperformed it. [(Liu et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/658721bc13b0fa97366d38c05a96bf0a9f4bb0ac) supports the plan's data-efficiency subsampling and full fine-tuning from a multi-task foundation model: a BERT-LARGE multi-task model refined on nine GLUE tasks reached 82.7% benchmark score, 2.2 points above BERT-LARGE, and in SNLI/SciTail adaptation with Adamax lr 5e-5, batch size 32, max 5 epochs, and 0.1 warmup, it reached 82.1% at 0.1% SNLI versus 52.5% for BERT and 85.2% at 1% versus 78.1%, with 91.6% full-data SNLI and 95.0% SciTail, indicating that multi-task pretraining can reduce the 100/1k/10k/100k instance subsampling burden for Boolean tasks. [(Lu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9915315f5cae822e98c94382ce3b0a6f9a7f8e5e) supports multi-task pretraining followed by task-specific full fine-tuning, with ViLBERT on 12 datasets and 4.4M instances trained for 40K iterations on 8 V100 GPUs for 5 days, improving average scores from 67.25 single-task full data to 69.55 and 70.24 all-task fine-tuned, a 2.99 to 3.21 point gain, which is analogous to fine-tuning a Boolean foundation model on held-out SAT tasks. [(Kokkinos, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/922197906907dc0a5e1b51fae40d3149333ecacf) supports shared multi-task representation learning and compute-aware training, using a VGG trunk, masked per-task losses, asynchronous SGD with effective batch sizes 2 for detection, 10 for other task-specific parameters, and 30 for the shared trunk, memory-efficient backprop on a 12GB GPU, and 2-to-4-fold acceleration from skipping absent branches, relevant to the plan's 16 tasks and A100 budget, though it did not freeze the trunk. Weaker support comes from [(Sanh et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d8d98847bd33fa48bc6448316f78ed7f131afe), which used shared word embeddings with task heads on ACE05 NER, EMD, CR, and RE and reported multi-task acceleration, but without exact frozen-head numbers, and from [(Wang et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c66014aaaf0d38f8186edd3566fdd594b046030c), which used contrastive-BERT pretraining plus fine-tuning on 2,335 molecules with a 6-layer 1024-hidden transformer on an RTX 2080 Ti 11GB, supporting pretrain-finetune but not frozen-head compute. Finally, [(Rahimian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee0657433d09ce24e10e5409510e4505cf2b7c07) supports measuring computational requirements in multi-task shared architectures by reporting parameter and FLOPs effects of task and instance gating on NYUv2, CityScapes, and MIMIC-III, although it does not test pure head-only training.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest frozen-backbone evidence does not validate pure head-only training: [(Houlsby et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29ddc1f43f28af7c846515e32cc167bc66886d0c) found that tuning only layer-normalization parameters, about 40k parameters, was about 3.5% worse on CoLA and 4% worse on MNLI, and that adapters were needed to approach full fine-tuning; on MNLI-m, top-layer fine-tuning with about 9M parameters reached 77.8% +/- 0.1%, adapter size 64 with about 2M parameters reached 83.7% +/- 0.1%, and full fine-tuning reached 84.4% +/- 0.02%, so the plan's 2-hour frozen experiment that trains only 16 task heads may be too restrictive if the Boolean GNN's shared 8 message-passing layers and 64-dimensional embeddings must adapt to held-out tasks. The next strongest contradiction is that simple shared-trunk plus task-head architectures can underperform task-aware feature selection: [(Liu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/619cf9d39abb93fe1ab17921c163fc5734ac1e70) reported that the Dense shared-network/head baseline was outperformed by MTAN on CityScapes and NYUv2, with MTAN achieving mIoU 53.04 versus 51.91 and pixel accuracy 91.11 versus 90.89 on CityScapes while using fewer parameters, suggesting that the plan's GPS++ shared GNN plus heads may need attention, gating, or task-specific feature routing rather than only frozen shared layers. [(Kokkinos, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/922197906907dc0a5e1b51fae40d3149333ecacf) contradicts the assumption that multi-task training will uniformly help all 16 Boolean tasks: in its seven-task VGG setup, adding more tasks reduced detection performance relative to a strong baseline, and task-weight experiments showed that increasing normal-estimation weight improved that task at the expense of others, so the plan's mean loss across 16 tasks may produce negative transfer in leave-one-task-out evaluation. [(Sanh et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3d8d98847bd33fa48bc6448316f78ed7f131afe) provides weaker but direct evidence of task-specific negative transfer, reporting that CR performed slightly better as a single task than in the compared multi-task configuration A versus E, although exact F1 values are absent. [(Lu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9915315f5cae822e98c94382ce3b0a6f9a7f8e5e) also cautions against assuming full fine-tuning is cheap: fine-tuning 12 task-specific models increased parameters from 270 million for one all-task model to 3 billion, and no frozen-trunk/head-only ablation was reported, which challenges the plan's 6-hour full fine-tuning budget across 16 tasks if each task requires a separate fine-tuned checkpoint. [(Rahimian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee0657433d09ce24e10e5409510e4505cf2b7c07) suggests that a fixed shared architecture may be suboptimal because DynaShare's task-specific policy and instance gating outperformed baselines on NYUv2 and CityScapes, with average task lifts of +14.4 and +5.0 and FLOPs reductions of 13% to almost 30% relative to AdaShare, implying that the plan should not treat frozen shared architecture as the only cheaper alternative. Weaker contradictions include the absence of direct Boolean/GNN evidence and the fact that [(Liu et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/658721bc13b0fa97366d38c05a96bf0a9f4bb0ac) and [(Wang et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c66014aaaf0d38f8186edd3566fdd594b046030c) do not isolate frozen shared architecture or report wall-clock compute, so they cannot confirm that head-only adaptation will match full fine-tuning on SAT graphs.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add intermediate adaptation conditions between full fine-tuning and frozen head-only training, such as task-specific adapter bottlenecks after each of the 8 message-passing layers or in the shared-embedding layer, and partial fine-tuning of the top 1-2 message-passing layers, because [(Houlsby et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29ddc1f43f28af7c846515e32cc167bc66886d0c) showed adapters within 0.4% of full fine-tuning with 1.14% new parameters per task and 1.19x total task-specific parameters versus 17x full fine-tuning, while layer-normalization-only tuning was 3.5-4% worse; for the plan, report trainable parameters, A100 GPU-hours, memory, and convergence curves for full, frozen-head, adapter, and partial variants. Second, make computational requirements explicit beyond the 24-hour pretraining, 6-hour full fine-tuning, and 2-hour frozen budgets: report per-task and aggregate trainable parameter counts, total task-specific parameters for 16 tasks, FLOPs or forward/backward cost, wall-clock time, and memory, following [(Houlsby et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29ddc1f43f28af7c846515e32cc167bc66886d0c) parameter-efficiency reporting, [(Rahimian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee0657433d09ce24e10e5409510e4505cf2b7c07) FLOPs and parameter reporting, and [(Lu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9915315f5cae822e98c94382ce3b0a6f9a7f8e5e) observation that 12 fine-tuned ViLBERT models reached 3 billion parameters. Third, replace or supplement the mean loss across 16 tasks with task-weighting ablations, including equal weights, uncertainty weights, DWA, or learned weights, and test task-specific gating or attention, because [(Kokkinos, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/922197906907dc0a5e1b51fae40d3149333ecacf) showed task-weight trade-offs and negative transfer, [(Liu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/619cf9d39abb93fe1ab17921c163fc5734ac1e70) showed MTAN attention outperformed a Dense shared-head baseline, and [(Rahimian et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee0657433d09ce24e10e5409510e4505cf2b7c07) showed dynamic task and instance gating improved multi-task performance while reducing FLOPs. Fourth, strengthen statistical and data-efficiency evaluation by using multiple random subsamples and seeds for the 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instance settings, reporting confidence intervals or standard errors and significance tests, as in [(Liu et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/658721bc13b0fa97366d38c05a96bf0a9f4bb0ac) five random subsamples per condition and [(Houlsby et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29ddc1f43f28af7c846515e32cc167bc66886d0c) standard error of the mean across seeds. Fifth, include compute-matched single-task pretraining and from-scratch baselines with the same 1M 3SAT instances, 100-variable graphs, 8 message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, Adam lr 0.0001, batch size 20, and A100 allocation, so that the foundation-model claim is not confounded by extra pretraining compute, as motivated by [(Lu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9915315f5cae822e98c94382ce3b0a6f9a7f8e5e) and [(Liu et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/658721bc13b0fa97366d38c05a96bf0a9f4bb0ac). Finally, document reproducibility details such as A100 count, number of fine-tuned checkpoints, dropout, normalization, pooling, maximum training iterations, and data-generation cost, because related work reproducibility depends on hardware, optimizer, batch size, and hyperparameters, e.g., [(Kokkinos, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/922197906907dc0a5e1b51fae40d3149333ecacf) 12GB GPU and asynchronous SGD, [(Liu et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/619cf9d39abb93fe1ab17921c163fc5734ac1e70) ADAM lr 1e-4 and batch sizes 8 and 2, and [(Wang et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c66014aaaf0d38f8186edd3566fdd594b046030c) RTX 2080 Ti 11GB with 10 pretraining epochs.

## Context: The plan tests whether multi-task diversity improves representations relative to single-task pretraining; Method: We will compare fine-tuning from our multi-task foundation model against fine-tuning from single-task pretrained models to determine whether task diversity contributes to representation quality.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed comparison is direct evidence that multi-task supervised pretraining followed by adaptation can outperform single-task pretraining followed by adaptation. [(Kameswari et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f54f0421f7d0c136926b5dbceda82d3dcd2707) trained a hierarchical multi-task model on POS, chunking, NER, and dependency parsing in a News source domain and fine-tuned it on Tweets, reporting POS 94.53 versus 93.33, dependency LAS 80.12 versus 78.21, NER F1 43.34 versus 41.25, chunking F1 85.77 versus 84.64, and median normalized relative gain 31.5 versus 21.5 for mono-task pretraining. This is the closest analogue to the plan because it explicitly compares multi-task and single-task pretrained checkpoints before fine-tuning, although it uses NLP sequence models rather than GPS++ Boolean graphs and reports no significance tests. A second, domain-closer support comes from logical relational reasoning over graphs. [(Sinha et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e9af8a6081dc00187bd4a6727751d1721bd7816) pre-trained GAT-E-GAT, GCN-RGCN, and related GNNs on multiple GraphLog worlds and adapted them to held-out worlds, finding that mixed similar and dissimilar worlds generally improved or maintained test accuracy, for example GCN-RGCN 0.448 to 0.476 and Param-RGCN 0.416 to 0.449, and that more diverse pre-training improved post-adaptation accuracy even when zero-shot accuracy fell. This supports the hypothesis that task diversity can improve representations for downstream fine-tuning in a logical GNN setting, but it does not include a matched single-task pretrained baseline. Stronger GNN foundation-model evidence is provided by [(Rong et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9a4e8e631890a14257539948e1813b5214c60dd), whose multi-task self-supervised GROVER pretraining on 11 million molecules improved downstream MoleculeNet performance by 6.1 percent overall, 2.2 percent on classification and 10.8 percent on regression, with a 23.9 percent relative gain on FreeSolv, although it lacks a single-task pretraining ablation. [(Lachi et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a237a78c73471f42dbb349caad319e8a666c9e39) also supports multi-task graph pretraining and fine-tuning, showing that a 75M-parameter model pretrained on 152 graphs and 7.3M tokens improved held-out accuracy by 2.1 percent over smaller models and that adding biological datasets improved Coauthor-CS and Amazon-Photo, but it does not compare against single-task pretrained controls. The newer graph foundation-model evidence from Rong and Lachi extends the older inductive GNN results of [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef) and [(Velickovic et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33998aff64ce51df8dee45989cdca4b6b1329ec4) by moving from single-graph node classification to multi-task pretraining and fine-tuning, although the Boolean-logic setting remains untested. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Chen et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0088c9f4d50706c7ab71efa13bcb4b42cf2058e2) for diverse self-supervised graph pretraining improving in-context learning on MAG240M and Wiki, [(Beaini et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a85db1f84c70ccebaf1d7551fc1d584ab267fce0) for a direct multi-task versus single-task molecular GNN baseline design although without quantitative results in the excerpt, [(Zhuang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/72c73d5fdf1e67a566cd534336d76b754bafdebd) for multi-task instruction tuning with single-task fine-tuned models although without numerical ablations, and [(Munikoti et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64220e816bc57d83b3bc1c769513ab740127286a) for the qualitative claim that broader task diversity reduces overspecialization. The broad-pretraining evidence from [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) supports scale and in-context adaptation, with 175B GPT-3 improving Winogrande from 70.2 zero-shot to 77.7 few-shot, but it does not test fine-tuning from multi-task versus single-task pretrained checkpoints. The weakest support is architectural: [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef) and [(Velickovic et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33998aff64ce51df8dee45989cdca4b6b1329ec4) validate inductive GNN and attention components, with GAT improving PPI micro-F1 by 20.5 percent relative to GraphSAGE and 3.9 percent over constant attention, while [(Kiperwasser, and Ballesteros, 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5054f6080e232c4ef456e2888950c211a6d0d268) and [(Qiu et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91fb815361fdbf80ff15ce4d783a41846bd99232) support shared multi-task training and frozen versus full fine-tuning protocols without direct Boolean-logic evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that broad or cross-domain pretraining may not dominate domain-specific or task-specific pretraining. [(He et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbdae06c37f67868983f4ba419cbbb9402c31e27) reports that cross-domain UniGraph pretrained on Papers100M outperforms dataset-specific self-supervised baselines, but dataset-specific self-supervised training exceeds cross-domain UniGraph to some extent, and same-domain pretraining improves downstream performance. This directly cautions that a multi-task Boolean foundation model may not beat single-task or 3SAT-specific pretraining, especially if the 16 tasks are heterogeneous. The second major caution is negative transfer from task misalignment. [(Mao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13f81979372850d9ab9d386c35bb00b4cc0e35f1) finds that using link prediction directly as a pretext task caused negative transfer for node classification, while reformulating node classification as link prediction produced positive transfer, and it leaves open whether co-training all tasks can avoid negative transfer. This is important for the plan's 16 Boolean tasks with different heads, because satisfiability, model counting, backbone prediction, core detection, and RL branching may have incompatible gradients. Together, these newer graph foundation-model cautions complicate the older broad-pretraining optimism in [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) and the multi-task self-supervised gains in [(Rong et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a9a4e8e631890a14257539948e1813b5214c60dd). A third contradiction is that excessive task diversity can saturate model capacity. [(Sinha et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e9af8a6081dc00187bd4a6727751d1721bd7816) shows that all GraphLog models peaked at 20 worlds and degraded after that, and zero-shot accuracy decreased as pre-training similarity to the evaluation world decreased, implying that adding all 16 tasks or very dissimilar tasks may hurt some held-out tasks. A fourth caution comes from the closest direct multi-task versus single-task comparison: [(Kameswari et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f54f0421f7d0c136926b5dbceda82d3dcd2707) reports only modest gains of 1.13 to 2.09 points, no statistical significance, and a 2.69 F1 drop in NER when target-domain automatic labels were added, showing that noisy or heterogeneous task labels can undermine multi-task pretraining. A fifth contradiction is that broad pretraining alone may still underperform task-specific fine-tuned systems: [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) reports 175B GPT-3 few-shot Winogrande 77.7 below fine-tuned RoBERTA 79 and state-of-the-art 84.6, and poor synthetic word-manipulation scores such as 15.1 on hard anagrams, suggesting that broad representations do not automatically solve structured reasoning without strong task-specific adaptation. Weaker contradictions include [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef), where supervised GraphSAGE outperformed unsupervised GraphSAGE by 0.041, 0.056, and 0.098 micro-F1 on citation, Reddit, and PPI, indicating that task-specific objectives can be stronger than task-agnostic pretraining, and [(Velickovic et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33998aff64ce51df8dee45989cdca4b6b1329ec4), where GAT's transductive citation gains over matched GCN were only 1.6, 1.6, and 0.0 percentage points, suggesting that architectural components such as attention may provide limited gains and should not be conflated with multi-task diversity.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement a compute-matched single-task pretrained baseline for every held-out task, because the strongest related evidence either lacks this control or is indirect. For each of the 16 tasks, train a single-task GPS++ model on the same 1M 100-variable 3SAT instances using only that task's labels, with the same 24-hour A100 pretraining budget, 64-dimensional embeddings, 8 message-passing layers, Adam 0.0001, and batch size 20, then fine-tune it for 6 hours full and 2 hours frozen exactly as the multi-task model. Also include a target-task pretraining upper bound and, if possible, single-task pretraining on each of the other 15 tasks to isolate whether diversity or target-task exposure drives gains. Report accuracy or R2, subsampling curves at 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances, validation performance per GPU-hour, and statistical significance across at least three or five seeds. The second most important action is to control for negative transfer and task similarity, motivated by [(Mao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/13f81979372850d9ab9d386c35bb00b4cc0e35f1) and [(Sinha et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7e9af8a6081dc00187bd4a6727751d1721bd7816). Add leave-one-category-out evaluations, ablate the number of pretraining tasks, use category-wise loss weighting or dynamic task weighting, and monitor per-task validation loss to detect tasks that degrade others. The third action is to ensure label quality and task-specific validity, because [(Kameswari et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f54f0421f7d0c136926b5dbceda82d3dcd2707) showed noisy automatic labels can hurt one task by 2.69 F1; all Boolean labels should be solver-derived and verified, and any approximate tasks such as RL-based branching should be reported separately or excluded from the primary claim. The fourth action is to strengthen the fine-tuning protocol by adding frozen linear probes, full fine-tuning, and gradual unfreezing, following [(Qiu et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91fb815361fdbf80ff15ce4d783a41846bd99232) and [(Lachi et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a237a78c73471f42dbb349caad319e8a666c9e39), so that the plan can distinguish representation quality from head capacity. The fifth action is to test domain and distribution alignment, motivated by [(He et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbdae06c37f67868983f4ba419cbbb9402c31e27) and [(Lachi et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a237a78c73471f42dbb349caad319e8a666c9e39), by ablating pretraining mixtures of 3SAT, 4SAT, 5SAT, and OOD distributions and by reporting size generalization from 100 to 150-600 variables. The least important but still useful action is to include architecture ablations such as batch, layer, and hybrid normalization, dropout, mean versus sum pooling, and a constant-attention baseline, following [(Velickovic et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33998aff64ce51df8dee45989cdca4b6b1329ec4) and [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef), but these should be secondary to the central multi-task versus single-task pretraining comparison.

## Context: Architecture ablations identify which normalization choice best supports multi-task learning; Method: We will systematically evaluate different normalization techniques (batch normalization, layer normalization, and our proposed hybrid normalization) across all tasks.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed architecture ablation is the GNN normalization comparison in [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee), which directly evaluates BatchNorm, LayerNorm, InstanceNorm, and GraphNorm in five-layer GCN and GIN models on eight graph-classification datasets and reports test accuracy with run-level standard deviations, for example GraphNorm 91.6 ± 6.5 versus BatchNorm 89.4 ± 5.6 and GraphNorm 93.5 ± 2.1 versus BatchNorm 92.4 ± 2.5 on several benchmarks, while LayerNorm is often lower such as 82.4 ± 6.4 versus 89.4 ± 5.6. This newer GNN evidence extends earlier CNN and MLP normalization ablations and is the most relevant to the plan because it uses GNNs, although it is single-task graph classification rather than the sixteen-task Boolean SAT setting and does not test the proposed hybrid. A somewhat weaker but still relevant support is [(Scholkemper et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f15512c01c3fb087abd1c842b23663384e27410), which shows that normalization and residual connections affect long-term message passing in deep GNNs by tracking distance to a dominant eigenspace and matrix rank over 256 message-passing iterations and 10 trials, supporting the need to test normalization in the planned eight-layer GPS++ GNN even though it does not report Boolean task accuracy. A more direct but weaker support is [(Ziaee, and Çano, 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d20f4c3aa9940b6f15c97c11a86daa28c0317a34), which proposes Batch Layer Normalization, a concrete hybrid of mini-batch and feature-wise layer normalization with learnable gamma and beta, and designs a controlled comparison against BatchNorm and LayerNorm on CIFAR-10 CNNs and IMDB RNNs using Adam, batch sizes 1 and 25, and epsilon 0.0001, but the supplied excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, so it supports the hybrid concept only weakly. Earlier CNN evidence from [(Huang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecffb9126f17d157b0a35b0e4f96f741d3f5faf9) supports normalization ablation and hyperparameter sensitivity, reporting IterNorm final test error of 3.56% on WRN-28-10 CIFAR-10 and an 8.5% relative improvement over the BatchNorm baseline, but it is single-task image classification and does not address GNNs or multi-task Boolean learning. Optimization-mechanism evidence from [(Santurkar et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94be567c32ae76bdaadabd4975807a94181e39b3) supports including BatchNorm in the ablation because noisy BatchNorm nearly matches standard BatchNorm and both outperform the non-BatchNorm network in a 25-layer deep linear regression task with 1000 full-batch samples and 10000 steps, but it is synthetic and does not establish transfer to layer normalization, hybrid normalization, or GNNs. The weakest support is [(Huang et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8270f523375d22fd417bb583a5b50411e5d5f58), which motivates normalization ablation design on PIE MLPs and CIFAR-10 CNNs with DBN group sizes G1 through G128, but the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes, statistical significance, hardware, or Boolean-logic relevance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method as currently scoped is that the most directly relevant GNN evidence, [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee), finds GraphNorm generally matches or improves test accuracy over BatchNorm, InstanceNorm, and LayerNorm across five-layer GCN and GIN models, and shows BatchNorm batch-level mean and standard deviation on PROTEINS remain far from dataset-level statistics during training, which contradicts the assumption that an ablation limited to BatchNorm, LayerNorm, and a generic hybrid will identify the best normalization for variable-size clause-variable graphs trained with batch size 20. This newer GNN evidence also contradicts the older assumption that BatchNorm is automatically preferable in graph models, although no formal significance test is reported and the evidence is based on mean and standard deviation across runs. A second important contradiction is that [(Scholkemper et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f15512c01c3fb087abd1c842b23663384e27410) shows deep GNN normalization must prevent rank collapse and collapse toward the dominant eigenspace, with GraphNormv2 and other centering or normalization methods preventing collapse in 256-iteration message-passing ablations, whereas the proposed BatchNorm, LayerNorm, and hybrid set does not explicitly address this deep-GNN representation failure mode. A weaker but still relevant contradiction is [(Santurkar et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94be567c32ae76bdaadabd4975807a94181e39b3), which contradicts a simple internal-covariate-shift rationale for BatchNorm by showing that noisy BatchNorm can nearly match standard BatchNorm while having less stable activation distributions, and that BatchNorm models can have similar or worse internal covariate shift yet better accuracy, so the proposed hybrid may not improve because of the assumed stabilizing effect of its BatchNorm component. Another weaker contradiction is [(Huang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecffb9126f17d157b0a35b0e4f96f741d3f5faf9), which reports that IterNorm improves over BatchNorm and DBN on WRN-28-10 CIFAR-10 with final test error 3.56%, suggesting that the proposed ablation may omit stronger normalization variants that could outperform BatchNorm, LayerNorm, and a simple hybrid. The direct hybrid evidence is also a limitation because [(Ziaee, and Çano, 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d20f4c3aa9940b6f15c97c11a86daa28c0317a34) provides no quantitative outcomes for Batch Layer Normalization versus BatchNorm or LayerNorm, so the proposed hybrid normalization is not yet empirically supported in any setting, let alone multi-task Boolean GNNs. The weakest contradiction is [(Huang et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8270f523375d22fd417bb583a5b50411e5d5f58), which lacks quantitative results and is limited to single-task MLP and CNN normalization comparisons, so it cannot support generalization of normalization rankings to the proposed multi-task Boolean foundation model.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to expand the normalization ablation beyond BatchNorm, LayerNorm, and the proposed hybrid by adding graph-native baselines, especially GraphNorm and GraphNormv2 or an eigenvector-centered variant, because [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee) shows GraphNorm can match or beat BatchNorm and LayerNorm on GNN benchmarks and BatchNorm statistics can be noisy on graphs, and [(Scholkemper et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f15512c01c3fb087abd1c842b23663384e27410) shows deep GNNs benefit from normalization that prevents rank and dominant-eigenspace collapse. The proposed hybrid should therefore be defined as a graph-aware hybrid, for example combining graph-level or eigenvector-based centering with feature-wise layer normalization and mini-batch normalization, and compared against the concrete Batch Layer Normalization formulation in [(Ziaee, and Çano, 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d20f4c3aa9940b6f15c97c11a86daa28c0317a34), including its inverse-mini-batch-size weighting, learnable gamma and beta, and placement after the nonlinearity. Next, the ablation should report optimization and representation diagnostics in addition to task accuracy and R2, including batch-level mean and standard deviation versus dataset-level statistics as in [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee), gradient cosine and l2-difference as in [(Santurkar et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94be567c32ae76bdaadabd4975807a94181e39b3), and distance to the dominant eigenspace or matrix rank as in [(Scholkemper et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f15512c01c3fb087abd1c842b23663384e27410), because these metrics can reveal whether normalization improves the planned eight-layer GPS++ GNN even when final task metrics are similar. The experimental protocol should also be statistically rigorous by running multiple seeds, reporting per-task and aggregate mean and standard deviation across all sixteen tasks, and testing significance or confidence intervals, since [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee) reports large run-to-run variance and no formal significance tests. Because the plan uses batch size 20, 100-variable 3SAT graphs, Adam with learning rate 0.0001, and A100 time budgets of 24 hours for pretraining, the ablation should include wall-clock time, memory, and convergence curves, and should test batch-size sensitivity or running-statistics variants for BatchNorm, since small batches and variable graph sizes may make BatchNorm less reliable than in the batch-size-128 graph experiments of [(Cai et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c36571ac50808c75fa8a5d37f1041af22e89e6ee) or the full-batch synthetic setting of [(Santurkar et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94be567c32ae76bdaadabd4975807a94181e39b3). Finally, if iterative or eigenvector-based normalizations such as IterNorm or GraphNormv2 are added, the team should bound their computational cost, as in the iteration and group-size sensitivity analysis of [(Huang et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecffb9126f17d157b0a35b0e4f96f741d3f5faf9) and the 256-iteration message-passing study of [(Scholkemper et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f15512c01c3fb087abd1c842b23663384e27410), and use the earlier DBN group-size ablation design of [(Huang et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8270f523375d22fd417bb583a5b50411e5d5f58) as a template for hyperparameter sweeps.

## Context: Additional ablations isolate the contribution of regularization, pooling, and attention components; Method: We will also investigate the effects of dropout, pooling methods (mean vs. sum), and self-attention components.

### Support

The strongest direct support for the proposed ablation of self-attention and regularization comes from [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f), which uses a clause-variable bipartite CNF graph GNN with 128-dimensional literal and clause embeddings, MLPs with three hidden layers, 26 message-passing iterations, two multi-headed attention layers with K=8 heads, 128-dimensional attention input/output, a final 1024-to-128 attention output, 2-norm regularization with scaling 1e-10, and global canonical gradient clipping at 0.65. It evaluates on uniform random 3-SAT near the 4.26 clause-to-variable phase transition, with 100, 150, 200, 250, and 300 variables, 10,000 instances per size, 8:1:1 splits, balanced labels, Adam learning rate 2e-5, and binary cross-entropy. This is close to the plan's GPS++ clause-variable graph, shared-embedding pooling, and task-specific heads, and it shows that self-attention and regularization can be instantiated for SAT satisfiability prediction. Its support is limited because it reports only a qualitative attention heatmap on a 5-variable/35-clause instance and no accuracy, baseline, loss, runtime, or statistical significance for the attention or regularization components. The next strongest support is [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c), which trains GNNs on CNF graphs for balanced uniform-random 3-SAT at clause-to-atom ratios 4.4, 6.6, and 10, with exactly three literals per clause, uniformly chosen atoms, uniformly assigned negations, and exactly 50 percent satisfiable instances, and reports approximately 70 percent test accuracy at 6.6 and approximately 65 percent at 4.4 and 10, significantly above a random baseline. This supports the plan's use of phase-transition 3-SAT and GNN satisfiability prediction, but it does not ablate dropout, pooling, or self-attention and omits hardware, optimizer, learning rate, number of layers, dropout, pooling, and attention settings. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which trains a neural unsatisfiable-core predictor on over 150,000 Z3-generated subproblems from SATCOMP problems through 2017 and hard scheduling problems with 60-second timeouts, and integrates it into CDCL solvers, improving SATCOMP-2018 main-track solving from 187 to 205 problems for MiniSat and from 186 to 206 for Glucose 4.1 under 5,000-second timeouts. This supports the broader premise that learned Boolean-logic signals and large generated training sets are useful, but it does not vary dropout, pooling, or self-attention and therefore provides only indirect support for the proposed component ablations.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the most directly relevant attention and regularization work, [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f), does not demonstrate that self-attention or regularization improves SAT prediction; it provides no quantitative comparison between an attention model and a non-attention baseline, no accuracy or R2 values, no loss curves, no runtime, and no statistical significance, only a heatmap on a small 5-variable/35-clause instance. This means the plan's proposed self-attention ablation is not validated by positive evidence and must be treated as a hypothesis. The next strongest contradiction is that [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c) already achieves about 70 percent test accuracy at the 6.6 clause-to-atom ratio and about 65 percent at 4.4 and 10 with a GNN on CNF graphs, without reporting dropout, pooling, or self-attention, suggesting that these components may not be necessary for basic satisfiability prediction and that any claimed benefit must be measured against a simple GNN baseline. This work also limits reproducibility because it omits hardware, optimizer, learning rate, number of layers, dropout, pooling, and attention settings, so it cannot justify the plan's specific ablation choices. The weakest contradiction is that [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) reports strong solver-level gains, but they are not attributable to dropout, pooling, or self-attention; the method uses DRAT-derived unsatisfiable-core labels, over 150,000 generated subproblems, SATCOMP-2018 evaluation, 5,000-second timeouts, 8 identical 64-core machines, 20 GPUs, fixed 100-second NeuroCore pauses for MiniSat, exponential backoff starting at 5 seconds with multiplier 1.2 for Glucose, and a kappa hyperparameter tuned on SATCOMP-2018. Thus, it does not directly contradict the plan, but it warns against inferring architecture-component benefits from end-to-end solver improvements or from data-generation scale alone.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to run a reduced, controlled ablation before committing to the full one-million-instance, sixteen-task pretraining budget. Use a representative subset of uniform-random 3-SAT instances at the phase transition, for example 10,000 to 100,000 instances with 100 variables, balanced satisfiable and unsatisfiable labels, and an 8:1:1 train-validation-test split, while also testing clause-to-variable ratios around 4.26, 4.4, 6.6, and 10 to cover the settings in [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f) and [(Bünz, and Lamm, 2017-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/451c0b729d31aa14d278e14a2e2465d63e62d99c). Ablate no attention versus one or eight attention heads, mean versus sum graph pooling, dropout at 0, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.5, and regularization off, 2-norm, and gradient clipping, with fixed seeds and paired comparisons. Report accuracy for classification tasks, R2 for regression tasks, convergence curves, GPU hours, and confidence intervals or bootstrap significance, and evaluate at least satisfiability, model counting, backbone prediction, unsatisfiable core detection, and RL-based branching rather than only satisfiability. A second priority is to include strong baselines: a random baseline, a simple GNN without attention, dropout, or pooling, a GPS++ model without self-attention, and single-task pretrained models, so that the foundation-model claim is separated from the benefit of added components. For unsatisfiable-core detection, consider using DRAT-derived core labels or solver-integrated evaluation inspired by [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), but keep training and evaluation sets disjoint and avoid tuning solver-integration hyperparameters on the test set. A third priority is to report full implementation details and computational constraints: A100 GPU hours, Adam optimizer with 0.0001 learning rate, batch size 20, eight message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, 24-hour pretraining, 6-hour fine-tuning, and 2-hour frozen fine-tuning, plus dropout rates, pooling method, attention heads, regularization parameters, normalization, hardware, and seeds. Finally, if the reduced ablations show that self-attention, dropout, or a particular pooling method does not improve task-level metrics, remove or simplify that component instead of carrying it into the full foundation model, and tune regularization rather than copying unvalidated settings such as 2-norm scaling 1e-10 or clipping 0.65 from [(Chang et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/481a1f2266495f7cfbafb9928d5bea6303b38d5f).

## Context: The implementation fixes the model and optimizer settings used for pretraining and fine-tuning; Method: We will use a two-layer MLP for encoding, eight message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, Adam optimizer with 0.0001 learning rate, and batch size of 20.

### Support

The strongest quantitative support for the proposed fixed GNN components is [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9), which shows that sum aggregation with an MLP readout, as in GIN-0, is more reliable than mean or max pooling and one-layer aggregation: GIN-0 reached 92.4±2.5 on REDDIT-BINARY and 57.5±1.5 on REDDIT-MULTI5K, whereas mean-MLP collapsed to 50.0 and 20.0 on unlabeled Reddit graphs, and even with degree features mean-MLP reached only 71.2±4.6 and 41.3±2.1. This directly supports the proposed two-layer MLP encoder and the plan's sum-versus-mean pooling ablation, although it does not validate eight message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, Adam at 0.0001, batch size 20, or SAT-specific graphs. The strongest task-specific quantitative support for one of the sixteen tasks is unsatisfiable core detection in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), which generated 150,000 labeled unsat-core examples using Z3, DRAT-trim, and March branching, then integrated a learned core-variable predictor into MiniSat and Glucose. On 400 SATCOMP-2018 main-track problems with a 5000-second timeout, neuro-minisat solved 205 versus 187 for MiniSat, with 125 versus 109 satisfiable instances and 80 versus 78 unsatisfiable instances; neuro-glucose solved 206 versus 186 for Glucose 4.1. This supports including unsat-core prediction, using large solver-derived labels, and evaluating learned Boolean models through solver integration, though the setup required 8 64-core machines and 20 GPUs and did not report formal significance. Direct SAT evidence for GNN data efficiency and transfer comes from [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), where Graph-Q-SAT learns a Q-function over SAT solver states, transfers control to MiniSat, and is evaluated with Median Relative Iteration Reduction on random 3-SAT near the phase transition. Its data-efficiency experiment shows that even one SAT-50-218 training problem can generalize across sizes and to unSAT instances, while increasing to 800 problems helps in most cases, and zero-shot transfer to flat graph coloring produced MRIR above 1 in five of eight cases. This supports the plan's focus on 3-SAT at the solubility phase transition, data efficiency, and transfer to graph coloring, although exact MRIR values are not provided and the method is a single-task RL branching policy rather than a sixteen-task foundation model. The closest methodological precedent for pretraining and fine-tuning is [(Zhang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9f0145a34d9903123e9c87f1524de27698f63a), which processes formula graphs with a GNN and a two-layer perceptron, pretrains on tens of millions of generated small SAT instances with solution labels, and fine-tunes on random-track SAT Competition instances from 2012, 2013, 2014, 2016, and 2017 before evaluating on 255 SAT Competition 2018 random-track instances under a 1000-second timeout on a TITAN RTX GPU. This supports the plan's generated-data pretraining and competition-style fine-tuning, but the supplied excerpt lacks numeric outcomes and exact architecture hyperparameters. Model counting is supported by [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), whose BPNN estimates partition functions and model counts on factor graphs, generalizes from tens of SAT training problems to instances harder for an exact counter, and is hundreds of times faster than handcrafted approximate counters, although it uses a belief-propagation-preserving architecture rather than the proposed fixed GNN. The learning-rate choice is weakly supported by [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09), which trains a 20-layer GCN with 32 channels on 40,000 SATLIB 3-SAT-derived MIS graphs using Adam at 0.0001, single-graph mini-batches, and a Titan X GPU, showing that the same optimizer setting has been used on SAT-derived graphs, albeit with deeper and narrower layers. Additional support for GNN-based branching comes from [(Gasse et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03097420a1c4b2500cec6f29740f9d3c2164168f), which uses a graph convolutional policy trained by imitation learning from strong branching to reduce node counts in SCIP branch-and-bound and generalize to larger set covering, combinatorial auction, and facility location instances, though it is not SAT-specific. Synthetic-to-real transfer in logic is supported by [(Aygun et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3313f5c49b756243191c3f39c5f93692c6e4a69), where models trained on synthetic FwdP theorems proved more theorems than models trained only on TPTP theorems, and by [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062), which evaluates neural SAT sampling on 100-variable threshold 3-SAT and ratio-3 3-SAT, matching the plan's phase-transition focus. Weaker support includes [(Bengio et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f13a5148f7caa51ea946193d261d4f8ed32d81a) for generating SAT instances that preserve target distributions, [(Hůla et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b51499cb9a5dded9ae6fd7f821bcd3e43043db94) for recent GNN message-passing SAT experiments on 10,000 random formulas with 5 to 40 variables, and [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90) for using log-scale MSE for approximate #SAT, which is relevant to the plan's regression metrics.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that GNNs can be less efficient and less effective end-to-end than simpler models even when they have higher predictive accuracy. In [(Aygun et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3313f5c49b756243191c3f39c5f93692c6e4a69), GNNs had higher clause-prediction accuracy than MLPs on 9 of 10 theorem-proving datasets but solved 30 fewer problems overall, and GNNs were on average 1633x slower than the basic prover and 10x slower than MLPs. This directly challenges the plan's assumption that a GNN foundation model will be both more data-efficient and more time-efficient for Boolean logic, especially under the fixed 24-hour A100 pretraining budget. A second major contradiction is that transfer from a pretrained SAT model may not dominate task-specific training. In [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), zero-shot transfer from SAT-50-218 to flat graph coloring had MRIR above 1 in only five of eight cases and was lower than direct training on flat-75-180 with model selection on flat-100-239, indicating that the proposed multi-task foundation model may still need substantial target-task fine-tuning or direct training to beat single-task baselines. Third, learned GNN branching policies are not uniformly superior to strong solver heuristics. [(Gasse et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03097420a1c4b2500cec6f29740f9d3c2164168f) reports that the graph convolutional policy generally dominates in solving time but not on easy combinatorial auction instances where LambdaMART is faster or medium maximum independent set instances where reliability pseudocost is faster, and that all machine-learning approaches solve fewer MIS instances than reliability pseudocost with high runtime variability. This warns that the plan's RL-based branching task may not reliably outperform MiniSat, Glucose, or strong branching baselines. Fourth, the NeuroCore results, while positive, are modest and evaluation-limited: [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500) report only 10% and 11% overall solved-instance improvements, a 3% improvement on unsatisfiable instances, no formal significance tests, a hyperparameter kappa coarsely tuned on SATCOMP-2018, and a heavy serving setup with 8 64-core machines and 20 GPUs. This contradicts the expectation of large, low-cost gains from learned Boolean representations. Fifth, non-GNN specialized models can be competitive on 3-SAT: [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062) finds that a recurrent sampler is better than DiffusionSat on 3-SAT, while DiffusionSat is better on 3-Clique, and it reports diversity metrics such as 61.5%±7.6 unique valid solutions on threshold 3-SAT, suggesting that the plan's accuracy/R2 focus may miss sampling quality and that architecture choice matters. Sixth, the plan's uniform-random 100-variable 3-SAT distribution may be too narrow for a foundation model: [(Bengio et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f13a5148f7caa51ea946193d261d4f8ed32d81a) emphasizes that industrial SAT instance generation requires problem-specific destruction and reparation primitives and feature-based cluster validation, implying that random 3-SAT alone may not capture the diversity needed for broad transfer. Seventh, GNNs may struggle with symmetry: [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f) reports that NeuroSAT and DG-DAGRNN fail to predict any SAT solutions on small symmetric circuits with asymmetric solutions, a risk for Boolean tasks with repeated clause or variable structure. Eighth, the proposed fixed depth and width are not validated by the closest SAT-derived GNN work: [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09) uses 20 GCN layers with 32 channels and single-graph mini-batches on 1,200-vertex SAT-derived graphs, so eight layers and 64-dimensional embeddings may be under-capacity for larger graphs or inefficient relative to single-graph batching. Ninth, the 1-million-instance scale may be larger than necessary for some tasks or may obscure data-efficiency claims: [(Hůla et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b51499cb9a5dded9ae6fd7f821bcd3e43043db94) evaluates NeuroSAT-style GNNs on 10,000 random formulas with 5 to 40 variables, and the original NeuroSAT reference used 100,000 formulas with 10 to 40 variables, although the excerpt lacks numeric outcomes. Finally, several related works lack quantitative evidence or reproducibility details, such as [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90), which reports no numerical RMSE or MRE values, and [(Zhang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9f0145a34d9903123e9c87f1524de27698f63a), whose results section is empty in the supplied excerpt, limiting their ability to support the proposed fixed configuration.

### Suggested Action

Most important, run architecture and optimizer ablations before treating the fixed configuration as final: compare the proposed two-layer MLP encoder, eight message-passing layers, 64-dimensional embeddings, Adam at 0.0001, and batch size 20 against one-layer encoders, mean versus sum pooling, 4/8/12/20 message-passing layers, 32/64/128-dimensional embeddings, batch sizes 20/64/128, and batch/layer/hybrid normalization. This is motivated by [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9), which shows sum-plus-MLP outperforms mean and one-layer variants, and by [(Li et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d77c0e84972c256a8922b952b04330e369f65f09), which uses 20 layers and 32 channels on SAT-derived graphs, indicating that depth and width should not be fixed without ablation. Second, compare the foundation model against strong task-specific baselines with matched data and compute, including MiniSat, Glucose, ApproxMC, strong branching or reliability pseudocost, NeuroSAT/DG-DAGRNN, BPNN, and recurrent or diffusion samplers, reporting solved counts, cactus and scatter plots, accuracy, R2, node counts, and wall-clock time. This addresses the modest NeuroCore gains in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500), the baseline-sensitive branching results in [(Gasse et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03097420a1c4b2500cec6f29740f9d3c2164168f), the model-counting speed of [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49), and the 3-SAT sampler comparison in [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062). Third, align metrics with each Boolean task: use log-scale RMSE and MRE against ApproxMC or exact counters for model counting as in [(Kuck et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/edbcaf80ad4218fe04e474036484b5543c6f8f49) and [(Saveri, and Bortolussi, 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fabacde74bd62b59eb6cad22efaf834b0ab32e90); use DRAT-trim-derived core labels and solver-integrated evaluation for unsat-core detection as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/769d7c07eb58fb4957543a486c9db9db8c700500); use node count and runtime for branching as in [(Gasse et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/03097420a1c4b2500cec6f29740f9d3c2164168f); and add diversity or uniqueness metrics for sampling tasks as in [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062). Fourth, broaden the pretraining distribution beyond one million uniform-random 100-variable 3-SAT instances by including variable sizes from small to 100, clause-to-variable ratios near and away from phase transition, SATLIB/SATCOMP instances, industrial-style instances, and structured problems such as graph coloring, Latin squares, Sudoku, logical circuits, 4-SAT, and 5-SAT. This responds to the industrial distribution warning in [(Bengio et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f13a5148f7caa51ea946193d261d4f8ed32d81a), the transfer results in [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52), the small-variable GNN SAT experiments in [(Hůla et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b51499cb9a5dded9ae6fd7f821bcd3e43043db94), and the large generated pretraining data in [(Zhang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab9f0145a34d9903123e9c87f1524de27698f63a). Fifth, add statistical rigor and reproducibility: report multiple seeds, confidence intervals, significance tests, hardware, GPU type, batch size, training time, and validation/test splits, and avoid tuning hyperparameters on the final test set. This is needed because [(Kurin et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb9764ae6d48abcb75036631a385af66170e3b52) reports five runs but no significance tests, and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) acknowledges coarse tuning of kappa on SATCOMP-2018. Sixth, measure inference efficiency explicitly, including GNN forward-pass latency, GPU serving cost, and frozen fine-tuning cost, because [(Aygun et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f3313f5c49b756243191c3f39c5f93692c6e4a69) shows GNNs can be 1633x slower than a basic prover and [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308) required 20 GPUs for solver queries. Seventh, test symmetry and structured robustness on symmetric circuits, Latin squares, Sudoku, and 3-Clique, and consider symmetry-aware features or equivariant message passing if failures appear, motivated by [(Yan et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9afc6d406e25bcd66e460a6fab5b08a5982928f), [(Hůla et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b51499cb9a5dded9ae6fd7f821bcd3e43043db94), and [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062). Eighth, replace the simple mean loss with task-weighted or uncertainty-weighted multi-task optimization and monitor per-task validation curves, because the sixteen tasks include classification, regression, and solver-state tasks with different scales. Ninth, use solver-generated labels and curriculum learning, including DRAT-trim cores, uniform solution generators, and small-to-large instance curricula, as in [(Selsam, and Bjørner, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cb92e876548db98a4a9dc2d587d9278c3f35308), [(Freivalds, and Kozlovics, 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9386b49cc4ece5231997ed50b7372c38bff00062), and [(Hůla et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b51499cb9a5dded9ae6fd7f821bcd3e43043db94). Finally, consider hybrid task heads or specialized baselines for model counting and sampling, such as belief-propagation-preserving GNNs or diffusion/recurrent samplers, before concluding that the fixed GPS++-style GNN is sufficient for all sixteen tasks.

## Context: The compute plan bounds the experimental budget for pretraining and fine-tuning; Method: We will allocate 24 hours for pretraining, 6 hours for fine-tuning, and 2 hours for frozen fine-tuning experiments on A100 GPUs.

### Support

Strongest support for the 6-hour fine-tuning budget comes from direct A100/RTX wall-clock measurements in [(Wang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba0b9250d5e91c01eb7cca8269afec23f24c515), where one-GPU fine-tuning of BERT-base and DistilBERT-related models on RTE, SST, CoNLL2003, IMDB, SQuAD v1/v2, and MNLI took 30 seconds to 3447 seconds, far below 6 hours, so 6 A100 hours is generous for a smaller GPS++ GNN fine-tuned on 100 to 100,000 3SAT instances with 16 task heads. Direct A100 fine-tuning evidence also comes from [(Bartelds et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1eeabaaef523baee23db070baf396dacddc3665e), which used a single 40 GB A100 for XLS-R 317M speech fine-tuning and reported about 160 fine-tuning runs of about 2 hours each, making the proposed 2-hour frozen or head-only budget plausible for a compact GNN if only task heads are trained. For the 24-hour pretraining budget, [(Wang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba0b9250d5e91c01eb7cca8269afec23f24c515) provides a methodological template by separating pre-training/distillation from fine-tuning and reporting a 175.5-hour four-GPU distillation/training run plus a one-GPU BERT-base pre-training estimate extrapolated from a 12-hour partial run, suggesting that 24 A100 hours is not absurd for a compact model but must be validated. [(Khanna et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27023f97f0920613339493e66acc3e32f3e29172) supports the possibility of compressing pretraining cost by using parameter-efficient extended pretraining, reporting 100 GPU hours for ViT-L extended pretraining versus more than 960 GPU hours for full domain-specific pretraining and 8x to 10x compute savings, which is relevant if the Boolean foundation model can use LoRA or partial unfreezing rather than full GPS++ pretraining. [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f9ceb5ffad8e7a066dfc9d9aaa5153b714740ee) supports A100 fine-tuning budgeting at a larger scale, showing full fine-tuning of a 7B vision-language-action model required 8 A100 GPUs for 5 to 15 hours per task and LoRA on 1 A100 for 10 to 15 hours, so a 6-hour single-A100 fine-tuning budget is reasonable for a much smaller GNN if parameter-efficient adaptation is used. [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) supports the broader premise that larger pretraining improves downstream task performance, with 175B GPT-3 achieving few-shot PIQA 82.8, ARC Easy 70.1, ARC Challenge 51.5, OpenBookQA 65.4, and Winogrande 77.7, but its 300B-token, 175B-parameter, V100-based pretraining is far larger than the proposed A100 plan and therefore only weakly supports the exact 24-hour budget. [(Scialom et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d304d0bdfa81fd10b187aa0e4f41d410eb19d6e3) supports efficient fine-tuning of a pretrained foundation model, retaining 99.8% of upper-bound performance for T0 11B and 98.0% for T0 3B after sequential fine-tuning with 100,000 examples per task and 1% rehearsal, which is analogous to the plan's leave-one-task-out fine-tuning and suggests 6 hours may be enough if the GNN is small and the held-out task uses up to 100,000 instances. Weaker support comes from [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f5c02e55b556ce88c4121be12b6958bc093a63) and [(Huang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dffa47c848c734f0ee77532041648f3e1bbd6ae2), which show that compute-budgeted partial training can be efficient, with GreenTrainer saving 17% to 27% training FLOPs relative to LoRA on OPT 350M to 6.7B summarization and ElasticTrainer achieving 2x to 4x training speedups on embedded vision fine-tuning, indicating that frozen or partial fine-tuning can be made efficient, although neither directly validates Boolean GNN A100 hours.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction to the 2-hour frozen fine-tuning budget is [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f9ceb5ffad8e7a066dfc9d9aaa5153b714740ee), which found that freezing the vision encoder or training only the last layer of a 7B vision-language-action model produced poor downstream success, while full fine-tuning was best and LoRA with rank 32 on all linear layers was needed to match full fine-tuning on a single A100 for 10 to 15 hours; this suggests that freezing the entire shared GPS++ GNN and training only task heads may underperform, and 2 hours may be too short if the shared architecture must adapt. A second major contradiction concerns the 24-hour pretraining budget: [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) shows that foundation-model-scale pretraining used 175B parameters, 300B tokens, 96 layers, 12288 hidden dimension, 3.2M-token batch, and V100 model parallelism, with downstream gains tied to scale, so 24 A100 hours is unlikely to produce a general-purpose foundation model if the plan expects GPT-3-like transfer, although it may be adequate for a compact GNN on 1M 3SAT instances. [(Wang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba0b9250d5e91c01eb7cca8269afec23f24c515) also contradicts an unvalidated 24-hour pretraining assumption by reporting a 175.5-hour four-GPU pre-training/distillation run and only an extrapolated one-GPU BERT-base pre-training estimate from a 12-hour partial run, meaning 24 hours is not directly confirmed and could be too low for stable multi-task pretraining. [(Bartelds et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1eeabaaef523baee23db070baf396dacddc3665e) provides a partial contradiction for pretraining cost, reporting about 70 A100 hours for further pre-training and about 390 total A100 hours for XLS-R 317M speech experiments, so the proposed 24-hour pretraining budget is lower than a comparable further-pretraining run, although the GNN may be smaller. [(Huang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dffa47c848c734f0ee77532041648f3e1bbd6ae2) contradicts rigid fixed-time budgets by showing that MobileNetV2 did not converge within a fixed 12-epoch budget and required extra epochs, implying that 24, 6, and 2 hour caps may stop training before convergence for some tasks or data subsamples. [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f5c02e55b556ce88c4121be12b6958bc093a63) suggests that frozen or partial fine-tuning may not always match full fine-tuning under a FLOPs budget, because GreenTrainer had to select tensors to save 17% to 27% FLOPs relative to LoRA and still match or exceed baselines, so a simple frozen-head 2-hour run may sacrifice accuracy. [(Scialom et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d304d0bdfa81fd10b187aa0e4f41d410eb19d6e3) weakly contradicts the plan's data-efficiency assumptions by showing that efficient sequential fine-tuning used 100,000 examples per task plus 1% rehearsal to retain 99.8% or 98.0% of upper-bound performance, whereas the plan subsamples down to 100 instances and does not include rehearsal, so fine-tuning from a multi-task Boolean foundation model may require more data or rehearsal to avoid task interference. Weaker contradiction comes from [(Khanna et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27023f97f0920613339493e66acc3e32f3e29172), which reports more than 960 GPU hours for full domain-specific pretraining versus 100 GPU hours for parameter-efficient extended pretraining, indicating that 24 hours may only be feasible with strong parameter freezing or LoRA rather than full GPS++ pretraining.

### Suggested Action

Most important, run a short A100 pilot to measure actual throughput and convergence for the GPS++ architecture on the 1M 100-variable 3SAT dataset, logging wall-clock hours, FLOPs, GPU memory, validation accuracy/R^2, and loss curves for pretraining, full fine-tuning, and frozen-head fine-tuning, following the measurement template in [(Wang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ba0b9250d5e91c01eb7cca8269afec23f24c515) and the FLOPs/wall-clock reporting in [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f5c02e55b556ce88c4121be12b6958bc093a63); use these measurements to revise the 24, 6, and 2 hour caps before the main experiments. Second, do not treat frozen fine-tuning as only training task heads; because [(Kim et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f9ceb5ffad8e7a066dfc9d9aaa5153b714740ee) found frozen encoders and last-layer-only training poor while LoRA matched full fine-tuning, add a LoRA/adapter or sandwich fine-tuning baseline for the GNN shared layers, with rank sweeps analogous to rank 32 in the VLA work, and compare it against the 2-hour frozen budget. Third, use parameter-efficient extended pretraining or partial unfreezing if 24 hours is insufficient, motivated by [(Khanna et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/27023f97f0920613339493e66acc3e32f3e29172), which achieved 79.3% top-1 on fMoW-RGB with 100 GPU hours and 6% trainable parameters versus more than 960 GPU hours for full pretraining; for the Boolean plan this could mean freezing most message-passing layers and training LoRA on attention, edge, or node encoders plus task heads. Fourth, include rehearsal or multi-task regularization in leave-one-task-out fine-tuning, because [(Scialom et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d304d0bdfa81fd10b187aa0e4f41d410eb19d6e3) retained 99.8% and 98.0% of upper-bound performance with 1% rehearsal over 100,000 examples per task, so the plan should test 1% to 10% rehearsal buffers from other Boolean tasks when fine-tuning on 100, 1,000, 10,000, and 100,000 instances. Fifth, adopt convergence-aware stopping rather than hard hour caps, since [(Huang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dffa47c848c734f0ee77532041648f3e1bbd6ae2) showed a fixed 12-epoch budget prevented MobileNetV2 convergence; use validation plateau, early stopping, and per-task time budgets, especially for harder tasks such as unsatisfiable core detection or model counting. Sixth, use scaling-law and data-contamination checks inspired by [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0), including filtering benchmark overlaps and reporting pretraining tokens/epochs for the 1M 3SAT corpus, because the GPT-3 paper reported a filtering bug and large compute dependence. Finally, consider partial-training or tensor-selection methods from [(Huang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dffa47c848c734f0ee77532041648f3e1bbd6ae2) and [(Huang et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33f5c02e55b556ce88c4121be12b6958bc093a63) to meet the 2-hour frozen budget, but only after validating that selected GNN tensors preserve accuracy on all sixteen tasks.
