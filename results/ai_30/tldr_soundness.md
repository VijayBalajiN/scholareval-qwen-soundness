## Strengths 
The strongest support is for the core BID architecture and incremental update idea, because [(Selvaganapathy et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5089a53019d766be2318a1496fcadd96050c7d36) demonstrates a broad-learning Android malware detector using static API, intent, and permission features, feature and enhancement nodes, sparse autoencoders, one-shot parameter computation, and incremental retraining with about 0.2 second scan time. The SRAE composite-loss idea is also supported by [(Gopakumar et al., 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b53a99633f5f1486e8c37d14d0a13e8c1948bc9), which shows that autoencoder-derived regularization plus a graph penalty can improve stable sparse feature selection, and by [(Hinton, 1989-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a57c6d627ffc667ae3547073876c35d6420accff) for weight-decay regularization. The evaluation plan is supported by [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/288d9b280299e1a7a88b0bb751c17c6c8fad50c5) for multi-dataset benchmarking and by [(Kim et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f39f1ddcee34e309501b6fb854d868ae991888a3) for reporting accuracy together with execution time, memory, and GPU usage when comparing malware detectors.

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the evaluation and efficiency claims. [(Pendlebury et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ab5a2b7d2caa8969c06e1f24dfda2a6f5c654f6) shows that temporally consistent Android malware evaluation can produce much lower F1 than biased random or cross-validated splits, with 24-month AUT F1 of 0.58 for SVM, 0.32 for random forest, and 0.64 for a deep model, so a simple 70/30 split may overstate real performance. [(Molina-Coronado et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/036ec2ff218d51c8edb8726c1061b202cd245b96) shows that optimized retraining with change detection and selective data can reduce retraining cost with small performance loss, so BID must compare against optimized retraining rather than only naive full retraining. [(Mesbah et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3e965c0b9b0e37349b17743569b5628085de1f00) reports that a continually updated detector still degraded from F1 94.40 percent in 2019 to below 80 percent in 2021, suggesting that a single pseudo-inverse-updated model may not preserve knowledge over long-term drift. Additionally, [(Chen et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/da1500380c9f5229f3a18aa14eea13e8981f8ef7) reports 99.1 percent accuracy for a non-incremental deep static pipeline, challenging the necessity of incremental broad learning for accuracy alone. Finally, [(Selvaganapathy et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5089a53019d766be2318a1496fcadd96050c7d36) provides limited quantitative accuracy evidence, reporting mainly scan time and qualitative incremental gains, so it cannot by itself confirm that BID will achieve high detection accuracy.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Redesign the static and incremental evaluations to be temporally consistent, prevalence-matched, and duplicate-aware, following [(Pendlebury et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7ab5a2b7d2caa8969c06e1f24dfda2a6f5c654f6) and [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/288d9b280299e1a7a88b0bb751c17c6c8fad50c5). Keep the planned 70/30 and 5:3:2 splits only as secondary checks, and add time-ordered or first-seen splits, monthly or yearly test windows, AUT or time-integrated F1, macro and weighted F1, per-class precision and recall, AUC, confidence intervals, and statistical significance across repeated splits.

Make the BID and SRAE mechanism reproducible and ablated by specifying the partitioned pseudo-inverse update equations, feature and enhancement node counts, SRAE architecture, loss terms, regularization, numerical stability safeguards, memory footprint, and update latency. Ablate data reconstruction, relationship reconstruction, and sparsity, and compare against random RVFLNN, standard sparse autoencoder, full BLS retraining, and optimized retraining baselines such as change-detection-triggered retraining from [(Molina-Coronado et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/036ec2ff218d51c8edb8726c1061b202cd245b96), while reporting initial training time, incremental update time, total update time, and complete retraining time on identical hardware.

Strengthen the baseline suite and robustness tests by adding strong simple and representation-learning baselines, including reduced-feature random forest or decision tree, permissions-based classifiers, and, where API features are available, MaMaDroid or MAPAS-style comparators, as supported by [(Polatidis et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ddde487f29910e0a91f2d8d055f002e9c1f4f9c2), [(Zaidi et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b180790aec6046bbce5137cfdcee70f27dcf59b), and [(Kim et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f39f1ddcee34e309501b6fb854d868ae991888a3). Also add repackaging, variant, or adversarial robustness tests and malicious-class precision and recall under 1:1, 1:5, 1:10, and 1:20 benign-to-malware ratios, following the motivation in [(Yuan et al., 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfbdf36d50066c39dedf688150d63ba4b84d7e52).
