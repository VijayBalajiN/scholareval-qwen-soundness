## Strengths 
The strongest support is for the core idea that a compact, coverage-maximizing representative subset can improve training over random or full data. Coverage-based demonstration selection showed that greedy coverage selection outperformed similarity-only and random baselines [(Gupta et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52350cfb03f4cccdbe141727334a5083bd613222), and greedy max-coverage selection improved noisy-label classifier training [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414). The similarity-graph component is supported by graph-based selection using k nearest neighbors and greedy node selection [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), and by cosine-neighborhood selection for ambiguous examples [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611). Balanced GPT-3.5 generation is supported by large-scale label-conditioned synthetic data generation [(Clavié, and Souli'e, 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4f9f0cc8c138047a61bdb11b1a352e3d1aed035), and the BERT-base fine-tuning and F1 evaluation design is supported by sentiment and active-learning baselines [(Mozafari et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/743d39f81ae653c0d5ebf6e8ef3d0a4d17dfe75e), [(Yuan et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e06b7f72270900544284e0898aac2bb564ff58b).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions are that the distinctive binary-search threshold mechanism and fixed 0.707 floor are not validated by the cited coverage or graph-selection literature. Greedy coverage selection is supported, but not monotonic cosine-threshold search [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414), and k-neighbor graph methods often avoid hard similarity cutoffs [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611). Synthetic data can be less diverse and less effective than real data, especially for subjective sentiment [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), and LLM-generated labels can be noisy [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c). The less-is-more claim is also challenged by evidence that synthetic performance can improve with volume before saturating [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f), and that balanced synthetic corpora can still contain harmful examples unless filtered or weighted [(Gao et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f7e03e4ccd26c762090e25dc5d2eb1e1f8c641d).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Directly test the binary-search monotonicity assumption before treating it as the main contribution: sweep similarity thresholds and target coverage on the 6,000 SST2-like and 12,800 FewRel synthetic corpora and on MNIST, report achieved coverage, selected subset size, per-label or per-relation coverage, accuracy, F1, confidence intervals, and paired significance tests, clarify whether 25 repetitions means 5 seeds times 5 variants or 25 independent seeds, and use grid search or interpolation if the threshold-coverage curve is not monotonic [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414), [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), [(Geifman et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dfb58733b367005945a2f2c2028145e4678efd7a).

Make coverage task-aware and ablate the fixed constraints: enforce positive/negative coverage for SST2 and coverage of all 64 FewRel relations, use a k=10 nearest-neighbor graph as a default, vary maximum outdegree and minimum similarity threshold around 0.707, include a low-similarity hard-case stratum, and compare ACS against random, k-Means, k-center, full synthetic, human-labeled, and quality-filtered baselines [(Gupta et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52350cfb03f4cccdbe141727334a5083bd613222), [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002bf0720404e5dc6bf43eff64f116ec755b405f), [(Yu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab83eae00db6a19de1ebdb23f7ceed29b5832675).

Add synthetic-data quality control and rigorous evaluation: use few-shot label-conditioned GPT-3.5 prompts, overgenerate examples, then filter by label consistency, validity, or human spot checks, estimate label noise, use disjoint train/test splits, and report macro and per-class or per-relation metrics with reproducibility details [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c), [(Xie et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf1e5a17b3adead7016eb52ddc3007673ed382f), [(Sellam et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b540745f4b51f95bf90fb3420e51edb037fc51a).
