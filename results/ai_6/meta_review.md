## To determine the optimal configuration for modified max coverage sampling, we will use binary search to find the optimal similarity threshold that achieves a desired coverage level.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed modified max-coverage idea is coverage-based demonstration selection in [(Gupta et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52350cfb03f4cccdbe141727334a5083bd613222), where SET-BSR greedily selects up to k=8 demonstrations from a 44,000-instance pool to maximize coverage of salient aspects and then orders them by relevance; on SMCalFlow-CS with Codex it reached 75.7 IID accuracy and 61.2 compositional accuracy, improving +21.0 and +49.3 over COSINE and outperforming BSR, RANDOM, COSINE, BM25, and LFCOV, while remaining competitive with trained EPR and CEIL on IID and much stronger on compositional transfer. This directly supports the research plan's core claim that a compact coverage-maximizing subset can outperform similarity-only or random selection, although it does not validate binary-search threshold tuning, a cosine-similarity graph, or synthetic classifier training. The next strongest support is the 2024 clustering work, which extends the 2023 coverage/diversity line to instruction-data fine-tuning: [(Yu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab83eae00db6a19de1ebdb23f7ceed29b5832675) uses k-center and k-means clustering over Cohere embeddings of Alpaca and WizardLM prompts/completions, where k-center is a covering-style objective and k-means-random is a directly comparable baseline; it reports that k-means-random beats random, quality-weighted k-means improves further, and iterative k-means-quality often outperforms baselines, while cluster count k strongly affects downstream performance and correlates with silhouette score. This supports using covering objectives and clustering baselines in the plan, but it optimizes cluster count rather than a similarity threshold and provides only qualitative results in the excerpt. [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a) supports the motivation for diversity-aware downsampling of synthetic data: across 10 classification tasks, real data had higher Remote Clique and Chamfer diversity than few-shot GPT-3.5-Turbo synthetic data, which in turn had higher diversity than zero-shot synthetic data, and few-shot synthetic data improved RoBERTa Macro-F1 and accuracy by 10.3 and 8.9 points over zero-shot synthetic data. This aligns with the plan's concern that LLM-generated corpora can be redundant or skewed, but it measures full-set diversity rather than selected subsets. [(Qin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b217b6bc340af9a10bebbf8acc36ea30871769bd) provides weaker but relevant support for balancing similarity and diversity: IDS selected k=4 demonstrations over q=3 iterations and averaged about 1.7 points above Top-k-Consistency across GSM8K, MATH, CommonsenseQA, LogiQA, BoolQ, and AGNews, with about a 4% relative gain on mathematical reasoning and intermediate Sentence-BERT similarity between pure similarity and random voting. Finally, the 2024 DENSITY work offers the weakest direct support because it extends coverage/diversity sampling to large-scale C4 pretraining but the excerpt does not report a standalone quantitative DENSITY result, binary search, or a similarity threshold: [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603) evaluates DENSITY in an iso-compute setting with T5-Small/Large over 111 tasks, but the available evidence is insufficient to support the specific binary-search threshold mechanism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradictory evidence is that synthetic data can be systematically less diverse and less effective than real data, which challenges the plan's assumption that coverage-based downsampling of synthetic data will automatically yield superior classifier performance. In [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), real training sets generally had higher diversity than few-shot synthetic sets, and real-data models outperformed synthetic-data models in most tasks; with RoBERTa, real data averaged 16.9 Macro-F1 and 14.9 accuracy points higher than zero-shot synthetic data and 6.7 Macro-F1 and 6.1 accuracy points higher than few-shot synthetic data, while zero-shot synthetic data caused large drops on high-subjectivity tasks. This does not refute coverage sampling per se, but it contradicts the idea that a coverage threshold alone can close the synthetic-to-real quality gap, especially if the GPT-3.5 generation is zero-shot or low-diversity. The next strongest contradiction is that newer 2024 work complicates the 2023 coverage optimism by showing that a single static diversity hyperparameter may be insufficient and that binary search over a similarity threshold is not the validated mechanism. [(Yu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab83eae00db6a19de1ebdb23f7ceed29b5832675) shows that downstream performance changes dramatically with cluster count k, that silhouette score can guide k before training, and that iterative k-means-quality sampling can outperform static baselines, while transfer to Llama-3-8B was mixed and iterative sampling slightly decreased reasoning performance. This suggests the plan should compare binary-searched thresholds against k/silhouette-based selection and possibly iterative feedback, rather than assuming threshold search is optimal. [(Gupta et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52350cfb03f4cccdbe141727334a5083bd613222) also contradicts a narrow cosine-neighbor implementation: its effective coverage was over salient aspects or substructures, not pairwise cosine neighbors, and it used fixed k=8 with relevance ordering rather than a binary-searched similarity threshold; COSINE similarity selection was a weak baseline, but the winning method did not rely on thresholded cosine coverage. [(Qin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b217b6bc340af9a10bebbf8acc36ea30871769bd) provides a more modest contradiction to monotonic improvement: IDS improved over Top-k-Consistency by only about 1.7 points on average, and performance did not always increase with more iterations q=1, 5, or 7, so the plan should not assume that increasing coverage or more selection iterations will monotonically improve BERT fine-tuning. The weakest contradiction is from [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603), where the available excerpt does not provide a standalone quantitative result for DENSITY and instead highlights that ASK-LLM quality scoring outperforms perplexity filtering; this weakly suggests that coverage/diversity sampling may need to be combined with quality scoring, but the evidence is limited.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to directly validate the binary-search assumption before treating it as the main contribution: sweep similarity thresholds and target coverage levels on the SST2-like and FewRel synthetic corpora, compute the pruned graph coverage, train BERT-base with the planned 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1, and plot accuracy/F1 against threshold, coverage, and subset size; include the planned MNIST monotonicity check but also text-domain checks, and report paired permutation tests or confidence intervals across the stated 25 repetitions. The second most important action is to make coverage task-aware rather than purely cosine-neighbor based: for SST2 enforce positive/negative and possibly nuance coverage, for FewRel enforce coverage of all 64 relation types, and consider salient-aspect or substructure coverage inspired by [(Gupta et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52350cfb03f4cccdbe141727334a5083bd613222), because that work shows aspect-based coverage can outperform similarity-only selection. Third, add the clustering and quality baselines implied by [(Yu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab83eae00db6a19de1ebdb23f7ceed29b5832675): implement k-center and k-means-random, choose k using silhouette or elbow, include quality-weighted sampling if a cheap quality scorer is available, and compare these to binary-searched ACS to show that threshold search is not merely a reparameterization of cluster count. Fourth, ablate the proposed constraints rather than fixing them: vary the minimum cosine threshold around 0.707, for example 0.50, 0.60, 0.707, and 0.80, and vary the maximum nearest-neighbor outdegree, then evaluate subset diversity with Remote Clique and Chamfer Distance from [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a) plus silhouette from [(Yu et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab83eae00db6a19de1ebdb23f7ceed29b5832675); if 0.707 is not supported, make it task-specific or learned. Fifth, improve synthetic generation and quality control: use few-shot GPT-3.5 prompts as in [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), generate balanced labels/relations, and optionally add quality scoring or filtering motivated by [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603), because coverage alone may not remove low-quality synthetic examples. Finally, clarify the evaluation protocol: define whether 25 repetitions means 5 seeds times 5 prompt or subset variants, report subset sizes, embedding model, hardware, API cost, macro F1 for FewRel, and human-labeled test-set leakage checks, so the less-is-more claim is reproducible and comparable to the baselines.

## To select representative synthetic samples, we will implement a greedy max-coverage approximation algorithm that selects k "representative" samples by pruning edges through our binary search procedure.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed greedy max-coverage component comes from [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414), which uses a greedy monotone submodular max-coverage objective to select a representative subset for training under noisy labels. That work is directly relevant to the ACS plan because it shows that coverage-based selection can preserve training capacity and improve downstream test accuracy: on CIFAR-10N and CIFAR-100N it improved re-labeling models such as DivideMix and SOP+ by up to 21.6 percent over standard cross-entropy training, and on ImageNet-N with 20 percent asymmetric synthetic noise it outperformed Uniform, SmallLoss, Margin, k-CenterGreedy, Forgetting, GraNd, SSP, and Moderate baselines by up to 8.6 percent at selection ratios 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, and 0.4. As an iterative progression, this later coverage-based pruning result provides stronger evidence for the proposed max-coverage mechanism than the earlier budgeted-selection work. The weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eeff1edde4fc66284e7bedeb8eb8878ed4560f4), which demonstrates that a small budgeted subset of representative samples can preserve downstream performance in a low-query-budget LLM text detection setting. Its sequential uncertainty-based selection improved DetectGPT by up to 3 percentage points in AUROC across XSum, SQuAD, and WritingPrompts, with 200 samples per dataset and nine source-proxy model combinations. This supports the broader idea that selecting a compact representative subset can reduce cost without sacrificing performance, but it is less directly supportive because it uses Gaussian-process predictive variance and a typical set rather than similarity-graph coverage, and it targets zero-shot detection scoring rather than downsampling synthetic training data for BERT fine-tuning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct negative result but a scope mismatch: the proposed method's distinctive mechanism, binary search over similarity thresholds to prune edges and achieve a target coverage level, is not supported by either related work. [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414) validates greedy coverage selection, but its coverage is defined by neighborhood confidence in noisy-label training, not by cosine-similarity graph edges, and it does not report binary-search threshold tuning, a minimum similarity threshold of 0.707, maximum nearest-neighbor constraints, LLM-generated text, BERT fine-tuning, or coverage levels from 0.0 to 1.0. Therefore, its positive results cannot be taken as evidence that ACS's specific edge-pruning binary search will improve SST2 or FewRel classifier training. A weaker but still important tension comes from [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eeff1edde4fc66284e7bedeb8eb8878ed4560f4), which suggests that uncertainty-based scoring may be a more effective budgeted selection signal than a simple representative-subset heuristic, since its GP surrogate selected samples by predictive variance and improved DetectGPT by up to 3 percentage points in AUROC. However, this is indirect because the task is detection rather than training, the excerpt does not provide exact AUROC values, confidence intervals, p-values, or statistical significance tests, and the method does not use max-coverage, similarity graphs, or synthetic training-data downsampling. Together, these works leave the monotonicity assumption, the 0.707 cosine floor, and the claim that coverage below 1.0 excludes noisy or less informative synthetic samples insufficiently tested.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the binary-search component directly testable and to report evidence for the monotonicity assumption before claiming ACS is optimal. The plan should sweep target coverage from 0.0 to 1.0 on the planned SST2 6,000-sample and FewRel 12,800-sample synthetic corpora, and on MNIST for cross-modal validation, while recording the selected k, pruned edge count, average degree, per-class or per-relation coverage, and training/test accuracy, F1, precision, and recall. It should compare binary-search threshold selection against fixed thresholds, random thresholds, and no pruning, with at least the planned 25 repeated runs or, if only 5 random seeds are used, explicitly reconcile the 25-run claim with the seed count and report confidence intervals and significance tests. The next priority is to strengthen the baseline suite using the baselines validated in [(Park et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca622fde1f31e86a3328504c17605298c6129414), including Uniform, SmallLoss, Margin, k-CenterGreedy, Forgetting, GraNd, SSP, and Moderate, and to add a confidence- or self-correctability-based coverage variant that mirrors Park's neighborhood-confidence objective. This would test whether ACS's cosine-similarity coverage is better than alternative coverage definitions for synthetic text and would allow reporting noisy-example ratio or label-artifact ratio analogues. A further improvement is to incorporate the budgeted uncertainty idea from [(Deng et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eeff1edde4fc66284e7bedeb8eb8878ed4560f4) as a baseline or hybrid: use LLM log-probabilities, embedding density, or GP/nearest-neighbor uncertainty to weight coverage, select a small typical set, and stop early when marginal coverage or uncertainty gain is low. Finally, the method should specify and ablate the constraints that are currently under-evidenced, especially the maximum nearest-neighbor limit and the 0.707 minimum cosine similarity, because text embeddings may require different similarity floors than image or confidence-based settings; the paper should also report computational resources, query or embedding costs, hardware, hyperparameters, and whether balanced label generation and per-relation coverage are maintained after pruning.

## To represent synthetic data relationships, we will embed synthetic text data into a latent space using pre-trained embeddings and construct a similarity graph where nodes represent data points and edges are weighted by pairwise cosine similarity.

### Support

The strongest support for embedding synthetic text with pre-trained embeddings and building a cosine-similarity graph comes from the 2024 FastGAS work, [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), which extends earlier graph-based selection and cosine retrieval work by constructing a data similarity graph over unlabeled instances by linking each instance to its ten nearest neighbors, partitioning the graph into K components, and using greedy node selection; it outperformed two selective-annotation baselines in 13 of 14 dataset-budget evaluations and four graph/core-set baselines in 10 of 14, with k=10 performing well and extreme k values hurting selection, directly supporting the proposed nearest-neighbor cosine graph and maximum-nearest-neighbor constraint, although the embedding model and statistical significance are not specified. A strong but less direct support is [(Lacombe et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cfb7cc9502505ae6c4db1e586d128f6a3df50c2), which precomputes cosine similarity between scientific text paragraphs and mean, max, and sentence query representations to retrieve relevant text for contrastive training, improving average AUROC by +4.26% over a graph-only GIN baseline and +1.54% over uniform-random sampling, with max and sentence similarity most consistent, supporting pre-trained embeddings, cosine similarity, and precomputed similarity for efficient sample selection, but it does not build a full pairwise text graph or coverage-based downsampling. [(Cui et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d4dcee4628745f79f5e352859172817fece3e83) also supports pairwise similarity as a relational structure by ranking node-pair similarities, selecting high-similarity positives and low-similarity negatives, and using adaptive thresholds, with clustering gains over GALA of 2.95% accuracy, 5.20% NMI, and 6.20% ARI on Cora and 12.29%, 18.45%, and 13.11% on Wiki, though it uses a one-layer 500-dimensional MLP, Laplacian smoothing k=2/3, 400 epochs, Adam lr 0.001, and not BERT fine-tuning on synthetic text. [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002bf0720404e5dc6bf43eff64f116ec755b405f) supports cosine-similarity thresholding by selecting low-similarity test pairs at or below 0.074, the tenth percentile, to create a 194-instance gold subset from 5,309 test instances, showing that embedding similarity can identify informative examples, but it targets test difficulty rather than training coverage. [(Zhang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/271ff9dbeacd05276f1f192453793e772ad91b69) supports graph-based representative subset selection and similarity-based retrieval, reporting qualitative outperformance over baselines and a significant drop when similarity retrieval is replaced by random retrieval at annotation budget 100, but it does not specify a cosine-weighted graph or numeric significance. The weakest support is [(Malaviya et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/628e570a6e992d68f141a2533cb9b3f6d994c7ad), which uses similarity-induced edges in a knowledge-graph completion setting and reports qualitatively that graph structure complements transfer learning and helps under subgraph sampling, but provides no numeric values, baselines, or direct evidence for synthetic text coverage.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension with the proposed method is also from the 2024 FastGAS work, [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), whose partition ablation found that too large a K cut many edges and caused high-degree representative nodes to be missed, and whose k ablation showed extreme k values hurt selection; this newer refinement contradicts a naive fixed maximum-nearest-neighbor constraint or fixed coverage threshold in Adaptive Coverage Sampling because high-degree nodes and dense similarity regions may be underrepresented if the graph is pruned too aggressively. A second important contradiction is from [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002bf0720404e5dc6bf43eff64f116ec755b405f), which selected low-similarity pairs at or below 0.074, the tenth percentile, as a challenging gold subset, suggesting that low-similarity or hard examples can be valuable, whereas the proposed minimum similarity threshold of 0.707 and coverage-based pruning may discard nuanced, mixed, or out-of-distribution synthetic examples that are not close to selected representatives. [(Malaviya et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/628e570a6e992d68f141a2533cb9b3f6d994c7ad) provides a weaker contradiction by reporting qualitatively that transfer learning was generally more effective than learning from graph structure, with graph structure only complementary, which cautions against treating the cosine-similarity graph as the primary training signal rather than an auxiliary structure. [(Lacombe et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3cfb7cc9502505ae6c4db1e586d128f6a3df50c2) also tempers the proposal because its cosine-similarity retrieval improved AUROC by only +1.54% over uniform-random sampling and +1.59% over relevance-based pruning, while a naive molecule-name relevance control improved only +0.06% and 256-character pruning decreased performance by -0.05%, indicating that similarity-based selection is not automatically superior and that relevance must be semantically meaningful rather than merely surface or length-based. [(Cui et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d4dcee4628745f79f5e352859172817fece3e83) offers a methodological contradiction to a static binary-search threshold because it updates similarity thresholds every 10 epochs and uses rank-based positive/negative sampling with balanced negatives, implying that threshold selection may need to be adaptive and task-dependent rather than fixed by a single monotonic coverage target. The weakest contradiction is from [(Zhang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/271ff9dbeacd05276f1f192453793e772ad91b69), which uses an influence-driven submodular graph objective and similarity-based prompt retrieval rather than a cosine-weighted max-coverage graph, and because it reports only qualitative outperformance without numeric tables or significance tests, it does not directly validate the proposed coverage formulation.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed similarity graph construction should be made explicit and validated before relying on binary search: specify the pre-trained embedding model, embedding dimension, cosine normalization, graph size, and computational cost, and use a k-nearest-neighbor graph with k=10 as a strong default from [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), while ablating k, partition K, maximum outdegree, and threshold to avoid missing high-degree representative nodes. Second, replace the fixed 0.707 minimum similarity threshold with a task-calibrated threshold search and include a low-similarity hard-case stratum, motivated by [(Wang et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/002bf0720404e5dc6bf43eff64f116ec755b405f) and [(Cui et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d4dcee4628745f79f5e352859172817fece3e83), by testing percentile-based thresholds, adaptive threshold updates, and manual spot-checks for trivial overlap or label leakage. Third, add direct baselines and ablations that isolate the contribution of the cosine graph: compare ACS against random sampling, k-means, full synthetic data, human-labeled data, FastGAS-style partitioned greedy selection, Cui-style rank-based positive/negative selection, Lacombe-style relevance retrieval, and Zhang-style influence/submodular selection, and test similarity-based retrieval versus random retrieval as in [(Zhang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/271ff9dbeacd05276f1f192453793e772ad91b69). Fourth, report reproducibility details and statistical evidence: hardware, embedding model, graph construction runtime, BERT-base hyperparameters, 25 repeated runs, confidence intervals, significance tests, and coverage-performance curves for SST2 6,000 and FewRel 12,800, with MNIST used only to validate monotonicity rather than as a primary NLP result. Fifth, incorporate submodular or influence-based marginal gains and partitioned greedy selection to improve diversity and avoid over-selecting dense clusters, while retaining coverage as one interpretable objective. Finally, because [(Malaviya et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/628e570a6e992d68f141a2533cb9b3f6d994c7ad) suggests graph structure is complementary rather than dominant, the plan should test whether the similarity graph improves over embedding-only or label-balanced sampling and should not assume that graph coverage alone explains downstream BERT performance.

## To improve sampling efficiency and diversity, we will impose two constraints to enhance efficiency and diversification: (1) a maximum nearest neighbors constraint to limit node outdegree and promote diversity, and (2) a minimum similarity threshold constraint (0.707 cosine similarity) to ensure meaningful representativeness.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed maximum nearest neighbors constraint comes from [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807), which constructs a similarity graph over 3,000 unlabeled instances per task by connecting each node to its 10 nearest neighbors, partitions the graph into K communities, and uses greedy node selection to choose annotation budgets of 18 or 100. This is the closest analogue to the ACS similarity graph and coverage-based representative sampling in the research plan, and it outperformed Vote-k and IDEAL in 13 of 14 task-budget scenarios and four graph baselines in 10 of 14 scenarios, indicating that a bounded outdegree graph can select small, representative subsets that improve downstream performance. This 2024 work can be read as an iterative extension of the older bounded-neighbor idea in [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611), which used a fixed N=10 cosine neighborhood for ambiguous sample labeling, by moving from local score-vector averaging to an explicit graph and greedy selection. The same paper's k ablation, where k=10 was used in main experiments and described as favorable for MNLI while too-small or too-large k can reduce the ability to distinguish representative nodes, supports making the maximum nearest neighbors constraint explicit but also suggests it should be tuned rather than assumed universal. A weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611), which used a fixed N=10 nearest-neighbor limit with pretrained DNN embeddings and cosine distance to average score vectors for high-ambiguity speech-to-text samples; adding 19,567 selected samples to an approximately 3,300-sample SVM training set improved accuracy on speech-to-text and manual transcript test sets, and selected labels matched manual labels at 67.8 percent versus 37.9 percent baseline on 615 high-ambiguity development samples. Its Dataset B results, showing relative error improvements over random high-ambiguity selection across labeled baseline sizes from 0.5 to 25 percent with 10-fold cross-validation, further support the less-is-more premise that a small selected subset can be useful for classifier training, although the setting is intent classification from speech transcripts rather than BERT fine-tuning on GPT-3.5-generated SST2 or FewRel synthetic data.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed fixed minimum similarity threshold of 0.707 is that both related works use top-10 nearest-neighbor construction without any minimum cosine threshold, yet still obtain effective selection. [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807) connects each node to its 10 nearest neighbors regardless of similarity magnitude, and its greedy selection outperformed active-learning and graph baselines across seven datasets and budgets of 18 and 100, suggesting that a hard 0.707 pruning rule may be unnecessary and could remove useful edges in sparse or low-similarity regions of the synthetic SST2 or FewRel embedding space. [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611) similarly ranks all available texts by cosine semantic distance and selects the first 10 neighbors without a 0.707 cutoff, using instead a median ambiguity threshold to define high-ambiguity samples; its improvements over random high-ambiguity selection, especially at small labeled baseline sizes from 0.5 to 25 percent in Dataset B, imply that uncertainty or ambiguity may be a more informative selection signal than a fixed similarity threshold. A further contradiction is that neither paper validates the binary-search monotonicity assumption central to ACS: [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807) tunes community count K rather than a similarity threshold, and [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611) uses a fixed neighborhood size and ambiguity threshold, so there is no direct evidence that coverage increases monotonically as a 0.707-style threshold is decreased in the proposed synthetic-data setting. The evidence is also limited by missing statistical significance tests, exact table values, hardware specifications, and embedding model details, and by task mismatch: the related work addresses intent classification from speech transcripts and in-context learning selective annotation, not BERT-base fine-tuning on GPT-3.5-generated SST2 or FewRel synthetic data.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace the fixed 0.707 minimum cosine threshold with a tunable threshold and include a top-k-only baseline, because both [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807) and [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611) use k=10 neighborhoods without a minimum similarity cutoff and still achieve effective selection. In the ACS experiments on 6,000 SST2-like samples and 12,800 FewRel samples, the binary search should sweep thresholds over a data-dependent range, report coverage curves, and compare threshold-pruned max coverage against unpruned k=10 coverage, random sampling, k-Means, full synthetic data, and human-labeled data. The second priority is to tune the maximum nearest neighbors constraint rather than treating it as fixed, using ablations over k such as 5, 10, 20, and 50, and considering class-aware or stratified neighborhoods for FewRel's 64 relation types with 200 samples per relation to avoid under-representing rare or nuanced relations. Third, incorporate an uncertainty, ambiguity, or synthetic-quality signal in addition to coverage, because [(Kons et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7d26ad8a2f814f90f79fec1cddd6551ebe0db611) showed that ambiguity-based selection improved labeling and classifier accuracy, suggesting that coverage alone may retain redundant or noisy GPT-3.5 examples. Fourth, strengthen statistical and reproducibility reporting by clarifying the 25 repetitions and N=5 seeds, reporting mean and standard deviation for accuracy, F1, precision, and recall, adding paired significance tests against baselines, and specifying embedding models, graph construction runtime, and hardware, since the related work lacks these details. Finally, validate the monotonicity assumption of the binary search on MNIST and on the synthetic tasks by explicitly measuring coverage as a function of threshold and outdegree, and report computational cost because larger k and dense similarity graphs can increase runtime, as noted in [(Chen et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e34d29cb05d1dca97525ef050f173bad0eddd807).

## To create balanced synthetic training data, we will utilize GPT-3.5 to generate synthetic training corpora for multiple downstream tasks, adopting prompt designs from prior work. We will ensure balanced generation with equal numbers of data points for each label in the classification task's label space.

### Support

The strongest support for using GPT-3.5 to generate equal per-label synthetic corpora is direct: [(Clavié, and Souli'e, 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c4f9f0cc8c138047a61bdb11b1a352e3d1aed035) prompted GPT-3.5 to produce 40 job-posting-style sentences for each of 13,890 ESCO skill labels, with programmatic checks showing more than 97% of skills received all 40 examples, and the resulting classifier and similarity baseline was competitive before reranking, while GPT-4 reranking reached RP@10 of 61.02 on House and 68.94 on Tech, improving over the previous best by 22.33 and 29.75 points. This validates the proposed equal-count, label-wise GPT-3.5 generation for a large label space, although it did not test downsampling. The next strongest support is [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590), which generated class-conditioned texts with a frozen PLM, overgenerated 10N candidates per class, selected top-N by the geometric mean conditional probability, and reported that the full pipeline outperformed zero-shot prompting and achieved better results than few-shot methods with smaller variance; it also showed that 100 samples per class produced high variance while 10,000 per class caused a slight drop, supporting balanced generation plus selection. [(Gao et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f7e03e4ccd26c762090e25dc5d2eb1e1f8c641d) directly supports the plan's less-is-more premise by sampling labels uniformly, generating balanced noisy synthetic text with GPT-2 XL under nucleus p=0.9, and showing that a 2% subset of about 20k IMDb examples outperformed training on the full 1,000k synthetic set, while removing noisy examples through bilevel weighting was critical. [(Hosseini et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44367758d419e623b10f2f5f45c6500f615aedc2) supports near-balanced synthetic classification data at scale: 684,929 NLI triples with 35.4% entailment, 31.1% contradiction, and 33.5% neutral, stratified by domain and length, improved T5-small average ROC AUC by 8.58 points over MNLI and 6.85 over the best single-dataset baseline, and 392K examples performed similarly to the full 671K corpus. [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e8d3c2dc0fc53949794fc00600e25558c4a2441) supports filtered LLM-generated subsets: WANLI, 4 times smaller than MultiNLI and primarily machine-written, improved Diagnostics by 4 points, HANS by 11 points, and Adversarial NLI by 9 points, and outperformed models trained on 9 times more data. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), which showed GPT-3.5-Turbo few-shot synthetic data improved zero-shot synthetic data by 10.6 Macro-F1 for BERT and 10.3 for RoBERTa across 10 classification tasks and even beat real data on tweet irony by 9.3 Macro-F1; [(Kumar et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f06ffb43f588575aa910d9e7cc38958e0410d496), which used label-conditioned GPT2, BART, and BERT generation for SST-2, SNIPS, and TREC with 10 or 50 examples per class and 15 repeats on a single V100, reporting intrinsic BERT development accuracy of 91.91%, 99%, and 94.13%; [(Lee et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e7289607fd5047ba68ab1ae3aca6931226e149de), where GPT-3 synthetic persona data improved Blender 90M F1 from 18.7 to 19.4 and C-score from 0.54 to 0.63; and [(Maas et al., 2011-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c61f9ef06fe74505775a833ff849185757199e7), which establishes that balanced positive and negative IMDB reviews with disjoint movie-level splits are a sound sentiment evaluation setting. Additional weaker support comes from [(Schick, and Schütze, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b769b629c8de35b16735214251d6b4e99cb55762) for counterlabel debiasing, [(Tarunesh et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e92653643e444d165523bb1f48763bf8d0f56772) for template-controlled labels with Fleiss kappa 0.81, [(Yang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e0157f005c4ef945c796725c8ee5c648c0b5b11) for validity filtering and diversity, [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c) for counterfactual diversity, [(Magron et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e676f0cfb7ef47d65457deadf3a351dbf7114a86) for controlled multi-skill labels, [(Mohapatra et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f3596364943bb03f14cd75d9595a2c465831edb) for relevance selection in low-resource settings, [(Chakravarti et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c5d73bb2ee2681f9bfd1199dca3c51fe89d87a0) for evidence that large-scale synthetic augmentation was not necessary, [(Alberti et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eb11d7e23cebe93ea58ddf25c8229e9f3a5a909) for roundtrip filtering, [(Maharana, and Bansal, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eddb4bffb5dda2a53a2a05da5914800c3dfa0db) for confidence-based filtering, and [(Brown et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90abbc2cf38462b954ae1b772fac9532e2ccd8b0) and [(Ye et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2145fcceeb69385e108bf1796d52f974854d4c0b) for qualitative evidence that LLMs can generate synthetic text.

### Contradictions

The most direct contradiction is [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a), which used GPT-3.5-Turbo to generate synthetic corpora for 10 text classification tasks and found that real data outperformed zero-shot synthetic data by an average of 16.9 Macro-F1 and 14.9 accuracy, and outperformed few-shot synthetic data by 6.7 Macro-F1 and 6.1 accuracy; on the six highest-subjectivity tasks, zero-shot synthetic-trained BERT dropped 27.4 Macro-F1 and 24.2 accuracy, and real data were more diverse than few-shot synthetic data, which were more diverse than zero-shot synthetic data, with Chamfer Distance Score drops significantly larger for high-subjectivity tasks at p less than 0.01. This directly challenges the assumption that balanced GPT-3.5 generation alone, especially for SST2-like subjective sentiment, will produce training data comparable to human labels. The next strongest contradiction is [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b), which showed that naive equal mixing of tasks was much worse than baseline: GLUE 76.13 versus 83.28, SQuAD 76.51 versus 80.88, and SGLUE 63.37 versus 71.36, while examples-proportional mixing with K=2^19 and temperature-scaled mixing with T=2 recovered much of the gap. Although this is task mixing rather than label mixing, it warns that equal counts per label may underperform if labels differ in informativeness, difficulty, or redundancy, and supports the plan's need for adaptive coverage rather than fixed quotas. [(Manousakas, and Aydöre, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8237e8643eab115018fb0c9eef548ec66595eaee) contradicts the idea that synthetic data can replace human data: training on original data outperformed training on synthetic data for all tested generative models, and the gap narrowed only as the generator was trained on 10%, 50%, or 100% of the original data; for high-dimensional long-context datasets, the LLM-based GReaT generator required more fine-tuning examples before matching deep generative models. [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c) provides a strong quality contradiction: manual annotation of 100 generated counterfactual examples found 41% label noise for IMDb and 40% for MNLI, even though downstream DeBERTa-base improved by up to 6.2 points on IMDb and up to 4 points on NLI, indicating that LLM-generated labels can be noisy and that benefits may depend on diversity rather than label correctness. [(Gao et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f7e03e4ccd26c762090e25dc5d2eb1e1f8c641d) also contradicts using the full balanced synthetic corpus without selection: without weighted training to remove noisy synthetic data, IMDb accuracy fell from 86.56 to 78.29, showing that balanced generation can still contain harmful examples. [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590) shows that generating too many balanced examples can hurt: 10,000 samples per class caused a slight performance drop attributed to lower-ranking noisier examples, while 100 samples per class produced high variance. [(Hosseini et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44367758d419e623b10f2f5f45c6500f615aedc2) cautions against aggressive downsampling in some settings: about 300K synthetic examples were usually needed for decent performance, and 392K performed similarly to the full 671K corpus, implying that coverage below 1.0 must be tested rather than assumed superior. Weaker contradictions include [(Mohapatra et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f3596364943bb03f14cd75d9595a2c465831edb), where simulated-data augmentation improved low- and medium-resource MultiWOZ settings but did not improve models trained on 100% human data; [(Samuel et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e682f314fadec3170222d37a9f1569064d695db), where GPT-4 synthetic augmentation helped CovidQA and PolicyQA but did not clearly improve TechQA; [(Maharana, and Bansal, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2eddb4bffb5dda2a53a2a05da5914800c3dfa0db), where QAP filtering was not statistically significant on SocialIQA with p=0.069 and CQA with p=0.093 and OptionGAN distractors were not statistically significant; and [(Alberti et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eb11d7e23cebe93ea58ddf25c8229e9f3a5a909), where more synthetic data and 50M-example full pretraining improved SQuAD2, with the best ensemble reaching 86.0/88.6 dev EM/F1, suggesting that less-is-more is not universal.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quality-control and filtering stage before treating the GPT-3.5 output as balanced training data. The plan should generate more than the final N examples per label, then apply label-consistency checks, counterlabel debiasing as in [(Schick, and Schütze, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b769b629c8de35b16735214251d6b4e99cb55762), roundtrip or consistency filtering as in [(Alberti et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8eb11d7e23cebe93ea58ddf25c8229e9f3a5a909) and [(Samuel et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e682f314fadec3170222d37a9f1569064d695db), a trained validity classifier as in [(Yang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e0157f005c4ef945c796725c8ee5c648c0b5b11), and human spot checks as in [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c) and [(Liu et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e8d3c2dc0fc53949794fc00600e25558c4a2441). For SST2-like sentiment and FewRel relation extraction, this should include manual annotation of at least 100 generated examples per task to estimate label noise, because [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c) found 40-41% noise and [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a) showed GPT-3.5 synthetic data can be substantially worse than real data. The next most important action is to use few-shot real examples and label-conditioned prompts rather than bare balanced prompts. [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a) found few-shot GPT-3.5-Turbo improved zero-shot synthetic data by 10.6 Macro-F1 for BERT and 10.3 for RoBERTa, and [(Kumar et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f06ffb43f588575aa910d9e7cc38958e0410d496) and [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590) show that conditioning generation on labels improves label retention. The plan should therefore include 3-5 real examples per label, style and length constraints, diversity prompts every n generations, and relation-specific templates for FewRel, while keeping equal final counts as one baseline. Third, the plan should test adaptive label weighting or coverage-aware quotas instead of assuming equal counts are optimal. [(Raffel et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c4b76232bb72897685d19b3d264c6ee3005bc2b) showed equal mixing underperformed size-aware and temperature-scaled mixing, and [(Gao et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f7e03e4ccd26c762090e25dc5d2eb1e1f8c641d) and [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590) show that sample weights or confidence-ranked top-N selection can remove noisy balanced examples. The proposed ACS should therefore compare equal-count sampling against temperature-scaled label quotas, confidence-weighted quotas, and coverage quotas, while preserving the BERT-base training protocol of 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, dropout 0.1, and 25 repeats. Fourth, the plan should explicitly evaluate the less-is-more hypothesis with size and coverage ablations. [(Gao et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f7e03e4ccd26c762090e25dc5d2eb1e1f8c641d) showed a 2% IMDb subset outperformed the full 1M synthetic set, [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590) showed 100 per class was too noisy and 10,000 per class slightly hurt, and [(Hosseini et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44367758d419e623b10f2f5f45c6500f615aedc2) showed 392K synthetic examples performed similarly to 671K, so the plan should report performance curves over target coverage from 0.0 to 1.0, minimum similarity threshold 0.707, and maximum nearest-neighbor constraints, and identify the smallest subset that matches or exceeds full synthetic training. Fifth, the plan should measure diversity and representativeness directly. [(Li et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/29f07e73b7aaa7e9e950c59710472c62316be74a) used Remote Clique Score and Chamfer Distance Score, [(Yang et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e0157f005c4ef945c796725c8ee5c648c0b5b11) used Self-BLEU and dependency paths, and [(Kumar et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f06ffb43f588575aa910d9e7cc38958e0410d496) used type-token ratio; the plan should add Self-BLEU, embedding coverage, label-wise cluster coverage, and nearest-neighbor redundancy metrics to verify that ACS selects diverse examples rather than merely dense clusters. Sixth, the evaluation should prevent leakage and report reproducibility details. [(Maas et al., 2011-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c61f9ef06fe74505775a833ff849185757199e7) showed that same-movie overlap can inflate sentiment classification, so the SST2-like synthetic experiments should use disjoint movie or entity splits for training and test, and FewRel should use relation-level or entity-level splits. The plan should also report GPT-3.5 API version, temperature, top-p, prompt templates, number of generated candidates, filtering thresholds, hardware, wall-clock time, and paired statistical significance tests across the 25 repeats, because many related works omit these details. Finally, the plan can improve synthetic diversity by borrowing retrieval-conditioned or counterfactual generation from [(Dixit et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4989c08930e42d322b3bfed167d7ea434a698f2c), simulator-based selection from [(Mohapatra et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f3596364943bb03f14cd75d9595a2c465831edb), template-controlled generation from [(Tarunesh et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e92653643e444d165523bb1f48763bf8d0f56772), and popularity-adjusted label combinations from [(Magron et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e676f0cfb7ef47d65457deadf3a351dbf7114a86), especially for FewRel where 200 samples per relation may still be redundant.

## To evaluate downstream performance, we will conduct experiments on two main tasks: 1. Sentiment Analysis: Using synthetic data mimicking the Stanford Sentiment Treebank v2 (SST2) dataset with 6,000 balanced positive/negative movie reviews 2. Relation Extraction: Using synthetic data for the FewRel dataset with 12,800 samples across 64 relation types (200 samples per relation).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed downstream evaluation comes from [(Mozafari et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/743d39f81ae653c0d5ebf6e8ef3d0a4d17dfe75e), which fine-tuned BERT-base on imbalanced hate-speech tweets using a [CLS] linear head or CNN head, with 3 epochs, dropout 0.1, Adam learning rate 2e-5, batch size 32, max sequence length 64, and stratified 80/10/10 splits, and reported weighted-average F1 of 81% and 88% on Waseem and 91% and 92% on Davidson; the Waseem training set had 15,817 examples with 1,693 racism, 3,337 sexism, and 10,787 neither, while Davidson had 19,832 training examples with 1,146 hate, 15,354 offensive, and 3,333 neither. This directly supports the plan's choice of BERT-base, 3 epochs, learning rate 2e-5, dropout 0.1, and F1-based evaluation for sentiment-like and imbalanced text classification, although the plan uses batch size 16 and synthetic SST2/FewRel data rather than real tweets. A second strong support is [(Arslan, and Cruz, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0426d8d3c993bbe09adaa0b6662fdca01d79f4), where fine-tuned BERT achieved accuracy 0.895, F1 0.978, precision 0.948, and recall 0.988 on an imbalanced French multi-label business corpus with 28,941 texts, 80 labels, and at least 50 to 100 examples per category, outperforming binary relevance, classifier chains, and label powerset; this supports BERT fine-tuning as a competitive downstream model for imbalanced multi-label text classification, which is relevant to the FewRel setting with 64 relation types and 200 samples per relation, though it does not test synthetic data or coverage sampling. A third support is [(Tang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bdf7bf9e81a6c12e22323d0402885b2ba62f623e), which used sentence-transformer embeddings and T-SNE to show that ChatGPT-generated synthetic text had a distribution distinct from the original data and argued that this distribution shift could explain performance gaps between synthetic- and original-data fine-tuned models; this supports the plan's embedding-based similarity graph and the motivation for coverage-based downsampling, although the evidence is qualitative and lacks numerical downstream metrics. A fourth support is [(Shaikh et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36b214d41276275ae82ec3949299c8f30de120a7), which trained BERT on automatically labeled IMDB data and achieved 79% accuracy on a 70/10/20 split of 50,000 balanced reviews, supporting the feasibility of BERT sentiment classification on balanced movie-review-like data comparable to the proposed 6,000 synthetic SST2 reviews, but it does not validate LLM-generated synthetic text or adaptive coverage. A fifth support is [(Yoosuf, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32877372d7d517c5110a2cc63b42a0f39cb4aa5c), which showed that BERT fine-tuning with sampling strategy, masked logits, and cyclic learning rates won a token-level propaganda detection competition with test F1 0.2488, precision 0.286, and recall 0.22, and that oversampling to 50% with sequential sampling improved precision; this supports the plan's attention to sampling and BERT fine-tuning, but the task is token-level and imbalanced rather than synthetic balanced SST2/FewRel. The weakest support is [(Chawla et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8cb44f06586f609a29d9b496cc752ec01475dffe), whose SMOTE-style synthetic minority oversampling and majority undersampling on tabular datasets shows that synthetic examples and undersampling can affect classifier performance, but its Adult result that synthetic oversampling did not outperform plain undersampling and that synthetic degree above 50% decreased AUC is more cautionary than supportive for the proposed less-is-more synthetic downsampling.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Chawla et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8cb44f06586f609a29d9b496cc752ec01475dffe), which found that on the Adult dataset, with 48,842 samples and 11,687 minority cases, the nominal-feature and continuous-feature variants of synthetic minority oversampling using five nearest neighbors did not outperform plain undersampling in AUC, and that increasing synthetic oversampling degree above 50% decreased AUC because synthetic minority samples overlapped the majority-class decision space and increased false positives. This contradicts the assumption that adding or retaining large volumes of synthetic examples is automatically beneficial, and it suggests that the proposed ACS must demonstrate that coverage-based pruning improves precision and F1 over simple random or k-Means undersampling, especially for the 12,800-sample FewRel corpus where redundant synthetic relation examples could create overlapping decision regions. A second contradiction is from [(Yoosuf, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32877372d7d517c5110a2cc63b42a0f39cb4aa5c), where oversampling propaganda sentences to about 50% helped learning but caused overfitting and reduced precision, and a multi-head binary classifier improved rarer classes while hurting common classes and lowering overall F1; retraining on a 1M News dataset also did not match the original model, and masking the last four layers produced a development F1 of 0.2. This warns that balanced or oversampled synthetic data can still produce precision-recall tradeoffs, and that the proposed balanced 200-per-relation FewRel design may need class-wise evaluation and possibly weighted or focal losses rather than relying only on balanced generation. A third contradiction is from [(Tang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bdf7bf9e81a6c12e22323d0402885b2ba62f623e), which reported that ChatGPT synthetic embeddings showed distinct distribution patterns from original data and that this shift could explain performance gaps, but provided no numerical accuracy, F1, precision, recall, or significance values; this contradicts the idea that synthetic data can be treated as a drop-in replacement for real data without quantitative distribution and downstream validation. A fourth contradiction is from [(Shaikh et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36b214d41276275ae82ec3949299c8f30de120a7), which used automatically generated Flair labels and reported 79% IMDB accuracy but omitted BERT architecture size, epochs, batch size, learning rate, dropout, seeds, and significance tests; this weakens confidence that automatically labeled or LLM-generated sentiment data will transfer cleanly to the proposed 6,000 synthetic SST2 experiment without explicit label-noise controls. The weakest contradiction is from [(Arslan, and Cruz, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0426d8d3c993bbe09adaa0b6662fdca01d79f4), which reported high BERT F1 on an imbalanced multi-label corpus but did not report architecture size, hardware, epochs, learning rate, batch size, seeds, or significance tests, so its results cannot be used to validate the proposed synthetic-data downsampling or the specific BERT-base hyperparameters.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add quantitative distribution and coverage diagnostics before and after ACS, including embedding-model specification, cosine-similarity threshold sensitivity around the 0.707 minimum, k-nearest-neighbor coverage, label/relation distribution, MMD or CKA between synthetic and human-labeled distributions, and a monotonicity check on the MNIST validation task; this directly addresses the distribution-shift concern in [(Tang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bdf7bf9e81a6c12e22323d0402885b2ba62f623e) and the synthetic-overlap warning in [(Chawla et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8cb44f06586f609a29d9b496cc752ec01475dffe). The second most important action is to strengthen baselines and evaluation for the two downstream tasks: for SST2 report accuracy and macro/weighted F1 with confidence intervals over 25 runs, and for FewRel report macro F1, per-relation F1, precision, and recall, while comparing ACS against random sampling, k-Means sampling, full synthetic training, human-labeled training, and an explicit random-undersampling baseline, because [(Mozafari et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/743d39f81ae653c0d5ebf6e8ef3d0a4d17dfe75e) and [(Arslan, and Cruz, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0426d8d3c993bbe09adaa0b6662fdca01d79f4) show that F1-based evaluation is essential for imbalanced text classification and that BERT can be strong but needs fair baselines. The third action is to tune the BERT training procedure beyond the fixed 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1 by testing batch size 32, learning-rate schedules, masked logits, early stopping, and class-weighted or focal losses, motivated by [(Mozafari et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/743d39f81ae653c0d5ebf6e8ef3d0a4d17dfe75e), which used batch size 32, and [(Yoosuf, and Yang, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/32877372d7d517c5110a2cc63b42a0f39cb4aa5c), which used cyclic learning rates and masked logits and showed precision-recall sensitivity to sampling. The fourth action is to control synthetic label quality by comparing GPT-3.5-generated SST2 and FewRel labels against human-labeled or high-confidence labels, measuring label noise, and optionally filtering low-confidence synthetic examples, because [(Shaikh et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36b214d41276275ae82ec3949299c8f30de120a7) shows automatic labels can support BERT training but may introduce unreported noise. The least important but still necessary action is to report full reproducibility details, including hardware, embedding model, graph construction hyperparameters, random seeds, train/validation/test splits, and statistical significance tests, because several related works, including [(Shaikh et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36b214d41276275ae82ec3949299c8f30de120a7), [(Arslan, and Cruz, 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4d0426d8d3c993bbe09adaa0b6662fdca01d79f4), and [(Tang et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bdf7bf9e81a6c12e22323d0402885b2ba62f623e), omit implementation details that limit comparability.

## To compare sampling strategies, we will compare ACS against three baselines: - Random sampling - k-Means clustering-based sampling - Full synthetic dataset training - Human-labeled data training (as upper bound).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed baseline suite comes from [(Yuan et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e06b7f72270900544284e0898aac2bb564ff58b), which explicitly compares random sampling, BERT-KM k-means sampling over pre-trained and fine-tuned BERT embeddings, and a full-data reference for active sentence classification on IMDB, SST-2, AG News, and PubMed using BERT-base or SciBERT, batch size 32, three fine-tuning epochs, AdamW with learning rate 2e-5, and five random seeds; this directly validates random and k-Means as natural baselines and full-data training as a reference, while also warning that fine-tuned embeddings are not always better than pre-trained embeddings and that high-dimensional distance concentration can affect diversity methods. The second strongest support is [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590), whose synthetic-data pipeline selects top-N generated examples by label-conditioned log probability and shows that increasing selected examples improves stability but using about 10,000 selected examples can cause a slight performance drop due to lower-confidence or noisier generated examples, supporting the plan's less-is-more hypothesis and the need to compare ACS against full synthetic training. Third, [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4) supports coverage and class balance conceptually by constructing WIT with 500,000 concept queries and a 20,000-pair per-query cap, yielding 400 million image-text pairs and zero-shot CLIP results competitive with a fully supervised linear probe on 16 of 27 datasets, including ImageNet by 2.0 points, although it does not ablate the per-query cap. Fourth, [(Kim et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d39c9e5eba4fdfe7637f3f68a8e2a750aae17a7) supports diversity-based subset selection by using model-derived meta-information and DPP to select 1,000 SST-5 or 600 IMP samples from 10,000 candidates, compared with Random and Entropy, aligning with ACS's goal of selecting informative diverse subsets. Fifth, [(Baldridge, and Osborne, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38a98a36998d3e6f91ed731153315c71c364723f) supports the inclusion of random and model-based selection baselines in parse selection, where self-selection was strongest and relatedness measured by average Spearman rank correlation affected reuse, with LL-CONGLOM and LL-CONFIG at 0.84 versus P-MRS and LL-CONFIG at 0.65. Sixth, [(Hu et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/58c04126a5196deb57ae31d6174cd4aae154f138) supports random warm-start as a meaningful baseline, using 5% i.i.d. examples, corresponding to 2,500 examples for CIFAR10 and CIFAR100 and 5,000 for Tiny ImageNet, before active acquisition. Finally, human upper-bound and synthetic-quality evidence is supported by [(Møller et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73051b7b25ef972c15ea8e7a221f4361991facbe), [(Kumar et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/038e39e800ecf8c59e45671376d409e4da9b5edf), and [(Pangakis, and Wolken, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feb83f8cd4fa982306ac02f817408c69e003b0a0), which show that human labels often outperform synthetic or LLM-labeled training, that consistency filtering can make LLM labels approach expert labels, and that BERT trained on 1,000 human labels achieved median F1 0.624 versus 0.586 for 1,000 GPT-4 labels, justifying human-labeled training as an upper bound and cautioning that synthetic data may not reach it.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that model-based selection is not guaranteed to beat random sampling: [(Baldridge, and Osborne, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38a98a36998d3e6f91ed731153315c71c364723f) reports that for LL-CONFIG reuse, random sampling outperformed selection by LL-CONGLOM until nearly 70% accuracy and selection by P-MRS until about 73% accuracy, and reuse increased annotation cost, with LL-PROD needing 2300 discriminants when reusing LL-CONGLOM-selected data, a 10% increase over 2100 for self-selection, while LL-CONGLOM needed 5500 versus 4200 for self-selection, a 31% increase; this warns that ACS may not dominate random sampling unless the coverage selector is well aligned with the BERT learner. The second strongest contradiction is that more synthetic data may simply plateau rather than be improved by downsampling: [(Mekala et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/889045714af6c2e9c187dd5347f1b308447edcc9) finds that CONDA performance increases with generated samples per label and then plateaus, with 450 samples per label as the main setting and BERT-base-uncased trained for 4 epochs on AGNews, IMDb, Yelp, SST-2, NYT, and Yahoo, indicating diminishing returns and that full synthetic training may already be near the useful synthetic-data limit. Third, [(Yuan et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e06b7f72270900544284e0898aac2bb564ff58b) reports that after the first ten active-learning iterations test accuracy across methods converged, and clustering fine-tuned BERT embeddings was not always better than clustering pre-trained BERT embeddings, suggesting that k-Means and coverage-based methods may yield small or unstable differences and that embedding choice can dominate sampling gains. Fourth, [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4) does not provide an ablation isolating the 20,000-per-query coverage cap, so its strong WIT results do not prove that coverage-based downsampling improves over full large-scale data. Fifth, human-labeled data often outperforms synthetic data: [(Møller et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73051b7b25ef972c15ea8e7a221f4361991facbe) reports that human-annotated training generally outperformed synthetic-augmented and zero-shot models on balanced and sensitive tasks, and [(Pangakis, and Wolken, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feb83f8cd4fa982306ac02f817408c69e003b0a0) reports that BERT with 1,000 human labels had median F1 0.624 versus 0.586 for 1,000 GPT-4 labels, with GPT-trained models showing higher recall but lower precision; this does not directly contradict ACS versus full synthetic training, but it contradicts any expectation that sampled synthetic data will approach the human upper bound without additional quality controls. Sixth, [(Kumar et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/038e39e800ecf8c59e45671376d409e4da9b5edf) shows that filtered LLM labels can approach expert labels, but only with JSON/text agreement filtering on 408 clinical trial protocols, not coverage sampling, so it does not support ACS specifically and highlights that label-consistency filtering may be a confound if omitted.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the experiment should implement random, k-Means, full synthetic, and human-labeled baselines under an identical BERT-base protocol, using the stated 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1, while clarifying the 25-run and N=5 random seed design and reporting paired statistical tests or bootstrap confidence intervals, because [(Yuan et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e06b7f72270900544284e0898aac2bb564ff58b) shows that embedding choice and convergence matter and [(Kumar et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/038e39e800ecf8c59e45671376d409e4da9b5edf) uses 10,000 bootstrap resamples for uncertainty estimation. Second, add a quality-filtering or confidence/consistency baseline, such as top-N generator confidence or LLM label-agreement filtering, because [(Meng et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/23c265ba884b92ecbd9d18641078d964697e4590) and [(Kumar et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/038e39e800ecf8c59e45671376d409e4da9b5edf) show that noise and label consistency strongly affect synthetic-data performance; this will isolate whether ACS's coverage gain is due to diversity rather than implicit quality filtering. Third, for k-Means, test both pre-trained and fine-tuned embeddings and report computational cost, because [(Yuan et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e06b7f72270900544284e0898aac2bb564ff58b) warns that fine-tuned embeddings are not always better and high-dimensional distance concentration can affect diversity methods; for ACS, ablate the 0.707 minimum similarity threshold, maximum nearest-neighbor constraint, and coverage targets from 0.0 to 1.0, and validate monotonicity on MNIST and text tasks. Fourth, enforce per-label or per-relation coverage and class balance, because [(Radford et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6f870f7f02a8c59c3e23f407f3ef00dd1dcf8fc4) uses per-query caps to prevent frequent concepts from dominating and [(Møller et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73051b7b25ef972c15ea8e7a221f4361991facbe) shows that balanced synthetic augmentation helps unbalanced multiclass tasks; for FewRel's 64 relation types, report per-relation precision, recall, and F1 to avoid global coverage masking rare-relation underrepresentation. Fifth, include a DPP or meta-information diversity baseline if feasible, because [(Kim et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d39c9e5eba4fdfe7637f3f68a8e2a750aae17a7) shows DPP with model-derived signals is a credible diversity-selection alternative, and report selection cost and training cost separately, following [(Baldridge, and Osborne, 2004-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38a98a36998d3e6f91ed731153315c71c364723f), which shows that annotation or selection cost can change the ranking of sampling strategies. Finally, inspect synthetic samples for label leakage and lexical or semantic diversity, as in [(Mekala et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/889045714af6c2e9c187dd5347f1b308447edcc9) and [(Møller et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73051b7b25ef972c15ea8e7a221f4361991facbe), and use human-labeled test sets with the same evaluation metrics to ensure the human upper bound is a fair BERT fine-tuning comparison, as in [(Pangakis, and Wolken, 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/feb83f8cd4fa982306ac02f817408c69e003b0a0).

## To train classifiers, we will fine-tune BERT-base models on selected subsets using consistent hyperparameters: 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2×10^-5, dropout rate 0.1. Each experiment will be repeated 25 times (N=5 random seeds) to ensure robust evaluation.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Thakur et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/496658ac0483942a8720407f16bb94227f5627fe), which fine-tunes BERT-base uncased on selected gold plus silver sentence-pair subsets and shows that sampling strategy is decisive: random sampling usually decreased performance relative to no augmentation, while BM25 and KDE produced the best results, with 1 to 6 point in-domain gains over the un-augmented bi-encoder and outperformance of synonym replacement. This directly supports the plan's central comparison of coverage-based selected subsets against random sampling and full synthetic data, although it uses real unlabeled retrieval rather than GPT-3.5 synthetic examples. Next, [(Tang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bad3c6551f8ffd883b1f8b1847852c01bcb6470) supports the less-is-more hypothesis by showing BERT-base-uncased fine-tuning on selected prompt tuples can improve masked language modeling perplexity relative to baseline adaptation, with optima at k=500 to 10000 depending on corpus and criterion, and degradation when too many noisy tuples are included; this motivates sweeping coverage from 0.0 to 1.0 and selecting a sub-1.0 coverage on a development set. [(Sellam et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b540745f4b51f95bf90fb3420e51edb037fc51a) supports repeated random-seed runs and statistical reliability, using five fine-tuning seeds per checkpoint and 1,000 bootstrap samples, and showing that ignoring variation over seeds or test examples can make results appear more significant. [(Merchant et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2fd96a52ded7a64f60c1e54f5bb488c787629c0) supports BERT-base fine-tuning and repeated-run evaluation, reporting MNLI accuracy of 83.3 ± 0.1 across three trials and showing fine-tuned representations diverge more in-domain. [(Mendsaikhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10b0ca5025efdfc4e584bf1230a62545cb9cc94b) supports reduced, balanced BERT fine-tuning by using a 542,186-document balanced security/non-security subset and three epochs, noting fewer epochs performed better, which aligns with the plan's 3-epoch protocol and balanced synthetic generation, though it lacks BERT metrics and hyperparameters. [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) supports robust fine-tuning and evaluation protocols, including median over five runs for high-variance GLUE tasks, development-set early stopping, and ensembles from 6 to 17 checkpoints, which is relevant to the plan's repeated BERT-base runs. [(Hofmann et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33661b3345cb29e37c85cc702d2a2a3b428e1445) supports BERT-base sentiment classification with statistically significant F1 gains on Ciao and YELP, p < 0.01 by McNemar's test, though it does not test synthetic coverage. [(Williams et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ded2b8c64491b4a67f6d39ce473d4b9347a672e) supports subset-based training and transfer, with genre-specific models achieving cross-genre accuracy such as telephone to face-to-face 63%, but its subsets are genre partitions rather than coverage samples. Finally, [(Socher et al., 2013-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/687bac2d3320083eb4530bf18bb8f8f721477600) provides SST sentiment context and hyperparameter tuning, but is not direct evidence for BERT or synthetic coverage.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that none of the cited works directly validates adaptive coverage sampling of GPT-3.5 synthetic classifier data; the closest subset-selection evidence uses real unlabeled sentence pairs [(Thakur et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/496658ac0483942a8720407f16bb94227f5627fe) or prompt tuples [(Tang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bad3c6551f8ffd883b1f8b1847852c01bcb6470), so the plan's claim that coverage below 1.0 will outperform full synthetic data remains unproven. A second concern is that fixed hyperparameters may be suboptimal: [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) shows dropout removal improved ALBERT-xxlarge downstream average from 90.4 to 90.7, with SST-2 from 96.3 to 96.5, suggesting that a fixed dropout 0.1 for BERT-base may need ablation, especially with only 3 epochs. Third, the 25-repeat protocol is ambiguous and may not be the most informative statistical design; [(Sellam et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b540745f4b51f95bf90fb3420e51edb037fc51a) shows that ignoring variation over seeds or test examples can make results appear more significant, and recommends joint bootstrap over fine-tuning seeds and finite test examples, whereas the plan only says 25 times with N=5 random seeds without specifying whether 25 is 5 seeds times 5 folds, 25 independent seeds, or 25 model runs. Fourth, subset size optima are dataset-dependent and not guaranteed to be below 1.0; [(Tang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bad3c6551f8ffd883b1f8b1847852c01bcb6470) reports optima from 500 to 10000 tuples and degradation with more noisy tuples, but no significance tests, so the plan should not assume a universal coverage threshold. Fifth, older or less aligned work provides limited support: [(Socher et al., 2013-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/687bac2d3320083eb4530bf18bb8f8f721477600) uses recursive networks on real SST, not BERT or synthetic data; [(Mendsaikhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10b0ca5025efdfc4e584bf1230a62545cb9cc94b) does not report BERT numeric results or hyperparameters; [(Williams et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ded2b8c64491b4a67f6d39ce473d4b9347a672e) does not report statistical significance and its genre subsets are not coverage-based; [(Merchant et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b2fd96a52ded7a64f60c1e54f5bb488c787629c0) and [(Hofmann et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/33661b3345cb29e37c85cc702d2a2a3b428e1445) do not address synthetic data or coverage sampling.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a direct head-to-head ablation on the same GPT-3.5 synthetic corpora comparing ACS, random, k-means, full synthetic, and human-labeled upper bound, with development-set selection of coverage and statistical tests such as paired bootstrap over seeds and test examples or McNemar's test, reporting accuracy/F1 and precision/recall/F1 with confidence intervals. This directly addresses the plan's central hypothesis and follows [(Thakur et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/496658ac0483942a8720407f16bb94227f5627fe) and [(Sellam et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b540745f4b51f95bf90fb3420e51edb037fc51a). Second, tune and report hyperparameters rather than assuming 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1 are optimal; include early stopping on a held-out development split, a small grid over epochs, learning rate, batch size, and dropout, and consider dropout ablation inspired by [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) and reduced-epoch evidence from [(Mendsaikhan et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/10b0ca5025efdfc4e584bf1230a62545cb9cc94b). Third, clarify and strengthen the 25-repeat protocol by specifying 5 random seeds and 5 independent training runs per seed or 25 independent seeds, and use paired comparisons across seeds and test examples as in [(Sellam et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b540745f4b51f95bf90fb3420e51edb037fc51a) to avoid inflated significance. Fourth, validate the monotonicity assumption and coverage curve by reporting coverage versus performance for 0.0 to 1.0, including the MNIST validation experiment, and explicitly test whether the binary search threshold produces stable coverage under embedding noise. Fifth, improve reproducibility by reporting embedding model, similarity graph construction, max nearest-neighbor constraint, minimum similarity threshold 0.707, dataset sizes, hardware, optimizer, scheduler, and random-seed handling, following the level of detail in [(Lan et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a064df1aeada7e69e5173f7d4c8606f4470365b) and [(Tang et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7bad3c6551f8ffd883b1f8b1847852c01bcb6470). Sixth, include task-specific baselines and analyses, such as genre or relation-type stratified random sampling, because [(Williams et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5ded2b8c64491b4a67f6d39ce473d4b9347a672e) and [(Thakur et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/496658ac0483942a8720407f16bb94227f5627fe) show that subset structure and retrieval strategy matter, and use SST2-like and FewRel evaluation with label-balanced synthetic generation as planned.

## To identify optimal coverage levels, we will systematically vary target coverage parameters from 0.0 to 1.0 to identify optimal coverage levels, hypothesizing that coverage below 1.0 will yield superior performance by excluding noisy or less informative samples.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed coverage sweep comes from [(Geifman et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dfb58733b367005945a2f2c2028145e4678efd7a), which quantitatively shows that retaining a fraction of points can reduce selective risk: in an illustrative risk-coverage example, full coverage risk was about 0.29, risk at coverage 0.5 was about 0.06, and the optimal hindsight curve reached zero at coverage 0.71; across 42 averaged-early-stopping experiments on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet, k=30 always reduced excess AURC, NN-distance excess AURC on CIFAR-10 fell from 35.1 to 4.58, CIFAR-100 NN-distance improved by 22%, pointwise early stopping reduced softmax excess AURC by up to 18% on CIFAR-100, and after Platt scaling CIFAR-10 softmax NLL decreased from 0.193 to 0.163 and Brier score from 0.051 to 0.045. This directly supports the ACS hypothesis that a coverage parameter below 1.0 can identify a useful operating point, but it does so for confidence-score selective rejection, not for similarity-graph max-coverage selection of synthetic BERT training examples. A closely related and somewhat weaker support is [(Bar-Shalom et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11462c53277ae86727ed34d2987b9433ab6d51e7), which explicitly excludes coverage 1 by fitting C_target=10 target coverages uniformly over [0.1, 1] with delta=0.01 and kappa=1-Entropy on ImageNet validation, using 50,000 images split into 49,000 detection-training and 1,000 validation samples over 15 splits with ResNet50, MobileNetV3-S, and ViT-T, and reports AUROC above 90% at window size 50 and 86%+ AUROC/AUPR-In/AUPR-Out with ViT-T at window size 10, supporting the idea that lower coverage levels can be more informative than full coverage. The iterative line from [(Corbière et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a05dd1e5e2859ecb845ee75e4ede22805fb22a6) to [(Ao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/886f7a3c0a826fc2ef99cdbbcd8414e331e3aa54) further supports below-1.0 operating regions: ConfidNet, a five-layer dense confidence network, showed its largest advantage at high but incomplete coverage, about 0.80-0.95 on CIFAR-10 and 0.86-0.96 on SVHN, using VGG-16 or small CNNs and AUPR-Error/AUPR-Success/FPR@95TPR/AUROC, while Ao et al. found VGG13_bn and VGG19_bn risk-coverage curves overlapping from coverage 0.74 to 0.9 with an optimal point near 0.84 and label smoothing having the lowest risk from 0.65 to 1 on Tiny-ImageNet, with experiments on ImageNet, CI-FAR100, and Tiny-ImageNet using a Tesla P40 and 15-bin ECE. [(Straitouri et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651f6676a5ad1972ad368a71baca8c3c7363d68b) supports sweeping a threshold to find an interior optimum, reporting that performance increases monotonically up to alpha* and deteriorates beyond it in synthetic logistic-regression/multinomial-logit tasks with 20 features, 10,000 samples, 20% test, and 10 repeats, though the alpha sweep itself is qualitative and alpha is a conformal miscoverage parameter rather than a similarity-graph coverage parameter. For the synthetic-data premise, [(Wang et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e65b346d442e9962a4276dc1c1af2956d9d5f1eb) provides strong indirect support that filtering low-quality LLM-generated instruction data improves downstream performance: GPT3 finetuning on SELF-INST data raised SuperNI zero-shot ROUGE-L from 6.8 to 39.9, outperforming T5-LM at 25.7, T0 at 33.1, and GPT3 finetuned on T0 at 37.9, and reaching 51.6 when combined with SuperNI, using temperature 0, 2 finetuning epochs, and prompt loss weight 0. Finally, [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603) is conceptually closest to ACS because DENSITY uses a coverage/diversity score to select representative training examples, but the excerpt reports fixed sampling fractions of 10, 20, 40, 60, and 80 percent of C4, described as 184B tokens, iso-compute training for 524B tokens, T5-Small/Large, and 111 downstream tasks without numeric DENSITY outcomes or binary search over target coverage, making it weaker support for the specific 0.0-to-1.0 binary-search mechanism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the plan's less-is-more hypothesis is [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f), which studies synthetic instruction data for local LLMs on 8 A100 80GB GPUs and finds that Gemma 7B generally improves as synthetic data volume grows from 1k to 256k samples and saturates around 64k-256k, rather than showing that a deliberately reduced coverage level is superior; on 128k GPT4o-generated summarization data, LLaMA3 8B reached precision 87.02 with 99% coverage at threshold 50 and 94% coverage at threshold 70, still below GPT4o at 93.25/100%/100%, indicating that more synthetic data can be beneficial until a plateau, and no statistical significance tests are reported. This is not a direct test of similarity-graph coverage, but it directly challenges the assumption that ACS should prefer coverage below 1.0 over full synthetic data. A weaker contradiction is that the same synthetic-data literature does not validate the specific coverage mechanism: [(Wang et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e65b346d442e9962a4276dc1c1af2956d9d5f1eb) improves performance through low-quality filtering but does not specify a coverage threshold, and adding SuperNI data further improves ROUGE-L from 39.9 to 51.6, suggesting that full synthetic data plus real data may outperform a reduced synthetic subset. [(Bar-Shalom et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11462c53277ae86727ed34d2987b9433ab6d51e7) also limits the proposed sweep because it uses only 10 fixed coverages in [0.1, 1] excluding 1, not a continuous 0.0-to-1.0 binary search, and the excerpt does not provide a separate quantitative ablation isolating coverage level; its task is distribution-shift detection, not BERT fine-tuning on synthetic SST2 or FewRel data. [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603) provides a potential confound: DENSITY is evaluated at fixed sample sizes and iso-compute 524B-token training, so smaller sampled datasets are repeated for more epochs, which can make data-size effects and compute/epoch effects inseparable, and the excerpt gives no numeric DENSITY performance or statistical significance. Finally, [(Geifman et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dfb58733b367005945a2f2c2028145e4678efd7a) shows that coverage-related confidence methods are not uniformly beneficial: averaged early stopping with k=10 degraded CIFAR-10 softmax excess AURC from 4.78 to 4.81, a 0.7% degradation, and pointwise early stopping can be computationally expensive, with CIFAR-100 n=7000, T=250, and 100 MC-dropout forward passes corresponding to 175,000,000 forward passes; this cautions against assuming that a coverage sweep or threshold search will always improve the proposed ACS pipeline.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the plan should not assume that coverage below 1.0 is superior; it should treat the optimum as task-dependent and include full synthetic data, high-coverage points near 1.0, and random/full baselines, because [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f) shows synthetic-data performance can improve with volume and saturate rather than peak at reduced coverage, and no statistical significance tests are reported. The coverage sweep should therefore report achieved coverage, not only target coverage, and use a feasible range such as 0.1 to 1.0 or 0.05 to 1.0, following [(Bar-Shalom et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11462c53277ae86727ed34d2987b9433ab6d51e7), which excluded coverage 1 and used 10 uniformly spaced target coverages in [0.1, 1]; coverage 0.0 is infeasible for training a BERT-base classifier because it would select no examples, and the proposed minimum similarity threshold of 0.707 plus maximum nearest-neighbor constraint may make some target coverages unreachable, so binary search should be replaced or augmented by a grid search or monotone interpolation if the threshold-coverage curve is not strictly monotonic. Second, the plan should add direct data-quality and diversity baselines to isolate the benefit of max-coverage sampling: include Self-Instruct-style low-quality filtering and deduplication/rule-based filtering/decontamination from [(Wang et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e65b346d442e9962a4276dc1c1af2956d9d5f1eb) and [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f), and compare ACS against DENSITY-style coverage/diversity scoring and ASK-LLM/perplexity filtering from [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603), because these are the closest existing data-curation methods and the current plan only compares random, k-Means, full data, and human-labeled data. Third, evaluation should use coverage-aware metrics in addition to accuracy/F1: risk-coverage curves and excess AURC from [(Geifman et al., 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dfb58733b367005945a2f2c2028145e4678efd7a), AUPR-Error/AUPR-Success/FPR@95TPR from [(Corbière et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5a05dd1e5e2859ecb845ee75e4ede22805fb22a6), E-AUoptRC and Trust Index from [(Ao et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/886f7a3c0a826fc2ef99cdbbcd8414e331e3aa54), and AUROC/AUPR-In/AUPR-Out if any out-of-distribution or failure-detection analysis is added, following [(Bar-Shalom et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11462c53277ae86727ed34d2987b9433ab6d51e7); for the actual 6,000-example SST2 and 12,800-example FewRel experiments, primary metrics should remain human-test accuracy, macro/micro F1, per-class F1 for sentiment, and per-relation precision/recall/F1 for the 64 FewRel relations. Fourth, the MNIST validation experiment should explicitly test the monotonicity assumption of the binary search by plotting similarity threshold versus achieved coverage for the synthetic graph, reporting the number of binary-search iterations, failure cases where target coverage is unreachable, and the effect of the 0.707 minimum similarity and maximum nearest-neighbor constraints, because none of the cited coverage works directly validates binary search over a similarity-graph threshold for training-data selection. Fifth, the plan should control for compute and convergence: fixed 3 epochs with batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1 may penalize smaller subsets, so report training loss, convergence epoch, and optionally an iso-compute or convergence-matched variant, analogous to the iso-compute 524B-token setting in [(Sachdeva et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/31b2918acc77682b5a499da1aa111f34e76d9603) and the 8 A100 80GB GPU setup in [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f). Finally, the 25-repeat evaluation should be clarified and made statistically rigorous: report means and standard deviations or bootstrap confidence intervals over seeds and data splits, use paired tests comparing each coverage level to full data, and apply multiple-comparison correction across the coverage sweep, because several related works report improvements without significance tests or with only qualitative sweep evidence, as in [(Straitouri et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/651f6676a5ad1972ad368a71baca8c3c7363d68b) and [(Park et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1c2fe06e15b334a494c34e2ab80f4ec1c63c82f).

## To assess performance, we will assess performance using accuracy and F1-scores for sentiment analysis, and precision, recall, and F1-scores for relation extraction, all measured on human-labeled test sets.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation method is that document-level relation extraction work evaluates models on human-annotated test sets and reports F1-based relation extraction scores, directly matching the plan's precision/recall/F1 on human-labeled relation extraction tests. [(Xie et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf1e5a17b3adead7016eb52ddc3007673ed382f) uses DocRED, CDR, and GDA with cased-BERT base and RoBERTa large, AdamW learning rates of 5e-5 for the encoder and 1e-4 for other parameters, 6 percent linear warmup, batch size 4 documents, relation/evidence loss ratio 0.1, early stopping on development F1 for up to 30 epochs, one GTX 1080 Ti for BERT base and one RTX A6000 for RoBERTa large, and reports Ign F1 and F1 ablations, showing F1 is an accepted primary metric for relation extraction. This supports the plan's BERT-base FewRel evaluation, although Xie does not report precision/recall separately or sentiment accuracy. A second, weaker support is that [(Zhang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38b75403553508c7623196f10d625c1d162510e5) evaluates synthetic-trained models against a separate human-labeled benchmark of 9,218 examples across 895 tables using exact match accuracy, token-level F1, and rescaled BertScore F1, with 384,333 synthetic columns across 163,474 tables, K=10 query names, N=10 sampled cells, and 20-character truncation, which supports the plan's use of human-labeled test sets and F1-style metrics when training on synthetic data, even though the task is column-name expansion rather than sentiment or relation extraction. The weakest support is [(Ma et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ee952d25d8a192a7786f253eea76794a57803f), which provides human-annotated subject-predicate-object triples on 237 training and 155 test articles, uses a 768-dimensional BERT pipeline with 12 information-unit categories and three binary phrase classifiers, but reports no accuracy, precision, recall, F1, baselines, or significance, so it only supports the human-labeled triple evaluation setting, not the specific metric choices.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or limitation is that none of the cited works validates the proposed metric set as sufficient for the plan's synthetic-data sampling claim; [(Xie et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf1e5a17b3adead7016eb52ddc3007673ed382f) reports F1 ablations but no precision/recall, no statistical significance, and no sentiment accuracy, so the plan's assertion that accuracy/F1 for sentiment and precision/recall/F1 for relation extraction will robustly demonstrate ACS superiority is not directly evidenced. Xie also uses heuristic silver evidence covering 88.52 percent of test samples, with co-occurrence 54.46 percent, coreference 7.99 percent, and bridge 26.07 percent, and reports F1 gains such as 0.90 Ign F1 and 1.08 F1 for rule-constructed evidence, indicating that evaluation can be sensitive to evidence construction and model components, which the proposed evaluation does not diagnose. A second limitation is that [(Zhang et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/38b75403553508c7623196f10d625c1d162510e5) calculates scores only over predictions for which answers were successfully extracted, a practice that could bias evaluation and conflicts with the plan's need for reliable human-labeled test-set scoring across all ACS samples; it also does not report statistical significance or sentiment metrics, and its human performance uses only 6 annotators on 1,200 samples. The weakest contradiction is that [(Ma et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/42ee952d25d8a192a7786f253eea76794a57803f) uses only 155 annotated test articles and provides no quantitative results, suggesting that small human-labeled test sets and absent metric reporting can leave relation-extraction evaluation underpowered, which is a risk if the plan's human-labeled FewRel or SST2 test sets are not sufficiently large or stratified.

### Suggested Action

Most important, specify the exact human-labeled test sets and ensure they are disjoint from synthetic generation and stratified by label/relation; for FewRel's 64 relation types, report micro-F1, macro-F1, per-relation F1, and precision/recall, and for SST2-like sentiment report accuracy, macro-F1, and per-class precision/recall rather than only accuracy/F1. Second, add statistical reporting across the 25 repeated runs: mean and standard deviation or 95 percent confidence intervals, paired tests or bootstrap significance against random, k-Means, full synthetic, and human-labeled baselines, and clarify whether 25 runs means 5 seeds times 5 sampling configurations or 25 independent seeds, because Xie and Zhang do not provide significance and the plan's robustness claim requires it. Third, align relation-extraction training and evaluation diagnostics with [(Xie et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dbf1e5a17b3adead7016eb52ddc3007673ed382f) by using a development F1 for early stopping or at least reporting dev/test F1, and consider whether the plan's 3 epochs, batch size 16, learning rate 2e-5, and dropout 0.1 are sufficient compared with Xie's BERT-base/RoBERTa-large protocol of up to 30 epochs, batch size 4 documents, AdamW learning rates 5e-5/1e-4, 6 percent warmup, and relation/evidence loss ratio 0.1. Fourth, avoid Zhang's extraction-conditioned scoring by scoring all model outputs with a fixed normalization and reporting extraction failure rates if applicable, and include a human or oracle upper bound with confidence intervals. Finally, document test-set sizes, annotation protocol, and hardware/training resources, because Ma's 155-article test set and missing hyperparameters show that reproducibility and statistical power depend on explicit evaluation details.

## To validate the monotonicity assumption, we will conduct additional experiments on MNIST dataset to validate the monotonicity assumption underlying our binary search algorithm and demonstrate cross-modal applicability of our approach.

### Support

The strongest support for adding an MNIST validation is that coverage-based selection has already been shown to improve image classification on MNIST-derived training data. [(Tian et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26278fc7c0088e72b24866f926f9325df3d7a9c5) trained a ConvNet on 10,000 MNIST images and evaluated on SVHN, MNIST-M, SYN, and USPS using 32x32 RGB inputs, a conv-pool-conv-pool-fc-fc-softmax architecture, Adam lr 0.0001, batch size 32, 32 epochs, lambda 0.1, beta 0.01, and activation threshold 0.005; the coverage objective outperformed ERM and domain-generalization baselines and gave an average 4% accuracy improvement over a vanilla model trained on original and intensity-reversed MNIST. This supports the research plan's use of MNIST as a controlled cross-modal testbed and the broader claim that coverage can encourage diverse representations, although it does not test the proposed binary-search threshold. The next strongest support is that newer graph-based work directly connects coverage/distance-based sampling to generalization. [(Su, and Wu, 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f21832b6be9d3bb03b15420665b10c5954aca93) used k-center greedy and coverage-based sampling over graph structures, trained GNNs on k=5 vertices per class across 30 trials, and found that trials with smaller mean graph distance between training and remaining vertices achieved higher average accuracy, supporting the idea that representative coverage of the data space improves downstream performance. As an iterative line of work, this 2024 result extends the older 2021 MNIST coverage evidence by moving from neuron-activation coverage to structural graph coverage, but it still does not validate the proposed cosine-similarity threshold monotonicity. The weakest support is that sampling-based subset selection is a recognized image-classification problem on MNIST-like data. [(Belrose et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4563516164acbd3978d6c32811f0edb9297131c9) evaluated Gaussian optimal transport, maximum entropy sampling, and independent coordinate sampling on 32x32 or 64x64 datasets including MNIST, Fashion MNIST, CIFAR-10, SVHN, and a 200K CIFARNet, but the provided excerpt defers MNIST results to an appendix and does not report MNIST accuracy, F1, significance, or coverage-threshold behavior. Thus it supports the general premise that selected subsets can be evaluated on MNIST, but provides little direct evidence for ACS's binary-search coverage mechanism.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caveat is that none of the cited works validates the central monotonicity assumption that coverage increases monotonically as a similarity threshold decreases, which is required for the proposed binary search. [(Tian et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26278fc7c0088e72b24866f926f9325df3d7a9c5) uses a fixed neuron-activation threshold of 0.005 and a coverage regularization term, not a pruned similarity graph whose edge threshold is searched; therefore its 4% MNIST improvement cannot be used as evidence that coverage is monotonic in a cosine-similarity threshold. The next strongest caveat is that the closest coverage-sampling evidence is not in the proposed modality or metric. [(Su, and Wu, 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f21832b6be9d3bb03b15420665b10c5954aca93) operates on graph structural distance, small k=5-per-class training sets, and GNNs, and reports grouped trial accuracies without the numerical values, baselines, or significance tests needed to infer that cosine-similarity coverage on MNIST or synthetic text will be monotonic. The weakest but still important limitation is that MNIST may not demonstrate the research plan's core synthetic-text problem. [(Belrose et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4563516164acbd3978d6c32811f0edb9297131c9) treats MNIST as one of several image datasets for distributional sampling, with computational constraints from Gaussian optimal transport (O(d^3) compute and O(d^2) memory) and no MNIST-specific quantitative outcomes in the excerpt; this suggests that MNIST is a convenient but low-dimensional, non-LLM benchmark, so an MNIST monotonicity check should not be overclaimed as evidence that ACS handles LLM-generated redundancy, class skew, or semantic similarity in SST2/FewRel synthetic corpora.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the MNIST experiment a direct test of monotonicity rather than a generic coverage experiment: construct the same ACS similarity graph on MNIST (e.g., using raw pixels or a pretrained/ConvNet embedding), fix k and the max-nearest-neighbors constraint, vary the similarity threshold across a dense grid from the minimum meaningful threshold up to 1.0, compute coverage for each threshold, and report monotonicity diagnostics such as Spearman correlation, isotonic-regression fit, violation counts, and confidence intervals across at least the 5 random seeds used in the main plan. The second priority is to align the MNIST validation with the full ACS pipeline and baselines: use the same greedy max-coverage approximation, binary search, minimum similarity threshold (or an MNIST-appropriate analogue to 0.707), and compare against random sampling, k-Means clustering-based sampling, full-dataset training, and, if feasible, the sampling baselines from [(Belrose et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4563516164acbd3978d6c32811f0edb9297131c9); report accuracy and F1 on a held-out MNIST test split or the same unseen domains used by [(Tian et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26278fc7c0088e72b24866f926f9325df3d7a9c5) (SVHN, MNIST-M, SYN, USPS) with 32x32 RGB preprocessing, a specified ConvNet, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, epochs, seeds, and hardware. The third priority is to avoid overclaiming cross-modal applicability: state that MNIST validates the algorithmic monotonicity and threshold-search behavior in an image modality, not LLM text redundancy, and add a small synthetic-text pilot on SST2 or FewRel embeddings to show that the same coverage-threshold curve holds in the intended text setting. Finally, improve reproducibility by specifying the embedding model, graph construction details, threshold bounds, binary-search stopping criterion, target coverage values from 0.0 to 1.0, statistical tests for significance, and exact dataset sizes, because the related work leaves key implementation details such as seeds, hardware, and per-domain numbers underspecified.
