## To identify affected biological processes and candidate target genes, Transcriptomic Analysis: We will culture F. graminearum PH-1 in YEPD medium and treat with quinofumelin at EC90 concentration (1 μg/mL) for 48 hours, using DMSO as control. RNA will be extracted from treated and control mycelia for Illumina sequencing. We will perform quality control, principal component analysis, and differential expression analysis using DESeq2 with thresholds of P<0.05 and fold change ≥1.5. Gene Ontology and KEGG pathway enrichment analyses will identify affected biological processes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed transcriptomic analysis comes from Martins et al. 2016 because it used F. graminearum expression data and the PH-1 genome to prioritize candidate proteins and then performed structure-based virtual screening, which is directly analogous to the plan's use of transcriptomics to nominate targets and AutoDockTools docking. Specifically, Martins et al. assembled 57,282 ESTs from four wheat-Fusarium early-infection interactions into 34,970 unigenes, separated plant and fungal sequences by BLASTx, retained 5,094 Fusarium contigs, and integrated 13,321 genome-predicted proteins, 1,369 secretome proteins, and PHI-base pathogenicity/virulence/effector sequences; after BLAST2GO, PDB homology, manual curation, a <500 amino acid filter, and removal of orthologs with >60% identity, they docked 10,240 compounds and retained the top 15 per target for three proteins, endoglucanase-5, trichodiene synthase, and ERG6-D-(24)-sterol C-methyltransferase. This supports the proposed method's assumption that F. graminearum expression data can be converted into a tractable target list and that molecular docking can be used as a preliminary binding-prioritization step before SPR/MST. As an iterative progression, Martins et al. 2016 extends the older RNA-seq/fungicide transcriptomic logic of Liu et al. 2015 by moving from differential expression to target curation and virtual screening, but it also shows that expression data must be filtered for structural and functional accessibility. The next strongest support is Liu et al. 2015, which used RNA-seq in Penicillium digitatum after prochloraz treatment, mapped reads to a reference genome, quantified expression, performed differential expression and functional interpretation, and validated transporter-encoding genes by qPCR; although the organism and fungicide differ, it supports the proposed RNA extraction, Illumina sequencing, differential expression, and pathway-interpretation workflow for identifying fungicide-responsive genes. However, the Liu et al. evidence is weaker for the proposed method because the excerpt lacks fold changes, P values, adjusted P values, gene names, pathway enrichment scores, replicates, sequencing platform, read length, software, and significance thresholds, so it cannot validate the specific DESeq2 P<0.05 and fold change >=1.5 criteria or the 48-hour EC90 quinofumelin design.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is not a direct failure of the proposed transcriptomic method but a limitation in the related evidence: Martins et al. 2016 did not use quinofumelin-treated RNA-seq, DESeq2, KEGG enrichment, metabolomics, gene deletion, SPR, or MST, and its expression data came from infection-induced ESTs rather than fungicide-treated mycelia, so it cannot support the assumption that a single 48-hour EC90 quinofumelin transcriptome will directly reveal the primary molecular target. Its target selection also relied heavily on secretome/pathogenicity annotations, subcellular localization, structural accessibility, PDB homology, manual curation, and docking-score ranking, indicating that differential expression and pathway enrichment alone may prioritize indirect or non-accessible transcripts; the proposed method's DESeq2 P<0.05 and fold change >=1.5 thresholds, without an adjusted P-value/FDR criterion or explicit target-accessibility filter, may therefore be insufficient to identify a druggable primary target. The second contradiction is that Liu et al. 2015 provides only methodological precedent in P. digitatum exposed to prochloraz, not quantitative or organism-specific evidence for F. graminearum PH-1, quinofumelin, YEPD, DMSO control, EC90 1 ug/mL, or 48-hour exposure; because the excerpt omits biological replicates, sequencing depth, read length, alignment software, differential expression software, significance thresholds, and qPCR validation details, it cannot be used to justify the proposed statistical thresholds or to demonstrate that the same workflow will reproduce in the proposed system. Finally, both related works are weaker than the proposed plan's downstream validation because neither reports direct quinofumelin binding, kinetic constants, mutant recovery, or metabolite rescue, so the transcriptomic step remains only a hypothesis-generating component rather than proof of mechanism.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a qPCR validation layer for the top differentially expressed genes, following the Liu et al. 2015 RNA-seq-to-qPCR validation strategy, before committing to gene deletion, docking, SPR, or MST; this should include at least three biological replicates, reference genes, and reporting of fold changes and adjusted P values. The next most important action is to adopt the Martins et al. 2016 target-curation framework by intersecting DESeq2/KEGG hits with F. graminearum secretome, PHI-base/pathogenicity, subcellular localization, PDB homology, protein length, ortholog redundancy, and chemical accessibility, and by using docking as a ranking tool rather than a definitive binding assay; include negative-control compounds, known inhibitors, and explicit docking-score or interaction criteria. The proposed statistical analysis should also be strengthened by using DESeq2 with an adjusted P-value/FDR threshold, for example FDR<0.05 or 0.1, in addition to P<0.05 and fold change >=1.5, and by specifying sequencing depth, read length, alignment, batch correction, PCA, and replicate number. Finally, because a 48-hour EC90 time point may capture secondary stress responses, the transcriptomic experiment should be supplemented with an early time course and/or sublethal dose series, and the resulting candidate targets should be prioritized for the plan's metabolomics, recovery, gene deletion, SPR, and MST validation steps.

## To identify affected metabolic pathways and candidate metabolites, Metabolomic Analysis: Using samples from the same treatment groups as transcriptomic analysis, we will conduct widely targeted metabolome analysis using LC-MS. Metabolites will be extracted using methanol:water solution and analyzed for differential accumulation between treated and control groups. We will apply PLS-DA analysis with screening thresholds of P<0.05 and fold change ≥1.5 to identify significantly affected metabolites and perform KEGG pathway enrichment analysis.

### Support

Viewed as an iterative sequence, the related work moves from early Fusarium NMR/GC-MS profiling to LC-MS-based differential metabolomics and then to targeted LC-MS validation. The strongest support for the proposed widely targeted LC-MS method comes from [(Genga et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6185c631b6b043ed076b59058825721c24230441), which used LC-DAD-MS/HPLC-MS broad metabolite profiling of Salvia miltiorrhiza roots and combined it with PCA, HCA, and discussion of PLS-DA/OPLS-DA; it detected 44 secondary metabolites and 28 primary metabolites and showed PCA separation of freeze-, air-, and sun-dried samples, with treatment-associated changes such as increased proline, alanine, succinate, sucrose, glutamine, leucine, melibiose, and raffinose. This directly supports the proposed use of LC-MS to detect differential accumulation between quinofumelin-treated and DMSO-control F. graminearum samples and to use multivariate screening to prioritize candidates for recovery experiments. The next strongest support is [(Gunnaiah et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd51931de84e7111db422558231f9387563a43cc), which used LC-hybrid MS and XCMS to compare wheat Fhb1 NILs inoculated with F. graminearum, identifying differential constitutive and induced metabolites and supporting LC-MS-based differential profiling in a F. graminearum pathosystem, although it profiled host wheat rather than the fungus and did not report PLS-DA thresholds or KEGG enrichment. Newer work by [(Polturak et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5a9f7db16c86313fec88cf88de039cee2c159f) builds on this by using LC-MS to quantify triticein and flavonoids in MeJA-treated wheat and linking metabolite detection to bioactivity against Fusarium culmorum, supporting the plan's downstream validation logic, but it is targeted rather than a broad PLS-DA/KEGG workflow. [(Oiram et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8e580cbb4cfbaf7e66fa8ace25aedd98d00780a) weakly supports the approach by using UPLC-QTOF-MS E and chemometrics for a plant treatment against F. pallidoroseum, but lacks quantitative detail. Finally, [(Lowe et al., 2010-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f8591b971d660e927ba59073acff4671d6fe65) is the weakest but relevant support because it profiled Fusarium spp. by 1H NMR and GC-MS, indicating carbohydrate-rich composition and trichothecene analysis, confirming that Fusarium metabolite profiling is feasible, but it does not support the proposed LC-MS, PLS-DA, or KEGG enrichment details.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is to the proposed statistical assumption that PLS-DA with P<0.05 and fold change >=1.5 is sufficient. [(Genga et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6185c631b6b043ed076b59058825721c24230441) reported PCA-based separation and variable selection rather than validated PLS-DA with those thresholds, and [(Gunnaiah et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd51931de84e7111db422558231f9387563a43cc) used XCMS contrast-based differential profiling without reporting PLS-DA P-values or fold-change cutoffs; this suggests that the proposed screening may be under-specified and prone to overfitting unless cross-validation, permutation, VIP, and false-discovery correction are added. The second major gap is KEGG pathway enrichment: none of the cited LC-MS examples explicitly demonstrates KEGG enrichment for the treatment comparison, with [(Genga et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6185c631b6b043ed076b59058825721c24230441), [(Gunnaiah et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd51931de84e7111db422558231f9387563a43cc), [(Oiram et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f8e580cbb4cfbaf7e66fa8ace25aedd98d00780a), and [(Polturak et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fa5a9f7db16c86313fec88cf88de039cee2c159f) lacking reported enrichment statistics, so the proposed KEGG step is not directly evidenced and may be weak for fungal metabolite annotation. Third, the simple methanol:water extraction is not validated; [(Genga et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6185c631b6b043ed076b59058825721c24230441) compared four solvent-based extraction methods and found extraction treatment affected metabolite detection, implying that a single methanol:water protocol may miss metabolite classes in F. graminearum. Fourth, the widely targeted LC-MS plan may overlook Fusarium-specific secondary metabolites; [(Lowe et al., 2010-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f8591b971d660e927ba59073acff4671d6fe65) used GC-MS specifically for trichothecenes and NMR for carbohydrates, suggesting orthogonal targeted methods may be needed. Finally, the related work generally lacks quantitative fold changes, p-values, instrument parameters, replicate sizes, and statistical thresholds, so the proposed method cannot be assumed reproducible without adding detailed QC, normalization, and validation.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the PLS-DA screening statistically rigorous and tied to the transcriptomic design: use the same biological replicates as the EC90 quinofumelin/DMSO 48 h experiment, add QC samples, randomize injection order, perform PLS-DA with cross-validation and permutation testing, report VIP scores, use OPLS-DA for variable selection, and apply univariate tests with FDR or Bonferroni correction alongside fold change >=1.5, then validate candidates in an independent batch or time point. Second, specify and optimize the LC-MS workflow: define instrument, column, ionization mode, methanol:water ratio, internal standards, extraction recovery, normalization, and batch correction, and consider solvent optimization or orthogonal extraction based on [(Genga et al., 2011-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6185c631b6b043ed076b59058825721c24230441). Third, use a reproducible data-processing pipeline such as XCMS or equivalent for peak detection, alignment, and normalization, as in [(Gunnaiah et al., 2012-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd51931de84e7111db422558231f9387563a43cc), and combine KEGG enrichment with fungal-specific metabolite databases and transcriptomic co-enrichment to prioritize pathways for recovery experiments. Fourth, validate top candidate metabolites quantitatively using targeted LC-MS/MS, standards, or isotope dilution, and include orthogonal GC-MS or NMR for trichothecenes and carbohydrates as suggested by [(Lowe et al., 2010-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00f8591b971d660e927ba59073acff4671d6fe65). Finally, prioritize metabolites that are both statistically robust and biologically linked to quinofumelin's mode of action, then test them in CZA plate recovery assays and gene deletion studies to connect metabolomic changes to target validation.

## To genetically validate candidate target genes and assess gene essentiality, Gene Deletion Studies: We will generate deletion mutants of candidate target genes using protoplast transformation and homologous recombination. Deletion constructs will be designed with appropriate selection markers, and transformants will be verified by PCR analysis. Mutant phenotypes will be characterized on various media with and without metabolite supplementation to assess gene essentiality and pathway involvement.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Gene Deletion Studies comes from the newest related work, [(Wang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c825b35e476e9f1a2d424115887c5cfd63259fde), which used construction of gene deletion mutants in F. graminearum to test TRI-cluster positive regulators and UPR/HOG pathway components in toxisome-shaped structure formation and DON biosynthesis, and showed that the chemical ZJU212 blocks toxisome formation and DON biosynthesis. This directly supports the plan's logic of using deletion mutants to validate pathway involvement after chemical treatment of F. graminearum PH-1 with quinofumelin at 1 μg/mL, although it does not provide metabolite-rescue data. The next strongest support is [(Wang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69e2e73cb03a1faa61b26d3c046a41bdcf6f7429), which generated an Fg62 deletion mutant and complemented strain in F. graminearum, verified transformants by PCR, and connected Fg62 to virulence, early-infection expression, and signal-peptide-dependent cell death in N. benthamiana and soybean; this supports the proposed use of deletion plus complementation and PCR verification for candidate quinofumelin target genes, but it does not specify selection markers, protoplast protocols, or metabolite supplementation. Older work by [(Li et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74f9c1b579ff2eac7825af94b8513e54767128cd) supports the same experimental system and phenotypic logic by constructing FgSvf1 deletion, overexpression, and complementation strains in F. graminearum and characterizing conidial germination, sexual reproduction, wheat-head FHB progression, salt/carbon-source growth, PEP, and intracellular ROS, which parallels the proposed mutant phenotyping on various media, but it did not test quinofumelin or metabolite rescue. Finally, [(Lee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a680a3d1713d5febdf92c001b765251e05008ca) supports the feasibility of generating verified F. graminearum deletions, using CRISPR/Cas9 RNP-mediated short-homology integration for PKS12 and confirming deletion by Southern blotting, but this is less direct support for the proposed protoplast transformation and homologous recombination workflow because it uses a different deletion platform and does not report transformation efficiencies, selection markers, or phenotypic measurements. Viewed iteratively, Li 2019 established deletion/complementation phenotyping in F. graminearum, Wang 2023 extended deletion/complementation to virulence and host assays, Lee 2022 introduced a CRISPR/RNP deletion platform, and Wang 2024 integrated deletion with chemical and pathway readouts.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the feasibility of generating F. graminearum deletion mutants, but the strongest methodological contradiction is that the proposed plan relies on protoplast transformation, homologous recombination, selection markers, and PCR-only verification, whereas newer work by [(Lee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a680a3d1713d5febdf92c001b765251e05008ca) demonstrates a CRISPR/Cas9 RNP-mediated short-homology deletion strategy in F. graminearum for PKS12, with in vitro RNP cleavage testing, nuclear localization analysis, and Southern blot confirmation; this suggests the proposed method may be less efficient or less state-of-the-art for difficult targets and that PCR alone may be insufficient to confirm clean homozygous deletions. A second challenge comes from [(Wang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c825b35e476e9f1a2d424115887c5cfd63259fde), which tied deletion-mutant analysis to specialized readouts such as toxisome-shaped structure formation, DON biosynthesis, and HOG/UPR pathway involvement, and used the chemical ZJU212 to block toxisome formation and DON production; this contradicts the assumption that generic media growth and metabolite supplementation alone will capture quinofumelin's target phenotype, especially if quinofumelin affects subcellular structures, secondary metabolism, or pathogenicity. The weakest but important gap is that the cited works, including [(Li et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74f9c1b579ff2eac7825af94b8513e54767128cd), [(Wang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69e2e73cb03a1faa61b26d3c046a41bdcf6f7429), [(Lee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a680a3d1713d5febdf92c001b765251e05008ca), and [(Wang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c825b35e476e9f1a2d424115887c5cfd63259fde), lack quantitative deletion frequencies, statistical significance, and metabolite-rescue experiments, so the proposed use of mutant phenotypes with and without metabolite supplementation to infer gene essentiality and pathway involvement is not directly supported by the cited evidence and must be treated as an untested inference rather than an established outcome.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a CRISPR/Cas9 RNP short-homology deletion option alongside protoplast transformation and homologous recombination, as demonstrated by [(Lee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1a680a3d1713d5febdf92c001b765251e05008ca) for PKS12 in F. graminearum, including sgRNA design, in vitro RNP cleavage validation, nuclear localization checks if needed, and confirmation by Southern blot or long-range PCR rather than PCR alone. The next priority is to define a quantitative phenotyping panel that matches the research plan's quinofumelin treatment of F. graminearum PH-1 at 1 μg/mL and the related work's readouts: colony diameter and growth rate on CZA and other media, conidial germination, sexual reproduction/fertility, pathogenicity on wheat or susceptible hosts, DON production, and toxisome microscopy if relevant, with biological replicates, ANOVA, Fisher's PLSD, and pre-specified fold-change or significance thresholds. The third priority is to formalize the metabolite-supplementation rescue design, because none of the cited studies, including [(Li et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74f9c1b579ff2eac7825af94b8513e54767128cd), [(Wang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69e2e73cb03a1faa61b26d3c046a41bdcf6f7429), or [(Wang et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c825b35e476e9f1a2d424115887c5cfd63259fde), provides direct evidence that exogenous metabolites rescue quinofumelin-induced inhibition; the plan should specify metabolite concentrations, time courses, dose-response curves, rescue criteria, essentiality controls, complementation strains, and quinofumelin sensitivity/resistance assays. Finally, include complementation and, where possible, conditional expression or overexpression for essential candidate genes, as supported by [(Li et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/74f9c1b579ff2eac7825af94b8513e54767128cd) and [(Wang et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69e2e73cb03a1faa61b26d3c046a41bdcf6f7429), to distinguish target-specific pathway effects from general growth defects.

## To demonstrate direct binding of quinofumelin to candidate target proteins, Molecular Interaction Studies: For biochemical validation, we will express and purify candidate target proteins in E. coli systems. Molecular docking will be performed using AutoDockTools to predict binding conformations and calculate binding energies. SPR analysis using Biacore T200 will measure real-time binding kinetics and affinity constants between purified proteins and quinofumelin. MST analysis will provide additional validation of protein-ligand interactions using fluorescently labeled proteins across concentration gradients.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Martins et al., 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4175498ce07b5bd73a875316c29994baf43382b8), which used F. graminearum PH-1 genome data, 57,282 ESTs, 34,970 unigenes, 5,094 Fusarium-like contigs, 13,321 PH-1 proteins, 1,369 secretome proteins, and 682 PHI-base proteins to prioritize candidate targets, then docked 10,240 compounds into endoglucanase-5, trichodiene synthase, and ERG6-D-(24)-sterol C-methyltransferase and retained 15 compounds per target, for 45 candidates total. This directly supports the proposed plan to prioritize F. graminearum targets and use docking before biochemical validation, although it did not test quinofumelin or report numerical binding energies. Next, [(Naimuzzaman et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/316c2fe21afe4fbc0a4ac5345dae701cdb46edb0) supports the use of AutoDock-based docking by comparing PyRx and Glide, including ligand and protein preparation and pose generation, but it provides no quantitative energies, grid parameters, or experimental validation. [(Asano et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/572a46345f7ee51626c67228e6d189beb664d17c) supports recombinant F. graminearum protein preparation and interaction screening with yeast two-hybrid and MALDI-TOF/TOF, relevant to expressing and validating candidate proteins, but it addresses protein-protein interaction with Thi2.4 rather than direct quinofumelin binding. [(Kim et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81007a2745133f1e6e4cd8682f2463a71e641860) supports biochemical interaction validation using purified or immobilized proteins, PBM, and affinity purification MS, but for protein-DNA or protein-protein interactions, not small-molecule binding. Weakest support is [(Jacob et al., 2012-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ac9b3c8e7c6ce6c116316212f21772a57cb893f), which only indicates docking with DOCK, HTVS, or WinDock and provides no workflow, targets, scoring function, or metrics.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the use of SPR or MST, but several lines of evidence contradict treating docking alone or E. coli expression as sufficient. Strongest contradiction is that [(Martins et al., 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4175498ce07b5bd73a875316c29994baf43382b8) docked 10,240 compounds and selected 45 candidates based on docking score, yet reported no numerical docking energies, affinity constants, kinetic parameters, or experimental binding confirmation; this shows docking score ranking is not by itself evidence of direct binding. It also selected targets including ERG6-D-(24)-sterol C-methyltransferase and trichodiene synthase, which are membrane-associated or secreted enzymes, raising feasibility concerns for the proposed blanket E. coli expression, purification, and SPR/MST workflow. Next, [(Naimuzzaman et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/316c2fe21afe4fbc0a4ac5345dae701cdb46edb0) used AutoDock-based PyRx and Glide but provided no quantitative docking outcomes, statistical comparisons, grid dimensions, or experimental validation, contradicting the assumption that AutoDockTools docking will automatically produce reliable binding energies for quinofumelin. [(Asano et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/572a46345f7ee51626c67228e6d189beb664d17c) identified F. graminearum interactors through yeast two-hybrid and MALDI-TOF/TOF, but this is protein-protein interaction evidence, not direct small-molecule binding, so it contradicts using such interaction screens as proof of quinofumelin target engagement. [(Kim et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81007a2745133f1e6e4cd8682f2463a71e641860) used PBM and affinity purification MS for CCAAT-binding factors, again not small-molecule binding and without quantitative affinity or statistical significance, limiting its support for direct quinofumelin binding. Weakest contradiction is [(Jacob et al., 2012-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ac9b3c8e7c6ce6c116316212f21772a57cb893f), which lacks any docking workflow, scoring function, validation metrics, or reproducibility details, so it cannot support the proposed quantitative interaction studies.

### Suggested Action

Most important: reposition AutoDockTools docking as a preliminary filter, not proof of direct binding; report ligand preparation, protonation and tautomer states, grid box dimensions, scoring function, number of runs, binding energies, RMSD, enrichment over negative controls, and benchmark against at least one alternative scorer such as Glide or PyRx as in [(Naimuzzaman et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/316c2fe21afe4fbc0a4ac5345dae701cdb46edb0). Second: select candidate targets using the Martins criteria and feasibility filters: PH-1 genome, in planta ESTs, secretome, PHI-base, early infection, cytoplasmic or chemically accessible localization, under 500 amino acids, low ortholog conservation, and exclude or separately plan membrane, secreted, or glycosylated proteins because [(Martins et al., 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4175498ce07b5bd73a875316c29994baf43382b8) included ERG6 and trichodiene synthase, which may not be suitable for simple E. coli expression. Third: for E. coli expression, specify constructs, tags, solubility testing, refolding, activity assays, and if targets are membrane or eukaryotic, use yeast, insect, or mammalian expression; validate purified protein by SDS-PAGE, mass spectrometry, CD, or activity assay before SPR or MST. Fourth: design SPR on Biacore T200 with reference subtraction, mass transport checks, ligand immobilization density, quinofumelin concentration series, negative ligand controls, and report ka, kd, Kd with replicates and statistical tests; use MST with fluorescently labeled protein, concentration gradients, labeled and unlabeled controls, and Kd estimates. Fifth: integrate genetic and biochemical evidence by linking docking, SPR, and MST targets to transcriptomic and metabolomic pathways, deletion mutants, and recovery experiments, and use [(Asano et al., 2013-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/572a46345f7ee51626c67228e6d189beb664d17c) and [(Kim et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/81007a2745133f1e6e4cd8682f2463a71e641860) as precedents for interaction validation only when combined with direct small-molecule binding assays. Least important but still needed: document software versions, hardware, replicates, and statistical methods to improve reproducibility, addressing the missing details in [(Jacob et al., 2012-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ac9b3c8e7c6ce6c116316212f21772a57cb893f).

## To ensure robust quantitative comparisons across all experiments, Statistical Analysis: All experiments will include appropriate biological replicates and statistical analyses using ANOVA and Fisher's PLSD for mean comparisons.

### Support

The strongest support from [(Kanchiswamy et al., 2010-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f6e93d4ce4907a313ed35395837e5c9231b7ac) is that it contains a data and statistical analysis component for quantitative plant-biology measurements, which is conceptually aligned with the proposed plan's requirement for biological replicates and inferential statistics across experiments. A weaker but still relevant point is that the related work involves mutant genotypes, herbivore and mechanical damage treatments, intracellular Ca2+ measurements, GFP fusion expression, and cell-free protein synthesis, all of which are the kind of treatment-by-genotype comparisons that would normally require replicate-based mean comparisons. The weakest support is that the paper appears in BMC Plant Biology, a peer-reviewed plant biology venue, indicating that statistical analysis is an expected part of such biological studies, although the available text does not identify the exact test used.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Kanchiswamy et al., 2010-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f6e93d4ce4907a313ed35395837e5c9231b7ac) does not specify ANOVA, Fisher's PLSD, the number of biological replicates, software, or exact data inputs, so it cannot be used as direct evidence that the proposed generic statistical workflow is appropriate or reproducible. A second contradiction is that the related work reports no quantitative statistical outcomes, P-values, effect sizes, or mean comparisons in the available text, leaving no empirical support for choosing Fisher's PLSD over more conservative post hoc corrections. A third, more contextual contradiction is that the proposed plan applies ANOVA and Fisher's PLSD to all experiments, including transcriptomic DESeq2, metabolomic PLS-DA, molecular docking, SPR, MST, and phylogenetic analysis, whereas the related work only suggests statistical analysis for quantitative phenotypic or biochemical measurements and provides no evidence that a single ANOVA/Fisher PLSD approach is suitable for omics, binding-kinetic, or phylogenetic data.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to replace the blanket statement with an experiment-specific statistical plan: retain DESeq2 with adjusted P<0.05 and fold-change thresholds for transcriptomics, retain PLS-DA with permutation testing and FDR control for metabolomics, use one-way or two-way ANOVA with Dunnett or Tukey post hoc tests for colony-diameter and phenotypic comparisons, use binding-curve fitting with technical and biological replicates plus confidence intervals for SPR/MST, and report docking energies as descriptive computational outputs rather than ANOVA comparisons. The next priority is to define the number of biological and technical replicates, sample sizes, randomization, blinding where feasible, and a power analysis for each quantitative assay, especially recovery experiments across Fusarium species and strains. Then specify the exact ANOVA model, assumptions, normality and homogeneity checks, transformations if needed, multiple-comparison correction, software, and version, and report means, standard deviations or confidence intervals, effect sizes, and exact P-values. Finally, align the reporting with the level of detail expected in plant/fungal biology studies such as [(Kanchiswamy et al., 2010-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8f6e93d4ce4907a313ed35395837e5c9231b7ac), by documenting inputs, replicates, and statistical parameters rather than only naming a generic test.
