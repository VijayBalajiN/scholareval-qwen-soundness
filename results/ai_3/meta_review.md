## Context: To prevent unbounded weight growth without losing learned information, Method: 'We will develop Soft Weight Rescaling (SWR), a novel weight regularization method that directly reduces weight magnitudes by scaling them down at each training step.'

### Support

The strongest support for SWR comes from [(Cai et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253eba43d7e734ee6c5b00dca29be3c6c589f4a5), which directly studies weight and bias rescaling under batch normalization and shows that a common rescaling factor eta can approximately preserve first-epoch loss and accuracy for relatively large eta, while BNGD with separate learning rates for batch-normalization parameters extends the stable learning-rate range and can outperform optimally tuned gradient descent in some OLS and MNIST, Fashion MNIST, and CIFAR-10 settings; this supports the plan's proportionality argument and its use of CNN-BN, VGG-16, and lambda sweeps, although the paper uses one-time common rescaling rather than the proposed per-layer iterative Frobenius-norm-ratio EMA. The next strongest support is [(Qi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6fc129d8539aa72fd609d48edaeeb8f1222107f), which uses a simulated Lipschitz-constant estimator on 12-layer ResNet and Transformer variants with hidden dimension 1024, 10 input points and 10 Gaussian perturbations, and Xavier gain 2.0 to emulate weight growth, finding that weight-matrix gain strongly affects the simulated Lipschitz constant for dot-product attention and that K increases with hidden dimension; this supports the plan's goal of bounding weight magnitude and Lipschitz continuity, and suggests using gain-2.0 initialization as a stress test, but the evidence is synthetic and does not evaluate SWR. A further supporting line is [(Huang et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cc8fe49b353c77846af7437d8f98d3d7e3b56c3), which shows that orthogonal weight normalization can stabilize and accelerate training on MNIST MLPs with 4 hidden layers of 100 neurons, mini-batch 1024, and learning-rate sweeps, with OLM outperforming QR and several Riemannian baselines in training-loss stability and convergence; this supports weight-level regularization as a way to control optimization dynamics, but it imposes hard orthogonality rather than soft magnitude rescaling and does not test plasticity or continual learning. Weaker support comes from [(Sun et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ab1b2261579c4d3fdef95b70bf8a4d7483e674c), which observes in CIFAR-10 ResNet-101 trained with SGD at learning rate 1e-3 for 200 epochs that batch-normalization gamma and beta approximate pre-activation variance and mean after convergence, supporting the plan's focus on learnable final normalization parameters, but the evidence is limited to one randomly selected channel among 64 and does not test external rescaling. The weakest support is [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0), which uses a one-time data-dependent initialization for weight-normalized networks by sampling v with standard deviation 0.05, performing one minibatch feedforward pass, and setting b and g to match pre-activation mean and variance; this supports manipulating scale-related parameters to control layer-output magnitude, but it is not a per-step regularization and does not address unbounded weight growth or plasticity. Viewed iteratively, the older one-time scale-parameter idea in Salimans is extended by Huang's weight-constraint stability experiments, then by Cai's more direct analysis of scaling with batch normalization, and finally by Sun's observation that BN parameters track pre-activation statistics and Qi's connection between weight magnitude and Lipschitz behavior.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Cai et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253eba43d7e734ee6c5b00dca29be3c6c589f4a5), because the batch-normalization scaling property is only approximate: for small rescaling factors eta, the variance regularization epsilon = 1e-3 shifts loss and accuracy curves, and gradient descent or BNGD with identical learning rates becomes unstable beyond a maximum allowed learning rate; this contradicts the assumption that SWR can repeatedly scale weights down while exactly preserving functional behavior and optimization stability, especially in the plan's CNN-BN and VGG-16 experiments where BN epsilon, gamma, and beta interact with per-layer Frobenius-norm rescaling. The next strongest contradiction is from [(Qi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6fc129d8539aa72fd609d48edaeeb8f1222107f), which finds that increasing weight-matrix gain has a strong effect on the simulated Lipschitz constant of dot-product attention Transformers but little effect on ResNet, and that K is most sensitive to perturbation scale in attention; this contradicts a universal claim that reducing weight magnitude will uniformly bound Lipschitz behavior or prevent plasticity across all architectures, and it also warns that the 10-by-10 finite-difference estimator may be too coarse for precise claims. A further contradiction is from [(Huang et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cc8fe49b353c77846af7437d8f98d3d7e3b56c3), where weight constraints can be destabilizing if not carefully designed: the OLM variant without distortion minimization was unstable, Riemannian EI+QR, CI+QR, and CayT diverged at learning rates above 0.05, and QR projection was stable but had inferior final training loss; this contradicts the idea that any weight-level constraint will improve optimization and implies SWR must remain soft, avoid over-constraining, and be validated across learning-rate and depth regimes. Weaker contradiction comes from [(Sun et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ab1b2261579c4d3fdef95b70bf8a4d7483e674c), because the observed stabilization of gamma and beta to pre-activation statistics under standard training suggests that externally imposed per-step weight rescaling may disturb learned normalization statistics if the network cannot adapt quickly enough, although the paper does not directly test this. The weakest contradiction is from [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0), which provides no evidence that repeated scale manipulation preserves learned information, and its method is explicitly a one-time initialization procedure that cannot be directly applied to recursive models, limiting its relevance to the plan's feed-forward continual-learning scenarios. Viewed iteratively, Cai's later BN epsilon result and Qi's later architecture-dependent Lipschitz result both qualify earlier or more general claims about scale manipulation, while Huang's instability results and Sun's BN adaptation observation provide additional constraints on SWR design.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the normalization-layer interaction explicit and empirically validated, because [(Cai et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253eba43d7e734ee6c5b00dca29be3c6c589f4a5) and [(Sun et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ab1b2261579c4d3fdef95b70bf8a4d7483e674c) show that batch-normalization scaling is not exact and that gamma and beta track pre-activation statistics; SWR should therefore derive and report closed-form or learned updates for biases and BN parameters, especially the final normalization layer, test small scaling factors and high lambda values, sweep BN epsilon, use separate learning rates for BN parameters when appropriate, and monitor first-epoch loss, accuracy, pre-activation mean and variance, gamma and beta errors, and weight-norm ratios in the plan's warm-start, continual-learning, and single-task settings. The next most important action is to add optimization-stability diagnostics inspired by [(Huang et al., 2017-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6cc8fe49b353c77846af7437d8f98d3d7e3b56c3) and [(Cai et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/253eba43d7e734ee6c5b00dca29be3c6c589f4a5): include learning-rate sweeps, training-loss curves, depth comparisons from 3-layer MLP to VGG-16, mini-batch size ablations, and comparisons to L2, L2 Init, S&P re-initialization, and head reset, with ablations for per-layer versus global scaling, lambda_c versus lambda_f, and the EMA term, reporting means and standard deviations over the planned 5 seeds for smaller models and 3 seeds for VGG-16. The next action is to strengthen the Lipschitz and weight-growth evaluation suggested by [(Qi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a6fc129d8539aa72fd609d48edaeeb8f1222107f): use a finite-difference Lipschitz estimator with more input points and perturbations than 10 by 10, multiple random seeds, and architecture-specific reporting, and include a gain-2.0 Xavier or otherwise grown-weight initialization as a stress test to verify that SWR reduces unbounded growth while preserving accuracy and Lipschitz continuity. A further action is to incorporate data-dependent calibration inspired by [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0) and [(Sun et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4ab1b2261579c4d3fdef95b70bf8a4d7483e674c), such as using one minibatch of pre-activation statistics to initialize or adapt the EMA target, choosing lambda based on observed layer-output variance, or freezing or updating BN parameters in a controlled ablation. Finally, the plan should document reproducibility details that are missing in the related work, including hardware, software, random seeds, epsilon, learning-rate schedules, computational overhead of per-step Frobenius norms, and FP16 sensitivity of the EMA, so that the proposed method can be reproduced and compared fairly against the baselines.

## Context: To compute layer-wise rescaling without over-constraining the model, Method: 'Our SWR method will determine scaling factors for each layer based on the ratio between the Frobenius norm of the initial weight matrix and the current one. To prevent over-constraining the model, we will incorporate an exponential moving average approach where the scaling factor for the l-th layer is: cl = (λ × ||W^init_l|| + (1-λ) × ||Wl||) / ||Wl||, where λ controls the regularization strength.'

### Support

Strongest empirical support for the central plasticity hypothesis is [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c), which shows L2 Init, a penalty regressing current weights toward initial PyTorch uniform weights with strength λ, maintains high average online task accuracy and retained plasticity on all five continual image classification problems (Permuted MNIST, Random Label MNIST, Random Label CIFAR, 5+1 CIFAR, Continual ImageNet), is comparable to Continual Backprop and ReDO, and beats Continual Backprop on 4 of 5 problems; this directly supports anchoring to an initial weight scale, though it uses a loss term rather than direct Frobenius-norm rescaling. The strongest mechanistic support is [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5), whose ENorm applies layer-wise p=2 rescaling after SGD, initializes coefficients to one, preserves the implemented function, and updates momentum with the same diagonal rescaling matrices; this validates the proposed layer-wise norm-ratio scaling and functional proportionality, but the excerpt lacks accuracy, loss, ablation, or significance values. Next, [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61) supports shrink-based warm-start repair: positive scaling of ReLU/softmax networks preserves argmax, and shrink-and-perturb improves warm-start generalization, with reported warm-start accuracies of 54.4, 53.5, 52.9, 49.9, and 50.8 percent versus random-initialized 55.2 percent on ResNet-18 CIFAR-10/SVHN/CIFAR-100 settings, showing that reducing weight magnitude can help but that perturbation and full-weight modification matter. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Chiley et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9aecad9e5dd0ed2023e9f82b3d7fee935ef3536), whose Online Normalization uses EMA estimates of activation and gradient scales with decay rates alpha_f and alpha_b, outperforms Batch, Group, Layer, and Instance Normalization on CIFAR-100 ResNet-20, ImageNet ResNet-50, and U-Net segmentation, supporting EMA-based scale control and the need to handle normalization layers. Even weaker support is [(Gupta, and Asthana, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c2fbee1ad62cc42f9aa50a4888824c78602e87f), which uses EMA of weights and quantization scale factors during quantization-aware training on YOLO5/YOLO7 COCO, yielding about 2-3 mAP gains over LSQ and 4-6 mAP with post-hoc correction at 3-bit, showing EMA can stabilize layer-wise scale factors without backpropagation. The weakest support is [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0), whose data-dependent weight-normalization initialization sets gain and bias from one minibatch pre-activation statistics to zero mean and unit variance, supporting layer-wise scale control but only as a one-time initialization rather than continuous plasticity recovery.

### Contradictions

Strongest empirical contradiction is from [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61): shrink-and-perturb improves warm-started models but does not reach random-initialized performance, and warm-started models that match random accuracy require roughly the same training time as random initialization, so weight shrink alone may not fully recover plasticity or provide computational savings; last-layer-only shrink underperforms random init on CIFAR-10 48.8 versus 56.0, SVHN 86.0 versus 89.4, and CIFAR-100 16.4 versus 18.2, indicating that layer selection and perturbation are critical. A second important contradiction is that [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c) shows plain L2 regularization mitigates plasticity loss on only 2 of 5 problems (Permuted MNIST and Continual ImageNet) and performs poorly on Random Label MNIST, Random Label CIFAR, and 5+1 CIFAR, while Shrink and Perturb has consistently lower overall performance than L2 Init; this warns that simple magnitude control without direction anchoring or perturbation may be task-dependent, and that SWR's norm-only scaling may be weaker than L2 Init's attraction to the full initial weight vector. A direct method-level contradiction, highlighted by the need for bounded and balanced rescaling in [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5) and EMA-based scale control in [(Chiley et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9aecad9e5dd0ed2023e9f82b3d7fee935ef3536), is that the proposed c_l = (λ ||W^init_l|| + (1-λ) ||W_l||) / ||W_l|| can exceed 1 when the current norm is smaller than the initial norm, which contradicts the stated goal of scaling down and bounding growth, and the formula uses λ as a static mixing coefficient rather than an explicit EMA state. Third, [(Chiley et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9aecad9e5dd0ed2023e9f82b3d7fee935ef3536) contradicts the plan's limited normalization handling: Online Normalization normalizes both forward activations and backward gradients using EMA scales and outperforms Batch Normalization, suggesting that focusing only on learnable parameters of the final normalization layer may be insufficient for CNN-BN and VGG-16. Fourth, [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5) does not anchor to initial norms but balances layer norms via coordinate descent, so its functional-preservation guarantee does not directly validate bounding growth relative to W_init; if SWR only uses a static λ rather than an EMA state, it may over-constrain or under-constrain unlike ENorm's periodic balanced rescaling. Fifth, [(Gupta, and Asthana, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c2fbee1ad62cc42f9aa50a4888824c78602e87f) shows EMA is effective for quantization scale stabilization but not for plasticity, so its EMA evidence cannot be transferred to continual learning without new experiments. Weakest contradiction is [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0), whose one-time data-dependent initialization is not continuous and is not recommended for recursive models, so it does not support continuous SWR in long training.

### Suggested Action

Most important action is to replace the static λ formula with a true EMA and bounded scaling rule, for example maintain m_l = β m_{l-1} + (1-β) ||W^init_l|| / ||W_l|| and set c_l = min(1, m_l) if only downscaling is desired, or explicitly report the upscaling regime; sweep β/λ as in [(Chiley et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9aecad9e5dd0ed2023e9f82b3d7fee935ef3536) and [(Gupta, and Asthana, 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8c2fbee1ad62cc42f9aa50a4888824c78602e87f), including decay initialized to zero or tuned per layer. Next, add a small stochastic perturbation or compare against L2 Init and Shrink-and-Perturb, because [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61) shows shrink alone does not reach random initialization and [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c) shows initial-weight anchoring is effective on continual image classification; evaluate on the same five continual tasks (Permuted MNIST, Random Label MNIST, Random Label CIFAR, 5+1 CIFAR, Continual ImageNet) and the 50 percent to 100 percent warm-start setup with ResNet-18, SGD learning rate 0.001, no augmentation, 5 seeds, and wall-clock time on comparable hardware. Then, strengthen normalization-layer handling for CNN-BN and VGG-16 by either freezing or rescaling BN affine parameters consistently with proportionality, or using online/EMA activation and gradient scale estimates as in [(Chiley et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9aecad9e5dd0ed2023e9f82b3d7fee935ef3536), and test batch-size variants such as 128, 32, and 25. Also adopt implementation details from [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5): apply SWR periodically after SGD, update momentum with the same diagonal rescaling matrices, treat the number of rescaling cycles as a hyperparameter, and verify functional preservation with p=2 Frobenius norms. Finally, use data-dependent initialization from [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0) as a baseline or initialization ablation, and report test accuracy, balancedness ℓp,q ratios, hyperparameter sweeps, seeds, hardware, and statistical significance to make the method reproducible.

## Context: To ground SWR in network proportionality, Method: 'We will establish that for feed-forward neural networks with homogeneous activation functions, we can construct infinitely many proportional networks that maintain identical functional behavior.'

### Support

The strongest support for the claim that homogeneous feed-forward networks admit infinitely many function-preserving proportional networks comes from [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5), which explicitly studies Equi-normalization as a function-preserving weight-rescaling method for feed-forward networks, formalizes it as block coordinate descent over rescaling coefficients initialized to ones, uses p=2 by default, applies one ENorm cycle after each SGD step, and corrects momentum with the same rescaling matrices D_k. This directly validates the core SWR premise that layer-wise proportional rescaling can preserve the implemented function while changing parameter-space gradients, and it shows that such rescaling can be integrated with SGD on MNIST, CIFAR-10, and ImageNet, although the excerpt provides no accuracy, loss, or significance values. A second strong support is [(Li, and Arora, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00521e876d109fdcf344e027f218a768499060c8), which uses scale invariance in PreResNet32 on CIFAR-10 with initial learning rate 0.1 and momentum 0.9, freezing BatchNorm scale and bias parameters and the final fully connected layer so that remaining convolutional weights can be scaled without changing the network function; this supports SWR's need to treat normalization layers and classifier layers separately, including the plan's distinct lambda_c and lambda_f. A third support is [(Ji, and Telgarsky, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/221a1f9dce1e0ccab0debae7f7f3d4c4d91c876c), which analyzes L-positively homogeneous feed-forward networks with linear, convolution, ReLU, and max-pooling layers and proves that the normalized parameter trajectory W_t / ||W_t|| has finite length and converges, with normalized margins also converging under logistic or exponential loss when initial risk is below 1/n; its experiments on a modified homogeneous AlexNet, an unmodified DenseNet, and a 10,000-node-wide two-layer squared ReLU network with its Neural Tangent Kernel classifier provide qualitative evidence that homogeneous scaling governs late-phase optimization, which aligns with SWR's goal of bounding weight magnitude without destroying function. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Merrill et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f10a04a77fd1cd719792de344a60f3fd03f6b944), which reports that T5 parameter norm grows approximately proportional to sqrt(t) over 0 to 500,000 steps, appears uniform across 12 layers, and that gradient alignment rises to about 0.15 cosine similarity before decaying toward 0; although this is a diagnostic study of 220M-parameter transformer pretraining rather than a proportional-network construction, it supports the motivation for monitoring per-layer Frobenius norms and for intervening against sustained norm growth.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is from [(Li, and Arora, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00521e876d109fdcf344e027f218a768499060c8), which shows that unfixed BatchNorm scale and bias parameters, and the following convolutional layer, can grow exponentially under large growth rates and sometimes exceed the Float32 range, so scale invariance was maintained only by freezing all BatchNorm scale and bias parameters and the final fully connected layer. This contradicts the current SWR plan's narrower statement that normalization layers will be handled by focusing on the learnable parameters of the final normalization layer, because intermediate BatchNorm affine parameters can also break proportionality and cause numerical instability in CNN-BN or VGG-16 experiments. A second important contradiction is from [(Ji, and Telgarsky, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/221a1f9dce1e0ccab0debae7f7f3d4c4d91c876c), whose theory excludes biases and skip connections and treats only L-positively homogeneous components; its DenseNet experiment violates the theory because of skip connections and biases, whereas the SWR plan includes biases, fully connected layers, and normalization layers, so the claim of infinitely many proportional networks cannot be assumed to hold for all planned architectures unless exact bias and skip-connection scaling rules are proved. A third caution is from [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5), which notes that function-preserving rescaling is a jump in parameter space and therefore requires momentum to be updated using the same rescaling matrices D_k; if SWR applies c_l = (lambda ||W^init_l|| + (1-lambda) ||W_l||) / ||W_l|| at each training step without equivalent optimizer-state correction, it may perturb SGD momentum, Adam moments, or learning-rate-schedule dynamics, and the excerpt provides no quantitative evidence that the default one-cycle-per-SGD-step setting preserves accuracy. The weakest contradiction is from [(Merrill et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f10a04a77fd1cd719792de344a60f3fd03f6b944), which finds that the optimizer step size is not exactly constant under the T5 schedule, with the norm of delta_t growing gradually although the absolute change is less than 20 across 220M parameters, and that alignment decays toward 0; this does not directly refute SWR, but it suggests that periodic or per-step rescaling may interact with nonconstant optimizer steps and that conclusions from homogeneous feed-forward image models may not transfer unchanged to large transformer pretraining.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to revise the normalization-layer treatment by deriving and testing exact proportionality rules for all BatchNorm affine parameters, not only the final normalization layer, or by freezing/co-rescaling all BatchNorm scale and bias parameters and the final classifier layer as in [(Li, and Arora, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/00521e876d109fdcf344e027f218a768499060c8); this should include overflow checks in Float32 for CNN-BN and VGG-16 on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet, and separate lambda_c and lambda_f ablations. The second most important action is to add optimizer-state correction for SWR, following [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5), by rescaling momentum buffers, Adam first and second moments, or equivalent optimizer state with the same layer-wise c_l factors, and by reporting whether one rescaling per SGD step, periodic rescaling, or EMA-smoothed rescaling gives better warm-start, continual-learning, and single-task accuracy with 5 seeds for smaller models and 3 seeds for VGG-16. The third action is to tighten the theoretical scope by proving proportionality only for the homogeneous layers actually used, or by explicitly deriving bias, skip-connection, and normalization scaling rules in the spirit of [(Ji, and Telgarsky, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/221a1f9dce1e0ccab0debae7f7f3d4c4d91c876c), and by adding a controlled experiment on a homogeneous variant versus a biased/skip-connected variant to show where the theory breaks. The fourth action is to add diagnostic metrics that connect SWR to the evidence in [(Merrill et al., 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f10a04a77fd1cd719792de344a60f3fd03f6b944), including per-layer Frobenius norm trajectories, entry-wise lp,q-norm ratios, cosine alignment between optimizer steps and parameters, and step-size magnitude, so that the paper can show whether SWR reduces sqrt(t)-like norm growth and misalignment without sacrificing test accuracy. The least important but useful action is to include ENorm-style function-preserving rescaling as a baseline or ablation, because [(Stock et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dd29bd8bd4197a97d28614f3c13b18fc6c9e2b5) demonstrates a closely related p=2 rescaling procedure, and comparing SWR against it would isolate the benefit of the Frobenius-ratio EMA and layer-specific lambda values.

## Context: To make SWR compatible with common architectures and separate classifier/feature extractor behavior, Method: 'We will extend this approach to handle normalization layers by focusing on learnable parameters of the final normalization layer to maintain proportionality. The method will apply different coefficients λc for classifier layers and λf for feature extractor layers.'

### Support

The strongest evidence supporting the proposed normalization extension is that layer normalization is invariant to scaling the entire incoming weight matrix and adding a constant shift to all incoming weights [(Ba et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97fb4e3d45bb098e27e0071448b6152217bd35a5). This directly aligns with SWR's layer-wise Frobenius-norm scaling, because if the scaling factor c_l is applied uniformly to all incoming weights of a normalized layer, the normalized pre-activation statistics can remain functionally equivalent, provided biases and normalization affine parameters are treated consistently. A secondary but relevant point is that layer normalization uses learned per-neuron gain and bias parameters, initialized to 1 and 0 in the reported experiments, which suggests that the final normalization layer's learnable parameters are a natural locus for absorbing residual scale or shift mismatches when SWR is applied. Finally, the paper's observation that normalization standard deviation can implicitly reduce the effective learning rate and stabilize learning supports the broader motivation that weight scaling and normalization interact in ways that can improve stability, although this is weaker evidence because it does not quantify SWR's effect on plasticity or accuracy.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that layer normalization is not invariant to scaling individual weight vectors [(Ba et al., 2016-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/97fb4e3d45bb098e27e0071448b6152217bd35a5), so any implementation of SWR that scales weights non-uniformly across channels, neurons, or feature dimensions before a normalization layer may break the proportionality assumption and alter the normalized representation. A second contradiction is that the supplied evidence is qualitative and does not provide quantitative accuracy, loss, baseline comparisons, dataset sizes, optimizer, learning rates, or statistical significance, so it cannot confirm that SWR's λc and λf coefficients, EMA scaling, or final-normalization parameter focus will preserve performance in the planned MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, TinyImageNet, CNN-BN, and VGG-16 experiments. A third, weaker contradiction is that the paper studies layer normalization on six tasks with a focus on recurrent networks, not batch normalization or feed-forward image classifiers, and its invariance analysis does not address training dynamics, optimizer states, or continual learning, so the transfer to the proposed plasticity-loss setting is indirect.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to derive an explicit normalization-aware invariance rule for SWR: for each layer followed by or containing a normalization layer, apply the same scalar c_l to all incoming weights and biases, and either leave the normalization affine parameters unchanged or update them according to the layer normalization invariance conditions, rather than scaling individual weight vectors or only the final normalization layer's parameters. Next, add a controlled invariance test before the main benchmarks: on fixed inputs, compare pre-SWR and post-SWR outputs, logit scales, gradient norms, and normalized feature norms for MLP, CNN-BN, and VGG-16, reporting cosine similarity and relative change with multiple seeds. Then, ablate λc and λf separately for classifier and feature extractor layers, including a baseline where both use the same λ, and monitor whether separate coefficients improve balancedness without degrading warm-start or continual learning accuracy. Finally, extend the analysis to batch normalization and report effective learning-rate changes, because the related work's observation that normalization can implicitly reduce learning rate implies SWR may compound or conflict with normalization's implicit regularization.

## Context: To validate the theoretical properties of SWR, Method: 'We will provide mathematical proofs demonstrating that SWR bounds weight magnitude and balances weight magnitude between layers. We will also prove that SWR maintains Lipschitz continuity for networks with 1-Lipschitz activation functions.'

### Support

Strongest support for the theoretical validation comes from [(Combettes, and Pesquet, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d6fff3a863fff27534ff25897e01f1026a0095a), which gives an operator-theoretic method for Lipschitz constants of layered feed-forward ReLU networks using spectral norms of weight matrices and diagonal 0/1 matrices for ReLU subgradients, and reports over 1000 random 8-10-6-3 networks that theta_3 has average ratio 0.6699 and minimal ratio 0.5112 while vartheta_3 has average ratio 0.4528 and minimal ratio 0.2424 relative to the product of spectral norms, showing that layer-wise spectral-norm proofs are viable but naive product bounds can be loose. This directly supports the plan's goal of proving Lipschitz continuity for 1-Lipschitz activations by providing a template for tighter layer-wise analysis and a mathematical refinement of the product-of-spectral-norms view that later empirical work builds on. Next, the newer [(Ma et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9b799d50bf14f3b5b3af87a137a748be1854ef) empirically supports the relevance of bounding weight magnitude and Lipschitz constants: across MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, Caltech-101, and Imagenette, SGD-trained models have smaller layer-wise Lipschitz upper bounds and higher averaged robust accuracy than Adam and RMSProp, for example CIFAR-10 upper bound 26.81 versus 28.70 and 37.75 with robust accuracy 63.21 percent versus 57.71 and 56.47 percent, averaged over three seeds, indicating that weight-magnitude control is not merely theoretical. Then [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) supports weight scaling as a practical Lipschitz-like regularizer: spectral normalization keeps discriminator layer spectral norms in the 1 to 1.05 range, is more robust than WGAN-GP under aggressive Adam settings on CIFAR-10 and STL-10, achieves better inception scores than almost all compared methods on optimal settings, and has training time about 110 to 120 percent of weight normalization, although it uses spectral norm rather than SWR's Frobenius EMA. [(Anil et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0ded4902d7f9c111e50047f8c9494effb7282d1) supports the broader principle that layer-wise 1-Lipschitz constraints with GroupSort can construct K-Lipschitz networks, but the excerpt provides no quantitative outcomes. Finally, the newer [(Badings et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a39e0665a6e94174e024a9f5b7624e4d6de1d97b) and [(Fazlyab et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89fc3d8af739f3c41ff72a1bd0acf0571a8c533e) support the use of weighted-norm and SDP methods to compute tighter Lipschitz bounds, which can be used to verify SWR networks, although the excerpts lack numerical outcomes.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is the newer [(Zhou et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfb9ea4f70aef592c59817d70f69b832e605c773), which appears after the positive spectral-normalization results in [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) and shows that hard per-layer spectral normalization can converge to a suboptimal discriminator on two-dimensional toy data, fail to improve with more power-iteration steps or longer decreasing-learning-rate training, diverge under the WGAN objective on full CIFAR-10, and produce apparently worse Frechet Inception Distance than the maximum-gradient-penalty baseline under hinge or vanilla objectives, indicating that aggressive weight scaling to enforce Lipschitz continuity can underuse capacity or destabilize training. This is directly relevant because SWR must prove that its soft rescaling does not impose an overly restrictive global scaling. A second, more theoretical contradiction is that the same Lipschitz-bounding literature shows layer scaling factors multiply layer constants: [(Combettes, and Pesquet, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d6fff3a863fff27534ff25897e01f1026a0095a) uses spectral norms of weight matrices, [(Ma et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9b799d50bf14f3b5b3af87a137a748be1854ef) multiplies isolated layer constants, and [(Badings et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a39e0665a6e94174e024a9f5b7624e4d6de1d97b) optimizes weighted norms to minimize bounds; therefore SWR's factor c_l = (lambda times ||W_init_l|| + (1-lambda) times ||W_l||) / ||W_l|| can exceed 1 when ||W_l|| is smaller than ||W_init_l||, which would increase the layer spectral norm and potentially increase the network Lipschitz constant, so the claim that SWR maintains Lipschitz continuity needs a precise invariant and additional assumptions. Third, the same [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) also contradicts the idea that spectral weight scaling is uniformly superior: on STL-10 for the same number of iterations it fell behind orthonormal regularization, and orthonormal regularization was reported to be slightly better under one optimal setting, with no statistical significance tests or confidence intervals reported. Fourth, [(Anil et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0ded4902d7f9c111e50047f8c9494effb7282d1) uses hard norm-constrained layers rather than soft rescaling, so its construction does not validate SWR's proportionality or EMA balancing and may be too restrictive for plasticity. Finally, [(Fazlyab et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89fc3d8af739f3c41ff72a1bd0acf0571a8c533e) provides only qualitative claims of tighter SDP bounds in the excerpt, so it cannot by itself support or refute SWR's specific Lipschitz proof.

### Suggested Action

Most important, revise the theoretical claim to state exactly what is maintained: for each layer, the Frobenius norm after SWR is the convex combination lambda ||W_init_l||_F + (1-lambda) ||W_l||_F, so if ||W_l||_F is above the initial norm the step decreases it, but if it is below the initial norm the step increases it; therefore either use a one-sided factor such as min(1, the proposed factor) or prove a bounded invariant such as ||W_l^t||_F <= max(||W_l^init||_F, sup_t ||W_l^t before scaling||) and a corresponding Lipschitz bound L <= product_l ||W_l||_2 using ||W||_2 <= ||W||_F. Use [(Combettes, and Pesquet, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d6fff3a863fff27534ff25897e01f1026a0095a) operator framework and ReLU 1/2-averaged analysis to derive tighter bounds than a naive product of spectral norms, and include biases and normalization layers explicitly, noting that [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa) excluded normalization multipliers to avoid overtly violating the Lipschitz condition, so the plan's extension to final normalization learnable parameters needs a separate proof. Next, add empirical Lipschitz validation using the protocol in [(Ma et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bc9b799d50bf14f3b5b3af87a137a748be1854ef): compute isolated layer Lipschitz upper bounds and robust accuracy on MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, Caltech-101, and Imagenette, compare SWR to L2, L2 Init, S&P, and head reset, and report means over the plan's 5 or 3 seeds with confidence intervals. Then, to avoid the failure modes in [(Zhou et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bfb9ea4f70aef592c59817d70f69b832e605c773), include ablations of lambda, one-sided versus two-sided scaling, and hard spectral normalization baselines, measuring singular-value distributions and training stability as in [(Miyato et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/84de7d27e2f6160f634a483e8548c499a2cda7fa), and report training time because norm computations may add overhead. Finally, use [(Badings et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a39e0665a6e94174e024a9f5b7624e4d6de1d97b) and [(Fazlyab et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/89fc3d8af739f3c41ff72a1bd0acf0571a8c533e) weighted-norm or SDP verifiers on trained SWR networks to certify Lipschitz constants, and consider [(Anil et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f0ded4902d7f9c111e50047f8c9494effb7282d1) GroupSort or 1-Lipschitz activation variants as a controlled comparison for strict Lipschitz bounds.

## Context: To evaluate warm-start plasticity recovery against baselines, Method: 'We will follow the setup from prior work where models are first trained on 50% of training data for 100 epochs, then trained on the entire dataset for another 100 epochs. We will compare SWR against L2 regularization, L2 Init regularization, S&P re-initialization, and head reset methods.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e69e24316ca5a7db9725302da477c07214229e7), which directly studies warm-start plasticity recovery by training on a reduced noisy subset and then retraining on the full dataset for 100 epochs on CIFAR-10 with ResNet-18, CIFAR-100 with ViT-Tiny, and Tiny ImageNet with VGG-16, and compares fresh, warm-start, L2, data augmentation, spectral regularization, weight regeneration, ReDo, CReLU, head reset, shrink and perturb, and a combined L2 plus augmentation plus shrink and perturb condition; it reports that warm-starting preserves near-perfect training accuracy but lowers test accuracy, with the baselines positioned between the fresh and warm-start bounds, which supports the proposed 50%-then-full-data warm-start evaluation and the inclusion of L2, S&P, and head reset as relevant baselines, although its 10% subset and 50% label noise make the stress condition more severe than the proposed 50% subset. The next strongest support is [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61), which uses the same 50%-then-full-data protocol on CIFAR-10, SVHN, and CIFAR-100 with ResNet-18 or three-layer MLPs, SGD learning rate 0.001, batch sizes 16 to 128, learning rates 0.001 to 0.1, five conventional repetitions, no data augmentation or learning-rate schedules, and a Tesla P100, and evaluates shrink-and-perturb and last-layer-only variants, thereby validating the proposed baseline family and the importance of wall-clock time and convergence behavior, even though its negative results are treated as contradictions below. Quantitative support for reset-based recovery also comes from [(Nikishin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69b80ce5ab6c2263b145b1eaa23664145938f351), where final-layer reset in SPR on Atari 100k improved interquartile mean from 0.380 to 0.478, median from 0.433 to 0.512, and mean from 0.578 to 0.911 over 20 seeds per game, while full-network SAC reset on DeepMind Control Suite improved interquartile mean from 501 to 656 and DrQ last-three-layer reset improved it from 569 to 762 over 10 seeds per task, showing that selective or full re-initialization can restore plasticity when prior experience is retained, albeit in RL rather than supervised warm-starting. Support for L2 Init and S&P as baselines comes from [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c), which reports total average online accuracy around 0.83, 0.86, 0.96, 0.67, and 0.83 for L2 Init and around 0.81, 0.82, 0.93, 0.45, and 0.80 for S&P across Permuted MNIST, Random Label MNIST, Random Label CIFAR, 5+1 CIFAR, and Continual ImageNet, with Adam stepsizes 1e-3 and 1e-4 and regularization sweeps from 1e-2 to 1e-5. Additional, weaker support comes from [(Juliani, and Ash, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1fc35e72b66f13c42d5e52fb24bc525b71fea2d), which finds that L2, shrink+perturb, soft shrink+perturb, regenerative regularization, and ReDo mitigate plasticity loss and warm-start problems in PPO gridworld, CoinRun, and Montezuma's Revenge, from [(Sokar et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c282567e9c452f81416214933b9b1e45ab3add4), which qualitatively shows ReDo reduces dormant neurons and avoids replay-ratio collapse in DQN on 17 Atari games, and from [(Taha et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c315ee8546702037bef3276529838858c7a00ad), whose Knowledge Evolution partial-reset strategy conceptually supports preserving a fit-hypothesis while resetting the remainder, although it provides no quantitative outcomes in the supplied excerpt.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61), because in the directly relevant 50%-then-full-data warm-start setting, pure warm start generally underperforms random initialization, shrink-and-perturb noise did not fully close the gap, and head-reset-like variants did not reach random initialization: random-initialized models averaged 55.2% validation accuracy in 34.0 minutes, while warm-started noisy models achieved 54.4, 53.5, 52.9, 49.9, and 50.8% with training times of 165.3, 38.0, 16.5, 14.6, and 13.6 minutes; on CIFAR-10 last-layer-only reached 48.8% and last-layer-then-full reached 50.9% versus 52.5% pure warm start and 56.0% random, on SVHN 86.0% and 88.2% versus 87.5% and 89.4%, and on CIFAR-100 16.4% and 16.5% versus 15.5% and 18.2%, with batch-size and learning-rate sweeps showing no computational benefit when matching random accuracy. This directly challenges the assumption that S&P re-initialization and head reset will be strong recovery baselines in the proposed fixed 100-epoch warm-start experiment. The next strongest contradiction is [(Juliani, and Ash, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1fc35e72b66f13c42d5e52fb24bc525b71fea2d), which reports that final-layer reset fails to address plasticity loss in any of the permute, window, or expand distribution-shift conditions in PPO, that ReDo underperforms the warm-start baseline in the expand condition, that CReLU fails in the permute condition, and that plasticity injection underperforms the warm-start baseline in all three conditions, although the setting is on-policy RL rather than supervised warm-starting. A weaker but relevant contradiction is that [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e69e24316ca5a7db9725302da477c07214229e7) shows warm-starting impairs generalization and that L2, augmentation, head reset, shrink and perturb, and the combined condition remain between the fresh and warm-start bounds without reported full recovery, while its 10% subset and 50% label noise protocol does not validate the proposed 50% subset and 100-epoch stages. Additional caution comes from [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c), which shows that S&P benefits are highly dependent on regularization and stage count: in the no-regularization setting T around 5 to 20 is most beneficial, but in the fully regularized DCW setting increasing T monotonically lowers test accuracy, indicating that an untuned S&P baseline could be misleading. The weakest contradiction is that the related work does not provide a direct quantitative validation of the exact proposed protocol, especially because [(Taha et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c315ee8546702037bef3276529838858c7a00ad) reports no numerical outcomes and [(Sokar et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c282567e9c452f81416214933b9b1e45ab3add4) provides only qualitative reset and weight-decay comparisons, so their transfer to the proposed supervised warm-start setting remains uncertain.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add fresh non-warm-start/random initialization and pure warm-start baselines, and to report both fixed 100-epoch and convergence or extended-training results with wall-clock time and gradient steps, because [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61) and [(Lee et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6e69e24316ca5a7db9725302da477c07214229e7) use fresh networks as upper bounds and warm-started networks as lower bounds, and Ash shows that warm-started settings may require longer training and that fixed budgets can obscure whether a method recovers plasticity or merely spends more compute. The next most important action is to define and tune S&P and head reset precisely: reset at the 50% transition and optionally periodically, compare final-layer-only versus last-layer-then-full training, sweep shrink factor, noise scale, and stage count T, and keep gradient steps matched to standard training, guided by [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c), which finds T around 5 to 20 useful without regularization but harmful in DCW, and [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c), which sweeps S&P shrinkage and noise from 1e-2 to 1e-5. The method should also sweep L2 and L2 Init strengths over 1e-2 to 1e-5 and specify optimizer, stepsize, momentum, weight decay, data augmentation, and the exact initial distribution used for L2 Init, because [(Kumar et al., 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/39be2eab576bbe918da77e56f8e8771a0b1afb8c) uses Adam stepsizes 1e-3 and 1e-4 and regularization sweeps, while [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c) shows that S&P benefits change under data augmentation, cosine annealing, weight decay, and distillation. Additional actions include adding ablations for normalization layers and reset scope, because [(Taha et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c315ee8546702037bef3276529838858c7a00ad) transfers batch normalization parameters across generations and [(Juliani, and Ash, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1fc35e72b66f13c42d5e52fb24bc525b71fea2d) compares LayerNorm and final-layer reset; increasing seed counts and reporting bootstrap confidence intervals or significance tests, as in [(Nikishin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/69b80ce5ab6c2263b145b1eaa23664145938f351) with 20 seeds per game and 95% bootstrap intervals, would strengthen the statistical reliability of the proposed 5-seed and 3-seed VGG-16 plan; finally, the experiment should include ResNet-18 or justify omitting it, report layer-wise Frobenius norms, balancedness, Lipschitz estimates, training and test accuracy, and hardware details, because the prior direct warm-start work uses ResNet-18 and MLPs and the proposed SWR theory depends on weight-norm control.

## Context: To evaluate continual learning under full and limited data access, Method: 'We will implement two settings - full access where models can access all previous data chunks, and limited access where models can only access the current data chunk. Training data will be split into 10 chunks with 100 epochs per chunk to evaluate repeated warm-start scenarios.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 10-chunk, 100-epoch full/limited-access protocol comes from [(Lee, and Storkey, 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f15766d05d090bd1987cb8e92db5bd92c37e4c9), which directly tests sequential chunk training without resampling previous chunks on CIFAR-100 and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet18, uses a 10-chunk split and 100 epochs per chunk for Tiny ImageNet, and shows that chunking alone explains 50.05% of the offline-to-continual drop on CIFAR-100 and 46.69% on Tiny ImageNet, while DER++ and other continual-learning methods perform roughly like plain SGD and a 50-chunk diagnostic shows current-chunk training accuracy reaches 100% but test accuracy falls, indicating forgetting rather than underfitting. This directly validates the proposed limited-access condition and the need for a method that preserves learned information while maintaining plasticity. The next strongest support is [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61), which uses the same warm-start spirit as the plan's 50%-to-100% CIFAR-10 protocol, training ResNet-18 on 50% then 100% with batch sizes 16-128 and learning rates 0.001-0.1, and tests shrink-and-perturb, showing that pure warm-start can fail to beat random initialization and that controlled shrinkage/perturbation is needed, supporting SWR's norm-based soft rescaling over naive warm-start. [(Hsu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe111c477ea0ce9186eb58ff847052976d9a2dd9) supports the full/limited access contrast by comparing no-rehearsal sequential fine-tuning with naive rehearsal on Split and Permuted MNIST using MLPs with 400 or 1000 hidden units, 4 or 10 epochs per task, and fixed replay buffers of about 1.1k or 4k images, finding rehearsal much better than online EWC and SI and that regularization methods degrade over longer sequences, which motivates comparing full replay against current-chunk-only training and testing SWR's lambda robustness. [(Rebuffi et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1703631a938b397ba7e858161ce16448f6046d6f) supports the full-access upper bound and limited-access exemplar setting on iCIFAR-100 by showing iCaRL's exemplar store and distillation approach a nearest-class-mean full-data classifier, with memory budgets from 100 to 5000 prototypes, and that exemplars prevent forgetting, although distillation can hurt at very small class batch sizes. Earlier, [(Thrun, 1995-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/371c9dc680e916f79d9c78fcf6c894a2dd299095) provides foundational evidence that previous-object support data improves scarce current-task recognition, with transfer methods around 74-75% versus non-transfer around 60% under one current example and significant 95% differences, supporting the premise that access to previous chunks can help. [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c) supports the need for SWR by showing Shrink & Perturb and layer-wise re-initialization can improve generalization over standard training on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet with ResNet-18, PreAct-ResNet-18, and MobileNetV2, but also cause sudden test-accuracy drops at re-initialization and, in the fully regularized DCW setting, increasing stages monotonically lowers accuracy, which motivates a softer norm-based rescaling that preserves function. [(Lange et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4) supports the need to tune stability hyperparameters such as SWR's lambda in task-incremental multi-head settings on Tiny ImageNet, iNaturalist, and RecogSeq, where PackNet leads and MAS is robust, while weight decay and dropout help only for specific methods, indicating SWR must be compared against parameter isolation and replay. [(Kirkpatrick et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2e55ba6c97ce5eb55abd959909403fe8da7e9fe9), [(Aljundi et al., 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/713b0d9005944f80af00addc81b162ca74ea4b14), [(Aljundi et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9f08152f0793256b9b5b3a6fde5d9d91994cb70), [(Oswald et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/191461705e9961a3b07273c19d89eb214819002c), [(Chandra et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9992e204b47c94324a51d34d6863d7468caf9f3b), and [(Meshkinnejad et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be566882db45f7aed7d35fe3bb97cc46e1693bd7) collectively establish that no-rehearsal regularization, importance/output regularizers, hypernetworks, online sample selection, and small rehearsal memories can mitigate forgetting under limited access, but none directly implements the proposed 10-chunk, 100-epoch full/limited warm-start protocol or norm-based SWR. [(Cubuk et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f6a7c014ce206ac5b57299c07e10667d194b39) supports the need for data-access-dependent regularization strength by showing optimal augmentation magnitude increases with model and training-set size on CIFAR-10 Wide-ResNet, implying fixed lambda may be suboptimal across full and limited access. [(Tan, and Le, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f2eda8077dc7a69bb2b4e0a1a086cf054adb3f9), [(Javed, and White, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/475d35a691580d0082ecae212a81d9a4b6be787d), [(Ahn et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85639b961f9af904a9468a2063a053a6c0b55b42), [(Kim, and Lee, 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad14f2359c8a4bbae63fa3ad1a75f9718173c702), and [(Lee et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/57db7f24f15150ef7ea0db1fed20e6ee752792ec) provide weaker but relevant support: warm-start transfer works, meta-learned representations improve online continual learning, adaptive regularization can preserve task-specific filters, task-order robustness and interference metrics should be reported, and mean-field theory shows forgetting is non-monotonic in task similarity, all of which justify the proposed evaluation design.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed repeated warm-start assumption is [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61), which finds that warm-starting ResNet-18 from 50% to 100% CIFAR-10 gives no computational benefit and last-layer warm-starting is worse than random initialization, with 52.5 versus 56.0 on CIFAR-10, 87.5 versus 89.4 on SVHN, and 15.5 versus 18.2 on CIFAR-100, implying that 10 chunks of 100-epoch warm-starts may not improve performance unless SWR explicitly prevents inherited weights from becoming a poor initialization. [(Lee, and Storkey, 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f15766d05d090bd1987cb8e92db5bd92c37e4c9) contradicts the expectation that standard continual-learning methods or simple full/limited access will solve the problem: chunking alone causes a large drop, accounting for 50.05% of the offline-to-continual drop on CIFAR-100 and 46.69% on Tiny ImageNet, DER++ and other methods perform roughly like plain SGD, and replay with only 500 examples helps only for very small chunks, so SWR must demonstrate that it specifically reduces chunking-induced forgetting rather than merely regularizing weights. [(Hsu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe111c477ea0ce9186eb58ff847052976d9a2dd9) contradicts reliance on a simple regularization term by showing that EWC, SI, and other regularization methods continue to degrade under longer task sequences, require task-boundary information and hyperparameter tuning, and that Adam is generally poor for the sequential fine-tuning baseline, suggesting SWR's lambda and optimizer choice may be fragile. [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c) contradicts the idea that repeated weight manipulation is uniformly beneficial: in the fully regularized DCW setting, increasing re-initialization stages monotonically lowers test accuracy, and Shrink & Perturb causes sudden drops at each stage, so SWR must avoid over-constraining and abrupt functional disruption. [(Lange et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4) contradicts the sufficiency of generic weight regularization by showing that weight decay and dropout help only for specific methods and larger capacity, while PackNet and MAS lead in task-incremental multi-head settings, implying SWR must be compared against parameter isolation and importance-based methods. [(Rebuffi et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1703631a938b397ba7e858161ce16448f6046d6f) contradicts a fixed regularization strength by showing distillation can reduce accuracy relative to prototype-only classification at very small class batch sizes, and that memory budget strongly affects performance, so SWR's lambda may need to adapt to chunk size and access level. It also notes that nearest-class-mean requires storing all training data and is therefore not a valid class-incremental method, reminding that full access is an upper bound rather than a practical limited-access method. [(Cubuk et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f6a7c014ce206ac5b57299c07e10667d194b39) contradicts using a single fixed strength across data-access regimes by showing optimal augmentation magnitude changes with model size and training-set size, with smaller sets gaining about 3.0% versus larger sets about 1.5%, implying lambda_f and lambda_c should be swept or adapted. [(Mallya, and Lazebnik, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47bc048efb90e7b8bae5c1fcc979a78b65763fe9) contradicts robustness to task order and aggressive weight manipulation: Stanford Cars error rises from 16.00% when added first to 19.91% when added third, and 90% pruning with retraining causes severe degradation on Flowers with 98.36% error, so SWR should test task order and avoid hard pruning-like damage. [(Javed, and White, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/475d35a691580d0082ecae212a81d9a4b6be787d) provides a weaker contradiction by showing OML improves all tested continual-learning algorithms and that the difference between online and experience-replay methods is less pronounced with OML, suggesting representation learning may be more important than weight rescaling alone. [(Ahn et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/85639b961f9af904a9468a2063a053a6c0b55b42), [(Oswald et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/191461705e9961a3b07273c19d89eb214819002c), [(Chandra et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9992e204b47c94324a51d34d6863d7468caf9f3b), and [(Meshkinnejad et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be566882db45f7aed7d35fe3bb97cc46e1693bd7) provide limited contradictions because they show adaptive regularization, hypernetwork output regularization, and selective distillation can be effective under limited access, but they do not demonstrate that norm-based SWR is superior; their qualitative or approximate results, such as the almost 2% improvement for IWR in [(Chandra et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9992e204b47c94324a51d34d6863d7468caf9f3b), set a bar that SWR must exceed.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the 10-chunk, 100-epoch full/limited-access protocol exactly as a controlled repeated warm-start benchmark and report chunk-level diagnostics from [(Lee, and Storkey, 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f15766d05d090bd1987cb8e92db5bd92c37e4c9): training loss, current-chunk training accuracy, per-chunk test accuracy, average accuracy, forgetting, backward transfer, DOI/LA accuracy, and weight-norm growth, to show that SWR reduces forgetting rather than underfitting. The second most important action is to compare SWR against the baselines named in the plan and directly relevant prior work: plain warm-start, random initialization, L2, L2 Init, head reset, Shrink & Perturb, layer-wise re-initialization, and EWC/SI/MAS/iCaRL/DER++/nearest-class-mean, using the warm-start setup of [(Ash, and Adams, 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/538dbb608312b0a107cf98ccbaca2e0f563e2a61) and the re-initialization settings of [(Zaidi et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36156025ba019fb2d73b585a2b06f8c0c68ec34c), with 5 seeds for smaller models and 3 for VGG-16, confidence intervals, and significance tests. Third, sweep and possibly adapt lambda_f and lambda_c across full access, limited access, chunk sizes, and architectures, motivated by [(Cubuk et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f6a7c014ce206ac5b57299c07e10667d194b39), [(Rebuffi et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1703631a938b397ba7e858161ce16448f6046d6f), and [(Hsu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe111c477ea0ce9186eb58ff847052976d9a2dd9), including small-chunk and large-chunk regimes analogous to 1000, 5000, and 50000 examples, and report the lambda sensitivity curve. Fourth, include replay/exemplar baselines with explicit memory budgets, for example 500, 2000, and 5120 examples, and a full-data nearest-class-mean upper bound, following [(Hsu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe111c477ea0ce9186eb58ff847052976d9a2dd9), [(Rebuffi et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1703631a938b397ba7e858161ce16448f6046d6f), and [(Meshkinnejad et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/be566882db45f7aed7d35fe3bb97cc46e1693bd7), so that limited-access SWR is judged against the cost of storing previous chunks, and use representative sample selection or balanced replay to avoid recency bias shown in [(Aljundi et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9f08152f0793256b9b5b3a6fde5d9d91994cb70). Fifth, test task-order robustness and multiple datasets/models: Split/Permuted MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Tiny ImageNet, 3-layer MLP, CNN, CNN-BN, and VGG-16, with random task orders as in [(Kim, and Lee, 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad14f2359c8a4bbae63fa3ad1a75f9718173c702) and task-order effects as in [(Mallya, and Lazebnik, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47bc048efb90e7b8bae5c1fcc979a78b65763fe9), and report per-task accuracy and interference. Sixth, validate the proportionality claim empirically by measuring output cosine similarity, Lipschitz constants, entry-wise lp,q-norm ratios, and layer-wise norm balance, and ensure normalization-layer handling does not break function, especially for CNN-BN and VGG-16. Finally, document optimizer, learning rate, batch size, hardware, epoch count, and random seeds to make the 100-epoch repeated warm-start protocol reproducible, and report wall-clock time and FLOPs because full access over 10 chunks with 100 epochs is computationally heavier than limited access, as prior work such as [(Mallya, and Lazebnik, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47bc048efb90e7b8bae5c1fcc979a78b65763fe9) reports pruning/retraining about 1.5x longer than fine-tuning, because [(Hsu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe111c477ea0ce9186eb58ff847052976d9a2dd9) and [(Lange et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/90e04f3ae23ca7df5f59b11453341e3db943b6f4) show that optimizer and stability hyperparameters materially affect continual-learning outcomes.

## Context: To assess generalization and scheduler compatibility in standard training, Method: 'We will conduct standard supervised learning experiments training models for 200 epochs to assess generalization performance. We will also test compatibility with learning rate schedulers by implementing decay at specific epochs.'

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed 200-epoch single-task method comes from [(Li et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ff80b0786c4286c7477560339f97387f69bcfe6), which trains ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 on ILSVRC2012 for exactly 200 epochs with SGD momentum 0.9, weight decay 1e-4, initial learning rate 1e-3, and a cosine learning-rate schedule, showing that a 200-epoch supervised protocol with an explicit scheduler is a reproducible standard for image classification. This directly supports the plan's single-task experiment and suggests that SWR should be evaluated under the same 200-epoch horizon and reported with optimizer, weight decay, and schedule details. Next, [(Ge et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a55845aa040374d448e31ad47c4888f28b0f8787) supports testing decay at specific epochs by comparing step-decay and exponentially decaying schedules against polynomial decay on a 44-layer residual network for CIFAR-10, using grid search over initial step size eta0 and decay parameter b; this is closely aligned with the proposed scheduler-compatibility tests and implies that step decay is a legitimate baseline for 200-epoch generalization studies. [(Li et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36451fe94b7b1f978b8301cf3f7305566bd3b454) provides strong evidence that the epoch at which learning-rate annealing occurs changes generalization and feature learning, because WideResNet16 on CIFAR-10 with annealing at epoch 30 delayed memorization of an easy patch relative to a fixed small learning rate, and clean-image validation accuracy dropped from about 83% to about 70% when the model relied on the patch; this supports explicitly testing decay at specific epochs rather than assuming scheduler compatibility. [(Smith, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2fb6899e046e1f42792ba6f22b0877149062eb) supports scheduler-aware evaluation by using learning-rate range tests on resnet-56 CIFAR-10, where validation/test loss revealed overfitting for learning rates 0.01 to 0.04 before test accuracy or training loss did, and by recommending a 1cycle policy with minimum learning rate one tenth of the maximum, momentum values 0.99, 0.97, 0.95, and 0.9, and weight decay around one significant figure; although it does not use step decay at fixed epochs, it supports the proposed method's goal of assessing scheduler compatibility and generalization. [(Liu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f8b247b492d8a3e7cbdd8158a8b4a296497f45c) supports the broader SWR hypothesis by evaluating Weight ReScaling, a norm-based method that rescales weight matrices after optimizer updates rather than adding a penalty, and by comparing it with weight decay and Adam variants on image classification and object detection; this is conceptually the closest prior work to Soft Weight Rescaling and supports the idea that direct weight-norm control can improve generalization. [(Zhang et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba618ec05a9dbef75310c5e4bcce8a559e0270b5) supports the plan's extension to normalization layers by showing that L2 regularization and weight decay behave differently under preconditioned optimizers such as K-FAC, especially with batch normalization, and that weight decay can reduce effective damping and affect generalization; this indicates that SWR's interaction with normalization and optimizer dynamics should be measured. Weaker support comes from [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0), which uses a data-dependent initialization to make pre-activations zero-mean and unit variance by sampling v with standard deviation 0.05 and setting b and g from one minibatch, supporting the importance of controlling initial weight and feature scales but not training-time rescaling. The weakest support is [(Courbariaux et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5733ff08daff727af834345b9cfff1d0aa109ec), which shows that binary-weight regularization can act as a regularizer on permutation-invariant MNIST, CIFAR-10, and SVHN, but provides no 200-epoch protocol, scheduler details, or continuous weight-rescaling evidence. Taken as an iterative line, the 2015 binary regularization, 2016 initialization, 2018 learning-rate and weight-decay analyses, 2019 schedule comparisons, and 2021 norm-rescaling and 200-epoch protocols collectively support the proposed 200-epoch scheduler experiment, with the strongest direct evidence coming from the newer 2021 200-epoch protocol.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction or caution is from [(Li et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36451fe94b7b1f978b8301cf3f7305566bd3b454), which shows that a learning-rate annealing point at epoch 30 can change which features are learned first and can harm clean-data generalization; in the patch-augmented CIFAR-10 experiment, a fixed small learning rate learned the easy patch quickly and clean validation accuracy fell from about 83% to about 70%, while annealed or noise-assisted runs delayed patch memorization. This contradicts a simplistic assumption that adding decay at arbitrary specific epochs is automatically compatible with SWR or standard training, because the decay epoch can interact with weight-magnitude dynamics and feature selection. Next, [(Smith, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2fb6899e046e1f42792ba6f22b0877149062eb) suggests that a fixed step-decay test may be insufficient if the learning-rate path is not tuned; its 1cycle recipe and learning-rate range tests on resnet-56 CIFAR-10 show that validation/test loss can reveal overfitting in the 0.01 to 0.04 learning-rate range, and that maximum learning rate, minimum learning rate, momentum, batch size, and weight decay jointly affect generalization. This contradicts the proposed method if it only implements decay at specific epochs without comparing alternative schedules or validating the learning-rate range. [(Ge et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a55845aa040374d448e31ad47c4888f28b0f8787) also cautions that schedule choice matters, because it compares step-decay/exponential decay with polynomial decay on a 44-layer residual network for CIFAR-10 and selects eta0 and b by grid search; if the proposed method tests only one decay schedule or one decay epoch, it may not establish scheduler compatibility. [(Li et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ff80b0786c4286c7477560339f97387f69bcfe6) weakly contradicts the emphasis on step decay at specific epochs by using a 200-epoch cosine schedule with initial learning rate 1e-3, SGD momentum 0.9, and weight decay 1e-4, indicating that continuous decay is also a standard 200-epoch protocol and should not be omitted. [(Zhang et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba618ec05a9dbef75310c5e4bcce8a559e0270b5) contradicts the assumption that weight-magnitude regularization can be evaluated independently of optimizer and normalization details, because under K-FAC the distinction between L2 regularization and weight decay can substantially affect generalization, and weight decay can alter K-FAC damping, especially with batch normalization. [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0) provides a weaker caution that initialization-based scale control only fixes pre-activation statistics for the initialization minibatch, so a method that anchors scaling to initial norms, such as SWR, may still face later statistical drift if not monitored during training. The weakest contradiction is from [(Courbariaux et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5733ff08daff727af834345b9cfff1d0aa109ec), which supports weight regularization but does not provide evidence that continuous soft rescaling is necessary or that binary regularization results transfer to the proposed 200-epoch scheduler experiments. As an iterative contrast, the newer 2019 and 2021 scheduler and norm-rescaling work complicates the older 2015 and 2016 regularization and initialization findings by showing that schedule timing, optimizer, and training-time weight-norm control can change generalization outcomes.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed 200-epoch single-task experiment should define explicit scheduler baselines and decay points rather than only saying decay at specific epochs: include step decay at common checkpoints (for example epochs 100 and 150 for a 200-epoch run, and also epoch 30 as a sensitivity point motivated by [(Li et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36451fe94b7b1f978b8301cf3f7305566bd3b454)), cosine decay as in [(Li et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ff80b0786c4286c7477560339f97387f69bcfe6), and exponential/polynomial or 1cycle schedules as motivated by [(Ge et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a55845aa040374d448e31ad47c4888f28b0f8787) and [(Smith, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2fb6899e046e1f42792ba6f22b0877149062eb). This will directly test SWR compatibility with the plan's standard supervised learning scenario and prevent conflating scheduler effects with SWR effects. Second, use validation/test loss and learning-rate range tests to choose and justify learning rates, following [(Smith, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2fb6899e046e1f42792ba6f22b0877149062eb), by reporting training loss, validation accuracy, validation loss, and generalization gap, and by testing momentum values such as 0.99, 0.97, 0.95, and 0.9 and weight decay around one significant figure where relevant. Third, add diagnostics for scheduler-induced feature bias and weight growth, including clean-subset validation accuracy if using patch or easy-feature controls from [(Li et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36451fe94b7b1f978b8301cf3f7305566bd3b454), layer-wise Frobenius norms, initial-to-current norm ratios, EMA scaling factors cl, entry-wise lp,q-norm balancedness, and weight-norm trajectories, because the research plan targets unbounded weight growth and plasticity. Fourth, compare SWR against weight decay, L2 regularization, L2 Init, S&P re-initialization, head reset, and Weight ReScaling from [(Liu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f8b247b492d8a3e7cbdd8158a8b4a296497f45c), and include optimizer/normalization ablations such as SGD, Adam/AdamW, and batch normalization, because [(Zhang et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ba618ec05a9dbef75310c5e4bcce8a559e0270b5) shows that weight-decay and L2 behavior can differ under preconditioned optimizers and BN. Fifth, report full reproducibility details for the 200-epoch protocol, including dataset splits, architecture, batch size, optimizer, momentum, weight decay, initial learning rate, decay epochs, random seeds (5 for smaller models and 3 for VGG-16 as planned), hardware, and statistical significance, using the level of detail in [(Li et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1ff80b0786c4286c7477560339f97387f69bcfe6) and [(Smith, 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9f2fb6899e046e1f42792ba6f22b0877149062eb). Sixth, calibrate initial weight norms and normalization parameters using data-dependent initialization ideas from [(Salimans, and Kingma, 2016-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d2c6941a9b4608ba52b328369a3352db2092ae0) so that the SWR target norm and EMA are not confounded by poor initialization. Finally, include a hard-rescaling or binary-regularization baseline only as a secondary comparison, since [(Liu et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f8b247b492d8a3e7cbdd8158a8b4a296497f45c) and [(Courbariaux et al., 2015-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5733ff08daff727af834345b9cfff1d0aa109ec) provide weaker but useful context for weight-magnitude regularization.

## Context: To test SWR on standard benchmarks, Method: 'We will use MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet datasets with various architectures including 3-layer MLP, CNN with 2 convolutional and 2 fully connected layers, CNN with batch normalization (CNN-BN), and VGG-16.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed benchmark matrix comes from [(Huang et al., 2017-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d80d5862f5406a46225e91a71bb046eda451c430), which shows that direct weight-norm control can improve supervised image classification without prohibitive cost. On SVHN with WRN-16-4, PBWN-Epoch achieved a test error of 1.607% with a standard deviation of 0.005%, improving over the reproduced WRN baseline of 1.644% with a standard deviation of 0.046% and over reported baselines of 1.92% for DSN, 1.75% for RSD, 1.69% for GPF, and 1.64% for the original WRN. This supports the plan's use of test accuracy on image classification benchmarks to evaluate SWR, although the detailed quantitative evidence is limited to SVHN rather than the proposed MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet. A secondary but important support is the efficiency analysis on Inception, VGG, and a 110-layer residual network, where PBWN-Epoch had wall-clock training time almost the same as normal training and PBWN required little extra time, suggesting that less frequent norm manipulation is computationally practical and relevant to SWR's per-step scaling and to the need to report overhead for VGG-16 and CNN-BN. Further support is the experimental setup: Torch implementation, one Tesla K80 GPU, SGD with momentum 0.9, dampening 0, weight decay 0.0005, mini-batch size 128, initial learning rate 0.01, learning-rate decay by 0.1 at epochs 80 and 120, 160 epochs, dropout 0.4, and input images scaled to 0 to 1, with SVHN containing 73,257 training images, 26,032 test images, and 531,131 augmented training images; these details provide a reproducible template for the proposed 200-epoch single-task and warm-start or continual experiments. The weakest support is the inclusion of MNIST in the paper's semi-supervised setting, which indicates MNIST is a viable small benchmark but does not provide supervised accuracy evidence for the proposed 3-layer MLP or CNN-BN.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that PBWN is a hard unit-norm projection method, not a soft Frobenius-norm rescaling toward initial norms, so it does not validate SWR's central proportionality-preserving mechanism. PBWN projects weights after optimization updates to unit-norm manifolds, whereas SWR uses c_l = (lambda times ||W_init_l|| + (1-lambda) times ||W_l||) / ||W_l|| and aims to preserve functional behavior; hard projection may alter outputs more and may require different normalization-layer handling. This mismatch means the positive SVHN result cannot be directly transferred to SWR. A second contradiction is the limited dataset and architecture coverage: the paper reports detailed accuracy only for SVHN with WRN-16-4, while the proposed method requires MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet with a 3-layer MLP, a CNN with 2 convolutional and 2 fully connected layers, CNN-BN, and VGG-16; the absence of reported detailed results for CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet, or MNIST and the use of WRN rather than VGG-16 or MLP leaves the proposed benchmark matrix unsupported. A third contradiction is that PBWN does not evaluate plasticity loss, warm-start learning, continual learning, or repeated warm-start scenarios, so it provides no evidence that norm control prevents unbounded weight growth while preserving learned information. A fourth, weaker contradiction is that PBWN-Riem required non-ignorable extra time and PBWN-Epoch was used because of computational cost on large datasets, implying that frequent norm operations can be expensive; although SWR's scaling may be cheaper than projection, the related work warns that per-step or per-epoch weight manipulation must be measured on the proposed architectures, especially VGG-16.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add PBWN and PBWN-Epoch as explicit baselines or ablations on the same MNIST, CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and TinyImageNet datasets and on the 3-layer MLP, 2-convolutional/2-fully-connected CNN, CNN-BN, and VGG-16 architectures, reporting test accuracy, standard deviations, and significance tests to isolate whether SWR's soft initial-norm scaling outperforms hard unit-norm projection. Second, the plan should report full results for every proposed dataset and architecture, including dataset sizes, batch size, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, dropout, weight decay, number of epochs, random seeds, and wall-clock overhead, using the PBWN paper's hyperparameters as a reference baseline where applicable: SGD momentum 0.9, weight decay 0.0005, batch size 128, initial learning rate 0.01 with decay 0.1 at epochs 80 and 120, dropout 0.4, and input scaling. Third, because PBWN does not address plasticity, the experiments should include warm-start and continual learning metrics, entry-wise l_p,q-norm ratios, weight-growth trajectories, and comparisons to L2 regularization, L2 Init regularization, S&P re-initialization, and head reset, to show that SWR preserves learned function while bounding norms. Fourth, the method should explicitly validate proportionality and normalization-layer handling by measuring output or loss changes before and after rescaling, especially for CNN-BN and VGG-16, and should consider per-epoch or adaptive SWR if per-step overhead is non-negligible, mirroring PBWN-Epoch's practicality. Finally, include computational resource details such as GPU model and training time, since PBWN's efficiency conclusions were based on one Tesla K80 and specific architectures, and the proposed VGG-16 experiments may have different overhead.

## Context: To quantify performance and statistical reliability, Method: 'We will measure test accuracy across all scenarios and assess model balancedness using entry-wise ℓp,q-norm ratios. We will perform hyperparameter sweeps for λ values and use multiple random seeds (5 for smaller models, 3 for VGG-16) to ensure statistical reliability.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation method comes from [(Kim et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/163b61ea53685ab9ec60c6818bd4f289e0060dac), which defines a continual-learning protocol using final average accuracy A_T and final average forgetting F_T over task-specific class sets and one-hot labels, and reports means and standard deviations across multiple random seeds with seed counts specified per experiment. This directly supports the plan's decision to measure test accuracy and use multiple random seeds, although it does not support entry-wise lp,q-norm balancedness or lambda sweeps. The next strongest support is [(Lindsey, and Litwin-Kumar, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6271880cc1d7ff6514672366fe124fdb1212fb2), whose local Oja-style rule w <- w + alpha(ab - b^2 w) and meta-learned alpha reduce uncontrolled weight growth and cross-task interference in nine-layer fully connected and six-convolutional-plus-two-fully-connected networks, with 400 examples per sinusoid and 25 examples per Omniglot class, averaged over two regression and five classification networks. This supports the broader hypothesis that weight-magnitude control can preserve plasticity and reduce forgetting, but it does not use global SWR, lambda hyperparameters, or entry-wise norm-ratio metrics. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Lee et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3649b3f2a2e74ec98c77c6cd0fe5fb2afc7515e9), which handles continual learning with normalization layers by reinitializing batch-normalization gamma and beta with source and target population statistics, using batch renormalization with r_max and d_max relaxed from 1/0 to 3/5, and selecting lambda_s, lambda_t, learning rate, and damping via target validation on ResNet-50 over CUB, Stanford Cars, and Flowers in six task orders with SGD momentum 0.9, batch size 32, and 100 epochs. This supports the need to treat normalization layers carefully and to use target-validation-based hyperparameter selection, but it provides no quantitative outcomes and does not evaluate SWR or balancedness.

### Contradictions

The strongest apparent contradiction is not a direct refutation but a competing mechanism in [(Lee et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3649b3f2a2e74ec98c77c6cd0fe5fb2afc7515e9): it addresses continual learning with normalization layers through extended K-FAC, merged weights, and batch renormalization rather than global weight rescaling, and it sets importance coefficients lambda_s and lambda_t analytically from the number of learned tasks while selecting learning rate and damping using target-task validation only. This suggests that the proposed broad lambda sweeps and direct SWR scaling may be less necessary than a second-order penalty plus normalization alignment, although the excerpt reports no test accuracy, source/target accuracy, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance, so the contradiction remains weak. The next tension comes from [(Lindsey, and Litwin-Kumar, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6271880cc1d7ff6514672366fe124fdb1212fb2), which shows that a local negative term proportional to b^2 w can stabilize weights and reduce the effect of updates on previously learned sinusoid outputs without global layer-wise rescaling, lambda-based scaling factors, or entry-wise norm-ratio balancedness; however, its results are qualitative, use small continual regression and Omniglot tasks, and average only two or five meta-trained networks, so it does not directly contradict SWR on image classification benchmarks. The weakest contradiction is from [(Kim et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/163b61ea53685ab9ec60c6818bd4f289e0060dac), which evaluates masked softmax rather than weight magnitude balancing and provides no numerical outcomes, implying that output-layer masking may be an alternative continual-learning strategy without SWR, but it does not test weight growth, lambda sensitivity, or balancedness.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to strengthen the evaluation protocol by adopting the continual-learning metrics from [(Kim et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/163b61ea53685ab9ec60c6818bd4f289e0060dac): report final average accuracy A_T and final average forgetting F_T in addition to test accuracy, provide per-task accuracy, means and standard deviations, specify the exact number of seeds for each experiment, and add confidence intervals or paired significance tests; the plan's use of 5 seeds for smaller models and 3 seeds for VGG-16 should be justified by variance estimates or increased if VGG-16 results are high-variance. A second priority is to make the entry-wise lp,q-norm balancedness metric operational by defining p and q, reporting per-layer and aggregate ratios, explaining how normalization layers are included, and correlating balancedness with A_T, F_T, and weight-norm trajectories. Next, the normalization-layer extension should be revised in light of [(Lee et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3649b3f2a2e74ec98c77c6cd0fe5fb2afc7515e9) by handling intermediate batch-normalization layers, not only the final one, through population-statistic alignment of gamma and beta or batch renormalization, and by evaluating CNN-BN and VGG-16 across multiple task orders with target-validation-based selection of lambda_c, lambda_f, and the EMA lambda. The lambda sweeps should also include task-count-informed priors and a clear selection criterion that avoids using source-task data, with sensitivity curves rather than a single chosen value. Finally, the plan should add plasticity and interference diagnostics inspired by [(Lindsey, and Litwin-Kumar, 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f6271880cc1d7ff6514672366fe124fdb1212fb2), such as measuring the effect of updates on previous-task outputs, comparing SWR against L2, L2 Init, S&P, head reset, and a local weight-stabilization baseline, and documenting hardware, batch sizes, learning-rate schedules, data splits, buffer sizes, and computational cost to ensure reproducibility.
