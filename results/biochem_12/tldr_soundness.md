## Strengths 
The strongest support is for using solution-state NMR to study S. aureus peptidoglycan-derived substrates and LSS/LytU-family catalytic domains, because NMR titration with soluble S. aureus peptidoglycan fragments and muropeptides can produce interpretable chemical-shift perturbations and structural models [(Maya-Martinez et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dea1a5f980021848949d211de2bf83f567c), and NMR characterization of purified LSS and LytU supports NMR-based analysis of catalytic-domain conformation, zinc binding, and pentaglycine hydrolysis [(Tossavainen et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1be5c05128f7f58decad25a91cf6789f6065d13) [(Raulinaitis et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e54bf104aab72adefcfe58a8e56b2263c19b147f). The use of pentaglycine as a minimal substrate and living-cell turbidity reduction as orthogonal validation is also supported by lysostaphin activity studies [(Mitkowski et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/469b18dd3e5e7eb317e4872a3be7d7e62d7c5c49).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the proposal's reliance on catalytic-domain-only kinetics and a fixed standardized NMR kinetic protocol. LSS CWT domain has higher stem-peptide affinity and LSS domains do not interact in vitro, so catalytic-domain-only assays may misassign specificity for complex substrates [(Tossavainen et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1be5c05128f7f58decad25a91cf6789f6065d13). Second zinc binding can inhibit LSS and LytU in a pH-dependent manner, so uncontrolled zinc, pH, or buffer conditions may make rates and product ratios condition-dependent rather than intrinsic [(Tossavainen et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1be5c05128f7f58decad25a91cf6789f6065d13) [(Raulinaitis et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e54bf104aab72adefcfe58a8e56b2263c19b147f). Synthetic stem-peptide or short pentaglycine ligands may not recapitulate native peptidoglycan recognition, as PBP4 recognized larger soluble peptidoglycan fragments but not high-molecular-weight peptidoglycan or synthetic stem peptides [(Maya-Martinez et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dea1a5f980021848949d211de2bf83f567c), and SH3b recognition may also affect apparent lysostaphin activity [(Mitkowski et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/469b18dd3e5e7eb317e4872a3be7d7e62d7c5c49).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Prevalidate the isotopic symmetry-breaking NMR assignment before kinetic studies by preparing selectively 13C-labeled pentaglycine, including Gly2 Cα and CO labeling plus synthetic cleavage-product standards, and acquiring 1H-13C HSQC, HMBC, and glycine-optimized HA(CA)CO spectra at 800 MHz and 25°C to confirm the proposed C-terminal 179.4 ppm and non-terminal 173.8 ppm 13CO assignments, with replicate integrals and error estimates [(Maya-Martinez et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dea1a5f980021848949d211de2bf83f567c).

Add full-length LSS/LytM or CWT/SH3b-containing constructs and metal/pH controls to the kinetic panel, comparing catalytic-domain-only versus full-length constructs for peptides 1-7 under 0.4 mM substrate, 2 and 50 μM enzyme, 25°C, 800 MHz, 20-s recycle, and 0.1 mM DSS, while explicitly testing zinc-free, zinc-saturated, chelator, and pH-rate conditions to prevent zinc-dependent inhibition from confounding kcat/Km and product ratios [(Tossavainen et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f1be5c05128f7f58decad25a91cf6789f6065d13) [(Raulinaitis et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e54bf104aab72adefcfe58a8e56b2263c19b147f) [(Mitkowski et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/469b18dd3e5e7eb317e4872a3be7d7e62d7c5c49).

Strengthen product identification and biological relevance by adding orthogonal LC-MS or native MS detection of hydrolysis products, inactive-enzyme and non-hydrolytic controls, and USA300 sacculus muropeptide or larger soluble peptidoglycan fragment comparisons, then correlate NMR product ratios with 16-h OD600 turbidity reduction at 50 μg/mL enzyme to ensure synthetic peptide findings reflect native S. aureus peptidoglycan cleavage [(Maya-Martinez et al., 2019-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/af8a8dea1a5f980021848949d211de2bf83f567c) [(Mitkowski et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/469b18dd3e5e7eb317e4872a3be7d7e62d7c5c49).
