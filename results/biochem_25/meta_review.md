## To test whether the nucleus has a unique protein quality control surveillance mechanism, the proposal states: We will use engineered destabilizing domains (DDs) as molecular tools to create compartment-specific unfolded protein stress.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Miyazaki et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/700fb459fbe0b33f23713befdb1f424d7c97c524) demonstrates a Shield-1-dependent destabilizing-domain construct, AgDD, can be switched from a controlled state to a defined protein stress state by ligand withdrawal, which is the same conditional logic used in the proposed NucDD, CytoDD, and ntDD experiments. This supports the feasibility of using DDs as inducible molecular tools in mammalian cells and suggests that Shield-1 withdrawal can produce a measurable cellular phenotype. A weaker but still relevant support is that the paper used aggregate quantification and bioinformatic comparison to known aggregates, indicating that DD-induced protein stress can be characterized against established PQC phenotypes; this is conceptually useful for the proposed RNA-seq, pathway enrichment, and comparison of nuclear versus cytosolic unfolded protein responses, although the related work did not test compartment-specific localization, p53 dependence, or the 0-240 minute time course.

### Contradictions

The strongest potential contradiction is that the related work reports AgDD withdrawal leading to aggregate formation rather than simply a transient pool of unfolded protein destined for proteasomal degradation. If the proposed DD-sGFP constructs similarly form aggregates after Shield-1 withdrawal, the experiment may measure aggregate-specific stress, proteasome sequestration, or clearance of insoluble material rather than the intended compartment-specific unfolded protein stress, which could confound the interpretation of nuclear versus cytosolic PQC and p53 activation. A weaker contradiction is the absence of quantitative details in the supplied excerpt, including cell type, construct sequence, Shield-1 concentration, withdrawal duration, expression level, degradation kinetics, imaging or flow cytometry parameters, statistical tests, and significance values; therefore it cannot be used to benchmark whether DD withdrawal produces a rapid, reversible, and proteasome-dependent unfolded protein signal in the time window planned for the NIH3T3 RNA-seq and degron reporter assays.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to verify that each DD-sGFP variant behaves as a soluble, proteasome-degraded unfolded protein rather than an aggregate in the intended compartment before testing transcriptional or p53 responses; this should include time-resolved fluorescence microscopy and flow cytometry to confirm NLS/NES localization, expression level, and degradation kinetics, plus aggregate detection such as native PAGE, size-exclusion or detergent-insoluble fractionation, and comparison with proteasome inhibitors to distinguish proteasome overload from aggregate stress. The next priority is to optimize the DD variant, expression level, Shield-1 concentration, and withdrawal timing so that the 0-240 minute window captures reversible unfolded protein stress, and to include non-targeted, proteasome-inhibited, and p53 knockout controls in RNA-seq and degron reporter experiments. Finally, the plan should add explicit statistical and bioinformatic benchmarks, such as differential expression with replicates, clustering, pathway enrichment, and comparison to known UPR, heat shock, and tunicamycin signatures, while using the related work only as a proof-of-concept for Shield-1-controlled DD stress rather than as quantitative evidence for nuclear-specific p53 surveillance.

## To identify common and distinct transcriptional programs, the proposal states: We will use bioinformatics approaches including differential gene expression analysis, clustering methods, and pathway enrichment analysis to identify common and distinct response features.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Love et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/817fc8de25f989b8229847fc7710750f273460b) describes a negative-binomial generalized linear model pipeline, DESeq2, that is directly applicable to the proposal's RNA-seq differential expression analysis of raw integer counts from nuclear, cytosolic, and non-targeted DD time-course experiments. It uses a design matrix encoding experimental factors, which can be extended to condition, time, and condition-by-time interactions for the 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 minute timepoints, and it provides Wald tests or likelihood-ratio tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR control, matching the need to identify commonly and distinctly regulated genes. Because only one related work is provided, the comparative evidence is limited to its internal benchmarks against DESeq, edgeR, and Cuffdiff rather than a series of newer studies, but those benchmarks indicate that the method controlled FDR at the nominal 5% level and outperformed DESeq, edgeR, and Cuffdiff in sensitivity and precision in simulations, with lower false-positive rates and higher concordance with microarray-defined differential genes; although the excerpt lacks numeric values, the simulation setup of 10,000 genes, 4 conditions, 3 replicates, 10% differential genes, and fold changes between 2 and 10 is reasonably analogous to a small-to-moderate RNA-seq experiment with biological replicates. A secondary support is that DESeq2's regularized log transformation can stabilize variance and generate transformed count matrices suitable for the proposal's clustering methods, PCA, heatmaps, and visualization of temporal response patterns. A weaker but still relevant support is that the method's optional minimum or maximum effect-size thresholds and independent filtering can help distinguish biologically meaningful nuclear versus cytosolic transcriptional programs from low-count noise, although the provided work does not test these choices in p53 knockout, proteasome reporter, or pathway enrichment contexts.

### Contradictions

The strongest limitation is that the related work does not support the pathway enrichment analysis component of the proposed method; [(Love et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/817fc8de25f989b8229847fc7710750f273460b) is a differential expression method and explicitly does not include pathway enrichment, so the proposal's claim to use pathway enrichment to identify common and distinct response features remains unsupported by this evidence. The next strongest contradiction is that the method does not address the proposal's p53 knockout, proteasome reporter, or compartment-specific PQC questions; it was benchmarked on generic RNA-seq condition comparisons and does not provide evidence that DESeq2 can detect p53-dependent nuclear unfolded protein response genes, proteasome competition targets, or differences between nuclear, cytosolic, and non-targeted DD stress in mouse NIH3T3 fibroblasts. A further limitation is that the benchmark context is not identical to the proposed design: the simulation used 10,000 genes, 4 conditions, 3 replicates, 10% differential genes, and fold changes between 2 and 10, whereas the proposal involves three DD conditions across six timepoints, potentially many more contrasts, and a rapid 0-240 minute response where low counts, batch effects, and multiple testing across timepoints may be more challenging; the excerpt also lacks numeric sensitivity, precision, p-values, fold changes, or gene counts, so the strength of the benchmark cannot be fully quantified. The weakest contradiction is that median-of-ratios normalization and empirical Bayes dispersion shrinkage assume a sufficiently stable majority of genes, which may be stressed by a global unfolded protein response, but the related work does not provide evidence that this assumption fails in the proposed system.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify the differential expression pipeline in detail, using a DESeq2-style negative-binomial GLM with a design matrix such as condition + time + condition:time, likelihood-ratio tests for joint time-course effects, Wald tests for pairwise nuclear versus cytosolic or DD versus non-targeted contrasts, Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at 5%, and explicit effect-size thresholds, as supported by [(Love et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/817fc8de25f989b8229847fc7710750f273460b). The next most important action is to add a separate pathway enrichment analysis plan, because the related work does not cover it; this should name methods such as gene set enrichment analysis or over-representation analysis against GO, KEGG, Reactome, or p53 pathway gene sets, with multiple-testing correction and validation by RT-qPCR. A further action is to define the clustering workflow using DESeq2's regularized log or variance-stabilizing transformation, followed by PCA, hierarchical or k-means clustering, and stability metrics, to operationalize the proposal's clustering methods. Additional actions include incorporating p53 knockout samples into the same RNA-seq design or a parallel design to test p53-dependent genes, adding proteasome reporter fluorescence analysis as a separate quantitative assay, and reporting normalization diagnostics, low-count filtering, batch correction, sample size, and statistical power to ensure reproducibility in the 0-240 minute time-course.

## To compare proteasome effects within the same versus different compartments, the proposal states: We will also create compartment-specific versions of degron reporters to assess how localized unfolded protein affects proteasome capacity within the same versus different cellular compartments.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed compartment-specific degron-reporter method comes from Beer et al., who used degron-tagged fluorescent reporter constructs to infer ubiquitin/proteasome-mediated degradation from fluorescence intensity, the same core readout proposed for monitoring proteasome capacity after Shield-1 withdrawal [(Beer et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b773b974d4ab13b96856205fe70133284f5e0198). A further, but more indirect, support is that Beer et al. showed membrane topology, organelle association, and ubiquitin ligase adaptor localization determine degron accessibility and degradation, which supports the rationale for making NLS-, NES-, and non-targeted degron reporters to compare same-compartment versus different-compartment effects. A weaker support is that Beer et al. reported that degron-tagged reporters do not degrade binding partners, which is consistent with the plan to co-express fluorescent degron reporters with DD constructs without assuming that the reporter will necessarily perturb the DD fusion, although this was not tested in the proposed NIH3T3 retroviral system.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that Beer et al. found that single membrane organelles protect degrons from degradation and that ubiquitin ligase adaptor localization determines degradation, which means compartment-specific reporter accumulation may reflect membrane topology, organelle protection, or ligase/adaptor accessibility rather than proteasome capacity itself; this directly challenges the plan's assumption that fluorescent degron reporter levels after Shield-1 withdrawal can be interpreted primarily as proteasome capacity in the nucleus versus cytosol. A second contradiction is that Beer et al. did not test nuclear versus cytosolic proteasome capacity, did not use Shield-1-dependent destabilizing domains, and did not provide quantitative fold changes, fluorescence intensity values, sample sizes, or statistical significance, so the provided evidence does not directly support the proposed specific comparison or its quantitative time-course design. A weaker contradiction is that the Beer et al. system used pIRES-hygro3 plasmids, light microscopy, phagocytosed cargo/corpse membrane breakdown contexts, and qualitative outcomes, which differs from the proposed stable retroviral NIH3T3 fibroblasts, flow cytometry, 0-240 minute timepoints, and RNA-seq-based transcriptional readout, limiting direct transferability.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, before using compartment-specific degron reporters to infer proteasome capacity, the plan should validate NLS, NES, and non-targeted reporter localization, expression levels, and baseline degradation kinetics in NIH3T3 cells, and include controls that distinguish proteasome capacity from ubiquitin ligase/adaptor localization and membrane/organelle protection, as highlighted by Beer et al. [(Beer et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b773b974d4ab13b96856205fe70133284f5e0198). Second, the method should use orthogonal reporters and perturbations, including both ubiquitin-dependent and ubiquitin-independent degron reporters, proteasome inhibitors such as MG132 or bortezomib, and nuclear/cytosolic fractionation or proteasome activity assays, to show that changes in reporter fluorescence track proteasome capacity rather than compartment-specific ubiquitination or accessibility. Third, the readout should be quantified with flow cytometry and/or microscopy, normalized to reporter expression and DD expression, measured at the proposed 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 minute timepoints, with biological replicates and explicit statistical tests, mirroring the fluorescence-intensity and statistical-evaluation approach in Beer et al. but adding the quantitative metrics absent from that paper. Finally, the plan should include negative controls for spectral overlap, nuclear envelope/membrane topology effects, and potential reporter interference with DD or binding partners, and pilot the Shield-1 withdrawal kinetics to ensure that the 0-240 minute window captures comparable degradation across NucDD, CytoDD, and ntDD constructs.

## To test whether p53 is required for the transcriptional response, the proposal states: We will also generate p53 knockout cell lines using CRISPR to test the requirement for p53 in observed transcriptional responses.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Ihry et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7b90250a4fc1ed8be4241a34709186d5a525d1e), which directly uses CRISPR/Cas9-mediated TP53 knockout in H1 human pluripotent stem cells to test p53 requirement in a defined stress response. The study introduced LentiCRISPR sgRNAs into H1-iCas9 cells, isolated clones, verified editing by CRISPR indel analysis from genomic DNA and protein-level assays, induced Cas9 double-strand breaks with doxycycline, and measured the transcriptional response by RNA-seq and qPCR, with viability assessed by live imaging of confluency, FACS, and microscopy. It reported that TP53 knockout reduced or abolished Cas9 double-strand break-induced p53 target gene induction and decreased Cas9-induced cell death, while non-p53 double-strand break response genes were less affected. This supports the proposed CRISPR p53 knockout in mouse NIH3T3 fibroblasts as a valid logic for testing p53 dependence, although the stress is Cas9 DSB rather than Shield-1 withdrawal of DD-GFP. The older work by [(Kirschner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e7d1dbb4f2647152acdc21f93a616ce04e8cfb5) provides weaker but complementary support: it used a p53-inducible H1299 p53-deficient human lung carcinoma line, acute and chronic p53 induction, microarray, ChIP-seq, qRT-PCR, motif analysis, recursive partitioning, luciferase, immunoblotting, proliferation, SA-β-galactosidase, and viability assays, and found phenotype-specific p53-responsive expression, distinct acute versus chronic binding profiles, chronic self-regulatory hubs, and p53 repression of SCD in senescence, apoptosis, and cancer contexts. As an iterative sequence, Kirschner et al. established that p53-dependent transcriptional outputs can be assessed in a p53-deficient or p53-controllable background, and Ihry et al. later strengthened the methodological precedent by replacing inducible p53-null logic with CRISPR knockout and RNA-seq validation in a defined stress response.

### Contradictions

No direct contradiction is reported, but the strongest caveat is that neither study directly tests the proposed nuclear versus cytosolic DD unfolded protein stress in mouse NIH3T3 fibroblasts, so they do not prove that p53 knockout will abolish the specific 0-240 minute transcriptional response expected from Shield-1 withdrawal. The closest study, [(Ihry et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7b90250a4fc1ed8be4241a34709186d5a525d1e), uses Cas9 double-strand breaks in H1 human pluripotent stem cells and reports only qualitative reduction or abolition of the p53-dependent program without fold changes, differentially expressed gene counts, p-values, sequencing depth, statistical thresholds, or computational resources in the supplied excerpt; therefore the magnitude, significance, and reproducibility of p53 dependence for a proteasome-competition stress remain unquantified. It also shows that non-p53 double-strand break response genes were less affected, which contradicts any assumption that p53 knockout will eliminate all stress-induced transcriptional changes and suggests the proposal should expect a p53-dependent subset rather than a complete loss of response. The older study, [(Kirschner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e7d1dbb4f2647152acdc21f93a616ce04e8cfb5), is a weaker contradiction because it uses an inducible p53-null H1299 system rather than CRISPR, microarray rather than RNA-seq, and acute versus chronic p53 conditions with phenotype-specific outputs; its qualitative findings, including distinct binding profiles and self-regulatory hubs in chronic p53, indicate that p53 responses are context- and duration-dependent, so a 0-240 minute acute DD stress may not capture all p53-dependent programs. Neither paper specifies induction reagent concentration, time course, array platform, sequencing depth, statistical thresholds, hardware, or sample size, limiting direct methodological transfer to the proposed RNA-seq and CRISPR design.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to adopt the workflow from [(Ihry et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7b90250a4fc1ed8be4241a34709186d5a525d1e) as the core of the p53 knockout arm: generate multiple independent NIH3T3 TP53 knockout clones using at least two sgRNAs, verify editing by indel analysis or targeted sequencing, confirm loss of p53 protein by immunoblot and loss of p53 mRNA by qPCR, and include wild-type, empty-vector, and, if feasible, inducible p53 rescue controls to distinguish p53-specific effects from CRISPR or clonal artifacts. All knockout and wild-type lines should express the same NucDD, CytoDD, and ntDD constructs at matched levels, with localization confirmed by fluorescence microscopy and degradation kinetics by flow cytometry before Shield-1 withdrawal. For the transcriptional experiment, run biological replicates at 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 minutes, specify RNA-seq depth and alignment/DE pipeline, use FDR-controlled differential expression, clustering, and pathway enrichment, and validate selected p53-dependent genes by RT-qPCR. To address the evidence from [(Kirschner et al., 2015-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5e7d1dbb4f2647152acdc21f93a616ce04e8cfb5) that p53 outputs are phenotype-specific and acute versus chronic programs differ, include p53 target gene panels, motif or ChIP-seq validation if possible, and compare the acute 0-240 minute DD response with a longer chronic condition to ensure that transient p53 engagement is not missed. Finally, include non-p53 stress-response controls and proteasome reporter readouts to separate p53-dependent transcription from general proteasome competition, and report effect sizes, significance, and replicate-level variability for each comparison.

## To ensure robust and reproducible results, the proposal states: All experiments will include appropriate controls, biological replicates, and statistical analyses to ensure robust and reproducible results.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method's requirement for controls, biological replicates, and statistical analyses comes from [(Broz et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87470ea28edc3b5d3fc01d9cc49640fec3caa38d), which used combined ChIP-seq and RNA-seq in DNA damage-treated primary mouse embryo fibroblasts to identify direct p53-regulated target genes, then validated selected findings by qPCR and FACS. This is directly aligned with the plan's use of NIH3T3 mouse fibroblasts, RNA-seq time courses, p53-dependent transcriptional readouts, and FACS/cell-cycle assays, and it demonstrates that p53 can drive transcriptional and cell-fate programs, including autophagy and apoptosis, in a transactivation-dependent manner. Although the excerpt does not report fold changes, p-values, sample sizes, sequencing depth, or statistical thresholds, the integrated ChIP-seq/RNA-seq design supports the plan's need for statistical target-gene identification and orthogonal validation. As an iterative extension, [(Nassiri et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4fe93ed0c4eab5fe0f25724eec63f2084c1b2f) supports the broader statistical workflow by using transcriptomic profiling of cell line models treated with Doxorubicin, estradiol, or TNFα, differential expression analysis, PLS-DA, network-based gene set enrichment, and RT-qPCR/Western validation; however, it provides weaker support for the p53-specific and compartment-specific DD hypothesis because it does not use p53 knockout cells, destabilizing domains, or nuclear/cytosolic localization.

### Contradictions

The strongest limitation that undermines the proposed method is that neither related work directly tests the proposed DD-based, compartment-specific unfolded protein stress or the p53 knockout requirement: [(Broz et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87470ea28edc3b5d3fc01d9cc49640fec3caa38d) used DNA damage in primary MEFs and autophagy-deficient cells, while [(Nassiri et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4fe93ed0c4eab5fe0f25724eec63f2084c1b2f) used Doxorubicin, estradiol, or TNFα in cell line models, so their evidence cannot confirm that nuclear versus cytosolic unfolded protein specifically activates p53 or that the proposed 0-240 minute RNA-seq window will capture the relevant response. A second, reproducibility-relevant contradiction is that both papers under-specify the controls, replicates, and statistical thresholds that the proposed method claims to include: Broz does not provide sequencing depth, biological replicate number, statistical thresholds, treatment doses, or platform details, and Nassiri does not provide cell line identities, treatment doses, time points, replicate numbers, sequencing platform, bioinformatics parameters, or statistical cutoffs. This weakens the assumption that a generic statement about appropriate controls, biological replicates, and statistical analyses is sufficient. A weaker but important contradiction is that Broz found autophagy deficiency did not compromise DNA damage-induced cell cycle arrest or survival but impaired p53-dependent apoptosis and transformation suppression, which argues against treating EdU/DNA-content cell-cycle analysis as the primary functional readout for p53-dependent unfolded protein stress; apoptosis, viability, and transformation assays may be more directly supported.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to replace the generic statement with an explicit, pre-registered statistical and replication plan: use at least three independent biological replicates per condition and timepoint, include technical replicates for RNA-seq and RT-qPCR, define differential expression thresholds such as |log2FC| >= 1 and FDR < 0.05, apply multiple-testing correction and batch correction, report effect sizes and confidence intervals, and perform a power analysis for the 0, 15, 30, 60, 120, and 240 minute time course. Next, add direct p53 binding evidence using ChIP-seq or CUT&Tag at candidate target genes, as in [(Broz et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87470ea28edc3b5d3fc01d9cc49640fec3caa38d), because p53 knockout alone cannot distinguish direct p53 targets from indirect transcriptional responses. Next, incorporate apoptosis, viability, proliferation, and transformation assays, such as Annexin V/caspase activity, clonogenic survival, and soft agar or transformation suppression, in wild-type and p53 knockout NIH3T3 cells, because Broz shows p53-dependent apoptosis and transformation suppression rather than only cell-cycle arrest. Next, adopt the statistical separation and network enrichment strategy of [(Nassiri et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a4fe93ed0c4eab5fe0f25724eec63f2084c1b2f) by using PLS-DA or another supervised classifier to distinguish NucDD, CytoDD, and ntDD transcriptional states, with permutation testing, module stability assessment, and independent validation by RT-qPCR and Western blot. Finally, specify DD implementation controls: empty vector, ntDD, Shield-1 present and withdrawn, matched expression levels, confirmed localization, degradation kinetics, proteasome reporter controls, and isogenic p53 knockout controls with CRISPR validation and rescue if feasible.
