## Strengths 
The best-supported methods are supervised fine-tuning of ESM-family Transformers on DMS scores with a regression head, standardized DMS benchmarking, and mutation-position/wildtype-relative scoring. [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) directly validates fine-tuning a 34-layer Transformer on DMS collections with 80/20 splits, five-fold cross-validation, and Spearman rho, while [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5) shows ESM fine-tuning improves MaveDB, ProteinGym, and ClinVar metrics under leakage-controlled protein holdouts. [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39) supports the proposed benchmarking framework by providing ProteinGym v1.0 assays, curation criteria, and zero-shot/supervised baselines. The mutation-position and wildtype-relative inductive biases are supported by [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617), and [(Sun, and Shen, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879f4a0d8dcd5e727442278543637ea7b439caa1), which show position-specific representations and mutant-versus-wildtype scoring are useful for DMS fitness. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) supports the need for held-out validation, cross-validation, and sequence-distance or interval splits for transfer evaluation.

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the assumption that an ESM2-only, sequence-centric framework will match multimodal PreMode or that simple size scaling and test-time adaptation will reliably improve generalization. [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) shows predicted structure can improve sequence recovery and that non-random structural holdouts can expose overfitting, challenging ESM2-only and monotonic scaling assumptions. [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5) and [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556) indicate that structural features or structure-aware ESM2 models can improve DMS ranking, so omitting AlphaFold2 structures and MSAs may understate PreMode's advantage. [(Serrano et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b91202d01eb928ac74fd8e15ea0f2503bcbe093) cautions that protein-LM scaling laws may not cleanly transfer to downstream mutation-effect prediction, and [(Notin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3e40d51c53b70a11c55ba962f90da428ef28b60) shows ESM-family performance can depend on MSA depth and specialized baselines. The proposed four-head ablation is not directly validated by prior work, and mean-sequence heads are the least supported relative to position-specific wildtype-relative scoring. Finally, [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52) and [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6) warn that split choice and test-time input diversity can materially change conclusions, so random splits or uncontrolled Test-Time-Training may overestimate transfer.

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Adopt a leakage-controlled, pre-registered DMS benchmark protocol: use ProteinGym v1.0 and MaveDB-style filtering with 20 percent coverage/identity clustering, protein/domain holdouts, log-scale normalization, and explicit split taxonomy including mutation-level, position-level, temporal, sequence-interval, and sequence-distance splits, as recommended by [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39), [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825), and [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52); report Spearman, rBME, bin-specific errors, multiple seeds, and confidence intervals.

Add structure and MSA controls to test whether ESM2-only is sufficient before claiming superiority over PreMode: include AlphaFold2 structure baselines, MSA retrieval baselines, local contact or SASA/B-factor features, and a fair re-run of PreMode on the same nine DMS splits, following evidence from [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5), [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), and [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff).

Reframe model-size, LoRA, and Test-Time-Training experiments as compute-matched downstream ablations with explicit simple baselines: compare static ESM2 embeddings, ESM-1v/ESM2 log-odds and LMHead difference scores, the four proposed heads, full fine-tuning, LoRA rank ablations, partial layer unfreezing, and controlled TTT with early stopping and input-diversity checks, while reporting FLOPs, parameter counts, learning rates, batch sizes, hardware, seeds, and non-monotonic scaling behavior, as motivated by [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617), [(Zhao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/805d4dfa665dd24d2fd377e6e69899ee8b9e2d29), [(Serrano et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b91202d01eb928ac74fd8e15ea0f2503bcbe093), and [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6).
