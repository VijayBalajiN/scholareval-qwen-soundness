## Context: To create a systematic ESM2-based approach for mutation functional effect prediction. Method: We will develop ESM-Effect, a framework that systematically optimizes ESM2-based functional effect prediction through extensive ablation studies.

### Support

Read as an iterative sequence, the strongest support for the proposed ESM-Effect plan is [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), which directly tests supervised fine-tuning of ESM models, including ESM-2, on normalized MaveDB DMS scores and reports quantitative gains: micro-averaged Spearman on held-out MaveDB increased from 0.478 to 0.503, ProteinGym average Spearman increased from 0.331 to 0.396, and ClinVar auROC increased from 0.891 to 0.902, with balanced ClinVar micro auROC increasing from 0.860 to 0.877 and macro auROC from 0.880 to 0.891; ESM-1b also showed up to a 25.6% relative ProteinGym Spearman gain. This supports the plan's core hypothesis that fine-tuning ESM2 on DMS functional scores can improve prediction, and its leakage controls, including 20% sequence coverage and identity clustering, 25 training proteins, 5 held-out proteins, 142,696 missense variants, 8,636 unique positions, 24 ProteinGym assays with 115,093 variants, and 114,221 ClinVar variants, provide a concrete template for the proposed consistent train-test splits and cross-protein transfer tests. The next strongest support is [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39), which supplies ProteinGym v1.0 with 217 substitution assays containing 2.4M mutants, 66 indel assays containing 289k mutants, clinical substitution and indel sets, 42 zero-shot substitution baselines, 20 zero-shot indel baselines, 9 supervised substitution baselines, 3 supervised indel baselines, and thermostability filtering using a Spearman correlation threshold above 0.2; this directly supports the plan's need for standardized DMS benchmarking, zero-shot versus supervised regimes, and careful assay curation. Earlier, [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) supports the proposed mutation-position inductive bias by showing that ESM-1v amino-acid probabilities and entropy at mutated positions correlate with conservation, binding sites, buried core residues, and PSSM-like hydrophobic versus hydrophilic patterns, although it provides no DMS Spearman, calibration, or fine-tuning results. [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) supports systematic ablation and regression-head thinking by showing that auxiliary solvent-accessible surface area and B-factor tasks improve LGN pre-training, with linear regression coefficients of 0.989 and 1.008, p-values below 0.001, Pearson correlations of 0.884 and 0.791, and that auxiliary-task weights from 0.05 to 0.1 are preferable, while log-odds scoring and an optional MLP score head can be fine-tuned on DMS data. [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) supports ablation-driven input optimization and DMS evaluation through Tranception's 700M-parameter, 1024-token, UniRef100-trained model, retrieval-augmented MSA inference, and forward/reverse averaging, but the excerpt lacks numerical ProteinGym outcomes. Finally, [(Jiang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f1ec71cff73df23e8e4b07618b597418aa07c60) supports fine-tuning protein language model mutation scorers and experimental validation across LbCas12a, T7 RNA polymerase, creatinase, a non-natural nucleic acid polymerase, and VHH, but because it provides no numerical baselines it is the weakest direct support.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), which shows that structure-based inverse folding with predicted AlphaFold2 backbones improves zero-shot insertion-effect prediction for AAV-related sequences and improves sequence recovery from 39.2% to 50.8% for GVP-GNN-large and from 38.3% to 51.6% for GVP-Transformer when adding predicted structures; this challenges the plan's ESM2-only sequence-centric assumption, especially for AAV and indel or insertion effects, and suggests that structural information may be necessary to match multimodal PreMode. A second, more direct but less severe contradiction is [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), because although it supports ESM fine-tuning, its MaveDB Spearman gain is only 5.2% relative, from 0.478 to 0.503, and its ClinVar auROC gain is only 1.23% relative, from 0.891 to 0.902, and it does not compare against PreMode, AlphaFold2 structures, MSA retrieval, LoRA, ESM2 size scaling, or the proposed rBME metric, so it does not establish that ESM2 fine-tuning will significantly outperform static or multimodal methods. A third contradiction is [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), whose retrieval-augmented MSA mode and 700M-parameter UniRef100 model imply that MSA retrieval and large-context sequence modeling may be important for DMS performance, potentially undermining an ESM2-only framework if MSA or structure features are omitted; however, the excerpt provides no quantitative ProteinGym scores, so this contradiction is inferential. [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) also contradicts a purely sequence-based ESM2 approach by showing that structure-based equivariant graph convolution with SASA and B-factor auxiliary tasks improves pre-training and variant scoring, although it does not report a final DMS Spearman value or a direct ESM2 comparison. The weakest contradiction is [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), because its unsupervised log-odds and additive multi-mutation scoring do not validate the proposed relative wildtype-mutant regression heads, DMS benchmarking, or rBME, and the absence of quantitative mutation-effect metrics means it cannot support the plan's fine-tuning hypothesis.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the leakage-controlled DMS protocol from [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5) and [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39): use ProteinGym v1.0's 217 substitution and 66 indel assays, filter assays similar to training proteins, cluster sequences at 20% coverage and identity, hold out proteins or domains, normalize DMS targets to a log scale with synonymous variants mean 0, nonsense variants mean -1, and cap values to [-2, 2], and report Spearman plus rBME with multiple seeds and confidence intervals. Second, add structural and MSA controls to test whether ESM2-only is sufficient, especially for AAV insertions and PreMode comparison: include AlphaFold2 structure baselines, MSA retrieval baselines, and inverse-folding or structure-aware ablations inspired by [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), and [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff), and if ESM2 underperforms, incorporate structure embeddings or auxiliary SASA, B-factor, or conservation features rather than claiming sequence-only superiority. Third, implement the proposed regression-head ablations with explicit wildtype-mutant relative scoring and mutation-position emphasis, using [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) for position-specific log-odds and entropy and [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) for score-head fine-tuning and auxiliary-task weight ablations; compare mean embedding, wildtype-mutant linear combination, mutation-position embedding, and wildtype-mutant position combination, and test losses such as MSE, KL divergence, and normalized-score regression, while treating LoRA as a parameter-efficient ablation rather than a primary claim because none of the related works directly validates LoRA for ESM2 mutation-effect prediction. Fourth, include input and training ablations supported by [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), such as forward/reverse sequence averaging, UniRef100 fine-grained data, random slicing and imputation, and context-length effects, and by [(Jiang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f1ec71cff73df23e8e4b07618b597418aa07c60), such as homologous-sequence fine-tuning and wet-lab validation on a small set of proteins. Least important but still useful, define rBME as a binning metric that penalizes rare gain-of-function and non-clustered regions, visualize prediction errors by fitness bin, and report transfer on sequence intervals, structured versus disordered regions, and cross-protein-family tests to avoid overgeneralizing from random splits.

## Context: To systematically evaluate each component of ESM-Effect. Method: We will conduct comprehensive ablation experiments across multiple DMS datasets (including AAV, GB1, GFP, SNCA, NUDT15, PTEN) to systematically evaluate each component. We will test different model sizes, fine-tuning approaches, and regression head designs using consistent train-test splits.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed ablation method comes from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), which directly validates the central ESM-Effect idea of fine-tuning a protein language model on DMS variant-effect data and using a regression head for mutation effects. That work fine-tuned a 34-layer Transformer on 21,026 variant-effect measurements from nine experimental scans and 712,218 mutations across 42 scans, used 80% training/20% testing with five-fold cross-validation, evaluated Spearman correlation, and outperformed a supervised structural/evolutionary baseline on 10 of 12 proteins as well as fine-tuned LSTM baselines. This supports the plan's fine-tuning strategy, DMS benchmarking, and regression-head evaluation, although it does not ablate ESM2 model sizes, LoRA, partial fine-tuning, or rBME. The next strongest support is from [(Sun, and Shen, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879f4a0d8dcd5e727442278543637ea7b439caa1) and [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5), which perform component-wise ablations on DMS fitness data, including UBEX MOUSE, two BLAT ECOLX studies, and four supplemental protein sets, and show that mutant-position averaged embeddings capture the fitness landscape better than all-position averaging, that family sequence fine-tuning and structure-informed fine-tuning improve high/low fitness cluster separation with higher silhouette coefficients, and that adding structural log-odds improves Spearman rho and AUROC over sequence-only scores. These findings directly support the proposed regression-head emphasis on the mutation position and the fine-tuning ablations, but they do not test ESM2 model-size scaling, LoRA, or the exact AAV, GB1, GFP, SNCA, NUDT15, and PTEN datasets. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) supports the requirement for consistent and biologically meaningful train-test splits by recommending a roughly 20% held-out test set, validation-based hyperparameter selection, n-fold cross-validation for small datasets, and sequence-distance or mutagenesis-round splits when testing generalization, which aligns with the plan's transfer-capability assessment. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) supports the use of ESM-family sequence representations and position-specific log-odds scoring, conceptually related to the proposed mutation-position embedding head, although the excerpt lacks quantitative DMS metrics. Finally, the older work of [(Sruthi, and Prakash, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/185f1b56b5eae8b2465e4e99535119be9d5f7b36) provides less direct but useful support for ablation-style component evaluation on DMS data: in E. coli beta-lactamase with 4,997 mutations at six ampicillin concentrations, feature ablation by fixing inputs at their mean and measuring MSE showed BLOSUM substitution features contributed most, sequence-only alanine-scan R2 was 0.55 versus 0.44 for structure-only, and overall R2 increased from 0.62 at 15% data to 0.71 at 50% and 0.74 at 85%, but it used hand-engineered features rather than ESM2 fine-tuning or regression-head ablations.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that an ESM2-only ablation may understate the contribution of structural or multimodal information. [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5) and [(Sun, and Shen, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879f4a0d8dcd5e727442278543637ea7b439caa1) show that structure-only distributions do not outperform sequence distributions, but adding structural log-odds, especially contact-map distance bins derived from Cβ distances within 8 Å, secondary structure, and relative solvent accessibility binarized at 0.25, improves Spearman rho ranking and AUROC classification, and structure-informed fine-tuning produces stronger high/low fitness cluster separation than family sequence fine-tuning alone. This suggests that the proposed ESM2-only framework, particularly when compared with PreMode, which uses ESM2 embeddings, AlphaFold2 structures, and MSAs, may miss important accuracy gains from structure. A second contradiction is that DMS measurement noise and concentration-dependent effects can limit scalar fitness prediction. [(Sruthi, and Prakash, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/185f1b56b5eae8b2465e4e99535119be9d5f7b36) reports predicted-fitness variability comparable to experimental trial-to-trial variation, poor amino-acid-specific regressions when effects do not span the full fitness range, and sigmoidal dose-response patterns across six ampicillin concentrations that were not modeled quantitatively; this implies that a single fitness target and standard Spearman or rBME evaluation may not fully capture rare gain-of-function or concentration-dependent behavior unless the metric and target explicitly account for such regions. A third, conditional contradiction is that inconsistent or random splits can overestimate generalization. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) warns that split construction must match the intended use, recommending sequence-distance or round-based splits for generalization rather than random splits; if the proposed consistent train-test splits are random within each DMS dataset, conclusions about model size, fine-tuning, and regression heads may be optimistic, although the plan's distinct sequence-interval transfer tests partially mitigate this. Finally, the related work does not directly validate the proposed model-size scaling or parameter-efficient fine-tuning claims: [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) uses one 34-layer Transformer and does not compare ESM2 sizes, LoRA, or partial fine-tuning, and [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) reports no quantitative DMS mutation-effect metrics, leaving the plan's scaling-law and LoRA ablations without direct prior evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to freeze biologically meaningful, dataset-specific splits before running ablations, following [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825): use approximately 20% held-out test data, validation-based model selection, five-fold cross-validation for small DMS datasets, sequence-distance or sequence-interval splits for transfer, and report hyperparameter search, optimizer settings, learning rates, random seeds, and hardware for each AAV, GB1, GFP, SNCA, NUDT15, and PTEN dataset. The second most important action is to prioritize regression-head ablations around mutation-position embeddings, because [(Sun, and Shen, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879f4a0d8dcd5e727442278543637ea7b439caa1) and [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5) show mutant-position averaging outperforms all-position averaging; test all four proposed heads but rank mutation-position and mutant-plus-wildtype position linear combinations first, and include the additive per-position regression head from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) as a multi-mutation baseline. The third action is to structure the fine-tuning ablation to compare static ESM2, full fine-tuning, LoRA, partial layer unfreezing, and family-specific fine-tuning, using [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) as the supervised DMS fine-tuning baseline and reporting Spearman correlation, rBME, AUROC, and silhouette coefficients where applicable, with statistical significance across seeds and datasets. The fourth action is to add a structure-informed or multimodal control, at least a contact-map/local 8 Å Cβ feature baseline or the PreMode comparison, because [(Sun, and Shen, 2023-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/879f4a0d8dcd5e727442278543637ea7b439caa1) and [(Sun, and Shen, 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c700084399f566664f097b8e35316ab6f0d93c5) show structural log-odds improve ranking and classification and the plan explicitly compares against PreMode. The fifth action is to include data-fraction and noise-aware analyses inspired by [(Sruthi, and Prakash, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/185f1b56b5eae8b2465e4e99535119be9d5f7b36): report performance at 15%, 50%, and 85% training coverage, per-amino-acid and fitness-range regressions, and, where possible, concentration-dependent or binned fitness targets to address trial-to-trial variation and rare gain-of-function regions. The sixth action is to include static ESM2 log-odds and entropy baselines from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) to separate unsupervised position-specific signal from supervised fine-tuning gains, and to validate rBME by showing how it correlates with Spearman and AUROC while emphasizing challenging, non-clustered, and rare gain-of-function bins.

## Context: To choose how mutation effects are computed from ESM2 embeddings. Method: We will systematically evaluate four regression head architectures: (1) mean embedding of mutant sequence, (2) linear combination of mean embeddings from mutant and wildtype sequences, (3) embedding at mutation position of mutant sequence, and (4) linear combination of mutation position embeddings from mutant and wildtype sequences.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed four-head ablation comes from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), which fine-tunes a 34-layer ESM-1b Transformer with a task-dependent regression head on position-specific hidden representations and backpropagates through pretrained weights to predict scaled mutational effects using mutant and wildtype identities. This directly supports the research plan's inductive biases that effects are wildtype-relative and mutation-position-specific, and it provides quantitative DMS evidence: the fine-tuned model outperformed Envision on 10 of 12 proteins in a 21,026-measurement, 9-scan collection and outperformed fine-tuned LSTMs in a 712,218-mutation, 42-scan collection, with 80/20 splits and five-fold cross-validation under Spearman rho. The next strongest support is [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617), which uses a masked mutation-position LMHead and computes thermal-stability effect as the log-probability difference between mutant and wildtype amino acids on ProTherm, 1042 single mutants from 126 proteins with an 80:20 non-overlapping wildtype-protein split; its structure-aware model achieved higher Spearman rank-order correlation than the sequence-only baseline, supporting position-specific wildtype-relative heads and fine-tuning, although it uses log-probability differences rather than ESM2 embedding linear combinations. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), which scores single mutations by a position-specific ESM-1v log-odds ratio between mutant and wildtype amino acids and assumes additivity for multiple mutations, reinforcing the local mutant-versus-wildtype formulation but without quantitative DMS results in the excerpt. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) supports the broader evaluation design by recommending a held-out test set, validation-based model selection, n-fold cross-validation for small mutagenesis datasets, and splits that reflect intended use, which aligns with the plan's consistent DMS splits, ablations, and transfer evaluation. Finally, [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5) provides indirect support for pretrained sequence models and parent-relative mutation-effect decisions, reporting 70% versus 68% accuracy for distinguishing beneficial from deleterious stability mutations with and without pretraining, but it does not benchmark ESM2 regression heads.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the cited works directly compares the four proposed ESM2 embedding heads, so the specific claim that mean mutant, mean mutant-wildtype, position mutant, and position mutant-wildtype linear combinations should be ranked by ablation is not yet evidenced. Most related methods use ESM-1b, ESM-1v, or non-ESM graph models, and they use log-probability differences or MLPs on predicted amino-acid distributions rather than learned linear combinations of sequence or position embeddings. For example, [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) uses position-specific hidden representations but does not isolate mean versus position embeddings, and [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617) uses a masked-position LMHead log-probability difference rather than an explicit embedding regression head. A second contradiction is that mean-sequence embedding heads are the least supported: the strongest mutation-effect evidence is position-specific and wildtype-relative, while [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5) shows that heldout-family language-modeling accuracy is lower than random-split accuracy and that lower perplexity does not necessarily improve downstream tasks, suggesting that global sequence embeddings may not generalize as well as local, task-relevant representations. Third, [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) provides no DMS performance for its variant-effect head and shows that increasing auxiliary-task weights can reduce average Spearman correlation, with a small peak only between lambda = 0.1 and 0.2, warning that added architectural or training complexity may not improve mutation-effect prediction. Fourth, [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) and [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) provide limited quantitative evidence in the supplied excerpts, so they cannot by themselves justify the proposed ESM2-specific head ranking or the expected superiority of fine-tuned ESM2 over static embeddings.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add explicit baselines that separate the value of ESM2 embedding regression heads from simpler position-specific wildtype-relative scores: implement ESM-1v or ESM2 log-odds and LMHead probability-difference baselines inspired by [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617), and [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff), and compare them against the four proposed heads using the same DMS splits, Spearman rho, and rBME. Second, adopt the evaluation protocol from [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) by reserving a fixed test set, using validation only for head and hyperparameter selection, applying n-fold cross-validation for small DMS datasets, and using sequence-distance or interval-based splits for transfer experiments to avoid overestimating generalization; also report optimizer, learning rate, batch size, fine-tuning steps, hardware, multiple random seeds, confidence intervals, and significance tests. Third, prioritize the position-specific wildtype-relative heads, especially heads 3 and 4, and add controls such as wildtype-only, mutant-only, fixed-weight versus learned linear combination, and a position-pooled or attention-pooled variant, because [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) and [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617) provide the strongest evidence for local, mutant-versus-wildtype scoring. Fourth, follow the Rives-style fine-tuning setup by reporting sensitivity to training-data fraction, five-fold cross-validation where appropriate, 80/20 splits, and non-overlapping wildtype-protein splits as in [(Mansoor et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1bf538d497997a9ec5f8a94ecb8a33ae126bb617), while monitoring OOD heldout-family performance as cautioned by [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5). Finally, if auxiliary tasks or larger ESM2 variants are added, use small auxiliary weights and ablate them as in [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff), because added complexity may reduce Spearman correlation if not carefully tuned.

## Context: To determine whether adaptive ESM2 training improves functional effect prediction. Method: We will compare static ESM2 embeddings against fine-tuned approaches, testing multiple fine-tuning strategies including full fine-tuning, LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation), and partial fine-tuning (unfreezing specific layers).

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), which fine-tunes a 34-layer ESM-1b Transformer with a regression head over mutant and wildtype representations on DMS sets of 21,026 and 712,218 mutations, using 80/20 splits, five-fold cross-validation, and Spearman rho; it reports exceeding Envision on 10 of 12 proteins in the first dataset and outperforming fine-tuned LSTM baselines on the second, directly supporting the plan's core assumption that adaptive ESM-family training can improve quantitative functional-effect prediction and that mutant/wildtype regression heads are useful. Next, [(Zhao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/805d4dfa665dd24d2fd377e6e69899ee8b9e2d29) supports the LoRA branch by applying rank-8 LoRA to 650M ESM-1v, training only 1.35M parameters, a 99.79% reduction, on 34 DMS or random-mutagenesis datasets across 27 proteins with 20% holdouts, low-N settings of 48, 96, 168, and 240 variants, and 10 trials, although the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) supports the static-embedding baseline and the relative-to-wildtype inductive bias by scoring mutations with log odds between mutant and wildtype amino acid probabilities in ESM-1v and showing lower entropy at binding and active sites, but only qualitatively. Finally, [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) supports the benchmarking context by evaluating ESM-1v, MSA Transformer, EVE, DeepSequence, and Tranception on ProteinGym DMS assays and reporting that UniRef100 training, 1024-token context, and sequence mirroring improve downstream performance, though it does not test ESM2 fine-tuning.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that the closest static ESM-family evidence, [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), shows unsupervised ESM-1v log-odds and entropy already localize binding and active-site residues without fine-tuning, which challenges the necessity of adaptive ESM2 training if the goal is functional-site identification; however, it provides no DMS Spearman or rBME comparison, so the contradiction is limited. [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) weakly contradicts the ESM2-specific claim because its fine-tuned ESM-1b model is only described as comparable to Envision overall despite exceeding it on 10 of 12 proteins in one dataset, suggesting gains from fine-tuning may be dataset-dependent and not uniformly large; it also does not compare static embeddings, LoRA, or partial layer unfreezing. [(Zhao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/805d4dfa665dd24d2fd377e6e69899ee8b9e2d29) contradicts direct transfer of the LoRA result to ESM-Effect because it uses ESM-1v, a contrastive fitness objective, and no reported quantitative comparison against full fine-tuning, partial fine-tuning, static embeddings, or rBME. [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) provides the weakest contradiction: its ProteinGym evaluation is qualitative in the excerpt and does not show that ESM2 fine-tuning is inferior, but it also does not provide numerical evidence that alignment-free ESM2 fine-tuning will outperform alignment-based or multimodal methods such as PreMode.

### Suggested Action

Most important, implement the proposed head-to-head comparison on identical DMS splits and identical ESM2 checkpoints: static ESM2 embeddings, full fine-tuning, LoRA, and partial layer unfreezing, reporting Spearman and rBME with multiple seeds, train/test fractions, and hardware and hyperparameters, because none of the related works directly validates this ESM2-specific ablation. Second, adopt LoRA details from [(Zhao et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/805d4dfa665dd24d2fd377e6e69899ee8b9e2d29) by testing rank-8 as a baseline and adding rank ablations, trainable-parameter counts, and low-N 48, 96, 168, and 240 settings with 10 trials, while changing the objective to the plan's regression heads rather than only contrastive fitness. Third, include static log-odds and entropy baselines inspired by [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) to ensure the static ESM2 arm is not under-specified, and test whether mutation-position or mean embeddings plus mutant/wildtype linear combinations improve over simple log-odds scores. Fourth, use the DMS benchmarking lessons from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) and [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) by reporting five-fold or repeated cross-validation, performance across training fractions, and comparisons against ESM-1v, MSA Transformer, EVE, DeepSequence, and PreMode on the same nine DMS datasets and splits, with rBME emphasized for rare gain-of-function regions. Finally, document model-size scaling from 35M to larger ESM2 variants, partial-layer selection criteria, and transfer or interval splits to make the ablation reproducible and to avoid overclaiming from single-seed or single-dataset results.

## Context: To determine optimal scaling relationships for functional effect prediction. Method: We will evaluate different ESM2 model sizes (35M to larger variants) to determine optimal scaling relationships for functional effect prediction, investigating whether NLP scaling laws transfer to this biological domain.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Hesslow et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c790c1a4233f5201b052b149a3c6c76261aee68), which directly tests whether larger autoregressive transformer protein language models improve mutation-effect prediction on ProteinGym DMS assays. It pretrains RITA models on UniRef-100, evaluates 94 DMS assays including substitution and indel benchmarks, and reports that Large and XLarge variants outperform ESM-1v on substitution scoring and score indels on par with specialized models, although the excerpt does not provide exact Spearman values or statistical significance. This supports the proposed ESM2 size sweep because it shows model size can improve DMS functional-effect prediction, although it does not isolate ESM2, fine-tuning, or regression-head design. Building on the earlier fine-tuning evidence of [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), the proposed method is also supported by that paper's demonstration that a 34-layer Transformer with a regression head can predict scaled mutational effects on two DMS collections, 21,026 measurements from 9 scans and 712,218 mutations from 42 scans, using Spearman rho, 80/20 protein-level splits, and five-fold cross-validation; it outperformed a feature-based baseline on 10 of 12 proteins and fine-tuned LSTM baselines, but it evaluates one large model rather than a 35M-to-large ESM2 sweep. [(Notin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3e40d51c53b70a11c55ba962f90da428ef28b60) supports the benchmarking context by evaluating ProteinGym, approximately 1.5 million missense variants across 87 DMS assays and approximately 300,000 indels across 7 assays, plus ClinVar with approximately 31,000 variants across approximately 3,000 disease genes, and by comparing ESM-1b and ESM-1v against Tranception, EVE, MSA Transformer, RITA, and Progen2 with aggregate Spearman/AUC and stratification by MSA depth, taxon, mutation depth, and indels; this validates standardized DMS/clinical benchmarking and ESM-family baselines, though it does not test ESM2 size scaling. [(Kulmanov et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2659df3861089967ee7b4a079eda4105596d702) provides weaker but relevant support for ESM2 embeddings as functional features: DeepGO-SE uses ESM2 embeddings to predict Gene Ontology terms on 77,647 UniProtKB/Swiss-Prot proteins, achieving Fmax 0.554 for molecular function, 0.432 for biological process, and 0.721 for cellular component, with graph-enhanced variants reaching 0.525, 0.435, and 0.736, and on neXtProt 239 proteins reaching 0.386 molecular function and 0.350 biological process; this supports ESM2 functional representational value but not mutation-effect scaling. The weakest support is [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), which shows ESM-1v can score mutations by log odds between mutant and wildtype amino acids, combine multiple mutations additively, and use per-position entropy to identify binding, active-site, and buried residues, but it provides only qualitative structural evidence and no DMS Spearman, rBME, size sweep, or statistical significance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Serrano et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b91202d01eb928ac74fd8e15ea0f2503bcbe093), which studies compute-optimal scaling for encoder-only protein language models and finds power-law fits with N_opt proportional to C^0.27 and D_opt proportional to C^0.71, but also reports a loss floor around 2.4, small alpha_N and alpha_D sensitivity coefficients, an extraordinarily large N_c, an unreasonable D_c, and no clearly superior model size or token count within the explored range. This directly cautions against assuming that NLP-style scaling laws will cleanly transfer to ESM2 functional-effect prediction or that a simple size sweep will identify an optimal ESM2 checkpoint; pretraining loss scaling may not predict downstream DMS performance, and it postdates the more optimistic size-scaling evidence in [(Hesslow et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c790c1a4233f5201b052b149a3c6c76261aee68). A second contradiction/limitation is [(Notin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3e40d51c53b70a11c55ba962f90da428ef28b60): although it validates ProteinGym benchmarking, it shows TranceptEVE outperforms ESM-1b and other baselines in aggregate and by MSA depth, and is only on par with ESM-1v on high MSA depth, while the ClinVar excerpt lacks numeric performance values; this implies that ESM-family performance is not uniformly superior and may depend on MSA depth, taxon, mutation depth, and specialized multimodal or MSA features, challenging any assumption that increasing ESM2 size alone will dominate all functional-effect baselines. [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) also limits the proposed scaling claim because it evaluates a single 34-layer Transformer rather than a controlled ESM2 size sweep, omits hardware, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, and exact Spearman values, and therefore cannot establish a size-performance curve. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) is a weaker contradiction because its ESM-1v mutation scoring and entropy analysis are qualitative, lack DMS quantitative metrics, statistical tests, dataset sizes, and scaling comparisons, so it cannot support the proposed quantitative scaling-law hypothesis. Finally, [(Kulmanov et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2659df3861089967ee7b4a079eda4105596d702) is the weakest contradiction/limitation: it uses ESM2 embeddings for Gene Ontology annotation rather than mutation-effect prediction, does not vary ESM2 size, fine-tuning strategy, or regression head, and therefore cannot validate the proposed ESM2 size-scaling method for DMS functional effects.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed ESM2 size-scaling experiment should be redesigned as a downstream, compute-matched DMS scaling study rather than an assumption that NLP scaling laws transfer directly. Following [(Serrano et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3b91202d01eb928ac74fd8e15ea0f2503bcbe093), report pretraining loss, downstream Spearman and rBME, confidence intervals, multiple random seeds, compute or FLOPs, optimizer, learning rate, batch size, and sequence length for each ESM2 size, and explicitly test whether performance saturates or is non-monotonic; include a range from 35M to larger ESM2 checkpoints with a fixed fine-tuning budget and, where possible, compute-matched training steps. Second, adopt the ProteinGym/ClinVar benchmarking structure from [(Notin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a3e40d51c53b70a11c55ba962f90da428ef28b60): use the same DMS assays, stratify by MSA depth, taxon, mutation depth, and indels, and compare against ESM-1b, ESM-1v, RITA, EVE, MSA Transformer, Tranception or TranceptEVE, and PreMode, reporting aggregate and subgroup Spearman, AUC, and rBME so that ESM2 size gains are not conflated with MSA-depth or dataset-specific effects; apply this to the plan's AAV, GB1, GFP, SNCA, NUDT15, and PTEN datasets as well as ProteinGym. Third, integrate the fine-tuning and regression-head evidence from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) by testing static embeddings, full fine-tuning, LoRA, partial layer unfreezing, and the four proposed heads, mean mutant, mean mutant plus wildtype, position mutant, and position mutant plus wildtype, on the same DMS splits, using Spearman and rBME with protein-level 80/20 or five-fold cross-validation where appropriate. Fourth, use [(Hesslow et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c790c1a4233f5201b052b149a3c6c76261aee68) as a complementary scaling baseline: include RITA likelihood scoring on the same ProteinGym DMS assays to check whether ESM2 size trends match autoregressive protein-LM scaling, and report indel performance if the ESM-Effect framework is extended beyond substitutions. Fifth, add ESM-1v log-odds mutation scoring and entropy from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) as an interpretable baseline and sanity check for mutation-position sensitivity, especially for multiple mutations and buried or active-site residues, but do not rely on it for quantitative scaling conclusions. Finally, use [(Kulmanov et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2659df3861089967ee7b4a079eda4105596d702) as an auxiliary functional-embedding validation: if ESM2 embeddings are frozen, evaluate Gene Ontology or functional annotation performance, Fmax, AUPR, and AUC, on held-out proteins to confirm that larger ESM2 checkpoints improve general functional representation, while keeping DMS mutation-effect prediction as the primary endpoint.

## Context: To enable robust and standardized benchmarking of functional effect prediction. Method: We will establish standardized evaluation protocols including: (1) consistent datasets with clear train-test splits, (2) the relative Binned-Mean Error (rBME) metric to emphasize prediction accuracy in challenging, non-clustered, and rare gain-of-function regions, and (3) visualization methods that provide realistic assessment of model performance.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed standardized evaluation protocol comes from [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39), which directly addresses the need for shared DMS-based functional-effect benchmarks by curating 217 substitution assays containing 2.4 million mutants and 66 indel assays containing 289,000 mutants, plus clinical benchmarks with 2,525 substitution genes containing 63,000 mutants and 1,555 indel genes containing 3,000 mutants. Its assay-selection criteria, including public availability, throughput, replicate noise, dynamic range, assay type, evolutionary constraint, and mutation type, support the proposed emphasis on consistent datasets and clear evaluation regimes, and its separation of zero-shot and supervised baselines, with 42 zero-shot substitution baselines, 20 zero-shot indel baselines, 9 supervised substitution baselines, and 3 supervised indel baselines, is highly relevant to the ESM-Effect plan’s comparison of static ESM2 embeddings, fine-tuned ESM2 variants, and PreMode. The next strongest support is from [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52), which provides concrete evidence that standardized train-test separation and visualization improve mutation-effect benchmarking. It uses 15 protein-specific datasets from Protherm with at least 50 measured ΔΔG mutations per protein, 1537 total mutations, 1211 point mutations, and 31293 Rosetta-simulated point mutations, and evaluates mutation-level cross-validation, position-level cross-validation, and leave-one-out while excluding test data from both model learning and Bayesian scaling. Its quantitative results, including improved correlation from 0.55 to 0.65 and reduced RMSE from 1.63 to 1.35 kcal/mol after Bayesian scaling, plus learning curves, kernel-weight plots, and scaling-transformation plots, support the proposed visualization methods and the need to report more than a single global metric. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64970d6e80a60c9210a07a7a49ab6f7592bb6e41) provides weaker but still relevant protocol-level support by recommending a held-out test set of about 20 percent, separate validation for hyperparameter selection, n-fold cross-validation for small datasets, temporal mutagenesis-round splits, and sequence-distance constraints for generalization to unrelated families, all of which align with the plan’s transfer capability assessment on distinct sequence intervals and cross-protein-family tests. The weakest but conceptually useful support is from [(Hopf et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/675c3c958640275c2cbafbbcf4973d4c8183549b), which uses an epistatic evolutionary Hamiltonian to predict context-dependent mutation effects and compares against an independent maximum entropy baseline; this supports the proposed need to test whether ESM2 fine-tuning captures context-dependent and epistatic effects, but the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes, dataset sizes, split definitions, hardware, hyperparameters, and metric definitions, so its support is indirect and does not directly validate ESM2, LoRA, PreMode, rBME, or the proposed visualization protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that even the most directly relevant DMS benchmark, [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39), does not fully solve the standardization problem: it explicitly leaves fully standardized reprocessing of all raw data for future work, retains noisy and biased clinical labels with circularity concerns, and filters thermostability assays using a Spearman correlation threshold above 0.2 with evolutionary models, which may remove difficult or low-signal assays. The excerpt also does not specify train-test split construction, hardware, model hyperparameters, or the proposed rBME metric, so it contradicts the assumption that existing curation alone provides a robust, reproducible benchmark for rare gain-of-function regions. The next strongest contradiction is from [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52), which shows that evaluation protocol choice materially changes conclusions: position-level cross-validation caused a larger accuracy drop than mutation-level cross-validation, and data fusion reduced that drop, indicating that random or mutation-level splits can overestimate performance when nearby positions share information. Although this work supports standardized splits and visualization, it targets protein stability ΔΔG rather than DMS-based functional effects and uses correlation and RMSE rather than rBME, so it does not validate the proposed metric for rare gain-of-function enzyme mutations. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64970d6e80a60c9210a07a7a49ab6f7592bb6e41) provides a weaker contradiction because it recommends a roughly 20 percent test set and sequence-distance constraints but reports no quantitative outcomes, no baseline comparisons, and no statistical significance, and it does not address rare high-effect bins or rBME; a fixed 20 percent holdout may underrepresent rare gain-of-function mutations, so the proposed rBME motivation is not directly supported by this protocol. The weakest contradiction is from [(Hopf et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/675c3c958640275c2cbafbbcf4973d4c8183549b), whose epistatic Hamiltonian framework implies that mutation effects are strongly context-dependent and that non-epistatic baselines may be insufficient, but the excerpt provides no quantitative prediction accuracies, correlations, AUC values, error metrics, statistical significance, dataset sizes, split definitions, hardware, or hyperparameters. Therefore, it cannot directly contradict ESM-Effect, but it cautions that ESM2-based models may need explicit context or epistasis controls to demonstrate that their gains are not merely due to sequence-context memorization.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to pre-register an explicit split taxonomy for each DMS dataset in the ESM-Effect benchmark, including mutation-level, position-level, leave-one-position, temporal or mutagenesis-round, sequence-interval, and sequence-distance splits, and to ensure that test sequences, positions, and rounds are excluded from all preprocessing, normalization, scaling, feature extraction, and hyperparameter selection. This directly addresses the evidence from [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52) that position-level evaluation is harder than mutation-level evaluation and from [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64970d6e80a60c9210a07a7a49ab6f7592bb6e41) that validation and test splits should reflect intended use conditions. The protocol should report Spearman correlation, rBME, RMSE or MAE, bin-specific errors, and rare gain-of-function bin performance, with learning curves and binned-error visualizations modeled on the learning-curve and scaling-plot practice in [(Jokinen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bae0bd50a4ff6834c6d5e809bfdc2a58ff8fca52). The second most important action is to adopt the curation and baseline structure of [(Notin et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0d6c4d483b36100b2dfa5a68411945d0d28eea39) by documenting assay-level metadata such as replicate noise, dynamic range, function type, substitution coverage, indel type, and filtering thresholds, while adding sensitivity analyses for assays removed by quality filters so that difficult rare gain-of-function regions are not silently discarded. The benchmark should include zero-shot and supervised baselines, static ESM2 embeddings, fine-tuned ESM2 variants, LoRA and partial fine-tuning, and PreMode under identical splits, and should explicitly compare against the nine DMS datasets used by PreMode for direct comparability. The third most important action is to add context-dependent or epistatic baselines inspired by [(Hopf et al., 2015-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/675c3c958640275c2cbafbbcf4973d4c8183549b), such as Potts-model or evolutionary-coupling baselines, sequence-context baselines, and non-epistatic maximum entropy controls, to test whether ESM2 fine-tuning captures epistasis rather than only local sequence similarity. The fourth action is to follow the validation protocol in [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/64970d6e80a60c9210a07a7a49ab6f7592bb6e41) by using a held-out test set, a separate validation set for hyperparameter selection, n-fold cross-validation for small datasets, and sequence-distance constraints for cross-family generalization, while stratifying results by structured versus disordered regions and by mutation position. The least important but still necessary action is to document all reproducibility details, including ESM2 model sizes, LoRA rank, learning rates, regression-head architectures, random seeds, hardware, training time, and visualization code, so that the proposed rBME and visualization protocol can be independently reproduced and compared against existing DMS benchmarking work.

## Context: To assess performance against the current state of the art. Method: We will compare our optimized ESM-Effect against the state-of-the-art multimodal PreMode method, which incorporates ESM2 embeddings, AlphaFold2 structures, and multiple sequence alignments. We will use the same nine DMS datasets and train-test splits as PreMode for direct comparability.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), which fine-tuned a 34-layer Transformer pretrained on UR50/S to regress scaled mutational effects on 21,026 measurements from nine DMS datasets and 712,218 mutations from 42 DMS datasets, using 80/20 protein-level partitions and five-fold cross-validation, and reported Spearman rho; it outperformed Envision on 10 of 12 proteins, directly supporting the proposed plan to fine-tune a protein language model and benchmark on DMS effect data. Next, [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556) supports ESM2-based structure-aware VEP by using frozen ESM2-t33 node features, a k=20 residue graph, a 6-layer EGNN with 1,280 hidden neurons, ADAM learning rate 0.0001, 8,192-token batches, and an NVIDIA Tesla A100 80 GB GPU, and reports consistent outperformance on 100 assays by TPR at 5, 25, and 50 percent and top-rank Spearman on 86 ProteinGym deep-mutation assays, indicating that ESM2 representations plus spatial structure can be highly competitive in the same DMS benchmarking space as PreMode. [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) supports controlled DMS benchmarking by evaluating Tranception, a 700M-parameter model trained on approximately 250M UniRef100 sequences with 1024-token context, against ESM-1v, MSA Transformer, EVE, DeepSequence, EVmutation, and other baselines on ProteinGym substitution and indel assays, using single-seed scoring for fair comparison and separate ensembles, which aligns with the proposed need for a standardized ESM-Effect versus PreMode comparison. [(Huang et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5694e46284460a648fe29117cbc55f6c9be3fa3c) supports the ablation and efficiency logic by benchmarking DenseNet-BC depths and growth rates on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and ImageNet with fixed validation splits, augmentation, parameter counts, and FLOPs, showing that controlled model-size ablations and compute reporting are essential when comparing architectures. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) supports the evaluation protocol by recommending a held-out test set of about 20 percent, validation or n-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter selection, temporal splits for iterative mutagenesis, and minimum sequence-distance constraints for generalization, all of which should be applied to the nine PreMode DMS splits. [(Riesselman et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d0b51a1b13d0d7d8fa1cdc98b863f3ff2af8d9) supports DMS-based bidirectional mutation-effect prediction and calibration by estimating likelihood ratios and mapping them to observed effects, although it does not quantify ESM-Effect. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) supports the relevance of ESM-family sequence models and MSA information by showing that ESM-1v and MSA Transformer log-odds scores and entropy patterns correlate with conservation, binding sites, core versus surface residues, and hydrophobic preferences, but it is qualitative and not a direct PreMode comparison. [(Frazer et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0537c62e32299b8e9b4e3cac44d82ffa123cbbe1) and [(Wirnsberger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecb830ab5c7900c159bf09a25b9405b2cf3674a7) provide weaker but useful support for benchmarking variant-effect models against functional assays and controlled DMS train-tune-test splits, respectively, with Wirnsberger using avGFP, Pab1, and GB1, test sets of 5,000 entries, tune sets one-fifth of training size or fixed at 5,000, and three random subsets to reduce split bias.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the assumption that an optimized ESM-Effect sequence-centric model will easily surpass PreMode is [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), whose structure-aware ESM2-t33 plus EGNN model outperformed competing models of varying parameter sizes on 100 assays by TPR and reached top-rank Spearman on 86 ProteinGym deep-mutation assays, suggesting that spatial structure can provide a substantial advantage over sequence-only or less structure-aware ESM2 variants. A second important contradiction is [(Wirnsberger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecb830ab5c7900c159bf09a25b9405b2cf3674a7), where structure-informed CNN, SepConvMixer, and DenseNet models trained on avGFP, Pab1, and GB1 DMS data outperformed a sequence convolution baseline, with SepConvMixer reaching up to 1.75 times the performance of sequence convolution under pre-training and augmentation, especially on smaller datasets, implying that PreMode's AlphaFold2 structure component may be difficult to match without equivalent structural inductive bias. [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) and [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) further contradict a purely sequence-only framing by showing that MSA retrieval or MSA Transformer inputs can be important for mutation-effect prediction, even though the excerpts lack exact numeric benchmarks. [(Frazer et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0537c62e32299b8e9b4e3cac44d82ffa123cbbe1) also complicates the proposed focus by showing that an unsupervised VAE over UniRef100 MSAs, EVE, outperformed supervised MISTIC, M-CAP, and Polyphen2 on clinical significance and was as predictive as high-throughput experiments, which argues that supervised fine-tuning alone is not guaranteed to dominate all variant-effect baselines. Methodological contradictions or risks include [(Huang et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5694e46284460a648fe29117cbc55f6c9be3fa3c), which kept ResNet hyperparameters fixed when benchmarking DenseNet, a controlled but potentially unfair design if PreMode is not re-tuned under identical ESM-Effect optimization; and [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825), which warns that validation and test sets should be separated by sequence distance or temporal order to avoid overestimating generalization, so if the PreMode nine-DMS splits are random or not sequence-distance controlled, the proposed comparison may overstate transfer performance. Finally, none of the provided works directly evaluates ESM-Effect against PreMode on the exact nine DMS datasets and splits, so the central claim lacks direct empirical validation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the PreMode comparison exactly reproducible and fair: use the same nine DMS datasets, exact train-test split definitions, metric definitions, and evaluation code as PreMode, report Spearman correlation and rBME with confidence intervals or multiple random seeds, and if published PreMode numbers are not directly comparable, re-run PreMode under the same hardware, seeds, and preprocessing. Second, add structure and MSA ablations to test whether ESM-Effect can match PreMode without AlphaFold2 structures and MSAs, or whether a multimodal ESM-Effect variant is needed; this is motivated by [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), and [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), which show that spatial structure and MSA information can improve mutation-effect prediction. Third, adopt the evaluation safeguards recommended by [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) and [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145): hold out final test data, use validation or five-fold cross-validation for hyperparameter selection, report protein-level or sequence-distance-controlled splits, and include the nine-DMS and larger 42-DMS or ProteinGym-style generalization checks. Fourth, include small-data and augmentation ablations inspired by [(Wirnsberger et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ecb830ab5c7900c159bf09a25b9405b2cf3674a7), testing training sizes such as 50, 100, 250, 500, 1,000, 2,000, and 6,000 entries, pre-training, data augmentation capped at 20,000 samples, and top-100 recall or budget-based ranking in addition to Spearman and rBME, while explicitly validating rBME on clustered, non-clustered, and rare gain-of-function regions. Fifth, report compute and architecture details as in [(Huang et al., 2016-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5694e46284460a648fe29117cbc55f6c9be3fa3c) and [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), including ESM2 model sizes, LoRA rank, unfrozen layers, regression-head variants, parameter counts, FLOPs, context length, batch size, learning rate, hardware, and seeds, so that the ablation over 35M to larger ESM2 variants is reproducible. Sixth, add calibration, uncertainty, or entropy analyses where appropriate, following [(Riesselman et al., 2017-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c9d0b51a1b13d0d7d8fa1cdc98b863f3ff2af8d9) and [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), and include pathogenicity or clinical baselines only as secondary context, as in [(Frazer et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0537c62e32299b8e9b4e3cac44d82ffa123cbbe1), because the primary goal is bidirectional functional effect prediction.

## Context: To measure realistic generalization to new biological contexts. Method: We will evaluate model generalization by testing on distinct sequence intervals rather than random splits, providing realistic measures of the model's ability to generalize to new biological contexts. We will analyze performance on structured versus disordered protein regions.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed sequence-interval and structured-region transfer assessment comes from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), which evaluates ESM-1v on subsequences and shows that position-specific entropy is significantly lower at binding sites and buried core residues than at surface residues, directly motivating interval-level and structured-region analyses for ESM-Effect. The next strongest support is [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), which uses a non-random topology-held-out CATH split with Gene3D leakage filtering and reports core versus surface sequence recovery of 72% versus 39%, showing that structured-region stratification can reveal generalization differences missed by aggregate metrics. [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5) supports moving beyond random splits by showing heldout Pfam-family accuracy is consistently lower than random-split accuracy, although it tests evolutionary families rather than sequence intervals. [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5) supports ESM fine-tuning and non-random protein-level holdouts, with NLR fine-tuning increasing held-out MaveDB micro-averaged Spearman from 0.478 to 0.503 and ProteinGym average Spearman from 0.331 to 0.396. [(McPartlon et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0776c5b63f009afb54ca0117331748ab0be745fb) supports structure-aware generalization, outperforming sequence-only PLMs on 12 DMS datasets and improving stability prediction for 8 of 10 mini-proteins, but it does not use interval splits. [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556) supports ESM2-based zero-shot mutation-effect benchmarking on 100 assays and 86 ProteinGym assays using Spearman and TPR at 5%, 25%, and 50%, while [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) supports fine-tuned Transformer mutation-effect prediction on 21,026 and 712,218 DMS measurements with Spearman evaluation, though it uses 80/20 mutation splits rather than intervals. Weaker support includes [(Alley et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06eb3c3ccae16fced2222f8a45877906f54f2164) for qualitative generalization of UniRep to de novo proteins and GFP fitness, [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8) for large-scale non-random generalization and confidence calibration on 617 million MGnify90 sequences with pLDDT-LDDT Pearson r = 0.79, and [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) for standardized ProteinGym DMS benchmarking, although the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative results.

### Contradictions

The strongest quantitative contradiction is that non-random structural holdouts can reveal overfitting rather than monotonic scaling: [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) reports that scaling GVP-GNN from 1M to 21M parameters on CATH alone reduced sequence recovery from 42.2% to 39.2% on a topology-held-out test set, while larger models benefited from 12 million predicted structures; this challenges the ESM-Effect assumption that larger ESM2 models will automatically show better transfer under realistic non-random splits. The next strongest tension is that the closest interval-related evidence is not quantitative: [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) evaluates ESM-1v subsequences and entropy but does not report interval length, overlap, split construction, or interval-level performance metrics, so distinct sequence intervals are not yet validated as a transfer metric. A further contradiction is that structured-region evidence mostly covers buried/surface or core/surface, not disordered regions: [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) reports 72% core versus 39% surface recovery, and [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) reports buried versus surface entropy, but neither provides disordered-region annotations or mutation-effect performance on intrinsically disordered intervals. [(McPartlon et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0776c5b63f009afb54ca0117331748ab0be745fb) contradicts a purely sequence-interval interpretation by showing zero-shot mutation-effect performance correlated with structural similarity to training data but was independent of sequence identity, implying interval splits must control for local structural context. [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5) cautions that lower perplexity on heldout language-modeling sets does not necessarily correspond to better downstream task performance, so interval-level sequence likelihood or entropy may not directly predict functional accuracy. Weaker contradictions include [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), whose 80/20 within-protein mutation splits do not establish that interval splits are necessary; [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), which uses protein-level holdouts rather than intervals; [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), whose confidence calibration is for structure prediction rather than DMS mutation effects; and [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12), whose supplied excerpt provides no quantitative ProteinGym results to support or refute interval-based transfer.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define the sequence-interval protocol explicitly and validate it against alternative splits: specify interval length, overlap, train/test interval assignment, leakage controls, and multiple random seeds, then compare ESM-Effect performance on interval splits with random mutation splits, protein/family holdouts, and topology/structure holdouts, using evidence from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), [(Rao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ec7c9b201fc1ce18b4e0131691c9418f519a71c5), [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), and [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5). The next priority is to make structured versus disordered analysis operational by annotating intervals with disorder predictors, core/surface or buried/exposure labels, binding-site labels, and structural similarity metrics, following [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), and [(McPartlon et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0776c5b63f009afb54ca0117331748ab0be745fb), and report performance separately for ordered, disordered, core, surface, and binding intervals. Third, expand evaluation metrics beyond Spearman to include the proposed rBME, TPR at 5%, 25%, and 50% from [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), auROC for pathogenicity subsets from [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), and confidence calibration from [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), with statistical significance and multiple seeds. Fourth, benchmark against relevant baselines and data sources, including fine-tuned ESM/Transformer DMS models from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), zero-shot ESM2/structure-aware models from [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556) and [(McPartlon et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0776c5b63f009afb54ca0117331748ab0be745fb), ProteinGym/MaveDB assays from [(Notin et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/832aa77be74d7d3a8b33b9abc0f48b8b7babac12) and [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), and de novo or remote contexts from [(Alley et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/06eb3c3ccae16fced2222f8a45877906f54f2164). Finally, report reproducibility details such as dataset sizes, split construction, hardware, hyperparameters, and random seeds, as partially provided by [(Tan et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b9be41a23d3f39181e84a47fa5b6abf80b744556), [(Lin et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c49a0912595a1cc70aab63524f64ed08c92194a8), and [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145).

## Context: To assess broader applicability and limitations of the framework. Method: We will conduct additional experiments including Test-Time-Training approaches and cross-protein family generalization tests to assess the broader applicability and limitations of our framework.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed validation method comes from [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), which directly evaluates cross-protein generalization of ESM-based variant effect predictors by fine-tuning ESM-1v ensemble checkpoints, ESM-1b, and ESM-2 on 142,696 normalized missense variants from 30 MaveDB proteins, using MMseqs2 20 percent coverage and 20 percent identity filtering, a 25-protein training split, and independent MaveDB, ProteinGym, and ClinVar benchmarks; it reports micro-averaged Spearman improvement from 0.478 to 0.503 on MaveDB, 0.331 to 0.396 on 24 ProteinGym assays, and auROC improvement from 0.891 to 0.902 on ClinVar, with the largest ESM-1b ProteinGym gain at 25.6 percent, supporting the plan's cross-protein family tests and ESM2 fine-tuning premise. The next strongest support is methodological: [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) recommends reserving an unseen test set, using validation for model selection, n-fold cross-validation for small training sets, and enforcing a minimum sequence distance between training and validation/test examples when testing cross-protein-family generalization, which directly justifies the proposed distance-aware family holdouts. [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) extends this OOD logic to structural topology, using CATH topology-level splits and TM-score filtering, and reports sequence recovery of 42.2 percent for a 1M-parameter GVP-GNN, 50.8 percent for GVP-GNN-large and 51.6 percent for GVP-Transformer after training on approximately 12 million AlphaFold2 UniRef50 structures, and 49.7 to 53.6 percent on a remote SARS-CoV-2 RBD, supporting the addition of structural/domain distance to the proposed cross-family evaluation. [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) supports the core ESM-Effect fine-tuning approach by fine-tuning a 34-layer Transformer on deep mutational scanning data with a regression objective over mutant and wildtype identities, evaluating Spearman rho under five-fold cross-validation and 80/20 splits across datasets containing 21,026 and 712,218 variant measurements, although it does not implement Test-Time-Training or explicit cross-family distance filtering. [(Sadeh et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b663fc4dfed59dde2469ff0de86a825b51b21dd) provides weaker but relevant support for broader applicability by zero-shot scoring 36 DMS datasets and testing Pfam family representation generalization for beta-lactamase, SH3, and WW using t-SNE and 1-nearest-neighbor error, with ESM1b-based peptide models trained for 25 epochs using Adam, learning rate 0.0001, inverse square-root decay, 10K warmup, and batch size 8192. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) supports the conceptual move toward adapting pretrained ESM models to new sequence contexts through ESM-1v log-odds mutation scoring and unsupervised fine-tuning or zero/few-shot transfer baselines, but it does not provide quantitative outcomes for TTT or cross-family mutation-effect regression. [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6) is the weakest direct support for Test-Time-Training because it studies inference-time MSA subsampling rather than training, yet it shows that test-time input diversity matters: diversity-maximizing selection surpassed ESM-1b with only 16 sequences, diversity-minimizing selection required 256 sequences, one high-diversity sequence outperformed 31 low-diversity sequences, and the MSA Transformer improved top-L long-range contact precision by 15 points, reaching 43.4 on CASP13-FM versus 45.7 for trRosetta. Finally, [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) supports transfer and self-supervised pretraining for variant effect prediction using an equivariant GNN, BLOSUM62-smoothed amino acid prediction, auxiliary SASA and B-factor losses with Pearson correlations of 0.884 and 0.791 and p-values below 0.001, and KL-divergence fine-tuning on DMS, but it is structure-based rather than ESM2-based and does not test TTT or explicit cross-family holdouts.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that none of the related works directly validates Test-Time-Training for ESM2-based functional mutation-effect prediction, and the closest evidence shows that uncontrolled adaptation can hurt out-of-distribution performance. [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) reports that scaling GVP-GNN from 1M to 21M parameters on CATH alone overfit and reduced sequence recovery from 42.2 percent to 39.2 percent, and that adding predicted structures to the 1M model reduced recovery to 38.6 percent, with worse performance as more predicted structures were added; this contradicts the assumption that more test-time adaptation or larger adaptation on limited data will automatically improve ESM-Effect generalization. The next strongest caution comes from [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6), where test-time input selection strongly affects performance: diversity-minimizing MSA selection required 256 sequences to surpass ESM-1b, random selection was intermediate, and one high-diversity sequence outperformed 31 low-diversity sequences, implying that a naive TTT procedure using arbitrary, redundant, or locally similar test-time sequences may fail to improve cross-protein-family performance. [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825) also contradicts a simplistic interpretation of transfer capability by emphasizing that cross-family generalization requires validation and test examples to be at least a minimum sequence distance away from all training examples; if the proposed plan relies only on distinct sequence intervals or random splits, it may overestimate generalization. [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5) provides a more nuanced contradiction to the expectation that cross-protein adaptation will yield large uniform gains: MaveDB micro-averaged Spearman improved only from 0.478 to 0.503, a 5.2 percent relative gain, and ClinVar auROC improved only from 0.891 to 0.902, a 1.23 percent relative gain, even though ProteinGym improved from 0.331 to 0.396, suggesting that TTT or cross-protein fine-tuning may have limited benefit on some functional-effect benchmarks. [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) is less directly contradictory but shows that earlier DMS evaluation used five-fold cross-validation and 80/20 protein-level splits rather than explicit cross-family distance filtering, so its cross-protein results should not be read as evidence for the stricter family-generalization tests proposed here. [(Sadeh et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b663fc4dfed59dde2469ff0de86a825b51b21dd) weakens the evidence base because its Pfam family generalization test is representation-level, using t-SNE and 1-nearest-neighbor error on beta-lactamase, SH3, and WW, rather than mutation-effect regression, and its zero-shot DMS results are not numerically detailed in the excerpt. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) provides no quantitative outcomes for unsupervised fine-tuning, zero-shot, or few-shot transfer, so it cannot support the proposed TTT claim. Finally, [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff) does not contradict ESM-Effect directly, but its structure-based equivariant GNN and auxiliary structural objectives suggest that a purely sequence-centric ESM2 framework may miss structural inductive biases that can improve variant effect prediction.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the cross-protein family generalization tests explicit and leakage-controlled, following [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825), [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f), and [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5): define held-out protein families, domains, or CATH topologies, enforce minimum sequence distance or MMseqs2 20 percent coverage and 20 percent identity filtering, optionally add TM-score filtering, and report per-family Spearman and rBME rather than only aggregate scores. The second most important action is to define Test-Time-Training as a controlled experiment rather than an untested assumption, using self-supervised objectives such as masked language modeling, log-odds consistency, or auxiliary structural predictions inspired by [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff), with limited adaptation steps, early stopping on a validation subset, and comparisons against zero-shot, static-embedding, and fine-tuned ESM2 baselines; because [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6) shows test-time input diversity matters and [(Hsu et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f9c95ea9bcd529309c1d743f0926b5137f8ed60f) shows overfitting can reduce OOD performance, the TTT protocol should vary input diversity, adaptation length, and regularization, and report when TTT degrades performance. The third action is to standardize DMS normalization and evaluation, adopting the log-odds normalization used by [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5) where synonymous variants have mean 0 and nonsense variants mean -1 with values capped in [-2, 2], using Spearman correlation as in [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) and [(Sadeh et al., 2022-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7b663fc4dfed59dde2469ff0de86a825b51b21dd), adding the proposed rBME metric, and using n-fold cross-validation or repeated splits for small datasets as recommended by [(Yang et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/730fba26faa7f91dc6742a0c3521eb439670a825). The fourth action is to include strong baselines and ablations that isolate the contribution of TTT and cross-family generalization, including ESM2 model sizes, LoRA and partial fine-tuning, the four regression heads, PreMode, ESM-1v/ESM-1b baselines from [(Lafita et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b87a2bc2f7e8395fcea89285a3909e5ee9d8f7e5), structure-based variant effect predictors from [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff), and MSA-diversity controls from [(Rao et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deee48c5e0ac0407a1e002905caaf2b174bdb0e6). The least important but still necessary action is to report full reproducibility details, including dataset sizes, filtering thresholds, hardware, optimizer settings, batch sizes, learning rates, TTT steps, random seeds, and statistical significance, because several related works omit these details, as seen in [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145), [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7), and [(Zhou et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2c4830e18f47bb20ae51f20bca9fc55e712e93ff).

## Context: To ensure statistical reliability and comprehensive evaluation across all experiments. Method: All experiments will use consistent hyperparameters, multiple random seeds for statistical reliability, and both traditional metrics (Spearman correlation) and our proposed rBME metric for comprehensive evaluation.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) because it directly validates the core premise that fine-tuning a large protein Transformer with a task-specific regression head improves mutation-effect prediction on DMS data. It uses a 34-layer Transformer trained on UR50/S, adds a regression head for scaled mutational effects, evaluates on two DMS collections comprising 21,026 measurements from 9 scans and 712,218 mutations from 42 scans, uses 80/20 train-test splits, five-fold cross-validation, and Spearman rho, and reports that the fine-tuned Transformer outperformed a supervised structural/evolutionary/biochemical baseline on 10 of 12 proteins and outperformed fine-tuned LSTM baselines on the larger collection. This supports the proposed plan to fine-tune ESM2-based models, use DMS datasets, and retain Spearman correlation as a traditional metric. The weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) which shows that ESM-family single-sequence models can be used to score mutations by comparing mutant and wildtype amino acid probabilities, using a log odds ratio at the mutated position, and that ESM-1v entropy localizes binding sites and buried core residues. This supports the proposed inductive biases that effects are relative to wildtype and concentrated at mutation positions, and motivates comparing ESM2 against ESM-1v, TAPE, ProtBERT-BFD, UniRep, and MSA-based baselines. However, Meier et al. provide only qualitative evidence and do not benchmark the proposed evaluation protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work validates the proposed statistical-reliability protocol of multiple random seeds, fixed hyperparameters, and rBME. [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) does not report Spearman correlations, quantitative benchmark values, statistical significance, train-test splits, hyperparameters, random seeds, compute resources, or rBME; its outcomes are qualitative entropy and probability-distribution observations, so it cannot support the claim that ESM-family mutation-effect prediction is already comprehensively evaluated. The next strongest limitation is in [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) which reports five-fold cross-validation mean and standard deviation but does not provide multiple random seeds, per-protein Spearman values, statistical significance tests, exact fine-tuning hyperparameters, hardware, or rBME. This contradicts the assumption that prior DMS fine-tuning work establishes the statistical reliability that the proposed method seeks to ensure. A weaker contradiction is that Meier et al. assume additivity of position-wise log odds ratios for multiple mutations, whereas the proposed plan tests non-additive regression heads and relative wildtype/mutant embeddings; if the proposed method is to claim superiority, it must explicitly benchmark against this additive baseline rather than treating it as equivalent. Finally, both papers omit implementation details such as hardware and exact hyperparameters, which weakens the reproducibility foundation for the proposed claim of consistent hyperparameters across all experiments.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to operationalize the proposed statistical protocol by fixing and documenting hyperparameters, random seeds, train-test splits, hardware, and optimization settings for every ESM2 size, fine-tuning strategy, and regression head, then reporting per-protein Spearman and rBME with confidence intervals, paired significance tests, and seed-level variance. Second, validate rBME as a meaningful metric by showing its relationship to Spearman, its sensitivity to rare gain-of-function and non-clustered DMS regions, and its behavior on the AAV, GB1, GFP, SNCA, NUDT15, and PTEN datasets, including the same nine DMS splits used by PreMode. Third, adopt the strongest evaluation practices from [(Rives et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18a93dc1558bf9d7534d0b416633cebaf75c1145) by using DMS collections, 80/20 or five-fold cross-validation where appropriate, and comparing against supervised, MSA-based, and fine-tuned LSTM baselines, while adding the missing multiple-seed and significance reporting. Fourth, include the log-odds ratio scoring from [(Meier et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0cfce36622a017a86c2248ebe5dfdd0f8d643df7) as an explicit ESM-family baseline, test its additivity assumption for multiple mutations against the proposed relative wildtype and mutation-position regression heads, and use its entropy/position analyses to interpret whether ESM2 captures binding-site and buried-core constraints. Finally, report computational resources and ablation details so that the consistent-hyperparameter claim is reproducible and the rBME metric can be independently audited.
