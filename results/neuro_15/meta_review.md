## To compare auditory learning and cortical processing between adolescent and adult mice, we will employ a comprehensive approach combining behavioral training, electrophysiological recordings, and optogenetic manipulations.

### Support

The strongest direct support for the proposed optogenetic component comes from [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125), whose section headings indicate that optogenetic inactivation of auditory cortex impaired detection of acoustic signals in noise and that cortical inhibitory neurons contributed to signal detection. This is the closest available evidence to the plan's use of AAV-mediated inhibitory opsins and bilateral optical fiber implants to test whether auditory cortex is causally necessary for auditory task performance, and it supports the general logic of pairing a behavioral auditory task with cortical silencing. The support is nevertheless limited because the excerpt provides no d-prime values, hit rates, false alarm rates, effect sizes, sample sizes, opsin/virus details, fiber placement, or statistical tests, and the task is detection in noise rather than the plan's pure-tone Go/No-Go discrimination. A second, more methodologically convergent support comes from [(Cruces-Solís et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a149aa6bb67d031b494d792fe9e4b448abf995f7), which combined auditory discrimination behavior, single-unit electrophysiology, ROC analysis, classification-accuracy modeling, and causal cortical inactivation while examining frequency coding and discrimination. This supports the plan's integration of behavioral training, electrophysiological recordings, and causal manipulation, and especially its use of ROC-based single-neuron discriminability and population-level decoding to link neural coding to behavior. It also supports the plan's passive listening and naive-versus-expert comparisons because predictable and random sound exposure produced long-lasting changes in inferior colliculus response gain, best frequency, excitation/inhibition balance, and frequency coding. However, this support is weaker for the specific adolescent-versus-adult design because the paper does not report age groups, Neuropixels recordings, layer 5/6 analysis, pure-tone Go/No-Go training, or optogenetic silencing, and it focuses on subcortical inferior colliculus plasticity rather than auditory cortical learning.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension with the proposed method comes from [(Cruces-Solís et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a149aa6bb67d031b494d792fe9e4b448abf995f7), which reported that predictable sound exposure decreased behavioral spontaneous frequency discrimination acuity while producing long-lasting inferior colliculus changes, and that corticofugal input had only a minor role in that collicular plasticity. This does not directly contradict the plan's active Go/No-Go learning paradigm, but it challenges two assumptions embedded in the plan: first, that auditory exposure or learning will monotonically improve discrimination acuity, and second, that auditory cortex is the dominant causal site for all auditory frequency discrimination changes. If subcortical mechanisms can drive frequency coding changes and even reduce acuity, then differences between adolescent and adult mice could arise from peripheral, cochlear, or brainstem maturation rather than cortical learning, and auditory cortical silencing may underestimate or misattribute the source of behavioral change. A weaker but important limitation is that [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125) provides only heading-level evidence, with no quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, statistical significance, task parameters, noise levels, opsin details, or recording hardware. Therefore it cannot validate the plan's specific metrics, such as d-prime, hit rates, false alarm rates, response bias, or the expected magnitude of cortical silencing effects, nor can it support the plan's focus on adolescent versus adult mice, pure-tone discrimination, Neuropixels recordings, or infragranular layer 5/6 analysis. Both related works also lack the detailed reproducibility information needed to benchmark the proposed method, including species/age/sex, sample size, electrode types, sound frequencies, exposure durations, hyperparameters, and statistical thresholds.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the optogenetic causal test quantitatively rigorous and directly comparable to the behavioral metrics in the plan. The experiment should pre-register or report d-prime, hit rate, false alarm rate, response bias, and trial-by-trial variability before and after bilateral auditory cortex silencing, with control virus injections, randomized 50% light trials, and explicit light intensity, opsin expression, fiber placement, and recording/behavioral validation. Because [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125) links auditory cortex and inhibitory neurons to signal detection in noise, the plan should include a pure-tone detection or detection-in-noise baseline, or at least a sensory threshold control, to separate basic auditory sensitivity from learned discrimination, and it should consider whether inhibitory cortical populations contribute to the silencing effect. The second priority is to address the subcortical confound highlighted by [(Cruces-Solís et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a149aa6bb67d031b494d792fe9e4b448abf995f7) by measuring spontaneous frequency discrimination acuity, passive tone responses, and, if feasible, inferior colliculus or cochlear nucleus responses in both age groups before and after training. This would allow the plan to distinguish adolescent cortical learning from age-related maturation or exposure-induced subcortical plasticity, and it would justify interpreting auditory cortical silencing as causal for task performance rather than for general auditory sensitivity. The third priority is to specify the statistical and reproducibility details that the related work lacks: sample sizes per age/sex/housing group, exact Neuropixels probe model and sampling rate, target coordinates for dorsal, primary, ventral, and temporal association cortex, layer 5/6 segmentation criteria, tone frequencies and sound pressure levels, training difficulty progression, mixed-effects model random effects, multiple-comparison correction, and effect-size reporting for ROC, LDA, and behavioral metrics.

## To test whether adolescence affects auditory discrimination learning, we will train adolescent mice (starting at postnatal day 20-37) and adult mice (starting at postnatal day 60-77) on a Go/No-Go pure tone discrimination task with two difficulty levels.

Finally, add passive listening frequency tuning across 4-40 kHz and optogenetic auditory cortex silencing with control virus groups, because the related work does not establish causal necessity or basic tuning, and document all hardware, stimulus, hyperparameter, and statistical details to improve reproducibility.

## To characterize initial behavior and enable simultaneous neural recordings, we will use two complementary behavioral paradigms: an automated home-cage system (Educage) for initial behavioral characterization, and a head-fixed paradigm that enables simultaneous neural recordings during task performance.

A weaker but relevant support is that the paper reports learning-induced changes in A1 population responses and parvalbumin-positive neuron plasticity during perceptual learning, which supports the plan's comparison of naive and expert mice and passive listening to assess learning-induced plasticity in frequency response areas. However, the supplied excerpt does not provide numerical effect sizes, sample sizes, p-values, probe type, animal age, or stimulus parameters, so the support is conceptual and methodological rather than quantitative.

### Contradictions

The most important limitation, and the closest thing to a contradiction, is that [(Maor et al., 2018-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/839c94cb5e156c03434892f926f0c6280cd18471) does not provide quantitative evidence that Educage home-cage performance reliably predicts or transfers to head-fixed task performance, nor does it report d-prime, hit rates, false alarm rates, response bias, or behavioral variability metrics that the proposed plan relies on. This weakens the assumption that Educage can be used as a sufficient initial behavioral gate before head-fixed Neuropixels recordings. Finally, because only one related work is supplied, there is no iterative older-to-newer contrast to show that newer evidence has resolved the uncertainty about Educage-to-head-fixed transfer or adolescent auditory learning.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a pilot validation of the Educage-to-head-fixed transition: train a small, power-analyzed cohort of adolescent and adult mice in Educage, then test the same animals head-fixed, and report effect sizes, d-prime, hit and false alarm rates, response bias, trial-to-trial variability, and the correlation between home-cage and head-fixed performance before committing to large-scale Neuropixels recordings. The next priority is to make the age comparison explicit and reproducible by pre-registering the postnatal-day windows, sample sizes, sex balance, housing counterbalancing, and linear mixed-effects models with random effects for animal, repeated sessions, and co-housed animals, because the related work lacks age and sample-size details. For neural recordings, the method should explicitly target layers 5 and 6 with Neuropixels, report probe placement, channel counts, and sorting quality, and include passive listening with the 4 to 40 kHz tone protocol to establish frequency response areas before and after learning. Finally, the optogenetic silencing component should be strengthened with control virus injections, randomized 50 percent light trials, and pre-specified performance drop thresholds, since the related work provides no causal manipulation evidence.

## To quantify task performance, we will measure behavioral performance using signal detection theory metrics (d-prime) and analyze response bias, hit rates, false alarm rates, and behavioral variability.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method is that [(Heimburg et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5270415281244f7377afa2f437b90c2ccba5676c) used a sensory discrimination task in freely moving mice in which correct and incorrect responses could be mapped onto hit and false-alarm categories, directly supporting the use of signal-detection-theory metrics such as d-prime, hit rate, false-alarm rate, and response bias to separate sensory discrimination from response tendency in the proposed Go/No-Go pure-tone task. A weaker but still relevant support is that the same study combined behavioral training with electrophysiological and optical recordings and included sensory-deprivation and lesion controls, which is conceptually aligned with the research plan's integration of behavioral performance, Neuropixels recordings, and optogenetic causal tests. The weakest support is that the related work demonstrates only the general feasibility of SDT-style quantification in a rodent sensory discrimination paradigm, not the specific auditory modality, head-fixed setup, two-tone difficulty levels, or adolescent/adult comparison proposed here.

### Contradictions

There is no direct contradiction in [(Heimburg et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5270415281244f7377afa2f437b90c2ccba5676c) against using SDT metrics, but the strongest limitation is that the provided excerpt does not report exact hit-rate, false-alarm-rate, d-prime, bias, or statistical significance values, so it cannot validate the expected effect sizes, performance thresholds, or statistical power for the proposed behavioral metrics. A secondary limitation is that the study used freely moving mice performing whisker-mediated aperture discrimination and rule learning, rather than head-fixed mice performing auditory Go/No-Go pure-tone discrimination, so the behavioral and neural constraints differ. A further limitation is that the related work does not address the proposed two difficulty levels, naive versus expert comparisons, or adolescent versus adult age groups, meaning it provides only indirect evidence that the planned SDT analysis will reliably distinguish age-related sensory changes from learning-related response-bias changes.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to operationalize the SDT analysis for the Go/No-Go task by explicitly defining hits as Go responses on Go-tone trials, false alarms as Go responses on No-Go-tone trials, correct rejects as no-Go on No-Go trials, and misses as no-Go on Go trials, then computing d-prime, response bias, hit rate, false-alarm rate, and trial-by-trial variability for each mouse, day, and difficulty level. The next priority is to use linear mixed-effects models with random effects for mouse, day, task difficulty, age group, sex, and housing, and to report confidence intervals, effect sizes, and statistical significance for d-prime and bias, while testing whether age differences in d-prime remain after controlling for response bias. A further action is to predefine expert-level criteria and conduct a power analysis or pilot calibration based on expected d-prime and variability, because [(Heimburg et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5270415281244f7377afa2f437b90c2ccba5676c) does not provide quantitative SDT outcomes. Additional improvements include adding behavioral controls such as randomized Go/No-Go trial order, catch or no-reward trials, lick-port calibration, and reporting reaction-time or inter-trial variability to reduce motor, attention, and reward-history confounds. Finally, align the behavioral SDT trial categories with the planned neural analyses by using hits, false alarms, correct rejects, and misses as trial types for ROC and LDA decoding, and document implementation details such as zero-rate handling, bias estimator, software, and statistical assumptions to improve reproducibility.

This supports the research plan’s use of pre-learning and post-learning neural recordings, ROC-based single-neuron discriminability, and population-level decoding, although the related work did not itself use LDA, because it demonstrates that experience can produce measurable, metric-quantifiable shifts in auditory coding that can be separated from baseline processing. Finally, the region-specific finding that predictable exposure produced collicular effects with minor corticofugal involvement supports the plan’s need for region-specific recordings and controls, although it does not directly support an auditory cortical focus.

The strongest contradiction is that [(Cruces-Solís et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a149aa6bb67d031b494d792fe9e4b448abf995f7) does not test the proposed naive-versus-expert, adolescent-versus-adult design: it used passive predictable or random sound exposure rather than active Go/No-Go pure-tone discrimination training, and it did not include age groups comparable to the proposed P20-37 adolescent or P60-77 adult windows, trained expert mice, auditory cortical layers 5/6, Neuropixels probes, or optogenetic silencing. A third contradiction is that the plasticity was localized to the inferior colliculus, with no reported changes in cochlear nucleus or anterior colliculus and only a minor role for corticofugal input, whereas the proposed method centers on auditory cortex and bilateral auditory cortical silencing; this suggests that the dominant exposure- or learning-related plasticity may be subcortical and that cortical silencing may not establish the full causal mechanism. A final limitation is that the excerpt lacks quantitative effect sizes, sample sizes, statistical significance, exact stimulus parameters, animal ages, probe types, and statistical thresholds, so it cannot justify the expected magnitude, power, or reproducibility of the proposed adolescent/adult naive-expert comparison.

It should also control for stimulus predictability and randomness in the Go/No-Go tone sequences, because predictable and random exposure produced different mechanisms and predictable exposure reduced spontaneous discrimination acuity. Finally, the method should specify sample sizes, effect sizes, statistical thresholds, mixed-effects random effects, and power analyses for adolescent and adult groups, because the related work lacks these quantitative details and the proposed hierarchical design requires them for reproducible inference.

## To quantify neural discriminability, we will analyze single neuron discriminability using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis to quantify stimulus-related activity (hits vs false alarms) and choice-related activity (false alarms vs correct rejects), and population-level analysis will employ linear discriminant analysis to decode trial outcomes from neural activity patterns.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed single-neuron ROC and population LDA discriminability method comes from [(Angeloni et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dcd1e526def9d2bfef0d1d294a8d61e5d0dca7f), which used a mouse target-in-background auditory detection task with simultaneous auditory cortical electrophysiology and explicitly assessed single-unit discriminability with ROC analysis separating target-present from target-absent trials, population-level trial-outcome decoding with a population classifier, and linear-nonlinear modeling linking population activity to behavior. This is the closest methodological precedent to the proposed auditory Go/No-Go plan because it uses auditory cortex, behavioral detection, and neural discriminability metrics in the same general sensory domain, and it supports the idea that auditory cortical codes can predict individual behavioral performance and that cortical gain is behaviorally relevant. A second, more general support comes from [(Rabinovich et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a5acee28afbd24515d2d3e436e29a3d6bdafdc), which showed in head-fixed mice that conditioning, but not repeated stimulus exposure, enhanced object representation and that training enhanced decodability of stimulus and choice in a discrimination task, supporting the proposed comparison of naive versus expert mice and the expectation that learning should alter population-level trial-outcome decodability. As an iterative sequence, the 2022 Rabinovich study extends the 2021 Angeloni auditory detection framework by emphasizing longitudinal learning and decodability, although it shifts to somatosensory discrimination. A weaker but useful support is that both studies motivate additional controls beyond simple LDA: [(Angeloni et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dcd1e526def9d2bfef0d1d294a8d61e5d0dca7f) used population response metrics and linear-nonlinear modeling, while [(Rabinovich et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a5acee28afbd24515d2d3e436e29a3d6bdafdc) emphasized temporal tuning, population time encoding, movement controls, and temporal surprise, all of which are relevant to the proposed Neuropixels recordings from auditory cortical layers 5 and 6 during Go/No-Go trials.

The strongest countervailing evidence is that neither related work provides the quantitative validation needed to confirm the proposed ROC and LDA pipeline: the [(Angeloni et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dcd1e526def9d2bfef0d1d294a8d61e5d0dca7f) excerpt reports no numerical ROC AUC values, classifier accuracy, d-prime values, hit rates, false alarm rates, statistical significance, animal numbers, probe type, or statistical thresholds, and the [(Rabinovich et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a5acee28afbd24515d2d3e436e29a3d6bdafdc) excerpt reports no numerical ROC areas, LDA decoding accuracies, d-prime values, hit rates, false alarm rates, statistical significance, sample sizes, age ranges, imaging frame rates, decoding hyperparameters, or statistical thresholds. This absence means the proposed method is plausible but not directly benchmarked against chance, effect size, or statistical reliability. The next strongest limitation is that the newer [(Rabinovich et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a5acee28afbd24515d2d3e436e29a3d6bdafdc) study is not a direct test of the proposed method because it uses whisker-based object detection, barrel cortex, longitudinal 2-photon calcium imaging, virus injection, cranial window implants, and histological confirmation rather than Neuropixels electrophysiology in auditory cortical layers 5 and 6, and it does not explicitly state that ROC analysis or LDA were used; therefore its evidence for stimulus and choice decodability cannot be assumed to transfer to fast auditory pure-tone Go/No-Go discrimination in adolescent versus adult mice. A weaker limitation is that [(Angeloni et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dcd1e526def9d2bfef0d1d294a8d61e5d0dca7f) used a target-in-background detection task rather than the proposed Go/No-Go pure-tone discrimination, did not compare adolescent and adult mice, did not specify infragranular layers 5 and 6, and did not include optogenetic silencing, so its support for the exact proposed age-by-learning design is indirect.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to pre-register a fully quantitative ROC and LDA validation protocol for the proposed auditory Go/No-Go task, specifying trial definitions for hits, false alarms, correct rejects, and omissions or misses; time windows relative to tone onset and response; baseline subtraction; cross-validation scheme; chance-level permutation tests; multiple-comparison correction; and reported metrics such as AUC, LDA accuracy, confusion matrices, effect sizes, and confidence intervals, because the related works lack these numerical benchmarks. The next most important action is to align the neural discriminability analysis with the behavioral signal detection theory plan by correlating single-neuron ROC AUC and population LDA accuracy with behavioral d-prime, hit rate, false alarm rate, and response bias across adolescent and adult groups, easy and hard tone separations, and naive versus expert stages, using linear mixed-effects models with random effects for animal, repeated measurements, co-housing, and sex. A further improvement is to add the controls and complementary analyses suggested by the related work: include population response metrics and linear-nonlinear or GLM modeling as in [(Angeloni et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8dcd1e526def9d2bfef0d1d294a8d61e5d0dca7f), and control for temporal dynamics, movement, trial history, and temporal surprise as in [(Rabinovich et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e2a5acee28afbd24515d2d3e436e29a3d6bdafdc), while also reporting layer-specific results for auditory cortical layers 5 and 6. Finally, the plan should add power analyses, sample-size justification, probe and layer targeting details, decoding hyperparameters, and optogenetic control-virus comparisons to ensure that the causal silencing experiments and age-by-learning comparisons are reproducible and not confounded by recording or training differences.

## To establish causal necessity of auditory cortex, we will use optogenetic silencing of auditory cortex, employing AAV-mediated expression of inhibitory opsins with bilateral optical fiber implants, and test task performance by applying light stimulation in 50% of trials in a randomized fashion.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed optogenetic silencing method comes from [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125), which used the same core causal-test architecture as the plan: bilateral AAV-mediated expression of an inhibitory opsin in mouse auditory cortex, bilateral optical fiber implants, and light delivered on a randomized subset of trials during a trained auditory task. That study compared light and no-light trials with signal detection theory metrics, including d-prime, hit rate, and false alarm rate, and found that auditory cortex inactivation impaired signal-in-noise detection, directly supporting the plan's use of d-prime to test whether auditory cortex is necessary for pure-tone Go/No-Go discrimination in adolescent and adult mice. A weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Lohse et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/921a54ca38f3d7d51e00acc143b8df96a75f6cca), which established that optogenetic silencing of mouse auditory cortex can causally modulate auditory processing, although it focused on subcortical excitability and reliability rather than behavioral task performance and did not report the AAV construct, opsin, fiber placement, light wavelength or intensity, or trial randomization details. Together, these works form an iterative progression: Lohse et al. demonstrated that auditory cortex silencing is a feasible causal manipulation in mice, while Lakunina et al. extended the approach to trained auditory behavior with randomized light/no-light trials and detection-theory metrics, making the proposed 50% randomized light design and d-prime-based evaluation methodologically credible.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the proposed use of bilateral AAV inhibitory-opsin silencing with randomized light to test auditory cortex necessity. The most consequential tension is from [(Lohse et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/921a54ca38f3d7d51e00acc143b8df96a75f6cca), which reported that auditory cortex silencing modulated subcortical excitability and reliability but left subcortical contrast gain control independent of cortex; this suggests that auditory cortex may not be necessary for all auditory computations, so the plan should avoid overgeneralizing from Go/No-Go performance to basic auditory processing or subcortical tuning. A weaker limitation is that [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125) used a signal-in-noise detection task rather than the plan's pure-tone Go/No-Go discrimination, and the excerpt does not provide exact d-prime values, effect sizes, p-values, sample sizes, or light parameters, so the expected magnitude of impairment and statistical power for the proposed adolescent/adult comparison cannot be benchmarked. This is not a contradiction, but it means the proposed method's effect-size assumptions and age-specific predictions remain underconstrained by the cited evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to adopt the exact behavioral evaluation framework used by [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125): randomize light and no-light trials across 50% of trials, analyze d-prime, hit rate, false alarm rate, and response bias separately, and use linear mixed-effects models with random effects for repeated measurements, animals, and co-housing to test age, learning, and light effects. Next, the plan should specify the optogenetic implementation in enough detail to reproduce the bilateral silencing approach: AAV serotype, inhibitory opsin, expression level, bilateral fiber coordinates over dorsal, primary, ventral auditory cortex, and temporal association cortex as relevant, light wavelength, intensity, pulse train, and verification of opsin expression and silencing efficacy. A further improvement is to include control virus injections and, if feasible, a cell-type-specific silencing experiment or at least interpret whole-cortex silencing cautiously, because [(Lakunina et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/899b45cec4747745c5fe3329adb6540462429125) showed that inactivating cortical inhibitory neurons also impaired detection, implying that whole-cortex silencing may conflate excitatory and inhibitory circuit contributions. Finally, drawing on [(Lohse et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/921a54ca38f3d7d51e00acc143b8df96a75f6cca), the plan should pair behavioral silencing with passive listening and tuning or spectro-temporal receptive-field-like analyses to distinguish task-specific cortical necessity from changes in basic frequency tuning or subcortical reliability, and it should pre-register a power analysis once effect sizes from pilot light/no-light trials are available.

## To ensure valid inference, we will use appropriate control groups throughout, including control virus injections for optogenetic experiments, and will counterbalance factors such as sex and housing conditions, with statistical analyses accounting for hierarchical data structures using linear mixed-effects models with appropriate random effects for repeated measurements and co-housed animals.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80), which used awake, head-fixed mice in a two-alternative forced-choice task and applied bilateral blue-light activation of PV interneurons to inactivate auditory cortex. This directly mirrors the proposed optogenetic silencing of auditory cortex with bilateral optical fiber implants and randomized light delivery, and it supports the plan’s causal manipulation of auditory cortex during a discrimination task. That study also decomposed repeated choice behavior into task-dependent, stimulus-related, and experimental-variable contributions, which supports the proposed need for hierarchical modeling of repeated behavioral measurements and for separating stimulus, task, and experimental effects in the Go/No-Go pure tone discrimination task. A second, somewhat weaker but still relevant line of support is the newer [(Ding et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f4e1dba8aa5f53d6426c7514e5c4eed189116ba), which tested Cln3-/- and wild-type mice across 3 to 9 months and found sex- and age-dependent auditory duration MMN and AEP/ABR outcomes, including similar peripheral hearing but divergent central auditory processing. This supports the proposed counterbalancing of sex and age and the use of repeated auditory measurements, because the related work shows that auditory outcomes are not uniform across sex or age. The weakest support is inferential: neither paper fully specifies the exact mixed-effects model, random effects for co-housed animals, or counterbalancing details, so they only indirectly justify the proposed linear mixed-effects framework rather than directly validating its implementation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that neither related work provides direct evidence for the specific statistical design proposed, namely linear mixed-effects models with random effects for repeated measurements and co-housed animals; [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80) reports behavioral modeling of repeated choice measurements but does not specify the mixed-effects structure, and the newer [(Ding et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f4e1dba8aa5f53d6426c7514e5c4eed189116ba) organizes analysis by sex and age but does not report random effects, housing controls, or model diagnostics. A second contradiction is the absence of quantitative validation: both excerpts lack effect sizes, p-values, sample sizes, and baseline comparisons, so they cannot demonstrate that the proposed control groups, counterbalancing, and hierarchical models will yield sufficient power or valid inference for the plan’s d-prime, hit rate, false alarm rate, and ROC/LDA neural metrics. A third, weaker contradiction is that [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80) found optogenetic auditory cortex inactivation produced perceptual hyposensitivity rather than a fully specified task-performance impairment, which means the proposed 50% randomized silencing trials may need careful interpretation because silencing could alter perceived tone intensity or salience as well as discrimination. Finally, [(Ding et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f4e1dba8aa5f53d6426c7514e5c4eed189116ba) shows sex-specific and age-dependent central auditory deficits while peripheral hearing remained similar, which does not contradict the plan but warns that sex and age may interact with learning and neural decoding, making a simple main-effects analysis insufficient.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to pre-specify the linear mixed-effects models before data collection, including fixed effects for age group, sex, housing condition, virus control, task difficulty, trial number or session, and their interactions, with random intercepts for animal and, where applicable, random effects for co-housed animals or cage, and random slopes for repeated behavioral or electrophysiological measurements. This should be paired with a power analysis and reporting of variance components, model fit, and sensitivity checks for co-housing random effects, because neither [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80) nor [(Ding et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f4e1dba8aa5f53d6426c7514e5c4eed189116ba) provides a template for that exact design. The next priority is to strengthen the optogenetic control design by including control virus injections, sham light or no-light trials, and explicit counterbalancing of sex and housing, following the direct optogenetic auditory cortex inactivation paradigm in [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80) while adding the missing counterbalancing and statistical details. Because [(Ding et al., 2025-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3f4e1dba8aa5f53d6426c7514e5c4eed189116ba) demonstrates sex- and age-dependent auditory MMN and AEP/ABR trajectories, the plan should include age-by-sex interactions in behavioral d-prime, electrophysiological tuning, and Neuropixels decoding analyses, and should report peripheral hearing controls to distinguish central auditory processing changes from peripheral hearing differences. Finally, the team should report quantitative outcomes, sample sizes, effect sizes, and statistical significance for each control and counterbalanced factor, and use the behavioral decomposition approach from [(McGill et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/55296beb5a9bb7439a8f2eb18a123b6b62bd6d80) to separate task-dependent, stimulus-related, and experimental-variable contributions in the Go/No-Go discrimination task.
