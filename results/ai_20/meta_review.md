## Context: This is the central proposed model that formalizes prioritized capacity sharing, stochastic capacities, rewards, and movement costs. Method: We will develop the MSB-PRS (Multiple-play Stochastic Bandits with Prioritized Resource Sharing) framework consisting of:

**Model Components:**
- K plays, each with priority weight αk and movement cost vector ck
- M arms, each with stochastic capacity Dm and per-unit reward distribution Rm
- Prioritized capacity sharing where plays are ranked by priority weights in descending order
- Utility function combining weighted rewards minus movement costs

### Support

The related work forms an iterative line that supports MSB-PRS. The strongest support is [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a), which already studies multiple-play stochastic bandits with arm capacity sharing, stochastic rewards, bipartite-graph/matching action profiles, and UCB-based OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v, using the same synthetic scope as the plan (M=5, K=10, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1, sigma=0.2, Inc/Dec/U-Shape patterns, movement-cost scale, and priority weights as an extension). This directly validates the plan's core decomposition into matching-based optimization plus UCB exploration and justifies using OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v as baselines. A second strong support is [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), which shows that multiple-play allocation with unknown integer capacities is learnable, that capacity estimation requires Omega((m_k / mu_k^2) log(1/delta)) explorations, and that OrchExplore with uniform confidence intervals outperformed Hoeffding-based variants and baselines in a K=9, N=7, 200-realization simulation; this supports the plan's stochastic-capacity component and suggests that capacity learning should be treated as a first-class regret term. A third strong support is [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), which establishes the multiple-play top-L Bernoulli bandit formulation and compares MP-TS, CUCB, MP-KL-UCB, and Exp3.M, confirming that suppressing simultaneous selection of suboptimal arms is central to multiple-play regret; this supports the plan's move from symmetric top-L selection to prioritized capacity sharing. Cost-aware evidence from [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c) supports the plan's movement-cost term: KUBE and fractional KUBE use reward-to-cost UCB indices over a knapsack action set, achieve O(log(B/c_min)) regret with regret/log(B/c_min) constants roughly 4 to 7, and report 95% confidence intervals, with KUBE up to 40% lower regret than fractional KUBE in moderately diverse costs and up to 70% lower regret than epsilon-first baselines. Resource-constrained UCB is further supported by [(Badanidiyuru et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5df142536d1b1e80930b5ad1f22db62d41d37e3a), whose PrimalDualBwK uses dual resource-cost estimates and UCB/LCB indices to obtain sublinear regret under budgets, and by [(Basu et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9efcde6c193fec4818f268837c7a8a330dcdc799), which shows UCB-LP delayed exploitation can handle availability and delays. Combinatorial UCB precedent is provided by [(Chen et al., 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40640d997dab261d37743e1bffc09273c9937a) and [(Chen et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b50cee7c4ce8d576cb98e2da42d82b979eac4ff), where CUCB and SDCB/Lazy-SDCB use oracle-based optimistic selection and distribution discretization to control computation, while [(Ayyagari, and Dukkipati, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eecc7b123a2aa44d878e241094f042c991f95a41) supports discretization as a way to avoid combinatorial distribution-support blowup. Finally, the foundational UCB evidence from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), including UCB1, UCB1-TUNED, UCB2, and epsilon_n-greedy on 100,000-play synthetic Bernoulli instances averaged over 100 runs, supports the plan's use of confidence bounds and synthetic regret evaluation, while [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) and [(Zhou, and Tomlin, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab310584ba1e68090acc4c257aed475ae78058f0) support the need for asymptotically optimal UCB coefficients and budgeted multiple-play regret theory.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not that MSB-PRS is impossible, but that the closest prior work, [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a), already provides OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v for multiple-play stochastic capacities and matching-based UCB allocation, so the proposed priority weights and movement costs must be shown to produce quantitative regret or runtime gains beyond that homogeneous-play baseline; the excerpt provides no numerical regret values, making the claimed advantage unverified. A second strong contradiction/limitation comes from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), whose lower bound and simulation show that learning capacities is materially more costly than estimating reward means, and that uniform confidence intervals outperformed Hoeffding-based intervals; if the plan's approximate UCB relies on fixed Hoeffding-style bounds and does not explicitly allocate exploration to capacity estimation, it may be suboptimal in the stochastic-capacity regime. A third important limitation is from [(Basu et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9efcde6c193fec4818f268837c7a8a330dcdc799), which shows that UCB-LP decisions can be correlated with arm availability unless delayed exploitation is used; if MSB-PRS uses current UCB values directly with stochastic capacities D_m and matching, the regret proof may need additional decorrelation or delayed matching. A fourth limitation is computational: [(Chen et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b50cee7c4ce8d576cb98e2da42d82b979eac4ff) and [(Ayyagari, and Dukkipati, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eecc7b123a2aa44d878e241094f042c991f95a41) show that combinatorial distribution-based UCB can suffer memory/time blowup unless discretization or lazy updates are used, and Ayyagari and Dukkipati require known horizon T for epsilon selection; the plan's T=10^4 synthetic setting is compatible, but the method must specify how stochastic capacity/reward distributions are represented without exhaustive search. A fifth limitation is that cost-aware UCB evidence from [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c) is for deterministic costs and single-play budgeted knapsack selection, so its reward-to-cost density indices do not automatically validate priority-dependent movement costs c_{k,m} or stochastic capacities; the plan must prove that movement costs can be incorporated into the UCB index without breaking logarithmic regret. A sixth limitation is from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), where UCB1-TUNED substantially outperformed UCB1 and epsilon_n-greedy was sensitive to per-distribution tuning; this warns against using a fixed Hoeffding variance term or un-tuned exploration in the proposed approximate UCB. Weaker but relevant contradictions include [(Badanidiyuru et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5df142536d1b1e80930b5ad1f22db62d41d37e3a) and [(Zhou, and Tomlin, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab310584ba1e68090acc4c257aed475ae78058f0), which already establish resource/budget-constrained bandit regret theory, so the plan's lower bounds must be stronger than generic budgeted MP-MAB bounds; [(Chen et al., 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40640d997dab261d37743e1bffc09273c9937a) relies on an oracle and does not address capacity or movement costs; and [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) assumes a factorized slot-arm reward model that is not the plan's prioritized capacity-sharing utility.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should benchmark directly against OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v from [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a) under the exact default settings M=5, K=10, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1, sigma=0.2, and verify that MSB-PRS reduces to OnlinActPrf when all priority weights are equal and movement costs are zero; report cumulative regret, regret divided by log T or log(B/c_min), 95% confidence intervals, random seeds, runtime, and memory for M in {5,10,15} and K in {10,15,20}. Second, incorporate capacity-learning mechanisms from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), including parsimonious individual and united exploration, uniform confidence intervals rather than only Hoeffding bounds, a known-capacity baseline, and the Omega((m_k / mu_k^2) log(1/delta)) capacity-estimation lower bound in the theoretical analysis. Third, adapt cost-aware and resource-constrained UCB ideas from [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c), [(Badanidiyuru et al., 2013-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5df142536d1b1e80930b5ad1f22db62d41d37e3a), and [(Basu et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9efcde6c193fec4818f268837c7a8a330dcdc799) to movement costs and stochastic capacities: use reward-minus-movement-cost or reward-to-cost indices, dual/LP constraints for capacity, and delayed exploitation or delayed matching if stochastic D_m creates availability correlation. Fourth, make the bipartite matching oracle explicit and computationally bounded, following [(Chen et al., 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40640d997dab261d37743e1bffc09273c9937a), [(Chen et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1b50cee7c4ce8d576cb98e2da42d82b979eac4ff), and [(Ayyagari, and Dukkipati, 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eecc7b123a2aa44d878e241094f042c991f95a41): specify edge weights for marginal utility, prove polynomial-time matching, and use discretized CDFs or lazy updates if stochastic capacity/reward distributions cause support expansion. Fifth, include multiple-play and single-play baselines adapted to the special cases: MP-TS, MP-KL-UCB, CUCB, and Exp3.M from [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), plus UCB1, UCB1-TUNED, UCB2, and epsilon_n-greedy from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), with exact regret values and statistical significance rather than qualitative plots. Sixth, derive instance-dependent lower bounds using KL/asymptotic techniques from [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) and budgeted multiple-play theory from [(Zhou, and Tomlin, 2017-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab310584ba1e68090acc4c257aed475ae78058f0), and report how priority weights alpha_k, movement-cost scale eta, reward standard deviation sigma, and resource-reward correlation change the regret coefficient.

## Context: This method provides the combinatorial optimization representation needed to handle the nonlinear prioritized allocation structure. Method: **Bipartite Graph Formulation:** Model the problem as a weighted bipartite graph where nodes represent plays and arm-rank pairs, with edge weights capturing marginal utility contributions

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed bipartite graph formulation is the direct methodological precedent in [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a), which models offline prioritized allocation as a weighted bipartite graph with K plays on one side and M arm-rank pairs on the other, uses edge weights for marginal utility under priority weights, movement costs, and capacity constraints, connects action profiles to U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matchings, and applies an approximate UCB rule to avoid exhaustive search. Its synthetic MSB-PRS setup matches the research plan in detail: M=5, K=10, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1, sigma=0.2, variations M in {5,10,15}, K in {10,15,20}, eta in {1,2,10}, sigma in {0.1,0.2,0.3}, Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, and U-Shape rewards, movement costs ck,m = eta*|(k mod M)-m|/max{K,M}, priority weights half 3 and half 1, baselines OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v, and cumulative regret as the metric. This directly supports the plan's core representation, but the supplied excerpt reports no numerical regret values, confidence intervals, or significance tests, so the support is structural rather than empirical. The next strongest support is [(Combes et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a872dc9bc45cb5577c424d69dd40de8a45746a), which treats matching as a combinatorial bandit domain and uses UCB1/KL-UCB-style indexes with ESCB and EPOCH-ESCB to reduce computational complexity; this supports the plan's approximate UCB and matching-based optimization, although the excerpt gives only qualitative statements that ESCB-1 outperforms ESCB-2 and EPOCH-ESCB reduces complexity, without numerical regret curves, baselines, confidence intervals, or matching instance specifications. The next support is [(Esfandiari et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7892ac74a24ace1eaab47c69d8e4c2e350c29537), which uses a bipartite user-arm graph and decomposes batched assignments into C-anonymous matchings, with a greedy construction having approximation factor alpha=K and regret O~(N C K sqrt(T)) under U >= C+1, B = log log T batches, and 2C+2 feedback rounds; this supports the broader idea that bipartite matching/decomposition can be integrated into bandit learning with theoretical regret, but its anonymity and degree constraints are not the plan's priority, movement-cost, and stochastic-capacity structure. Then [(Banerjee et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6650a2088ece1c0da330433adf8fde121aa544ad) supports the use of bipartite graphs for online allocation by showing that graph-topology-aware exploration matters: on complete bipartite graphs with binary rewards, no online algorithm exceeds competitive ratio n_U/(2n_I) for one recommendation or r n_U/(2n_I) for r recommendations, inverse-degree exploration attains r/(8e) on a constructed family, and deterministic exploit rules can be noncompetitive; however, it is theoretical, unweighted, and recommendation-specific. The next support is [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c), which shows that cost-aware structured allocation in budget-limited MABs can achieve logarithmic regret: KUBE and fractional KUBE use unbounded knapsack-style density ordering, normalized regret by log(B/c_min) converges to constant factors around 4 to 7, KUBE has up to 40% lower regret than fractional KUBE in moderately diverse costs and about 30% lower in extremely diverse costs, and both beat budget-limited epsilon-first baselines by up to 70% and 50%, respectively, on 100 arms with cost ranges [5,10], [1,10], and [1,20]; this supports the plan's emphasis on cost/resource constraints but does not include multiple plays, priority weights, movement costs, or stochastic capacities. The weakest support is [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2010-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958d0ee051e322afb35c73813a21ccdbeb8fe14c), which evaluates a budgeted-first multi-armed bandit with uniform exploration and reward-cost ratio exploitation on 6 arms, budgets 400 to 4000, Gaussian rewards with variance 0.1 and support [0,20], fixed costs, and 95% confidence intervals; it reports 50% lower total loss than modified epsilon-greedy in moderately diverse arms and 80% lower in extremely diverse arms, while UCB-based exploration did not significantly outperform uniform exploration, supporting cost-ratio allocation but only indirectly related to the proposed weighted play/arm-rank matching.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caveat is that bipartite matching/decomposition can introduce approximation and regret penalties that are not automatically sublinear or logarithmic. [(Esfandiari et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7892ac74a24ace1eaab47c69d8e4c2e350c29537) shows that a greedy bipartite anonymous decomposition has approximation factor K and yields expected regret O~(N C K sqrt(T)), with lower bounds tight up to K sqrt(C) and a necessary T^{2/3} dependence when no lower bound U is available; this contradicts any assumption that the proposed U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matching will automatically give efficient sublinear regret unless the plan proves that its matching approximation and approximate UCB error remain controlled under stochastic capacities and K=10 to 20 plays. The next strongest caveat is that a bipartite graph representation alone is not sufficient for good online performance. [(Banerjee et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6650a2088ece1c0da330433adf8fde121aa544ad) proves that deterministic exploit-when-possible and exploit-above-fixed-threshold rules on bipartite access graphs are noncompetitive, with competitive ratio smaller than any epsilon and threshold ratio 1/n on an n-by-n complete bipartite instance, while inverse-degree exploration attains r/(8e); this suggests that the proposed weighted bipartite formulation must be paired with topology-aware or index-based exploration, not merely solved as a static matching. The next contradiction is that simpler cost-aware knapsack methods may already capture much of the benefit of resource-constrained allocation. [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c) shows KUBE achieves logarithmic regret and up to 40% lower regret than fractional KUBE in moderately diverse costs, about 30% lower in extremely diverse costs, and up to 70% lower regret than budget-limited epsilon-first baselines on 100 arms; if the plan's priority weights, movement costs, and stochastic capacities do not create a genuinely non-knapsack structure, the bipartite matching formulation may add computational complexity without a corresponding regret advantage, and the paper's note that fractional KUBE has better computational complexity but higher empirical regret highlights a complexity-regret tradeoff. The next caveat is that exploration design may dominate the allocation representation. [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2010-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958d0ee051e322afb35c73813a21ccdbeb8fe14c) finds that a budgeted-first uniform-exploration algorithm with reward-cost ratio exploitation outperforms modified epsilon-greedy by 50% in moderately diverse arms and 80% in extremely diverse arms, while UCB-based exploration did not significantly outperform uniform exploration in 6-armed simulations with budgets 400 to 4000 and 95% confidence intervals; this does not refute the bipartite method, but it warns that the plan should not assume approximate UCB over matchings is automatically superior to simpler exploration. The weakest caveat is that the most directly relevant work lacks empirical validation: [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a) reports no numerical regret values, confidence intervals, or statistical significance tests, and [(Combes et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a872dc9bc45cb5577c424d69dd40de8a45746a) provides only qualitative matching results, so the proposed method's claimed sublinear regret and computational efficiency gains remain unconfirmed by the available related-work evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a quantitative synthetic evaluation that fills the empirical gap in [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a) and validates the proposed bipartite graph formulation under the exact planned settings: M=5,10,15; K=10,15,20; T=10^4; delta=1/T; eta=1,2,10; sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3; Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, and U-Shape near-normal rewards; movement costs ck,m = eta*|(k mod M)-m|/max{K,M}; priority weights half 3 and half 1; stochastic capacities; and cumulative regret with 95% confidence intervals, multiple random seeds, statistical significance tests, and runtime/memory comparisons against OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v. The second most important action is to derive a regret bound that explicitly accounts for the approximation error of the U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matching and the approximate UCB rule, using the composition lesson from [(Esfandiari et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7892ac74a24ace1eaab47c69d8e4c2e350c29537), where a decomposition approximation factor alpha leads to regret O~(N C sqrt(alpha K T)) and lower bounds show T^{2/3} dependence without a lower bound U, and the combinatorial UCB precedent in [(Combes et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a872dc9bc45cb5577c424d69dd40de8a45746a); the plan should specify analogous lower bounds on stochastic capacities, priority gaps, or arm-rank popularity to avoid a K-factor or superlogarithmic regret term. The third action is to include adapted cost-aware knapsack baselines from [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c) and [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2010-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/958d0ee051e322afb35c73813a21ccdbeb8fe14c), such as KUBE, fractional KUBE, budgeted-first uniform exploration, and reward-cost ratio greedy, modified for multiple plays and priority weights, to demonstrate that the bipartite matching formulation is needed for priority, movement-cost, and capacity-sharing effects rather than merely for cost-aware allocation. The fourth action is to design and test exploration rules that avoid the noncompetitive deterministic exploit/threshold behavior identified in [(Banerjee et al., 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6650a2088ece1c0da330433adf8fde121aa544ad), for example by using inverse-degree or topology-aware sampling over the play/arm-rank graph, UCB over edge weights, or forced exploration of low-degree arm-rank pairs, and by evaluating complete, sparse, and adversarially structured bipartite instances. The fifth action is to report full reproducibility details that are missing from several related works, including hardware/system specs, implementation language, solver or matching algorithm, hyperparameters, random seeds, confidence intervals, and normalized regret metrics such as regret divided by log horizon or log(B/c_min) where applicable. The least important but still useful action is to construct the planned lower-bound instances with zero movement costs, equal priority weights, and deterministic capacities, then compare them to classical multi-armed bandit lower bounds and to the upper bound of the approximate UCB algorithm, to verify that the nonlinear prioritized capacity-sharing structure indeed produces the claimed new regret dependence on priority weights and resource characteristics.

## Context: This method characterizes feasible prioritized action profiles in a way that can be optimized or approximated. Method: **Matching-Based Optimization:** Establish connections between action profiles and U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matchings

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Matching-Based Optimization method is [(Liu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b182a38a1fe1e6dc3ed30ef1f73d6600a9f6fbd6), because it is the closest prior work to priority-compatible matching: in a decentralized matching market with N players and L arms, CA-UCB resolves conflicts by arm preference order, maintains UCBs, repeats the previous arm with delay probability lambda=0.1, and selects from a plausible set excluding arms previously won by a player whom the arm strictly prefers; it reports convergence toward stable matchings and stable-regret trends for N=5,10,15,20 over T up to 5000 on 10 random markets, with no discernible regret or stability difference across preference-correlation beta values. This directly supports coupling priority-compatible matching constraints with UCB learning, although it does not validate U-saturated or V-monotone matchings, stochastic capacities, movement costs, or the MSB-PRS weighted utility. A weaker but still relevant support is [(Avner, and Mannor, 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e55acb04edc59b0683577285a160e15b2581f78), which uses CSM-MAB to allocate N=10 users to K=12 channels over T=120000 slots in 50 repetitions, constructs user preferences from UCB indices, and shows that system potential decays toward stable-marriage configurations, with one instance containing 305 stable configurations; this supports representing action profiles as bipartite matchings and using UCB-based preferences, but it lacks priority weights, stochastic arm capacities, movement costs, and U-saturated/V-monotone constraints. A further weaker support is [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), whose capacity-aware Oracle in OrchExplore ranks arms by estimated per-load means and fills capacities greedily in a K=9, N=7 Bernoulli multiple-play bandit with capacities 2,4,3,3,2,1,3,4,2 and optimal expected reward 5.7; this supports feasible action-profile construction under capacity constraints and the importance of learning capacities, but it is not a matching method and does not address priority compatibility. The weakest support is [(Kalathil et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95eb582a0bb28bcd489a711042e9334b6a545c16), which introduces a distributed bipartite matching dBM based on Bertsekas auction in a decentralized Markovian MAB setting, providing a conceptual precedent for matching-based allocation, but the excerpt contains no quantitative regret, baseline comparison, convergence rate, or evidence for the proposed constraints.

### Contradictions

No cited work directly refutes U-saturated or V-monotone matching, but the strongest evidence against the proposed method is that matching may be unnecessary for capacity-constrained action profiles: [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa) reports that a greedy capacity-aware selector in OrchExplore achieves lower cumulative regret than ETC-UCB and MP-SE-SA, outperforms Hoeffding-based variants when using uniform confidence intervals, and shows that the price of learning capacity is much larger than estimating reward means alone in a K=9, N=7 setting with capacities 2,4,3,3,2,1,3,4,2 and delta=2/T; this suggests that an explicit U-saturated/V-monotone matching layer must demonstrate regret or computational gains over a simple capacity-aware greedy baseline, especially because the paper does not include priority weights or movement costs. A second contradiction is that matching/stability mechanisms can impose coordination overhead that slows reward acquisition: [(Avner, and Mannor, 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e55acb04edc59b0683577285a160e15b2581f78) reports that CSM-MAB often reaches near-optimal configurations when N<K but acquires reward more slowly than dUCB4 because of a fixed coordination-to-exploitation ratio, with dUCB4 requiring about 4e5 slots before meaningful reward and 802.11n 2000-bit/25 Mbps translating to about 32 seconds while the 64-bit variant exceeds one minute; this contradicts the assumption that matching-based optimization is automatically regret-efficient unless coordination overhead is explicitly controlled. A third, weaker contradiction is that priority-compatible behavior may be achievable without explicit matching optimization: [(Liu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b182a38a1fe1e6dc3ed30ef1f73d6600a9f6fbd6) uses CA-UCB with lambda=0.1 and plausible-set exclusion to obtain stable-regret and stability trends, but only qualitatively over T up to 5000 and 10 markets, so it does not validate that U-saturated/V-monotone matching optimization is needed or beneficial. The weakest contradiction is the absence of quantitative evidence in [(Kalathil et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95eb582a0bb28bcd489a711042e9334b6a545c16), which provides a distributed auction/bipartite matching method but no numerical regret, significance, convergence, or constraint validation, leaving the proposed matching properties unsupported by that line of work.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, the proposed Matching-Based Optimization should formalize and prove the exact correspondence between MSB-PRS action profiles and U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matchings, including how priority weights alpha_k, stochastic capacities D_m, movement costs c_k,m, and weighted utility map to edge weights in the bipartite graph; this is necessary because none of the cited works validates U-saturated or V-monotone matchings, and the closest priority-compatible evidence is only CA-UCB's conflict-avoiding UCB in [(Liu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b182a38a1fe1e6dc3ed30ef1f73d6600a9f6fbd6). Second, the method should add capacity-aware greedy and matching baselines to the planned OnlinActPrf comparisons, specifically a Wang-style Oracle that ranks arms by estimated per-load means and fills learned capacities, a CSM-MAB-style stable-marriage UCB baseline from [(Avner, and Mannor, 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e55acb04edc59b0683577285a160e15b2581f78), and a CA-UCB-style priority-compatible baseline from [(Liu et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b182a38a1fe1e6dc3ed30ef1f73d6600a9f6fbd6), so that the added value of U-saturated/V-monotone matching is isolated from generic capacity or stability effects. Third, the experimental protocol should report quantitative regret with confidence intervals, statistical significance, runtime, and coordination overhead, using at least the plan's M=5, K=10 default and varied M=5,10,15 and K=10,15,20, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1,2,10, sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3, and priority weights half 3/half 1, while also reporting metrics analogous to Avner's policy changes, time in unstable configurations, and system potential, and Liu's fraction of time in stable matchings. Finally, the method should incorporate distributed or low-overhead matching mechanisms inspired by [(Kalathil et al., 2014-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/95eb582a0bb28bcd489a711042e9334b6a545c16)'s auction-based dBM and [(Avner, and Mannor, 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e55acb04edc59b0683577285a160e15b2581f78)'s signalling protocol, but only if they can be shown to reduce regret or runtime relative to the greedy capacity-aware selector; otherwise the matching formulation should be restricted to a proof device or an offline optimization subroutine rather than the primary online algorithm.

## Context: This is the core learning algorithm for stochastic rewards and capacities under prioritized resource sharing. Method: **Approximate UCB Design:** Develop computationally efficient UCB-based algorithms that avoid exhaustive search

### Support

The strongest support is from [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b), which directly validates the proposed approximate UCB design goal of avoiding exhaustive search in an exponential combinatorial action space. In p-by-p grid routing bandits with d=8,32,72,128 links, basis sizes 4,16,36,64, and path counts 4,56,792,11440, TTC and MTTC used a barycentric-spanner basis, ordinary least squares delay estimates, a confidence radius, and a top-two shortest-path comparison with a polynomial perturbation subroutine to eliminate suboptimal paths. With T=5000 to 25000, mu_max=1000, normal noise R=0.1 or 1, 200 iterations, and a 90-second budget, TTC runtimes were 0.01,0.16,1.00,1.14 seconds for p=2,4,6,8, while OFU exceeded 90 seconds for p>=6 and Thompson sampling took 29.60,35.14,39.81,51.96 seconds; in low noise TTC and MTTC achieved lower time-average regret than OFU and Thompson sampling except p=8 T<=15000. This is the closest evidence that a confidence-based approximate decision rule can make combinatorial UCB tractable, which is exactly the MSB-PRS need when M=5,10,15 and K=10,15,20 make exhaustive matching search infeasible. Next, [(Liu et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/036f8e838075d50a67cdae61e66a2dd46fe0dd56) supports the approximate-confidence component by showing that a bounded approximation gap can preserve near-KL-UCB regret at much lower cost. Across 10,000 runs on Bernoulli 9-arm means 0.1 to 0.9, Bernoulli 10 low-mean arms, and Beta(i,2) arms, UCBoost(epsilon) matched KL-UCB regret at epsilon=0.01 or 0.001 while running in 7.67,8.76,8.33 microseconds per arm per round versus 933,986,907 microseconds for KL-UCB and 0.31,0.30,0.33 microseconds for UCB1, using MATLAB, py/maBandits, Newton accuracy 1e-5, theory c=3 and experiment c=0. This supports using approximate KL or weak semi-distance indices for MSB-PRS reward and capacity estimates, provided epsilon is tuned. Then [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306) supports replacing an expensive ellipsoidal confidence region with a skewed L1 octahedron that has only 2n extremal points, enabling oracle optimization or polynomial-time halfspace polytope optimization, with O*(n^{3/2} sqrt T) regret versus O*(n sqrt T) for the L2 version and an instance-dependent O(n^2 Delta polylog T) bound when a gap exists. This is relevant to MSB-PRS if the priority-compatible matching polytope can be represented by halfspaces or a low-dimensional spanner. Further support comes from [(Chen et al., 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40640d997dab261d37743e1bffc09273c9937a0), which uses UCB-adjusted base-arm means and an oracle to select super arms without enumerating all super arms, and from multi-play UCB work [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) and [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f), which show top-L UCB selection and arm-specific UCB statistics for non-equivalent plays, respectively; Komiyama's CUCB uses sqrt(3 log t/(2N_i)) and MP-KL-UCB uses a KL constraint, while Vorobev's factorized reward model F(k,a)=p(k)r(a) gives an asymptotically optimal coefficient. The older foundational UCB and KL-UCB literature, including [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), [(Capp'e et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161c91acafc365ab0b131dd7e2043a52a68972e3), and [(Lattimore, 2016-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd10f1610b2029b402af7bab465f2e4eca98c1d8), supports the confidence-bound template; Auer's UCB1 experiments used 100,000 plays over 100 runs on Bernoulli 2- and 10-armed bandits, with UCB2 alpha=0.001, but only qualitative regret and optimal-arm percentage, and Capp'e's simulations used f(t)=log t in practice, 50,000 Bernoulli Monte Carlo runs, T=20,000 and 10,000 replications for truncated Poisson and exponential rewards truncated at 10, with kl-poisson-UCB regret about ten times smaller than UCB, UCB-V, and generic kl-UCB. Newer application work [(Heide et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6afdec385f0937ef62e8341cafcfb8f63ac303f5) and [(Tong et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae3f672c85f2eef16e4e49c22760ee29ba3bd94d) provides weaker but directionally consistent evidence; Heide compares Sampling-UCB with QRM1 and SR and Elimination with BUCB, while Tong evaluates Quick-Init UCB and BP-UCB for federated client selection, but both lack the MSB-PRS priority, capacity, and movement structure.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is also from [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b), because in the high-noise R=1 setting TTC was worse than Thompson sampling in the main T=5000 to 25000 experiments, and only large-horizon runs, such as T=5e4 to 1e5 for p=2 and T=1e8 to 5e8 for p=4, showed TTC and MTTC finishing with lower regret. This contradicts the assumption that approximate UCB will uniformly outperform alternative confidence or posterior-based methods in the MSB-PRS plan, especially since the plan tests sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3 at T=10^4 and currently lists only OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v as baselines rather than Thompson sampling or exact UCB. Next, [(Liu et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/036f8e838075d50a67cdae61e66a2dd46fe0dd56) shows that approximate UCB is not automatically better: UCB(d_h) was worse than UCB1 in Bernoulli Scenario 1, and matching KL-UCB regret required epsilon=0.01 or 0.001 with runtimes of 7.67,8.76,8.33 microseconds, still about 25 times slower than UCB1 at 0.31,0.30,0.33 microseconds. This contradicts a naive design that chooses a loose approximation gap for speed without proving that the resulting regret remains sublinear or competitive. Then [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306) provides a regret-quality contradiction: the computationally easier L1 octahedron has O*(n^{3/2} sqrt T) regret, weaker than the L2 ellipsoid O*(n sqrt T), and the lower bound Omega(n sqrt T) for a zero-gap construction shows that polylogarithmic regret in T is not generally achievable. This warns that MSB-PRS approximations may inherit worse constants or exponents, particularly if priority weights, movement costs, or stochastic capacities create small or zero gaps. Older UCB evidence also limits overconfidence: [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) reports that UCB1-TUNED has no proven regret bound and that epsilon-greedy requires per-distribution tuning, so heuristic confidence adjustments can be empirically attractive but theoretically unsafe. [(Capp'e et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161c91acafc365ab0b131dd7e2043a52a68972e3) further shows that empirical KL-UCB support grows with pulls and is mainly suitable below about 10,000 rounds, while generic kl-UCB gave no significant improvement over UCB on truncated exponential rewards, so the plan cannot assume that any KL-style index will be both fast and regret-optimal at T=10^4 with larger M and K. Finally, the absence of direct MSB-PRS evidence is a limitation: [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) reports no quantitative outcomes and lacks priority, capacity, movement, and matching; [(Chen et al., 2014-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c40640d997dab261d37743e1bffc09273c9937a0) has no empirical evaluation; [(Lattimore, 2016-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd10f1610b2029b402af7bab465f2e4eca98c1d8) is single-arm and analytical; [(Heide et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6afdec385f0937ef62e8341cafcfb8f63ac303f5) is preliminary without numerical regret or runtime; and [(Tong et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae3f672c85f2eef16e4e49c22760ee29ba3bd94d) provides only section headings without quantitative results. Together, these contradictions mean the proposed approximate UCB design is plausible but not yet validated for prioritized multi-play capacity sharing.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the MSB-PRS approximate UCB algorithm explicitly combinatorial rather than a generic top-L UCB extension. Inspired by [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b), maintain confidence radii for reward and capacity estimates, compute the current best priority-compatible matching, then find a second-best matching by perturbing the edge weights of the current matching one at a time and taking the best perturbed matching; if the estimated utility gap exceeds twice the confidence radius, eliminate or commit to the current matching, otherwise explore. This directly addresses the plan's bipartite matching formulation and avoids exhaustive enumeration over all play-arm-rank allocations. Second, add Thompson sampling, exact UCB where feasible, greedy, and random baselines, and evaluate high-noise sigma=0.3 and longer horizons beyond T=10^4, because [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b) shows approximate UCB can lose to Thompson sampling at moderate horizons under high noise. Report cumulative regret with mean and standard deviation or confidence intervals over multiple seeds, plus per-round runtime, total runtime, oracle calls, matching solver time, and hardware, since the related work often lacks statistical significance and computational reproducibility. Third, adopt an epsilon-controlled approximate confidence index from [(Liu et al., 2018-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/036f8e838075d50a67cdae61e66a2dd46fe0dd56) for per-arm reward and capacity estimates, test a grid of epsilon values, and prove or empirically verify that the approximation gap does not destroy sublinear regret; avoid loose approximations that underperform simple UCB1. Fourth, use the L1 or spanner-based confidence geometry from [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306) to keep the matching oracle polynomial, especially if the priority-compatible matching polytope can be represented by halfspaces or reduced to a low-dimensional basis; include the weaker L1 regret exponent in the theoretical analysis. Fifth, extend the multi-play UCB insights from [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) and [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) by replacing top-L arm selection with priority-weighted, capacity-constrained, movement-cost-aware bipartite matching, and by using arm-specific statistics for stochastic capacities rather than treating all plays as homogeneous. Sixth, ground the confidence analysis in the foundational UCB and KL-UCB literature, including [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), [(Capp'e et al., 2012-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/161c91acafc365ab0b131dd7e2043a52a68972e3), and [(Lattimore, 2016-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bd10f1610b2029b402af7bab465f2e4eca98c1d8), by specifying the exploration bonus f(t), delta=1/T, initialization, concentration assumptions for stochastic capacities, and finite-time or asymptotic regret bounds that incorporate priority weights, movement costs, and zero-gap cases.

## Context: This method establishes fundamental learning limits for the proposed MSB-PRS model. Method: We plan to establish fundamental learning limits by:
1. Constructing special instances of MSB-PRS composed of carefully designed independent groups of classical multi-armed bandits
2. Applying existing lower bound techniques to these constructed instances
3. Proving both instance-independent and instance-dependent regret lower bounds

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed lower-bound method comes from [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f), which studies a multi-play bandit with non-equivalent plays or slots and factorized rewards p(k)r(a), uses Chernoff-Hoeffding concentration and a UCB statistic under one-dimensional concave log-likelihood and finite second-moment assumptions, and proves asymptotic regret liminf Reg_t/log t equals sum over suboptimal arms of (mu_a_m - mu_a_j)p_m I(a_j,a_m), matching the paper's lower bound. This directly supports the plan's goal of proving fundamental limits and designing approximate UCB algorithms for a multi-play setting with heterogeneous plays, although it does not include priority weights, movement costs, stochastic capacities, or bipartite matching. The next strongest support is methodological and represents a newer extension of lower-bound construction in constrained bandits: [(Agarwal et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a8bc14e49cde9d9820136ff95fd936dd0bae52) constructs hard distributions D_{Delta,psi} for multi-pass streaming bandits, proves lower bounds by induction over B passes, uses Hoeffding's inequality with c=5 and union bounds, and shows memory thresholds such as M=O(1) versus o(K/B^2), with matching upper bounds including O(sqrt(KT log T) log log T) for B=log log T and instance-dependent O(sum Delta_j^{-1} log^2 T); this validates the plan's strategy of building special instances and applying information-theoretic lower-bound techniques in constrained bandits, even though the constraints are memory and passes rather than priority or capacity. Subsequent work by [(Kalvit, and Zeevi, 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/369b7f41135d06611da858ce2ba5eab03c219194) further supports constructing restricted instances for complex bandit structures: its two-type reservoir construction reduces a countable-armed problem to a two-armed bandit with gap Delta, and its coupon-collector full-information variant yields Omega(K Delta log K) when the optimal-type fraction alpha1 is bounded away from 1, providing a template for lower bounds involving sampling, types, and combinatorial discovery that can be adapted to stochastic capacities and arm groups. Older foundational support is provided by [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), which analyzes top-L multiple-play Bernoulli bandits, derives a necessary condition for optimal algorithms, and proposes MP-TS with uniform priors and top-L posterior sampling, showing that optimal regret requires suppressing simultaneous selection of suboptimal arms; its simulations against CUCB with index mu_i(t)+sqrt(3 log t/(2 N_i(t))) and MP-KL-UCB are not quantitatively detailed, but the theoretical result supports the relevance of multiple-play regret limits. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b9be5d7d3a086c80f24206ae51475dcbbff1730), which uses an adversarial-attack reduction to derive exponent constraints for robust single-play bandits, beta >= 1/alpha for additive O(T^alpha+C^beta) and beta >= 1/alpha-1 for multiplicative O(T^alpha C^beta) with alpha in [1/2,1); this supports using existing lower-bound reductions but is farthest from MSB-PRS because it does not model multiple plays, priority, movement, or capacity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct empirical refutation but a structural mismatch: none of the cited works establishes lower bounds for the exact MSB-PRS ingredients, namely priority weights alpha_k, movement costs c_k, stochastic arm capacities D_m, per-unit rewards R_m, and U-saturated/V-monotone priority-compatible matchings, so the plan's assumption that independent groups of classical MABs suffice is not evidenced. The closest multi-play evidence actually highlights this limitation: [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) assumes factorized slot-arm rewards p(k)r(a), known or estimable ordering of slot examination probabilities and arm means, and observation only when the slot is examined; MSB-PRS utility combines weighted rewards minus movement costs and capacity sharing, which can make the best arm depend on the selected play set and movement state, violating the separability and fixed-order assumptions needed for its matching asymptotic bound. A second strong contradiction is that [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) studies ordinary top-L selection with distinct Bernoulli means and shows optimal regret requires avoiding simultaneous suboptimal selections; this supports top-L reductions only when movement costs are zero and priorities are equal, but the research plan explicitly hypothesizes that top-performing arms do not necessarily warrant optimal allocation due to movement costs and priority constraints, so a lower bound built from independent classical MAB groups may miss the combinatorial hardness created by those terms. Third, [(Agarwal et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a8bc14e49cde9d9820136ff95fd936dd0bae52) proves matching bounds for memory and pass constraints, with thresholds such as M=O(1) versus o(K/B^2) and pass caps T^{b/(B+1)}; these constraints are not equivalent to stochastic capacities or priority-based allocation, so its hard distribution D_{Delta,psi} cannot be copied without re-deriving indistinguishability under MSB-PRS's utility and matching constraints. Fourth, [(Kalvit, and Zeevi, 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/369b7f41135d06611da858ce2ba5eab03c219194) provides lower bounds for restricted policy classes, including admissible monotone policies, front-loaded reservoir-query policies, and full-information one-sample coupon-collector policies, and for countable reservoir sampling, not for general multiple-play policies with movement costs; its Omega(K Delta log K) bound requires alpha1 bounded away from 1 and does not account for K plays, priority weights, or capacity D_m. Fifth, [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b9be5d7d3a086c80f24206ae51475dcbbff1730) addresses adversarial reward attacks with known budget C and derives exponent conditions for single-play robustness; it does not contradict the stochastic MSB-PRS lower-bound plan directly, but it also does not support transferring its attack-budget construction to stochastic capacities or priority sharing. Finally, empirical support is weak: [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) reports simulations in a 5-armed fixed-arm scenario, another fixed-arm scenario, and a commercial search-engine click-log dataset, but the excerpt gives no number of trials, hardware, runtime, parameter values, confidence intervals, or significance tests, and the other works are purely theoretical, so there is no quantitative evidence that the proposed lower-bound instances will be tight or that the planned synthetic settings, M=5, K=10, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1, sigma=0.2, and Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, or U-Shape reward patterns, will validate the bounds.

### Suggested Action

Most important, redesign the lower-bound construction so that the hard instances explicitly encode the MSB-PRS-specific nonlinearities rather than only independent classical MAB groups: start with the plan's simplified regime of zero movement costs, equal priority weights, and deterministic capacity to reduce to top-L or non-equivalent-play bandits, then introduce priority weights, movement costs, and stochastic capacities as controlled perturbations and prove indistinguishability under the full utility. Use the two-type/coupon-collector style of [(Kalvit, and Zeevi, 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/369b7f41135d06611da858ce2ba5eab03c219194) and the hard-distribution/induction technique of [(Agarwal et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a8bc14e49cde9d9820136ff95fd936dd0bae52) to build instances where capacity sampling or priority ranking creates a combinatorial discovery problem, and ensure the lower-bound constants depend on priority gaps, movement-cost gaps, capacity gaps, and KL divergences between reward distributions. Second, align the lower bound with the planned approximate UCB by adapting the UCB/asymptotic-optimality template of [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) and the multiple-play index baselines of [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), including MP-TS, CUCB with mu_i(t)+sqrt(3 log t/(2 N_i(t))), and MP-KL-UCB, to derive instance-dependent lower bounds of the form sum over suboptimal allocation types Delta^{-1} log T or Omega(K Delta log K) analogues, and prove matching upper bounds up to logarithmic factors for the bipartite matching-based UCB. Third, specify the proof structure for both instance-independent and instance-dependent bounds: for instance-independent bounds, use worst-case gap Delta and high-probability concentration, such as Hoeffding or Chernoff inequalities, to obtain Omega(sqrt(KT log T)) or Omega(K Delta log K) style terms; for instance-dependent bounds, use KL-based indistinguishability and capacity/movement gaps to obtain sums over suboptimal matchings, and state the exact parameter regimes, M=5,10,15; K=10,15,20; eta=1,2,10; sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3; T=10^4; delta=1/T, where the bounds are tight. Fourth, add empirical validation of the theoretical lower bounds, not only algorithm comparison: simulate the hard instances and the approximate UCB, report cumulative regret, regret/log T, confidence intervals over multiple seeds, statistical significance against baselines OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v, runtime/memory, and hardware, and test whether the observed regret tracks the predicted lower-bound constants under Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, and U-Shape reward patterns. Fifth, if robustness to capacity or reward perturbations is desired, borrow the attack-budget exponent logic of [(Wang et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b9be5d7d3a086c80f24206ae51475dcbbff1730) to state when small adversarial perturbations can turn sublinear regret into linear regret, but keep this secondary because the core plan is stochastic lower-bound construction.

## Context: This method provides the upper-bound analysis for the proposed approximate UCB algorithm. Method: Analyzing the approximate UCB algorithm's performance using confidence bands and monotonicity properties

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed upper-bound analysis is provided by [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2), which directly analyzes an approximate UCB algorithm, Bootstrapped UCB, using a bootstrap quantile q_{1-delta}(y_{n_{k,t}} - ybar_{n_{k,t}}, w_B), B resamples, an approximation theorem, and a regret upper bound, with per-step computational cost O(Bt) bounded by O(BT); this is the closest analogue to the MSB-PRS plan of proving an upper bound for an approximate UCB algorithm through confidence-band control. The next strongest support comes from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), which establishes finite-time expected regret bounds for UCB1 and UCB1-TUNED using one-sided confidence intervals on Bernoulli rewards in [0,1], evaluates 2- and 10-arm synthetic instances over 100,000 plays averaged over 100 runs, and shows that variance-aware bonuses such as min{1/4, V_j(n_j)} improve UCB1, while UCB2 with alpha=0.001 and epsilon_n-greedy with tuned c provide useful baselines; this supports the proposed use of confidence radii and gap-dependent regret terms, although it does not address approximate UCB, matching, movement costs, or prioritized capacity sharing. Further support is provided by [(Garivier, and Cappé, 2011-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7a481032045284382dffa1be4ba90029d14eb41), which uses KL-UCB with a KL-divergence confidence term, Newton iterations, c=3 for theory and c=0 for practice, and KL-UCB+ with log(t/N[a]), and explicitly relies on monotonicity of the KL divergence, making it directly relevant to the proposed monotonicity-based confidence-band analysis. The theoretical decomposition in [(Maillard et al., 2011-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae8700dbc12e407eaee7054dae39d4b7b49434e8) also supports the plan because it bounds regret for KL-based UCB strategies by decomposing events where empirical estimates are too low from events where empirical distributions are far from truth, using convexity, continuity, level-set, and monotonicity properties, and obtains a Bernoulli bound with a second-order term of order (log T)^(2/3) under arm-specific constants. Support for handling constraints is provided by [(Kazerouni et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7975ba2697e11f0da76a015beea30e0eb01d67fe), whose CLUCB algorithm uses regularized least squares, sub-Gaussian confidence radii, and a conservative constraint relative to a known baseline, with experiments on 100 arms in R^4, N(0,I_4) features, N(0,1) noise, lambda=1, delta=0.001, T=40,000, and 1,000 runs, showing that constraint-aware UCB regret can be decomposed into optimistic-round regret plus a conservative-round penalty; this is relevant to MSB-PRS because priority weights, capacities, and movement costs can be viewed as structural constraints on the matching. Sub-Gaussian and martingale-based confidence support is provided by [(Kirschner, and Krause, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfecf9b3844d0046457dc350ad83ebe1fd65e348), which analyzes contextual UCB with a martingale difference term bounded by 4, Azuma-Hoeffding concentration, variance proxy sqrt(4+sigma^2), and beta_T tuning at T=1000 with L=10 and L=100, supporting the use of concentration inequalities for stochastic rewards or capacities in the MSB-PRS setting. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Arora et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e083fb44158c21824c1da9d0cf89dc157fd18ab4), which uses anytime UCB confidence bounds, median aggregation across 2 log(T) copies, and stability of UCB-I under rescaled rewards in synthetic Bernoulli instances with T=10^6, gaps 0.2 or 0.02, and six corralled algorithms, supporting robust high-probability UCB analysis. The weakest support is from [(Celis, and Salehi, 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8bd18527f780e8a541e45b7dce9f25175b3e945), whose networked UCBN method derives regret bounds interpolating between O(1) and O(K ln T) as neighbor exploration varies and claims a b-dependent reduction in complete networks, but it does not address approximate UCB, matching, priority weights, capacities, or movement costs, so its relevance to MSB-PRS is limited to shared stochastic UCB confidence reasoning.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caveat to the proposed method is that [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2) explicitly notes that a naive bootstrapped UCB can suffer linear regret in some cases, and that the valid regret guarantee depends on an approximation theorem controlling the bootstrap quantile error; this directly challenges the MSB-PRS plan unless the approximate UCB index error is tightly bounded and shown to preserve the monotonicity properties used in the regret proof. The next strongest caveat is from [(Garivier, and Cappé, 2011-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7a481032045284382dffa1be4ba90029d14eb41), which reports that UCB regret distributions are very poorly concentrated: most runs have regret of order log(n), but rare early misestimation events can produce regret of order n with probability decaying only slightly faster than 1/n, creating skewed upper tails; in MSB-PRS, rare early misallocation of high-priority plays or misestimation of stochastic capacities could similarly dominate expected regret even if the average-case confidence-band argument is correct. A further caution comes from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), where UCB1 has a leading regret constant proportional to 1/Delta_i^2, UCB2 can approach 1/(2Delta_i^2) only with a larger additive constant, and epsilon_n-greedy requires a known lower bound d on the best-second-best gap and is sensitive to its tuning constant c; this suggests that the MSB-PRS regret bound may be highly sensitive to small gaps among near-normal reward distributions, and that the planned T=10^4 horizon may be too short to reveal asymptotic behavior if gaps are small. The constrained-UCB result in [(Kazerouni et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7975ba2697e11f0da76a015beea30e0eb01d67fe) also introduces a potential contradiction because its regret decomposition assumes a positive baseline reward lower bound, bounded baseline gaps, and lambda <= D^2; if MSB-PRS priority constraints, movement costs, or stochastic capacities make the feasible baseline weak, infeasible, or poorly separated, the analogous decomposition may fail unless new assumptions are proved. The contextual UCB experiments in [(Kirschner, and Krause, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfecf9b3844d0046457dc350ad83ebe1fd65e348) provide a weaker but relevant caution because beta_T is tuned post hoc to minimize regret at T=1000, L=10 is less reliable than L=100, and no confidence intervals or statistical significance tests are reported; this implies that the planned delta=1/T and sigma=0.1, 0.2, 0.3 settings may require careful validation rather than being automatically sufficient. The corralling result in [(Arora et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e083fb44158c21824c1da9d0cf89dc157fd18ab4) is a weaker caveat because its empirical comparison is qualitative, base algorithms are run with default parameters, and the stability argument for UCB-I depends on rescaled rewards bounded by an epoch-dependent threshold, so it does not directly validate monotonicity under MSB-PRS matching. The weakest caveat is from [(Celis, and Salehi, 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8bd18527f780e8a541e45b7dce9f25175b3e945), where the excerpt contains a likely formatting artifact rendering a b-dependent regret reduction as O(K ln T b) rather than a clearer O(K ln T / b), and where empirical details, network topologies, parameter values, and baselines are not specified, limiting its evidential value.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the MSB-PRS approximate UCB proof should follow [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2) by explicitly bounding the approximation error of the UCB index, proving an approximation theorem for the bootstrap or other approximate quantile, and showing that the monotonicity of the index in empirical means, capacities, and matching marginals is preserved; the regret bound and runtime analysis should include the number of bootstrap resamples B, the plan should test multiple B values, and it should compare against exact UCB when computable to avoid the linear-regret failure mode of naive approximate UCB. Second, the confidence-band construction should combine the monotonicity and level-set decomposition of [(Maillard et al., 2011-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ae8700dbc12e407eaee7054dae39d4b7b49434e8) and [(Garivier, and Cappé, 2011-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7a481032045284382dffa1be4ba90029d14eb41) for KL-based confidence when rewards are Bernoulli or otherwise bounded, with the sub-Gaussian, martingale, and Azuma-Hoeffding confidence approach of [(Kirschner, and Krause, 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfecf9b3844d0046457dc350ad83ebe1fd65e348) or the variance-aware UCB1-TUNED radius of [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) for the plan's near-normal PMFs and sigma=0.1, 0.2, 0.3; the resulting instance-dependent bound should explicitly involve priority weights alpha_k, movement costs c_k, stochastic capacities D_m, and arm or allocation gaps, and should target second-order terms such as (log T)^(2/3) where the monotonicity structure permits. Third, because [(Garivier, and Cappé, 2011-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7a481032045284382dffa1be4ba90029d14eb41) shows that rare early misestimation can create O(n) regret tails, the MSB-PRS analysis should prove high-probability bounds with delta=1/T, consider KL-UCB+ style logarithmic corrections or robust/median aggregation inspired by [(Arora et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e083fb44158c21824c1da9d0cf89dc157fd18ab4), and report tail, median, and confidence-interval regret rather than only mean cumulative regret. Fourth, the proof should use the constrained-UCB decomposition of [(Kazerouni et al., 2016-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7975ba2697e11f0da76a015beea30e0eb01d67fe) to split regret into optimistic matching regret plus a penalty for priority, capacity, or movement-cost constraint violations, define a feasible baseline matching and a baseline gap, and either verify the analogous assumptions or prove replacements for the MSB-PRS setting. Fifth, the experimental design should keep the planned M=5, 10, 15 arms, K=10, 15, 20 plays, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1, 2, 10, sigma=0.1, 0.2, 0.3, and priority weights half 3 and half 1, but should add T=10^5 to match the 100,000-play scale used in [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), use multiple random seeds, report cumulative and per-step regret, constraint violations, runtime, B, delta, eta, sigma, and statistical significance, and compare against OnlinActPrf, OnlinActPrf-v, exact UCB when tractable, and adapted UCB1-TUNED, KL-UCB, or CLUCB baselines. Finally, the networked result of [(Celis, and Salehi, 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8bd18527f780e8a541e45b7dce9f25175b3e945) should be treated only as a weak analogy for shared information unless the MSB-PRS capacity-sharing mechanism induces correlated observations or neighbor-like information sharing.

## Context: This method empirically validates the algorithms under controlled synthetic settings and systematic variations of problem parameters. Method: We plan to evaluate our algorithms through synthetic experiments with:

**Parameter Settings:**
- M = 5 arms, K = 10 plays (with systematic variation)
- Probability mass functions following near-normal distributions
- Three reward patterns: Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, and U-Shape
- Movement costs: ck,m = η|(k mod M) - m|/max{K,M}
- Priority weights: half of plays with weight 3, half with weight 1
- Default parameters: T = 10^4, δ = 1/T, η = 1, σ = 0.2

**Experimental Variables:**
We will systematically vary:
1. Number of arms (M = 5, 10, 15)
2. Number of plays (K = 10, 15, 20)
3. Resource-reward correlation patterns
4. Movement cost scale (η = 1, 2, 10)
5. Reward standard deviation (σ = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3)

### Support

Read as an iterative line culminating in capacity-aware multiple-play evaluation, the strongest support for the proposed synthetic MSB-PRS method is methodological rather than direct: [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa) evaluates a capacity-aware multiple-play bandit algorithm, OrchExplore, on an MP-MAB-SA synthetic instance with 9 arms, 7 plays, Bernoulli per-load rewards, fixed integer capacities, a known optimal action of (2,4,1,0,...,0), expected optimal reward 5.7, δ=2/T, and 200 Monte Carlo realizations, comparing against MP-SE-SA, ETC-UCB, OrchExplore-KC, KL-UCB, Thompson Sampling, successive elimination, and implicit MAB baselines. This directly supports the proposed use of controlled synthetic regret experiments for multiple-play resource sharing and shows that capacity learning incurs a larger regret penalty than reward-mean estimation alone, which is relevant to the proposed stochastic capacity Dm and capacity-aware UCB. The next strongest support is for multiple-play synthetic evaluation: [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) evaluates multiple-play Thompson sampling selecting the top L arms from K Bernoulli arms against MP-KL-UCB, CUCB, and Exp3.M using cumulative regret, providing a template for the proposed K=10/15/20 plays and baseline comparisons, though without priority weights, movement costs, or capacity sharing. The next strongest support is for nonlinear aggregated feedback: [(Gopalan et al., 2013-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/797de63d3383a496df04ff2140830590f6fb128e) analyzes Thompson sampling for a MAX subset bandit with N Bernoulli arms, subset size M, and maximum-reward feedback, giving a high-probability regret scaling of N^-1 M log T / μ_min^2, which supports the proposed hypothesis that prioritized capacity sharing creates nonlinear combinatorial utility and that regret can depend on subset size and minimum gap, although it does not use UCB, movement costs, priority weights, or near-normal PMFs. Transition-cost relevance is supported by [(Reverdy et al., 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb6b5972fed7d10cd70ef0570a092cd3c677e891), which studies UCL and Block UCL for Gaussian bandits with transition costs, supporting the inclusion of movement costs ck,m = η|(k mod M)-m|/max{K,M}, but its evidence is theoretical and lacks synthetic regret curves. Combinatorial UCB is supported by [(Kveton et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71be31d0a28ccb5acf85b43132953943a9009074), whose CombCascade uses item-level UCBs and product-of-UCB action selection for cascading prefix feedback, supporting UCB-based combinatorial action selection and synthetic regret evaluation, but not priority weights or capacity sharing. Synthetic UCB parameter-sweep rigor is supported by [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6), which evaluates UCB-CV on K=10 arms, 100 repetitions, 95% confidence intervals, and sweeps sigma_v^2 over {1.0,1.5,2.0,2.5,3.0} and approximation error, supporting the proposed systematic variation of σ=0.1/0.2/0.3 and resource-reward correlation, although it is single-play. The foundational UCB/regret template is provided by [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f), which uses GP-UCB with a squared-exponential kernel, lengthscale 0.2, noise variance 0.025 or 5%, a 1000-point discretization, T=1000, δ=0.1, 30 trials, and comparisons against EI, MPM, max-mean, and max-variance, supporting UCB confidence terms and regret-based synthetic validation, but it is continuous single-play and does not address multiple plays. Finally, [(Ou et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe334e14d7773c0aa5d5ab0b252bca095fa41f2f) supports controlled synthetic regret evaluation with 1000 arms, 5-dimensional features, linear and semi-parametric environments, and baselines including TS-Lin, but it is the least relevant because it lacks multiple plays, capacity, priority, and movement costs.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that none of the related work directly validates the proposed core combination of priority weights, movement costs, stochastic capacity, near-normal PMFs, and Inc-Shape/Dec-Shape/U-Shape rewards. The closest, [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), uses fixed integer capacities, Bernoulli per-load rewards, 9 arms and 7 plays, and no priority weights or movement costs, so its qualitative finding that capacity learning increases regret cannot be extrapolated to the proposed priority-weighted, movement-costed MSB-PRS setting. This contradicts the assumption that existing capacity-aware multiple-play evidence is sufficient to support the proposed novelty. A second contradiction is baseline mismatch: the proposed baselines OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v are not present in any related work, while the related literature compares against MP-TS, MP-KL-UCB, CUCB, and Exp3.M [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), ETC-UCB, MP-SE-SA, OrchExplore-KC, KL-UCB, Thompson Sampling, and successive elimination [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), UCB1, UCB-V, Thompson Sampling, and EUCBV [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6), GP-UCB, EI, MPM, max-mean, and max-variance [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f), and CombUCB1 [(Kveton et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71be31d0a28ccb5acf85b43132953943a9009074). Without these baselines, the proposed evaluation may not demonstrate that MSB-PRS outperforms standard multiple-play or UCB methods. A third contradiction is the lack of quantitative evidence in much of the related work: [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), [(Kveton et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71be31d0a28ccb5acf85b43132953943a9009074), [(Reverdy et al., 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb6b5972fed7d10cd70ef0570a092cd3c677e891), and [(Gopalan et al., 2013-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/797de63d3383a496df04ff2140830590f6fb128e) do not provide numerical regret values, confidence intervals, or statistical significance in the supplied excerpts, so the proposed method cannot rely on them for expected regret magnitudes or significance thresholds. A fourth contradiction is that Thompson sampling evidence may undermine a UCB-only framing: [(Gopalan et al., 2013-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/797de63d3383a496df04ff2140830590f6fb128e) shows TS can have a smaller regret bound for nonlinear MAX subset feedback, [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e) evaluates multiple-play TS, and [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa) includes Thompson Sampling as a capacity-aware baseline, suggesting that excluding TS from the proposed baselines could overstate the advantage of the proposed approximate UCB. A fifth contradiction is parameter and evaluation mismatch: the proposed T=10^4, δ=1/T, η=1/2/10, σ=0.1/0.2/0.3, M=5/10/15, and K=10/15/20 are not covered by the related experiments, which use T=1000 and δ=0.1 [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f), δ=2/T with unspecified T [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), K=10 and 100 repetitions [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6), or no specified horizon [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), so the proposed defaults require independent justification. Finally, the proposed claim of computational efficiency gains is not supported by related work because most studies do not report hardware, runtime, memory, or solver details, including [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f), [(Ou et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe334e14d7773c0aa5d5ab0b252bca095fa41f2f), [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6), and [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), so efficiency must be measured directly rather than inferred.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add direct ablations that isolate the proposed mechanisms: first run zero-movement-cost and equal-priority-weight instances with deterministic capacity to connect to the lower-bound construction, then introduce ck,m = η|(k mod M)-m|/max{K,M}, priority weights 3/1, stochastic capacity Dm, and near-normal PMFs, reporting regret for each ablation. This is necessary because the closest related work, [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), validates capacity-aware multiple-play regret but not priority or movement costs. Second, expand baselines to include the standard multiple-play and UCB methods evidenced in the literature: MP-TS, MP-KL-UCB, CUCB, and Exp3.M from [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e); ETC-UCB, MP-SE-SA, OrchExplore/OrchExplore-KC, KL-UCB, Thompson Sampling, and successive elimination from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa); UCB1, UCB-V, Thompson Sampling, and EUCBV from [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6); and, where computationally feasible, GP-UCB or EI-style heuristics inspired by [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f) and CombUCB1-style combinatorial UCB from [(Kveton et al., 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/71be31d0a28ccb5acf85b43132953943a9009074). This will test whether MSB-PRS improves over both capacity-aware and multiple-play baselines, not only OnlinActPrf. Third, report quantitative regret evidence with 95% confidence intervals, at least 100-200 independent runs, fixed random seeds, hardware/runtime details, and statistical comparisons, following the 100-repetition CI protocol of [(Verma, and Hanawal, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f3dd6e865c778f3add9be8f9397b552d92b48e6) and the 200-realization protocol of [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), because many related works lack numerical regret values. Fourth, specify the synthetic reward and capacity construction in reproducible detail: define the near-normal PMFs, Inc-Shape/Dec-Shape/U-Shape means, resource-reward correlation patterns, stochastic capacity distributions, per-unit reward distributions, and the exact mapping from plays to arm-rank pairs, using parameter sweeps for M=5/10/15, K=10/15/20, η=1/2/10, σ=0.1/0.2/0.3, T=10^4, and δ=1/T, while also reporting sensitivity to δ=2/T as in [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa). Fifth, measure computational efficiency directly for the bipartite matching and approximate UCB algorithm: wall-clock time per round, total runtime, memory, graph size, matching solver, approximation error, and comparison to exhaustive search or OnlinActPrf, because related work generally does not provide runtime evidence. Finally, use the older UCB and transition-cost literature as design templates rather than direct benchmarks: adopt the confidence-term and regret-evaluation structure of [(Srinivas et al., 2009-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0c8413ab8de0c1b8f2e86402b8d737d94371610f), the transition-cost motivation of [(Reverdy et al., 2013-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/eb6b5972fed7d10cd70ef0570a092cd3c677e891), the nonlinear subset regret intuition of [(Gopalan et al., 2013-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/797de63d3383a496df04ff2140830590f6fb128e), and the controlled synthetic feature-based regret setup of [(Ou et al., 2019-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fe334e14d7773c0aa5d5ab0b252bca095fa41f2f) only where they inform ablations or baselines.

## Context: This method compares the proposed approach against existing prioritized action selection baselines. Method: We will compare against:
- OnlinActPrf: existing algorithm with expert feedback and homogeneous plays
- OnlinActPrf-v: variant enabling UCB on capacity distribution estimation

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), which evaluates OrchExplore, a capacity-aware multiple-play bandit algorithm for shareable arms with unknown capacities, using K=9 arms, N=7 plays, Bernoulli per-load means 0.9 to 0.1, capacities 2,4,3,3,2,1,3,4,2, optimal action (2,4,1,0,...,0), 200 realizations, and delta=2/T. This directly supports the proposed plan's core multiple-play capacity-sharing and UCB/elimination evaluation design, and its finding that OrchExplore outperforms ETC-UCB and MP-SE-SA in cumulative regret, and that uniform confidence intervals outperform Hoeffding-based intervals, supports using capacity-aware UCB baselines and careful confidence-interval choices for OnlinActPrf-v. The next strongest support is [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), which studies standard multiple-play Bernoulli bandits with K arms and L plays per round, proposes MP-TS that samples posterior means and selects top-L arms, and compares against Exp3.M, CUCB, and MP-KL-UCB using expected cumulative regret; this supports the proposed method's use of multiple-play UCB/TS baselines and regret-based evaluation, but it lacks priority weights, stochastic capacities, movement costs, and OnlinActPrf/OnlinActPrf-v. The weakest support is [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), which evaluates variance-aware UCB1-TUNED on single-play Bernoulli bandits over 100,000 plays and 100 runs, showing it substantially outperforms UCB1 and that epsilon_n-greedy is sensitive to tuning; this supports the proposed plan's UCB-based algorithmic strategy and evaluation protocol, but it is single-play and does not address capacity sharing, prioritized plays, movement costs, or the named baselines.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction or challenge is from [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), where the known-capacity variant OrchExplore-KC outperformed OrchExplore, indicating that learning capacities imposed a larger regret cost than estimating reward means alone; this challenges the proposed method's reliance on OnlinActPrf-v, a variant enabling UCB on capacity distribution estimation, because it suggests that capacity-UCB may not automatically recover known-capacity performance and that the plan must explicitly measure the capacity-estimation regret penalty. The next strongest challenge is from [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), which reports that UCB1-TUNED performs substantially better than UCB1 but does not provide a proven regret bound, and that epsilon_n-greedy requires per-distribution tuning of c and degrades rapidly when mistuned; this contradicts any assumption that UCB variants, including a capacity-distribution UCB in OnlinActPrf-v, are robust without careful tuning and theoretical guarantees. The weakest contradiction is from [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), which does not report quantitative cumulative regret values, confidence intervals, statistical significance, horizon values, number of trials, or hardware specifications for MP-TS, Exp3.M, CUCB, or MP-KL-UCB; this weakens the evidence that multiple-play UCB/TS baselines are empirically strong enough to serve as the primary comparison set, and its simpler setting without priority weights, stochastic capacities, movement costs, or OnlinActPrf/OnlinActPrf-v means it cannot directly validate the proposed baseline comparison.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add capacity-aware multiple-play baselines and a known-versus-unknown capacity ablation, because [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa) shows OrchExplore outperforms ETC-UCB and MP-SE-SA but also that OrchExplore-KC outperforms OrchExplore; the proposed method should compare OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v against OrchExplore-like capacity-aware UCB, CUCB, MP-KL-UCB, and MP-TS where feasible, and report the regret gap between known-capacity and learned-capacity variants to isolate the cost of capacity-distribution UCB. Next, strengthen the evaluation protocol using the empirical rigor of [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3): report cumulative regret and fraction of plays on optimal allocations over T=10^4 and longer horizons, average over at least 100 runs, provide confidence intervals or significance tests, use semi-logarithmic plots, and tune UCB constants and confidence widths rather than assuming default delta=1/T is sufficient. Third, incorporate variance-aware or distribution-aware UCB design and confidence-interval selection, since [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) shows variance-aware UCB1-TUNED can substantially improve over fixed Hoeffding UCB1, and [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa) shows uniform confidence intervals can outperform Hoeffding-based intervals; OnlinActPrf-v should therefore test Hoeffding, KL, variance-aware, and uniform confidence variants for capacity and reward estimation. Fourth, add ablations for priority weights and movement costs, including homogeneous-play and zero-movement-cost settings, to show when OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v are competitive and when prioritized capacity sharing changes the optimal allocation; this is necessary because none of the related works directly evaluates expert feedback, priority weights, or movement costs. Finally, specify reproducibility details missing from the related work, including number of random seeds, hardware, runtime, hyperparameters for OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v, capacity distribution parameters, reward standard deviations std=0.1,0.2,0.3, movement cost scale eta=1,2,10, and exact regret metric definitions.

## Context: This method defines the empirical evaluation criteria for regret and efficiency. Method: We will measure cumulative regret over time horizons and assess convergence rates to demonstrate sublinear regret achievement and computational efficiency gains.

### Support

The strongest support for measuring cumulative regret and computational efficiency in a combinatorial multi-play setting comes from [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc), which evaluates CMAB-SM against improved UCB over K-arm subsets using T=10^6, 30 runs, N=12 or 24, and K=2,3,5,7,11, reporting lower cumulative regret for K>3 and much lower computation and storage, with a scaling argument that UCB would need about 10 million years for N=30 and K=15 at 10^12 actions per second. This directly supports the proposed MSB-PRS plan to vary M=5,10,15 and K=10,15,20 and to show that regret and efficiency improve as the combinatorial action space grows. The next strongest support is [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0), which evaluates MG-EUCB and MG-EUCB+ for capacitated matching bandits against C-UCB and H-EUCB, showing that C-UCB converges almost immediately to a suboptimal solution while MG-EUCB converges faster to the greedy stationary-reward optimum, and that a Hubway incentive simulation achieves about 40% system improvement while matching less than 1% of users to an incentive; this supports the proposed bipartite matching formulation, greedy matching efficiency, and convergence-to-optimum evaluation, although it lacks priority weights and movement costs. The foundational empirical support is [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3), which uses UCB1, UCB1-TUNED, UCB2, and epsilon_n-GREEDY on Bernoulli 2- and 10-armed instances over 100,000 plays and 100 runs, plotting semi-logarithmic regret and optimal-arm play percentage, and showing that parameter choices can produce linear regret or logarithmic regret with large constants; this supports the proposed synthetic horizon-based regret evaluation and the need to vary reward gaps and noise, but it does not address multi-play capacity or runtime. This older protocol is iteratively extended by later structured, matching, and combinatorial studies, which add structure, capacity, and efficiency evidence. Further support comes from [(Gupta et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e1951b4fe8f9bc9993ba6eef76515de2de0560), where UCB-C and TS-C reduce the competitive set to C(theta*)=1 to 9 out of K=18 arms, report UCB-S cumulative regret of 2500 versus UCB-C variants in the 600 to 800 range in a synthetic hidden-parameter experiment, and show lower regret than classical UCB and TS on Movielens over 5000 rounds; this supports approximate UCB that avoids exhaustive exploration, but it is single-play and lacks runtime measurements. [(Lattimore, and Munos, 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f0a7d9f708774c4cb1808df616b9423f67385c2) supports cumulative-regret evaluation with 500 independent runs and normal unit-variance rewards, showing structured UCB generally beats standard UCB when the structure parameter theta is positive, while standard UCB can be slightly better when theta is negative. [(Verma et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6631af024790d4d6a0bb94f78ed106b07a5a0b7) supports the modern reporting practice of repeated synthetic runs, 20 repetitions, and 95% confidence intervals for cumulative regret, although it does not report numerical outcomes. Theoretical support for sublinear regret and computational-efficiency criteria is provided by [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306), which proves high-probability confidence events, bounds sum of squared instantaneous regrets by 8n beta_T ln T, gives worst-case O*(n sqrt T) for ConfidenceBall2 and O*(n^{3/2} sqrt T) for ConfidenceBall1, and shows ConfidenceBall2 can be NP-hard for some decision sets while ConfidenceBall1 is polynomial-time with oracle access. Weaker support comes from [(Nayyar et al., 2015-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e73f6f927997b7f86606a77ff9795d77948a53d), which organizes single-player and multiplayer bandit simulations but provides no protocol or results, and from [(Kaufmann et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a3325fcb27a22cb5cef0e801a5e2b808c4648a), which studies Bayes-UCB posterior quantiles and notes alpha_t=1/t and c=0 performed well in simulations, but the excerpt contains no quantitative regret comparison.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed method states it will assess convergence rates to demonstrate computational efficiency gains, but cumulative regret and convergence rates alone do not measure computational efficiency. Several related works discuss efficiency without providing wall-clock or memory evidence: [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc) claims lower computation and storage and gives a theoretical scaling argument but reports no runtime, [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0) highlights greedy matching over Hungarian matching but reports no wall-clock time, [(Gupta et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e1951b4fe8f9bc9993ba6eef76515de2de0560) discusses reduced exploration qualitatively but provides no hardware or runtime measurements, and [(Verma et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6631af024790d4d6a0bb94f78ed106b07a5a0b7) does not specify computational resources. Therefore, the proposed evaluation would be incomplete unless it adds explicit efficiency metrics. The next strongest contradiction is that structured, matching, or UCB variants do not uniformly outperform baselines across all regimes. [(Lattimore, and Munos, 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f0a7d9f708774c4cb1808df616b9423f67385c2) reports standard UCB slightly better than structured UCB when theta is less than zero because the structured algorithm suffers from worse constants; [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0) reports H-EUCB with marginally higher mean reward than MG-EUCB despite slower convergence to the greedy optimum; [(Gupta et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e1951b4fe8f9bc9993ba6eef76515de2de0560) finds informative variants did not significantly improve on Movielens because learned genre mappings were mostly flat; and [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) shows epsilon_n-GREEDY requires per-distribution tuning and that too-small parameters cause linear regret while too-large parameters cause logarithmic regret with large constants. This contradicts any assumption that the proposed approximate UCB will automatically achieve sublinear regret and efficiency gains without careful baseline selection, parameter sweeps, and hard-instance testing. A further contradiction is that many related empirical studies lack statistical significance and exact regret values. [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) provides no statistical significance tests or numerical regret values, [(Lattimore, and Munos, 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f0a7d9f708774c4cb1808df616b9423f67385c2) reports no confidence intervals or significance tests, [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0) reports no p-values or runtime, and [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc) reports no confidence intervals or significance tests; the newer [(Verma et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6631af024790d4d6a0bb94f78ed106b07a5a0b7) use of 95% confidence intervals suggests that older regret-curve-only reports are insufficient. Theoretical work also contradicts naive exhaustive optimization: [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306) shows ConfidenceBall2 can be NP-hard for some decision sets, so the proposed approximate UCB must explicitly prove polynomial-time matching or oracle-based optimization rather than relying on regret plots. Finally, the related work is only indirectly relevant because none of the cited empirical studies includes the full MSB-PRS combination of priority weights, movement costs, stochastic capacities, and priority-compatible bipartite matching; for example, [(Nayyar et al., 2015-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e73f6f927997b7f86606a77ff9795d77948a53d) and [(Kaufmann et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a3325fcb27a22cb5cef0e801a5e2b808c4648a) provide limited or no quantitative evidence for the proposed setting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define and report explicit computational-efficiency metrics in addition to cumulative regret, because the proposed method currently conflates convergence-rate assessment with efficiency gains. The plan should report wall-clock time per round and total runtime, CPU and memory usage, number of matching or solver calls, storage size, and asymptotic complexity for MSB-PRS, OnlinActPrf, OnlinActPrf-v, and any greedy or Hungarian baselines, using the planned M=5,10,15, K=10,15,20, T=10^4, delta=1/T, eta=1,2,10, and sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3, and optionally extend T to 10^5 or 10^6 to match the longer horizons in [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc) and [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3). The second most important action is to specify a rigorous regret and convergence protocol: use at least 30 independent runs, preferably 100 or 500 for structured or noisy settings as in [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) and [(Lattimore, and Munos, 2014-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7f0a7d9f708774c4cb1808df616b9423f67385c2), report 95% confidence intervals as in [(Verma et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c6631af024790d4d6a0bb94f78ed106b07a5a0b7), provide exact regret tables, use semi-log and log-log plots, estimate convergence slopes, and test statistical significance of regret differences. The third action is to expand baselines and ablations beyond OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v to include classical UCB, Thompson sampling or posterior-quantile baselines motivated by [(Gupta et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e1951b4fe8f9bc9993ba6eef76515de2de0560) and [(Kaufmann et al., 2012-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/76a3325fcb27a22cb5cef0e801a5e2b808c4648a), greedy stationary matching, Hungarian matching, no-priority and no-movement-cost variants, and exhaustive UCB for small M and K, so that the gains from prioritized capacity sharing are isolated. The fourth action is to design parameter sweeps that directly test the MSB-PRS mechanisms: vary M=5,10,15, K=10,15,20, eta=1,2,10, sigma=0.1,0.2,0.3, Inc-Shape, Dec-Shape, and U-Shape rewards, priority weights half 3 and half 1, movement costs ck,m = eta |(k mod M) - m|/max{K,M}, and stochastic capacities, while including hard small-gap and correlated-reward instances inspired by [(Auer et al., 2002-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1e1d35136b1bf3b13ef6b53f6039f39d9ee820e3) and [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0). The fifth action is to tie experiments to the theoretical analysis by constructing lower-bound instances with zero movement costs, equal weights, and deterministic capacity, comparing empirical regret to the planned lower and upper bounds, and verifying that the approximate UCB uses polynomial-time bipartite matching or oracle access rather than NP-hard confidence-region optimization as warned by [(Dani et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/551e19e5113cdff60a3c545d684fc4b9eb9a7306). The sixth action is to improve reproducibility by reporting hardware, software, random seeds, initialization, capacity distributions, reward PMFs, hyperparameters, and implementation details, and by releasing code and data. The least important but still useful action is to add a semi-real simulation, such as a bike-share or LLM queue workload analogous to [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0), to demonstrate practical gains, while keeping the planned synthetic experiments as the primary evidence.
