## Strengths 
The strongest support is for the bipartite matching plus approximate UCB core. [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a) already models prioritized allocation as a weighted bipartite graph over plays and arm-rank pairs, connects action profiles to U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matchings, and uses approximate UCB with OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v in the same synthetic scope. Stochastic capacity learning is supported by [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), which shows capacity-aware multiple-play allocation is learnable and that capacity estimation has a nontrivial regret cost. Multiple-play structure is supported by [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), and approximate combinatorial UCB tractability is supported by [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b) and [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2). Lower-bound construction is supported by [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f) and hard-instance methods in [(Agarwal et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a8bc14e49cde9d9820136ff95fd936dd0bae52) and [(Kalvit, and Zeevi, 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/369b7f41135d06611da858ce2ba5eab03c219194).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions are that the closest prior work already provides OnlinActPrf and OnlinActPrf-v, so the added value of priority weights and movement costs is not yet evidenced [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a). Capacity learning can dominate regret, and uniform confidence intervals outperformed Hoeffding-based intervals in a capacity-aware multiple-play setting [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa). Approximate UCB is not uniformly superior: it can lose to Thompson sampling under high noise, and uncontrolled approximation error can lead to linear regret [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b), [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2). Matching or decomposition can introduce approximation factors, sqrt(T) regret penalties, and coordination overhead [(Esfandiari et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7892ac74a24ace1eaab47c69d8e4c2e350c29537), [(Avner, and Mannor, 2015-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8e55acb04edc59b0683577285a160e15b2581f78). The planned lower-bound construction from independent classical MAB groups may miss the nonlinear effects of priority weights, movement costs, and stochastic capacities [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f), [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e). Finally, cumulative regret and convergence rates alone do not establish computational efficiency unless wall-clock time, memory, and solver calls are measured [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc), [(Fiez et al., 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30a6a5614727017e7d7981f87df57d17713501a0).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Formalize and prove the MSB-PRS matching/UCB pipeline: define edge weights from priority weights, movement costs, stochastic capacities, and per-unit rewards; prove that U-saturated, V-monotone, priority-compatible matching is polynomial-time; and add an approximation theorem showing that approximate UCB error preserves monotonicity and sublinear regret. Include exact UCB when tractable, Thompson sampling, greedy/Hungarian, and OnlinActPrf/OnlinActPrf-v baselines, with regret confidence intervals and runtime/memory measurements, following [(Chen, and Xie, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3d33f61738b6399107769d3c25ab21a1dd17301a), [(Zhu, and Modiano, 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/deffa40e5b458055f0f6fe21cf93dfbf684e016b), and [(Hao et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c503145e5cd84ebe7ed180360df923bb66322bc2).

Redesign the lower-bound construction to encode MSB-PRS-specific nonlinearities rather than only independent classical MAB groups: start with zero movement costs, equal priority weights, and deterministic capacity, then introduce priority weights, movement costs, and stochastic capacities as controlled perturbations; prove indistinguishability and instance-dependent bounds that depend on priority gaps, movement-cost gaps, capacity gaps, and KL divergences, and validate the hard instances empirically, using [(Vorobev, and Gusev, 2015-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4643e4a2a2b63a9b5185d0ff086a1f49d4de81f), [(Agarwal et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a8bc14e49cde9d9820136ff95fd936dd0bae52), and [(Kalvit, and Zeevi, 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/369b7f41135d06611da858ce2ba5eab03c219194).

Expand the experimental protocol to isolate mechanisms and efficiency: add ablations for zero movement cost, equal priority weights, deterministic capacity, known versus unknown capacity, and priority/movement-cost scales; compare against OnlinActPrf, OnlinActPrf-v, OrchExplore-like capacity-aware baselines, MP-TS, CUCB, MP-KL-UCB, and adapted KUBE/fractional KUBE; report cumulative regret, regret/log T, 95% confidence intervals, statistical significance, wall-clock time per round, total runtime, memory, solver calls, hardware, and random seeds, following [(Wang et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f0335a399db01bcaa9f55abd71d2b4cec9c3aa), [(Komiyama et al., 2015-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9caaac7761cf09bf1c6054a4affcbf1b98612d8e), [(Tran-Thanh et al., 2012-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8a0990173403c213c811511c31833b8346b0f5c), and [(Agarwal et al., 2018-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/77536fb265a48a7b8ae27a1f0b47d5bcd872f7cc).
