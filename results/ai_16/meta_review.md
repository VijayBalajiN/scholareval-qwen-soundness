## Our core methodological innovation involves generating enriched agent states by allowing agents to exchange information through a communication channel parameterized by neural network architectures. We will treat inter-agent spatial dependencies as a 2D sequence problem and utilize transformer architecture to model this sequence, conditioning the network on spatial relations between agents using 2D positional encoding based on normalized longitude and latitude. | Synthesis: This is the central proposed architecture for coordinating multiple intersections by modeling spatial dependencies and enabling communication with minimal state information.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), which directly tests learned attention-based inter-agent communication for traffic signal control by enriching each intersection's local state with vehicle counts and signal phase through a graph attention layer, using index-free parameter sharing with approximately O(mL) complexity and reporting average travel-time improvements of 6.98% on synthetic data and 11.69% on real-world data, including 8.08% and 19.89% gains over MaxPressure on synthetic regular and real-world dynamic traffic; this supports the proposed plan's central claim that attention can model spatial dependencies with minimal local state, although it uses Q-value prediction and fixed top-k neighborhoods rather than PPO and full 2D longitude/latitude positional encoding. The next strongest support is [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), which uses transformer encoders, an actor-critic policy, SUMO, and multi-scenario co-training, achieving the lowest tabulated mean trip time and delay on most zero-shot transfer scenarios such as 162.65 s on Grid4x4 and 88.49 s on Cologne8, thereby supporting transformer-based TSC, SUMO experiments, multi-network training, and generalization to unseen networks, though it does not explicitly implement the proposed 2D spatial communication channel. A further supporting signal is [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047), which applies a centralized transformer over node-time tokens to capture spatial and temporal dependencies in network-wide TSC, validating transformer attention for traffic networks, but it assumes CAV-derived detailed state and uses Double-DQN rather than decentralized PPO with minimal state. Additional support for modeling inter-agent spatial structure comes from [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876), which reports that directed hypergraph learning outperforms twelve baselines in average travel time, although the excerpt lacks numerical ATT values and significance tests, and from [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), which finds that coordination-based control scales in larger networks, supporting the value of inter-intersection coordination when vehicles traverse multiple intersections. Weaker but relevant support includes [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427), which shows structure-agnostic meta-learning can generalize across phase settings and cities, and [(Mirbakhsh et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3dc1bad77c9ddf5ce2b60576d7513ace34d233d4), which uses multi-agent reinforcement learning and evaluates delay, travel time, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions, aligning with the proposed environmental metrics but providing no quantitative excerpt.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed full 2D transformer communication design is also from [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), which found that performance was best with only 2 to 5 neighbors and degraded when more neighbors were added, and that multi-head attention improved only up to about 5 heads; this directly challenges the assumption that a transformer over all agents with normalized longitude/latitude positional encoding is always beneficial, because unmasked global attention may include distant or irrelevant intersections and increase variance. A second important contradiction is [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), where a coordination-based max-plus method did not significantly outperform a single-agent controller in a small saturated network and a state-bit extension performed worse, suggesting that inter-agent communication may not improve performance in small or locally dominated networks and that added state or coordination overhead can hurt. A third contradiction is that [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) uses a centralized transformer over all intersections and CAV-provided detailed traffic state with Double-DQN, which contrasts with the proposed decentralized, minimal-state, PPO-based design and therefore does not validate the specific claim that minimal local observations plus learned communication are sufficient. A fourth tension comes from [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), which achieves strong zero-shot transfer with transformer encoders and multi-scenario co-training but without an explicit inter-agent communication channel or 2D longitude/latitude positional encoding, implying that scenario-level transformer modeling and local encoding may already capture much of the benefit. Additional, weaker contradictions include [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876), whose directed hypergraph approach suggests that pairwise attention may miss higher-order spatial relations, and [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427), which shows that single-intersection meta-learning can generalize across phase structures and cities without inter-agent communication, indicating that the proposed method may need meta-learning or scenario co-training rather than relying solely on communication. The weakest contradiction is from [(Mirbakhsh et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3dc1bad77c9ddf5ce2b60576d7513ace34d233d4), which uses CAV-based MADQN rather than transformer communication but provides no quantitative excerpt, so it mainly highlights a different MDP formulation rather than a direct empirical refutation.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace or constrain the proposed full 2D transformer communication with distance-aware attention, such as exponential decay with normalized longitude/latitude distance, top-k neighborhood masking, or learned distance thresholds, and to ablate neighborhood size, number of attention heads, and positional-encoding variants, because [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) shows that 2 to 5 neighbors and about 5 heads were optimal and that more neighbors degraded performance. The next most important action is to establish a rigorous baseline suite and statistical protocol, including single-agent MLP, MaxPressure, CoLight, MetaLight, X-Light, GNN/MetaGAT/GESA, and a centralized transformer upper bound, with multiple random seeds, confidence intervals, and significance tests for average travel time, queue length, delay, waiting vehicles, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions, because several related works report improvements without sufficient significance or hardware details, as in [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), and [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876). Third, the experiments should explicitly test when communication helps by comparing small and large networks and by using local, nonuniform, and long-route traffic scenarios, motivated by [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), so the 7-agent ring and 73-agent grid should be accompanied by cases where vehicles cross multiple intersections and where local traffic dominates. Fourth, the plan should incorporate multi-scenario co-training, zero-shot transfer evaluation, and heterogeneous intersection normalization, as in [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) and [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427), including phase abstraction or a GPI-like module to unify variable lane and phase configurations before the PointNet lane encoder. Fifth, the variable state-information design should be validated against both minimal local states and richer CAV-style states, using [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) as a minimal-state reference and [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) as a centralized high-information upper bound, while retaining the proposed fuel and CO2 metrics inspired by [(Mirbakhsh et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3dc1bad77c9ddf5ce2b60576d7513ace34d233d4). Finally, the authors should report computational resources, model size, training time, complexity, and hyperparameters, and consider ablations for temporal tokens and higher-order spatial relations, as suggested by [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) and [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876), to ensure the proposed PPO and transformer architecture is reproducible and not merely a less efficient variant of existing attention or hypergraph methods.

## We will model TSC optimization as a multi-agent MDP where agents (intersections with traffic signals) can take actions in their respective action spaces, leading to a global reward. Each agent will have access to a subset of the global state consisting of observations in its proximity. | Synthesis: This defines the reinforcement-learning formulation that frames traffic signal control as decentralized agents with local observations and a shared objective.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed multi-agent MDP with local observations and a shared traffic-efficiency objective comes from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), which uses decentralized intersection agents with local vehicle counts and one-hot phase, phase actions, negative queue-length rewards, an RNN encoder producing a Gaussian latent variable, and a VAE-style intrinsic reward that uses neighbor policies during training but local observations at test time; in CityFlow with 3600-second episodes and 5-second action intervals, it outperformed Individual RL, MetaLight, PressLight, and CoLight by mean improvements of 827, 423, 411, and 43, and in transfer from Hangzhou to other networks it showed only a 9 percent drop versus 38 percent and 41 percent drops for Individual RL and PressLight, while CoLight was incompatible because it required full state information, directly supporting the plan's decentralized local-observation formulation, multi-network training, and minimal-state hypothesis although it uses meta-RL rather than PPO, transformer communication, SUMO, or fuel and CO2 metrics. The next strongest support is [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1), which models intersection agents on a directional adjacency graph with node features, relation-reasoning updates, LSTM temporal dependency over Delta t, a two-layer ReLU MLP for Q-values, replay buffer, target network, epsilon-greedy decay over the first 10 episodes, Adam learning rate 0.001, hidden size 64, 10-second action duration, and gradient clipping at 10; on synthetic 6x6 Bernoulli networks, a one-hour Hangzhou 4x4 dataset, and a Hefei four-intersection peak-hour dataset from 11/06/2018 to 11/12/2018, it achieved a 20.6 percent average travel-time improvement on Hefei and the smallest travel-time standard deviation, supporting the plan's premise that spatial dependencies among nearby intersections improve coordination, but it is an older graph/LSTM Q-learning method rather than the plan's transformer/PPO architecture and does not test variable observation levels or SUMO. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc), whose HumanLight uses FRAP/MPLight-style Q-learning with local person counts from active vehicles, acyclic phase actions among up to eight compatible phases, negative person intersection pressure rewards, CityFlow 10-second actions, 3-second yellow, 2-second all-red, 40 km/h maximum speed, 300 m segments, 10/60/30 turning ratios, and heavy demand with volume-to-capacity above 0.92; it converged in about 160 episodes, reduced person delays and queues by more than 55 percent in a high HOV 4x4 grid, reduced corridor delays by about 10 percent in a 300 m corridor and by more than 50 percent in an 800 m corridor under heavy demand, and showed that discount factor 0.60 outperformed 0.00, supporting local state design, phase compatibility, and multi-metric delay and queue evaluation, but it does not model inter-agent communication, PPO, transformer attention, 2D positional encoding, SUMO, or emissions.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that the most direct evidence for decentralized coordination and transfer does not come from transformer-based inter-agent communication or PPO; as an iterative progression, [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1) established graph/LSTM spatial dependency, [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) then showed that a local-only latent-variable meta-RL policy can transfer with only a 9 percent drop, and [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) later showed that person-centric Q-learning can improve delays without communication, suggesting that explicit communication may not be necessary for generalization and that the plan's transformer channel must be proven superior to local-only PPO and meta-RL. This challenge is reinforced by [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1), where graph/LSTM DQN captures spatial dependencies and obtains a 20.6 percent Hefei travel-time improvement without transformer attention, 2D positional encoding, PPO, or variable observation levels, and by [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc), where person-centric Q-learning improves delays and queues without any communication, indicating that gains may arise from local state and reward design rather than the proposed architecture. A second contradiction concerns reward structure: the plan specifies a global reward based on differences in vehicle waiting time, whereas the related methods use local or locally aggregated rewards, with [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) using negative queue length, [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1) using per-agent rewards, and [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) using negative person intersection pressure, so a global reward may introduce credit-assignment difficulty in decentralized training and is not validated by these works. A third, weaker contradiction is evaluation and reproducibility scope: [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1) reports average travel time and fairness standard deviation without statistical significance tests, [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) reports CityFlow average travel time with baselines averaged over three seeds but no formal significance tests and no hardware or training-time details, and [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) reports standard deviations but no formal significance tests and no hardware or software versions, so none provides the SUMO-based multi-metric evidence for fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, waiting vehicles, travel time, queue length, and delay, none tests a 73-agent network, and none validates 2D positional encoding or distance-based attention masking.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add a controlled ablation matrix that isolates the transformer communication contribution by comparing the full PPO transformer model against local-only PPO with the same PointNet lane encoder, a graph/LSTM DQN or PPO baseline inspired by [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1) using directional adjacency, hidden size 64, 10-second actions, Adam learning rate 0.001, gradient clipping at 10, and epsilon decay over 10 episodes, and a meta-RL or latent-variable baseline inspired by [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) that trains on one network and transfers to others, measuring the same 9 percent versus 38 percent and 41 percent transfer-drop protocol and including CoLight-style full-state baselines to show when centralized information is infeasible. Next, align the evaluation protocol with the strongest related evidence by using SUMO as primary but also CityFlow for comparability, 3600-second episodes, 5-second or 10-second action intervals, static and dynamic flows, high-flow conditions comparable to 4770 vehicles per hour, heavy demand with volume-to-capacity above 0.92, and metrics including average travel time, queue length, delay, fairness standard deviation, stopping frequency and duration, person delay and queue, fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, and waiting vehicles, with at least three random seeds, confidence intervals, and paired significance tests because [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f11209eb84a06ae7b135ec9b6c58baed7ae98fc1) and [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) lacked formal tests and [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) only averaged three seeds. Then incorporate the variable-observation and robustness lessons from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00) and [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) by testing no-traffic, limited high-level, and full sensor states, adding an intrinsic reward or latent variable to reduce dependence on neighbor policies, using person and active-vehicle states with acyclic phase compatibility constraints, and analyzing reward, discount-factor, PPO clip, and entropy sensitivity because [(Vlachogiannis et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8ee96d8c04ec7f1f44ac8aa8af65b29ee98939dc) showed discount 0.60 outperformed 0.00 and higher discounts increased phase changes. Finally, validate the 2D positional encoding and distance-based attention masking as a separate ablation against graph adjacency and no masking, report hardware, training time, convergence episodes, and hyperparameters, and use the automated randomized network pipeline to test variable input sizes and topologies, since none of the related works provides transformer-specific evidence for 2D positional encoding or large-scale 73-agent SUMO training.

## We will optimize the value function and policy using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), with actions representing traffic light phase changes and rewards based on differences in vehicle waiting time. | Synthesis: This specifies the learning algorithm and reward signal used to train the multi-agent signal-control policy.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed PPO method with phase-change actions and waiting-time-based rewards comes from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), which evaluates a decentralized multi-agent traffic signal control method in CityFlow with SUMO robustness checks, where each intersection agent observes local incoming-lane vehicle counts and the current signal phase, selects a signal phase as its action, and receives a reward equal to the negative sum of queue lengths; this directly matches the proposed phase-level action space and waiting/queue-based reward, and the method reports average travel time improvements of 827, 423, and 411 over Individual RL, MetaLight, and PressLight, and a mean improvement of 43 over CoLight, with transfer degradation of only 9% compared with 38% and 41% degradation for Individual RL and PressLight, supporting the research plan emphasis on local observations, multi-agent coordination, and cross-network generalization. A second, more domain-specific but less direct support comes from [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/091461cb18c413c77037a6a5e08415e91a01f4fc), which uses SUMO multi-agent signal control with waiting-time, throughput, and HBEFA-based CO, CO2, and fuel metrics, and includes PPO as a baseline, indicating that PPO is a relevant comparator in the same simulation environment and objective family, although its main algorithm is COMMA-DDPG with continuous green-duration actions and a training-only global agent. Weaker support comes from [(Mousavi et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41aa579df5f20230404714e0d9902de927113ac9), a single four-leg SUMO-GUI v0.28.0 intersection study using 128x128x4 grayscale frames, a CNN with 16 filters of size 8x8 stride 4 and 32 filters of size 4x4 stride 2, a 256-unit fully connected layer, Adam with learning rate 0.00001, discount factor 0.99, mini-batches up to 32, n-step returns, and delay-based reward, where the actor-critic and DQN methods reduced average cumulative delay and queue length by 67-68% and 72-73% relative to a shallow 64-unit queued-vehicle-count network, validating phase-level reinforcement learning and delay rewards but not multi-agent PPO. The weakest support is [(Wu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62876b014f23e081d3e07c98f78f59422df3101d), a single dual-access SUMO intersection study using DDPG, DQN, and NAF with experience buffer 3000, minibatch size 32, and a negative vehicle-occupation reward, which reports improved waiting-time and reward relative to a non-RL baseline requiring 338,798 steps but without numerical waiting-time reductions or significance tests, so it only weakly supports waiting-time rewards in SUMO.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/091461cb18c413c77037a6a5e08415e91a01f4fc), which directly includes PPO/IPPO in a multi-agent SUMO benchmark and reports that IPPO remains unstable while COMMA-DDPG outperforms IDQN, IPPO, FMA2C, MPLight, and MPLight full on delay, speed, time loss, system travel time, and total waiting time at two and five intersections, and outperforms a no-global-agent variant at 16 intersections; this suggests that vanilla PPO may be less stable or less effective than actor-critic continuous-action methods with global coordination for multi-intersection traffic signal control, and that phase-change actions may need to be compared against continuous green-duration actions. A second concern is that the same paper reports fixed-time control and MA-DDPG did not converge and provides no confidence intervals or statistical significance tests, so the evidence that PPO will reliably converge in the proposed 7-agent ring and 73-agent grid SUMO networks is not established. A weaker generalization caveat comes from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), where CoLight could not be evaluated in the transfer setting because it required full state information and a fixed input structure, while the meta-RL method used an RNN latent variable and intrinsic reward to improve transfer; this does not refute PPO, but it implies that PPO with a transformer communication module may still require explicit generalization mechanisms and may underperform if it relies on fixed full-state inputs. A still weaker limitation is from [(Mousavi et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41aa579df5f20230404714e0d9902de927113ac9), which is single-agent, image-based, trained for about 1050 epochs or approximately 2 million time steps with 10 episodes per epoch and 5 evaluation episodes, and reports no hardware specifications or statistical significance tests, so it does not directly support multi-agent PPO or transformer communication. The weakest limitation is from [(Wu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62876b014f23e081d3e07c98f78f59422df3101d), which is single-agent, reports that DQN converged slowly and was excluded from the final waiting-time comparison, and provides no numerical waiting-time reductions or significance tests, so it offers limited evidence against the proposed method but also limited evidence for it.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to mitigate the PPO instability identified in [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/091461cb18c413c77037a6a5e08415e91a01f4fc) by implementing a stable multi-agent PPO variant with clipped surrogate objective, generalized advantage estimation, advantage normalization, entropy regularization, reward scaling, and possibly a centralized critic or training-only global teacher, while benchmarking against COMMA-DDPG, TD3, FMA2C, MPLight, and IPPO in SUMO on 2, 5, 16, 7, and 73 intersections with multiple seeds, confidence intervals, and convergence diagnostics. The next priority is to test the action-space assumption by comparing phase-change actions with continuous green-duration actions, because COMMA-DDPG uses green-light duration and reports lower waiting time and emissions, so the plan should include an ablation showing whether phase changes are sufficient or whether green-duration control is needed. Third, adopt the evaluation and generalization protocol from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00): use local incoming-lane counts and phase as minimal state, negative queue or waiting reward, average travel time, waiting vehicles, queue length, and delay, and include cross-network transfer tests against Individual RL, MetaLight, PressLight, and CoLight where feasible, with at least three random seeds and reported mean improvements. Fourth, refine the reward from differences in vehicle waiting time to a well-specified waiting-time or queue-length signal, possibly with HBEFA-style CO, CO2, and fuel metrics as in [(Tang et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/091461cb18c413c77037a6a5e08415e91a01f4fc), and report reward normalization and sensitivity to reward scaling. Fifth, improve reproducibility by specifying SUMO version, network topology, traffic demand, episode horizon, hardware, PPO hyperparameters, transformer positional encoding, distance masking, and statistical tests, addressing the missing hardware and significance reporting in [(Mousavi et al., 2017-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/41aa579df5f20230404714e0d9902de927113ac9) and [(Wu, 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62876b014f23e081d3e07c98f78f59422df3101d).

## We will design three levels of state observation based on implementation cost: no traffic observation (only traffic light information), limited traffic observation (high-level metrics from platforms like Google Maps), and full observation (detailed sensor data). | Synthesis: This operationalizes the hypothesis that competitive performance can be achieved with minimal state information by comparing observation regimes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed three-level observation design comes from [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c), which evaluated a local rule-based traffic light controller on the Wetstraat network using only local red-light approaching-car counters, a minimum green phase, a platoon-preservation distance of 25, a platoon-breaking threshold of 3 cars, and no fixed phase or inter-intersection communication. Across traffic densities, five one-hour 3600-cycle simulations per condition showed average trip waiting time equal to 34% to 64% of a green-wave baseline, averaging 50%, and the authors estimated about 25% total travel-time improvement; this directly supports the plan's hypothesis that local, low-cost state can be competitive, although it uses vehicle counts rather than only signal-phase information and does not report confidence intervals. A second strong support for the broader transformer communication component is [(Sukhbaatar et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50295c19e177480ba3599300de1ab837cc62b08c), whose CommNet multi-agent traffic junction experiment showed that learned communication can reduce failure rates relative to independent control, with LSTM-module CommNet at 1.6±1.0% versus 9.4±5.6% independent, and discrete communication at 8.4±3.4%, while PCA and brake-event probing indicated sparse, near-zero communication vectors; this supports attention-based inter-agent exchange and the idea that limited communicated information can suffice, but it does not test traffic-light-only, high-level map-metric, or full-sensor observation regimes. Full-observation evidence is provided by [(Zheng et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa9e02a8ccdd24106d71b2155351f088299abf2d), whose FRAP DQN agent in SUMO used movement-level traffic state, phase demand modeling, flip/rotation invariance, and real arrival data from Jinan, Hangzhou, and Atlanta to outperform fixed-time, SOTL, formula-based, image-based DRL, and IntelliLight baselines under 8-phase settings, with 3-second yellow and 2-second all-red timing; this supports the full-observation level as a strong upper-bound benchmark, though it does not quantify the gap to minimal observation. [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) adds support for full lane-level vehicle-count and phase observations plus meta-learning, with MetaLight improving 5.52% over the best baseline in homogeneous Task-1, 22.57% in heterogeneous Task-2, and outperforming Random, Pretrained, MAML, and SOTL in most settings using CityFlow one-hour episodes, 10-second decisions, learning rates 0.001, 30 replay samples, and meta-updates every ten learner updates; this is relevant to the plan's multi-network generalization, but the homogeneous gain was described as not significant enough and the work does not ablate reduced observations. Finally, [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876) supports the need to model spatial and temporal inter-agent dependencies with full traffic-flow and phase observations, using directed hyperedges, actor-critic policies, and CityFlow grids D4x4, D6x6, DHangzhou, and DJinan, with sensitivity analysis showing beta=0.3 optimal and zeta=0.3 for Hangzhou but 0.0 for Jinan; this is weaker direct support for the observation-level hypothesis because it assumes detailed state, but it reinforces the plan's spatial-attention or transformer motivation.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), which evaluated TC-SBC, a compact state-bit congestion controller analogous to the proposed limited-observation regime, in a small multi-intersection network under highly saturated traffic with uniform destinations, equal lane lengths, and vehicle generation probability 0.2 per timestep. In the baseline scenario, TC-SBC performed worse than TC-1 and max-plus, with the paper attributing the result to slower learning caused by a larger state space; although the excerpt lacks numerical values and confidence intervals, this is the most direct evidence that a minimal congestion-state observation may not be competitive under saturated multi-intersection conditions, especially when the learning method cannot efficiently exploit the state. A weaker but important contradiction comes from [(Sukhbaatar et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50295c19e177480ba3599300de1ab837cc62b08c), where limited or structured communication did not uniformly help: discrete communication produced 15.8±9.3%, 15.2±2.1%, and 8.4±3.4% failure rates for MLP, RNN, and LSTM modules, and fully connected control produced 34.8±19.7% for RNN, showing that sparse or limited information can be insufficient if the communication architecture, module type, or task difficulty is mismatched; this cautions against assuming that any reduced observation or communication channel will yield competitive performance. A further indirect contradiction is that the strongest full-observation traffic signal control results, such as [(Zheng et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa9e02a8ccdd24106d71b2155351f088299abf2d), [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427), and [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876), rely on detailed movement-level, lane-level, or traffic-flow observations and report superior performance over baselines, but none directly compares no-traffic-only or high-level map-metric observations; this pattern suggests that detailed state may be necessary to reach state-of-the-art performance, even though it is not a direct falsification of the minimal-state hypothesis. Weaker still, [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c) does not validate the no-traffic-only level because its controller required local approaching-car counts, and its reported 34% to 64% waiting-time advantage over green wave lacked statistical significance tests or confidence intervals, so the evidence for minimal state is suggestive rather than conclusive.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the three observation regimes explicit and directly comparable in SUMO: no-traffic-only should contain only signal phase, timing, and local topology; limited observation should synthesize noisy, delayed, low-resolution platform-like metrics such as average speed, congestion index, or queue estimates rather than raw vehicle positions; and full observation should use detailed lane-level vehicle counts or positions; this addresses the gap left by [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c), which used local counts, and [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), which used a congestion state bit, and prevents conflating minimal state with full sensor access. Second, add strong non-learned and learned baselines matched to each regime, including a Cools-style local counter/threshold controller with minimum green, platoon-preservation, and platoon-breaking parameters, TC-1, TC-SBC, max-plus, fixed-time, SOTL, and full-observation DRL baselines such as FRAP or MetaLight, so that the plan can test whether the transformer PPO model beats both local rule-based and full-observation methods. Third, evaluate across traffic densities and saturated conditions, including Kuyer's uniform, nonuniform, and long-route destination scenarios and Cools' multiple density groups, with one-hour SUMO episodes, multiple independent seeds, confidence intervals, and statistical significance tests, because Cools used five one-hour runs without significance and Kuyer used ten runs but the excerpt lacks uncertainty. Fourth, include the signal-timing and metric details from related work: 3-second yellow and 2-second all-red intervals from [(Zheng et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/aa9e02a8ccdd24106d71b2155351f088299abf2d), average trip waiting time, stopped-vehicle ratio, queue length, average travel time, and the plan's fuel and CO2 metrics, with a primary metric and secondary metrics to avoid metric-specific conclusions. Fifth, ablate the transformer communication architecture against MLP, RNN, and LSTM modules, discrete versus continuous communication, local versus broadcast attention, and distance-decay masking, following [(Sukhbaatar et al., 2016-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/50295c19e177480ba3599300de1ab837cc62b08c), and report attention or communication statistics to verify that the model uses sparse inter-agent information. Sixth, for multi-network training and generalization, incorporate meta-learning or multi-task training inspired by [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427), but test whether standard MAML is insufficient as in that paper, and use dataset-specific sensitivity checks for spatial hyperparameters as in [(Lei et al., 2024-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62a026a233258781ddda1e33e9713c1d70991876), where zeta changed from 0.3 on Hangzhou to 0.0 on Jinan. Finally, document reproducibility details missing from several related works: SUMO version, hardware, training epochs, PPO hyperparameters, network topology, traffic flow generation, random seeds, and observation noise or delay, so that the observation-level comparison is reproducible and not confounded by implementation cost assumptions.

## We will use a PointNet-inspired encoder with shared MLP weights across agents to create canonical representations of lane-level information, handling varying intersection types through permutation-invariance and max-pooling operations. | Synthesis: This provides a permutation-invariant input representation that allows the model to handle heterogeneous intersection geometries and lane configurations.

### Support

The strongest support comes from direct validation of the exact set-encoding primitive: PointNet applies a shared MLP to each point and max-pools to a permutation-invariant global descriptor, with linear complexity, 0.8M parameters and 148M FLOPs for the vanilla model, 3.5M parameters and 440M FLOPs for the full model, and more than one million points per second on a 1080X GPU [(Charles et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d997beefc0922d97202789d2ac307c55c2c52fba). This directly supports using shared MLP weights and max pooling to canonicalize variable lane counts at 7 or 73 SUMO intersections, because lane order is arbitrary and input size varies. DeepSets independently reports 89.2 percent ModelNet40 accuracy with shared MLP pooling on 1024-point clouds, matching PointNet, supporting the same inductive bias for unordered variable-size inputs [(Zaheer et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/de4edcddc028b0088f7ee44ae6e4be261f20b6f6). The strongest traffic-specific support is MetaLight and FRAP++, which share embedding parameters across lanes, use fixed 1x1 convolutional filters, and average lane demands to reduce sensitivity to lane count, achieving an average 22.57 percent travel-time improvement in heterogeneous CityFlow tasks and cross-city gains across 11 phase settings, showing that configuration-robust lane and phase representations can improve traffic signal control [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427). CoLight also supports shared MLP observation embedding plus graph attention for TSC, improving average travel time by 6.98 percent on synthetic data and 11.69 percent on real-world data, with gaps of 8.08 percent and 19.89 percent versus MaxPressure, and finds 2 to 5 neighbors and up to about 5 attention heads useful, supporting the plan's transformer communication although it does not use permutation-invariant max pooling over lanes [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb). VectorNet and follow-ups support canonical lane and map encoding: VectorNet vectorizes polylines and improves Argoverse DE@3s from 4.17 to 4.01 on 333K sequences, and VectorNet-based context encoders and DenseTNT report strong trajectory prediction results, indicating that structured lane geometry is valuable for downstream traffic tasks [(Gao et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/296c808abc2b11c6b7cc44237357b6590bac4f35), [(Zhao et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2cc3bac78dde326eddbc96951df3ab4fc56d3b6), [(Gu et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70ed76ec9bff4eec17e6e0d023f85dffe760b9c1). PointNet++ and PointCNN provide weaker but relevant support for hierarchical and shared-weight set encoders, with PointNet++ motivating robustness to non-uniform sampling and PointCNN using 0.6M parameters on a P100 to report 86.14 part-averaged IoU on ShapeNet Parts [(Qi et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8674494bd7a076286b905912d26d47f7501c4046), [(Li et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6400c36efdb8a66b401b6aef26c057227266fddd). DGCNN supports shared MLP and max pooling while showing that dynamic graph recomputation and centralization can improve ModelNet40 to 93.5 percent overall accuracy with 2048 points and retain reasonable performance when half of input points are dropped, relevant to missing or variable lane observations [(Wang et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1799aaf23c12af6932dc0ef3dfb1638f01413d1). Finally, grid-based trajectory prediction shows that road-map context improves 5-second L2 error by 0.33 m, and set-pooling studies provide implementation context, but these are weaker because they do not test PointNet-style lane encoding for multi-agent traffic signal control [(Hong et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbe4481a9548a790bc5226dc05add2dd4633c353), [(Shahbazi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8843ba92df447d9b001219884b2d12c9bf015dd0).

### Contradictions

The strongest traffic-specific contradiction is that a lane-level graph baseline, GCN-lane, underperformed intersection-level STMARL and was described as failing to learn well in large-scale road networks due to increased graph complexity, while STMARL outperformed the best baseline by 20.6 percent on the Hefei real-world dataset and reported the smallest standard deviation of vehicle travel time; this cautions that lane-level local aggregation alone may be insufficient or scale poorly for the plan's 73-agent SUMO networks [(Wang et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/236b699de5ad17f2870cc96e0fbc4ddd252aa596). A direct architectural contradiction is that PointNet-style max pooling can fail to capture local structure, motivating later neighborhood and graph-based methods, which is relevant because traffic lanes have adjacency, phase, and turn relationships that a flat max-pooled set may discard [(Zhang et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92f2f4dc9ec0c6d91759ab301c249b5bfa379f7c). PointCNN provides quantitative evidence that local canonicalization improves over simpler point-set baselines: the X-transformation raised nearest-center feature classification from 76.83 percent to 94.72 percent, and PointCNN outperformed PointNet++ by 4.04 and 7.55 points on TU-Berlin and Quick Draw, suggesting that simple shared MLP plus max pooling may underuse lane geometry and local relational structure [(Li et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6400c36efdb8a66b401b6aef26c057227266fddd). DGCNN shows modest but consistent gains from centralization and dynamic graph recomputation on ModelNet40, about 0.5 and 0.7 percentage points respectively, and reaches 93.5 percent overall accuracy with 2048 points, implying that adding relational edge features could improve lane encoding beyond a PointNet baseline [(Wang et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1799aaf23c12af6932dc0ef3dfb1638f01413d1). VectorNet's improvement of Argoverse DE@3s from 4.17 to 4.01 using hierarchical polyline graphs suggests that vectorized lane and map structure may outperform a flat max-pooled lane set for geometry-sensitive tasks, although the task is trajectory prediction rather than signal control [(Gao et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/296c808abc2b11c6b7cc44237357b6590bac4f35). CoLight's shared MLP embedding assumes a fixed observation dimension and does not use permutation-invariant max pooling, so its success does not validate the proposed variable-lane set encoder and may indicate that fixed-dimensional lane or phase features are sufficient in some settings [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb). The weakest contradiction is that grid-based and vectorized trajectory methods use raster or polyline encoders rather than PointNet max pooling, but they do not directly refute the proposed method and provide only indirect evidence that alternative road-context encodings can be effective [(Hong et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbe4481a9548a790bc5226dc05add2dd4633c353), [(Zhao et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c2cc3bac78dde326eddbc96951df3ab4fc56d3b6), [(Gu et al., 2021-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70ed76ec9bff4eec17e6e0d023f85dffe760b9c1).

### Suggested Action

Most important, augment the PointNet-inspired encoder with a lane and phase relational module: keep shared MLP weights and max pooling for permutation invariance, but add edge features from lane adjacency, phase membership, stop-line and turn relationships, or a dynamic kNN and VectorNet-style polyline graph, because PointNet lacks local structure and PointCNN, DGCNN, and VectorNet show local relational gains [(Zhang et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/92f2f4dc9ec0c6d91759ab301c249b5bfa379f7c), [(Li et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6400c36efdb8a66b401b6aef26c057227266fddd), [(Wang et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1799aaf23c12af6932dc0ef3dfb1638f01413d1), [(Gao et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/296c808abc2b11c6b7cc44237357b6590bac4f35). Second, normalize lane-level statistics to be insensitive to lane count, using average or softmax-weighted lane demand per phase, fixed 1x1 filters, or set-size normalization, following FRAP++ and MetaLight, and explicitly test 4, 6, and 8 phase settings plus unseen intersections in SUMO [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427). Third, run ablations in the 7-agent ring and 73-agent grid comparing a fixed-dimensional MLP, PointNet max pooling, PointNet plus lane graph, and the full transformer, using PPO, one-hour SUMO episodes, waiting-time rewards, and metrics including travel time, queue length, delay, fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, and waiting vehicles, with multiple seeds and confidence intervals because several related works do not report statistical significance [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427). Fourth, constrain transformer communication based on CoLight by using 2 to 5 nearest neighbors or distance-decay masking, up to about 5 attention heads, and monitor complexity for 73 agents, since adding more neighbors degraded CoLight performance [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb). Fifth, evaluate robustness to missing or variable observations by random lane dropout or the plan's three state levels, inspired by DGCNN's dropout robustness, and report parameters and FLOPs to ensure the encoder remains lightweight relative to PointNet's 0.8M parameter and 148M FLOP profile [(Wang et al., 2018-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1799aaf23c12af6932dc0ef3dfb1638f01413d1), [(Charles et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d997beefc0922d97202789d2ac307c55c2c52fba). Least important, document implementation details such as lane sampling, max pooling over lanes versus phases, 2D positional encoding, and hardware, to improve reproducibility relative to sparse reports in related works [(Shahbazi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8843ba92df447d9b001219884b2d12c9bf015dd0), [(Hong et al., 2019-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fbe4481a9548a790bc5226dc05add2dd4633c353).

## We will implement attention-based communication using transformers, allowing agents to attend to other agents' states with optional distance-based attention masking that exponentially decays with spatial distance. | Synthesis: This specifies the attention mechanism and optional distance decay used to implement the proposed inter-agent communication.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed transformer attention communication with optional exponential distance decay is CoLight [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), which directly uses graph attention between intersections, constrains attention by geographic or node distance, and reports average travel time improvements of 6.98% on synthetic data and 11.69% on real-world data, with a gap over MaxPressure growing from 8.08% on synthetic regular traffic to 19.89% on real-world dynamic traffic; this supports attention and spatial distance constraints for TSC, although it uses a GAT/Q-learning architecture, fixed nearest-neighbor scope, and no exponential decay or PPO. The next strongest support is X-Light [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), which uses a transformer-based TonT Encoder with an actor-critic policy in SUMO and reports zero-shot average trip time and delay time improvements over baselines such as FTC, MaxPressure, MetaLight, GESA, and MetaGAT, supporting transformer modeling for traffic signal control, but it does not explicitly state exponential distance masking or 2D longitude/latitude positional encoding. SAC [(Qu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3deb356189623b83fd2b7fffb25b0fa1f6e1a7fa) provides conceptual support for distance decay by showing that the optimality gap of a κ-hop local communication method decays approximately exponentially with radius on an 8-node line graph, although the setting is a synthetic binary MDP rather than transformer-based TSC. Zheng et al. [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) supports centralized transformer spatio-temporal modeling for TSC in Cityflow, but the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes and uses a centralized node-time transformer rather than decentralized agent-to-agent communication. AgentFormer [(Yuan et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44121878a5f390f178e241310a65afef1ccac33c) supports transformer agent-aware attention for multi-agent dependencies, reporting competitive ETH/UCY results and outperforming Transformer-TF and STAR on nuScenes, but it is a trajectory prediction model without exponential distance decay or traffic control. AutoBot [(Girgis et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1bb8b017c469208bd7e3a80639bdb5f1726e2c) supports set-transformer social attention for multi-agent scene modeling, with decoder social attention reducing collisions and improving scene-level minADE/minFDE on TrajNet++, but it does not use distance-decayed attention. MTR [(Shi et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d532ade62543bfc52a4827f90fa4128dbfffb12f) supports local transformer attention over nearby map and agent tokens, using 16 neighboring tokens and 768 nearest map polylines on Waymo, with mAP +8.48% and miss rate reduced from 15.64% to 13.51%, but it is supervised motion prediction rather than MARL TSC. IC3Net [(Singh et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320f80ad1a95de5824b9477c4e3cec37ee007d10) supports learned communication and individualized rewards, with zero-vision Traffic Junction performance more than 30% better than CommNet and IRIC when the gate is fixed to 1, but it uses a gated continuous channel rather than attention or distance decay. TarMAC [(Das et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea18b3279afe3ffd54da5147dd5c1872315725bf) supports targeted inter-agent communication in Traffic Junction with a centralized-training/decentralized-execution actor-critic setup, but the excerpt lacks quantitative outcomes and does not use transformer attention or exponential masking. TBONE [(Jain et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7cec763510b951fcf8bb93b508e7a49ec5162875) supports explicit and implicit communication for spatially constrained joint action, with vocabulary size 8 improving explicit-only constrained accuracy from 31.8 ± 3.8 to 37.2 ± 4.0, but it is a visual indoor task rather than traffic signal control. VectorNet [(Gao et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/296c808abc2b11c6b7cc44237357b6590bac4f35) supports spatial dependency modeling over trajectories and map polylines, improving Argoverse K=1 DE@3s from 4.17 to 4.01, but it uses graph aggregation rather than transformer attention or exponential distance decay. Finally, DCC-MD [(Kim et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d50a2f71fda43f33328706223e9c6b5bb434da77) supports sparse or masked peer information, with pursuit performance improving as dropout increased from zero, peaking around p = 0.2 to 0.3, but it does not use attention, transformers, or distance decay.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is Wang et al. [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66ab093b76aa8842e7cc8c66fa8d84359b8ce88e), which reports that a communication-minimization method outperforms QMIX+TarMAC by a large margin and that TarMAC struggles in all tested StarCraft II unit micromanagement scenarios, evaluated with 5 random seeds and 95% confidence intervals; this directly undermines the assumption that attention-based inter-agent communication is automatically beneficial, especially in high-stochasticity tasks, and suggests the proposed transformer attention channel must be compared against sparse, gated, or communication-minimizing alternatives. A second important contradiction comes from CoLight [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), where increasing the neighbor count from 2 to 5 improved performance but adding more neighbors degraded it, leading the authors to conclude that attending to four nearby intersections plus itself is adequate; this contradicts a design that lets every agent attend to all other agents with only an exponential decay, because distant intersections may introduce noise and the decay may not sufficiently suppress irrelevant attention. SAC [(Qu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3deb356189623b83fd2b7fffb25b0fa1f6e1a7fa) also warns against assuming monotonic benefit from larger communication radius: although the optimality gap decays approximately exponentially with κ, improvement became small after κ > 1 and the apparent exponential decay stopped for κ > 4, which the authors attribute to increased training complexity and noise, suggesting exponential distance decay may have diminishing returns and training instability. DCC-MD [(Kim et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d50a2f71fda43f33328706223e9c6b5bb434da77) shows that communication masking is not uniformly beneficial: performance improved from p = 0, peaked around p = 0.2 to 0.3, and deteriorated at higher dropout, while simple DCC performed worse than fully decentralized control in the N = 8 pursuit case, indicating that the amount of peer information must be carefully tuned rather than assumed to help. IC3Net [(Singh et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320f80ad1a95de5824b9477c4e3cec37ee007d10) suggests that always-on attention may be wasteful because agents learned to suppress communication when unprofitable, with prey learning silence and competitive predators communicating mainly before reaching the prey; the proposed method currently lacks an explicit communication gate or cost. TBONE [(Jain et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7cec763510b951fcf8bb93b508e7a49ec5162875) provides a weaker contradiction because explicit plus implicit communication was only moderately better than implicit communication on accuracy, and just implicit communication outperformed just explicit communication on accuracy, suggesting that an explicit attention channel may not always improve performance when local visibility or observation is already informative. Finally, Zheng et al. [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) does not directly contradict the proposed mechanism, but it undermines confidence in decentralized distance-decayed attention because the excerpt reports no quantitative performance values, no statistical significance, and uses a centralized node-time transformer rather than explicit agent-to-agent communication.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the distance-based attention mask a first-class ablation rather than an optional detail: implement no mask, all-pair attention, exponential decay with multiple decay lengths, hard κ-hop or nearest-neighbor cutoffs, random message dropout, and communication cost, then report sensitivity curves; this directly addresses CoLight's finding that four nearby intersections plus itself is adequate [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), SAC's exponential but saturating optimality gap [(Qu et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3deb356189623b83fd2b7fffb25b0fa1f6e1a7fa), DCC-MD's moderate dropout optimum [(Kim et al., 2019-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d50a2f71fda43f33328706223e9c6b5bb434da77), IC3Net's learned gating [(Singh et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320f80ad1a95de5824b9477c4e3cec37ee007d10), and Wang et al.'s communication-minimization baseline [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66ab093b76aa8842e7cc8c66fa8d84359b8ce88e). Next, add strong traffic-specific and communication baselines: CoLight, X-Light, TarMAC or QMIX+TarMAC, IC3Net, a simple MLP, no communication, fixed-time, and MaxPressure, with PPO and Q-learning variants where appropriate, so the contribution of transformer attention, distance decay, and 2D positional encoding is isolated [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) [(Das et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea18b3279afe3ffd54da5147dd5c1872315725bf) [(Wang et al., 2019-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/66ab093b76aa8842e7cc8c66fa8d84359b8ce88e) [(Singh et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320f80ad1a95de5824b9477c4e3cec37ee007d10). The 2D positional encoding should be ablated against normalized longitude/latitude, relative offsets, graph distance, map polylines, and local attention over a fixed number of nearby tokens, because CoLight compares geographic and node-distance neighborhoods [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), MTR uses local attention over 16 neighboring tokens and 768 nearest map polylines [(Shi et al., 2022-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d532ade62543bfc52a4827f90fa4128dbfffb12f), VectorNet uses map polylines [(Gao et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/296c808abc2b11c6b7cc44237357b6590bac4f35), and AgentFormer and AutoBot use agent-aware or social attention [(Yuan et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/44121878a5f390f178e241310a65afef1ccac33c) [(Girgis et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c1bb8b017c469208bd7e3a80639bdb5f1726e2c). The plan should also test the three observation levels with sensor faults, limited coverage, and zero-vision conditions, because IC3Net shows communication can help in zero-vision Traffic Junction [(Singh et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/320f80ad1a95de5824b9477c4e3cec37ee007d10), TBONE shows visibility and explicit communication interact [(Jain et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7cec763510b951fcf8bb93b508e7a49ec5162875), and CoLight and X-Light rely on local intersection observations [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83). ## We will conduct comprehensive experiments using the SUMO simulation environment to validate our approach across multiple scenarios: | Synthesis: This establishes the simulation-based experimental platform for testing the proposed multi-agent traffic signal control method.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed SUMO-based experimental platform comes from [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/02262750e20410b55cc4f627cd2d3aa73d5d8ff3), which directly validates a SUMO + PPO traffic signal control pipeline with 30 parallel processes, 10 million environment steps, 12 constructed intersections, 8 training and 4 test intersections, 100 unique 30,000-second routes per intersection, a three-quarters training and one-quarter evaluation split, and average waiting time as the primary metric. Its reported 15% and 12% average travel-time reductions, and up to 32.9% and 41.3% reductions versus MPLight and AttendLight, support the proposed plan's use of SUMO, PPO, waiting-time evaluation, multi-intersection training, and generalization to unseen networks. The next strongest support is [(Azevedo et al., 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/304a562ce7992f71b578370d58229ae00a8a1233), which earlier established a SUMO/JADE/TraSMAPI multi-agent traffic light evaluation with distributed per-light Q-learning agents, neighbor discovery, phase selection, and metrics including per-vehicle waiting time, travel time, global metrics, and standard deviations; this supports the proposed simple 7-agent ring proof-of-concept and the inclusion of homogeneity metrics, although it is weaker because it reports no numerical values, statistical significance, hardware, hyperparameters, emissions, or large-scale networks. [(Ma et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab954896c78f4c1136e3178ef08a3316d996d316) provides more recent but narrower support by validating a SUMO-RL intersection benchmark with phase switching, stochastic arrivals, switching-cost-adjusted net value, and reported improvements of 6.2 to 7.0 net value with 100% responsible rate, which supports the proposed use of SUMO for policy evaluation and the need to measure switching behavior. The weakest support is [(Busch et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d29bde77a21d54e1331531487f2c6e4ba069db3), which only indicates policy-based reinforcement learning for traffic signal control and performance-comparison scenarios without quantitative outcomes, simulation platform, network size, hardware, hyperparameters, or metrics, so it cannot strongly support the proposed transformer-based multi-agent SUMO experiments.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the most detailed evidence, [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/02262750e20410b55cc4f627cd2d3aa73d5d8ff3), achieves strong generalization with a universal single-agent PPO controller rather than a multi-agent transformer communication architecture; its state used an 8-by-8 junction matrix, K = 8 historical snapshots, 5-second action interval, 150-meter vehicle observation, 3-second yellow phase, zero-padding, and two-layer fully connected actor/critic networks, with transformer variants evaluated as backbones rather than as inter-agent spatial communication. This suggests that the proposed transformer-based inter-agent communication must be justified against strong local-state PPO baselines, because multi-intersection generalization may be achievable without explicit agent-to-agent attention. The next strongest caution comes from [(Azevedo et al., 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/304a562ce7992f71b578370d58229ae00a8a1233), which found that global differences between semaphore plans were small, that no single plan was better for most vehicles, and that a semi-fixed plan could produce lower queue waiting times than Q-Learning B while increasing travel time; this contradicts the assumption that a learned multi-agent controller will uniformly improve all traffic metrics and implies that the proposed one-hour SUMO experiments need multiple metrics, per-vehicle and global aggregates, standard deviations, and statistical significance rather than relying on a single waiting-time score. [(Ma et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab954896c78f4c1136e3178ef08a3316d996d316) also contradicts a reward design based only on differences in vehicle waiting time by showing that switching costs can materially affect policy quality; its NAC method used switching-cost parameters c_l = 5 and c_t = 0 and evaluated switch proportion and responsible rate, indicating that the proposed PPO reward should penalize unnecessary phase changes or report switching stability. The weakest contradiction is the absence of quantitative evidence in [(Busch et al., 2020-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d29bde77a21d54e1331531487f2c6e4ba069db3), which provides no dataset, simulation platform, network size, hardware, hyperparameters, statistical tests, or baseline comparisons, so it cannot support the proposed specific claims about transformer communication, 73-agent grids, one-hour simulations, fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, or automated dataset generation.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the proposed SUMO experiments directly comparable to the strongest existing evidence by adding strong baselines and ablations: train the proposed transformer multi-agent PPO model against a local PPO/MLP agent, fixed-time control, Webster, SOTL, MPLight, AttendLight, and a UniTSA-like universal single-agent model using the same SUMO environment, PPO implementation, 30 parallel processes, 10 million environment steps, 5-second action interval, 3-second yellow phase, 150-meter observation radius, zero-padded variable intersection states, and multiple random seeds with statistical significance tests. The second most important action is to adopt the evaluation protocol suggested by [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/02262750e20410b55cc4f627cd2d3aa73d5d8ff3) and [(Azevedo et al., 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/304a562ce7992f71b578370d58229ae00a8a1233): use 100 unique routes per network, split routes into training and evaluation sets, test on unseen networks, report average waiting time, travel time, queue length, delay, fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, per-vehicle and global standard deviations, and convergence curves, while keeping the proposed one-hour simulations for final validation and using shorter runs for ablations. The third most important action is to incorporate switching-cost penalties or switching-cost-adjusted rewards, following [(Ma et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab954896c78f4c1136e3178ef08a3316d996d316), and report switch proportion, phase stability, and responsible rate to prevent the proposed waiting-time reward from favoring excessive phase changes. The fourth action is to explicitly test the transformer communication hypothesis by ablating inter-agent attention, 2D positional encoding, distance-based attention masking, permutation-invariant lane encoding, and the three state-information levels on the 7-agent ring and 73-agent grid, with hardware, compute time, and hyperparameters reported to ensure reproducibility. The final action is to use the automated dataset generation pipeline to create randomized networks and traffic demands with controlled hyperparameters such as number of intersections and average traffic density, then validate that the unified model generalizes to both synthetic and imported real-world networks, as supported by the multi-intersection and unseen-test protocol in [(Wang et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/02262750e20410b55cc4f627cd2d3aa73d5d8ff3).

## We will train both our transformer model and a baseline Simple MLP on a ring network with 7 agents using static traffic flow. We will test all three levels of state information availability to establish proof of concept and compare convergence rates and performance between models. | Synthesis: This simple-network experiment validates the basic architecture and isolates the effect of state information on a controlled benchmark.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805) because it directly validates the proposed simple-network logic: static SUMO traffic, small controlled networks, MARL baselines, five independent runs with standard deviations, and periodic 400-step evaluations after 1000 steps to produce learning curves. Its QCOMBO method, combining independent DQN loss, centralized global action-value loss, and a consistency regularizer, achieved queue length 1.80 +/- 0.05, wait time 0.03 +/- 0.00, and vehicle delay 3.30 +/- 0.01 on the 2x2 balanced network, outperforming independent DQN at 23.92, 191.98, and 3.44, COMA at 2.10, 0.05, and 3.26, QMIX at 25.10, 115.72, and 3.57, VDN at 38.12, 106.34, and 3.45, random, and static policies, which supports the plan's emphasis on comparing convergence and performance under controlled static flow and on the value of coordinating local agents. As an iterative successor to earlier rule-based and static-signal work, it shows that learned coordination can be evaluated on small static benchmarks. Next, [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01) extends this line to a larger dynamic SUMO setting and supports the importance of state information and training budget: its MAA2C agents use local wait, arrival, wave states, and relevant agent policies, and joint signal-plus-route control outperformed signal-only and route-only control in total arrived vehicles, average delay, average speed, and queue per approach over 10 episodes, with 3600-second episodes, 5-second control steps, 720 steps per episode, and 500,000 control steps of training. Then [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c) supports the choice of waiting and travel-time metrics and one-hour evaluation: its rule-based controller reduced average trip waiting time to 34-64% of green wave, with an average of 50%, and estimated about 25% total travel time improvement over a minimum 60-second route, using five one-hour runs of 3600 cycles. Weakest support is [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) because it demonstrates multi-agent traffic-light coordination through factorized Q-functions, DQN, max-plus, and transfer from two-agent source problems, but it provides no quantitative outcomes, uses a different reward, and does not test transformers, PPO, or the proposed 7-agent ring.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest controlled MARL evidence does not show a transformer or PPO advantage; [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805) used three-layer fully connected Q and critic networks, optional RNN or GRU variants, and a value-decomposition or consistency objective, and still achieved the best final outcomes on small static SUMO networks, with QCOMBO queue 1.80 +/- 0.05 and wait 0.03 +/- 0.00 on the 2x2 network, suggesting that a simple MLP baseline or value-decomposition method may be competitive and that the proposed transformer communication must be justified against stronger non-transformer MARL baselines. A second contradiction is that a non-learning local controller can already produce large gains, weakening the claim that learned inter-agent communication is necessary for the simple-network proof of concept: [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c) used only local counters of cars approaching red lights, a threshold, minimum green phase, platoon protection at 25 distance, and a high-density override at 3 cars, yet achieved average trip waiting time of 34-64% of green wave and about 25% total travel time improvement, robust across parameters, so the proposed transformer should be compared against such a rule-based baseline rather than only a Simple MLP. A third limitation is that the newer MARL traffic evidence uses an LSTM-based architecture and a different task, so it does not directly support transformer attention on a 7-agent static ring: [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01) used MAA2C with input-specific fully connected layers, an LSTM hidden layer, softmax policy and scalar value outputs, on a modified Sioux Falls network with 17 signal agents and 12 routing agents, time-varying demand, and joint route guidance, and it did not report statistical significance or confidence intervals, so it cannot supersede the more controlled quantitative comparisons in [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805). The weakest contradiction is [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) because it relies on factorized Q-learning, DQN, and max-plus coordination, with optimal finite-iteration guarantees only for acyclical coordination graphs, and it reports no numerical travel-time, convergence, or significance results, so it cannot validate the proposed transformer/PPO design and its different reward function limits comparability.

### Suggested Action

Most importantly, expand the baseline suite beyond Simple MLP to include the strongest evidence-based comparators: a rule-based local controller in the style of [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c) with local approach counters, threshold, minimum green, platoon protection, and high-density override; static or green-wave policies; independent PPO or DQN; and value-decomposition or centralized MARL baselines such as VDN, QMIX, COMA, or a QCOMBO-like consistency-regularized DQN inspired by [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805), because that work showed QCOMBO beating independent DQN, COMA, QMIX, and VDN on small static networks. Second, adopt the evaluation protocol that makes the simple-network proof of concept credible: use SUMO static flow on the 7-agent ring, run one-hour episodes as in [(Cools et al., 2006-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ea05b32801eb6f7d5037e9701e6e96d7b8b755c) and [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01), report queue length, wait time, vehicle delay, travel time, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions with mean and standard deviation over at least five independent seeds, and plot learning curves using periodic evaluation such as the 400-step evaluations after 1000 steps used by [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805); include convergence criteria and training budget, for example the 500,000 control-step scale reported by [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01) if the 7-agent ring requires comparable exploration. Third, make the three state-information levels operationally comparable and fair: define no-traffic, limited-traffic, and full-traffic observations with identical action spaces, rewards based on waiting-time differences, normalized and clipped inputs as in [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01), and ensure the Simple MLP and transformer receive the same information at each level so that the ablation isolates architecture rather than input richness. Fourth, report implementation details needed for reproducibility, including network topology, traffic flow rates, SUMO version, hardware, optimizer, learning rate, PPO hyperparameters, attention masking, positional encoding, and random seeds, because the related work often omits hardware and exact hyperparameters, as in [(Zhang et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1cb38a9b6c7f7b3dc4bd2c53354aedbc5e80b805) and [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01). Finally, if the transformer does not outperform the Simple MLP or value-decomposition baselines on the 7-agent static ring, treat that as a meaningful negative result and consider adding a factorized-Q or max-plus coordination baseline from [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) or a stronger centralized critic before scaling to the 73-agent dynamic grid.

## We will evaluate our transformer model on a complex grid network with 73 agents using dynamic traffic flow that varies over time. We will train the model with all levels of state information to assess scalability and robustness to network complexity. | Synthesis: This complex-network experiment tests whether the transformer-based communication approach scales to larger, time-varying traffic environments.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 73-agent dynamic-grid transformer experiment comes from [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), which is the closest prior method because it uses a transformer-based multi-agent TSC model in SUMO, an actor-critic policy, a TonT encoder with Lower and Upper Transformers, a GPI module for variable intersection structures, and multi-scenario co-training on seven SUMO scenarios with 3600-second episodes. It reports the best average trip time and delay on five test scenarios in zero-shot transfer, with gains over the best baseline of 1.5% on Grid4x4 trip time, 12.9% on Grid5x5 trip time, 5.1% on Arterial4x4 trip time, 2.7% on Ingolstadt21 trip time, 2.1% on Cologne8 trip time, and corresponding delay gains of 3.4%, 19.7%, 6.4%, 9.6%, and 7.1%; in seen-scenario evaluation it reports values such as 162.47 s trip time and 50.27 s delay on Grid4x4, 220.63 s trip time and 187.74 s delay on Grid5x5, and 88.55 s trip time and 24.31 s delay on Cologne8. This directly supports the research plan's use of transformer attention, actor-critic/PPO-style optimization, SUMO, and multi-network training, although it does not test 73 agents, explicit time-varying demand, or the three state-information levels. The next strongest support is [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047), which shows that a centralized transformer can model spatial and temporal dependencies in TSC by treating intersections and timestamps as tokens, using a maximum temporal window of 10, 200 training rounds, 4-hour simulations per round, and 100 Double-DQN epochs per round on a 6x6 Cityflow grid and a Manhattan 28x7 active-agent grid with taxi-derived flow; although it is centralized and CAV-based rather than a 73-agent PPO model, it supports the feasibility of transformer attention on grids larger than 73 and on temporal context relevant to dynamic flow. Older but still relevant, [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) supports attention-based coordination and dynamic-traffic benefits: its GAT Q-learning CoLight model improved average travel time by 6.98% on synthetic Arterial 1x3, Grid 3x3, and Grid 6x6 networks and 11.69% on real New York, Hangzhou 16-intersection, and Jinan 12-intersection networks, with advantage over MaxPressure increasing from 8.08% on synthetic regular traffic to 19.89% on real dynamic traffic, and it reports O(mL) complexity independent of intersection count; this supports the hypothesis that learned attention can scale and that dynamic real-world demand can amplify gains, but it is smaller than 73 agents and value-based rather than PPO. Finally, [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) supports the broader goal of generalizable TSC by showing MetaLight's meta-RL DQN framework improves over the best baseline by 22.57% in heterogeneous CityFlow tasks and outperforms MAML in cross-city settings, with 3600-second episodes, 10-second control steps, learner learning rate 0.001, 30-sample one-epoch learner updates, and meta-learner updates every ten learner updates; however, it does not use transformers, 73 agents, dynamic time-varying demand, or variable state-information levels, so its support for this specific complex-grid experiment is indirect.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the closest transformer multi-agent evidence does not guarantee large or uniform gains at the proposed scale: [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) reports small negative seen-scenario gaps of -0.7% on Grid4x4 trip time and -1.3% on Grid4x4 delay, and modest zero-shot gains such as 1.5% on Grid4x4 trip time and 2.1% on Cologne8 trip time, indicating that transformer-based multi-agent TSC can underperform or only marginally improve baselines in some scenarios, especially when the test network is already included in co-training; it also does not report explicit dynamic time-varying demand, 73-agent grids, or the proposed three state-information levels. A second important caution comes from [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), which found that attention performance was best with a small neighborhood of about four nearby intersections plus the target and that multi-head attention improved performance only up to about five heads, with diminishing or negative effects beyond that; this contradicts an assumption that a full 73-agent transformer with global 2D positional encoding will automatically benefit from attending to all agents, and suggests that without distance-based masking, local sparsification, or neighborhood ablations, the proposed model may over-attend, waste capacity, or become unstable. Third, [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) provides weaker support because the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, no statistical significance, and no comparisons to fixed-time or MaxPressure values, and its centralized transformer relies on CAV-derived observations, Double-DQN/imitation training, and a 10-step temporal window rather than decentralized PPO with minimal state information; therefore it does not validate the proposed variable state-information levels or multi-agent scalability to 73 agents. Fourth, [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) shows that generalization is not automatic: MetaLight's homogeneous Task-1 improvement was only 5.52% and described as not significant, MAML often performed close to random initialization in heterogeneous settings, and the method required meta-learning across 24 Hangzhou training scenarios and cross-city tasks; this suggests that the proposed multi-network training may need meta-learning, robust initialization, or explicit transfer evaluation to avoid poor performance on unseen complex grids.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the 73-agent transformer attention local and distance-aware, then ablate neighborhood size, number of heads, and masking, because [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) found best performance with about four nearby intersections plus the target and diminishing returns beyond about five attention heads; the proposed 2D positional encoding and exponential distance decay should be tested against top-k local attention, radius-limited attention, and a CoLight/GAT baseline to ensure scalability and avoid over-attention. The second most important action is to explicitly construct and report dynamic time-varying demand profiles, such as rush-hour, off-peak, incident, and heterogeneous flow patterns, and to evaluate time-resolved travel time, delay, queue length, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions over 3600-second SUMO episodes, because [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) showed larger advantages over MaxPressure under real dynamic traffic, while [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) does not provide explicit dynamic-flow evidence. Third, adopt the multi-scenario co-training and zero-shot transfer protocol used by [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) and [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427): train on multiple networks of varying complexity, hold out at least one network for zero-shot testing, report seen and unseen performance, and consider meta-learning or robust initialization if homogeneous gains are small. Fourth, systematically ablate the three state-information levels with statistical significance and multiple seeds, using full sensor or CAV-like observation as an upper bound inspired by [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047), limited high-level observation, and no traffic observation, while reporting waiting vehicles, travel time, queue length, delay, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions as specified in the research plan. Fifth, improve reproducibility by reporting SUMO version, hardware, training steps, optimizer, learning rates, PPO hyperparameters, number of seeds, episode length, and convergence curves, because the related works omit many implementation details; for example, [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) specifies 3600-second episodes, 10-second control steps, learning rate 0.001, 30-sample learner updates, and meta-learner updates every ten learner updates, and [(Zheng et al., 2021-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3ef211a46b6f24930b148b12d69a8dbab0390047) specifies temporal window 10, 200 rounds, 4-hour simulations, and 100 Double-DQN epochs per round, so the proposed experiment should provide comparable detail. Sixth, include strong baselines and ablations: fixed-time control, MaxPressure, CoLight, MetaLight, X-Light, IPPO/rMAPPO, a GNN ablation, a Simple MLP baseline, and if feasible a centralized transformer with temporal context, to isolate the contribution of multi-agent PPO, transformer communication, and variable state information.

## We will implement and test our automated pipeline for generating randomized road networks and traffic demands, sampling environments conditioned on hyperparameters such as number of intersections and average traffic density. This will enable training without reliance on limited real-world data. | Synthesis: This data-generation method creates scalable synthetic training environments to overcome limited real-world traffic data.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed automated pipeline is [(Li et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9dae8d598f9dd9d8c96f771699f5e37a27e71df3), which uses a hierarchical scenario-composition pipeline with managers to instantiate generated maps, rule-based traffic, replay traffic, and multi-agent settings, and reports that increasing the number of training scenarios improves generalization, with higher test success and lower violation/crash rates; its compute details, including 300 FPS for one agent with 10 rule-based vehicles, 60 FPS for 40 RL agents, RLLib trials using 2 CPUs and 8 parallel rollout workers, and 5 random seeds, support the research plan's need for scalable multi-network training and reproducible synthetic scenario sampling, although it does not explicitly condition on average traffic density. The next strongest support is [(Kheterpal et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7bc1dffcf9f72f1c801199ed5676967baa07cbd), which demonstrates a Flow task-design pipeline for SUMO that programmatically creates net.xml files, calls netconvert, optionally converts OpenStreetMap networks, specifies vehicle types, routes, IDM noise N(0, 0.2), and uses TraCI subscriptions, showing that synthetic SUMO environments can be generated and coupled to RL; its 230 m two-lane ring with 42 vehicles and average velocity increase from 2.83 m/s to 3.11 m/s is small-scale but directly relevant to automated environment construction. Then [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) supports the use of synthetic TSC networks by evaluating Arterial 1x3, Grid 3x3, and Grid 6x6 environments with 3, 9, and 36 intersections, four-way intersections, three lanes per direction, 300 m lane length, 3 m lane width, and uniform flows of 300 vehicles per lane per hour West-East and 90 vehicles per lane per hour South-North, reporting a 6.98% average travel-time improvement over baselines and an 8.08% average gap versus MaxPressure on synthetic regular traffic. Weaker but foundational support comes from [(Krajzewicz et al., 2012-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e62ded610aeb17cc65f9f7159477e48248a98a2), which documents SUMO's network creation/import, trip and route generation, traffic-light modeling, and TraCI online control, providing the simulation substrate for the proposed pipeline. The weakest support is [(Salenek et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2aaef2834fec90492be7967312125145fc17b9f), whose detector-based dfrouter route generation is useful only when entrance and exit detectors fully cover highway or motorway sections, implying that synthetic demand generation may be preferable when detector coverage is incomplete, but it provides no quantitative validation for randomized TSC environment sampling. Across the iterative literature, the 2012 SUMO substrate was extended by the 2018 Flow pipeline, then by the 2019 CoLight TSC testbeds, and later by the 2021 MetaDrive scenario-composition evidence, while the 2024 dfrouter work is a narrower demand-generation extension.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the closest TSC synthetic evidence, [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb), uses manually specified fixed networks and fixed regular flow rates rather than an automated pipeline conditioned on number of intersections and average traffic density, and its synthetic regular traffic produced a smaller average gap to MaxPressure, 8.08%, than the 19.89% gap on real-world dynamic traffic, suggesting that training only on regular synthetic demand may understate real-world difficulty and overestimate transfer. The next strongest contradiction is [(Kheterpal et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7bc1dffcf9f72f1c801199ed5676967baa07cbd), which, although it demonstrates programmatic SUMO scenario generation, is limited to predefined scenario generators such as a 230 m two-lane ring with 42 vehicles, does not sample randomized intersection grids, does not condition demand on average traffic density, and reports no statistical significance tests, hardware, training time, rollout count, or hyperparameters, so it does not validate the proposed density-conditioned multi-intersection pipeline. Then [(Salenek et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2aaef2834fec90492be7967312125145fc17b9f) contradicts any demand-generation design that relies on simple detector-count conservation in complex urban settings, because dfrouter assumes equal entering and exiting vehicle counts per interval, ignores transit time, and may generate implausible routes in more complex networks, which is a risk for multi-intersection TSC training if route plausibility is not checked. The weakest contradiction is [(Krajzewicz et al., 2012-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e62ded610aeb17cc65f9f7159477e48248a98a2), which does not provide a controlled quantitative benchmark for randomized road-network generation or traffic-demand synthesis, no baseline comparisons, and no statistical significance tests, so SUMO's general capabilities alone do not prove that the proposed automated pipeline will produce valid, scalable, and reproducible training environments. Read iteratively, the 2019 CoLight result tempers the 2018 Flow evidence by showing that small or regular synthetic scenarios may not capture real-world TSC difficulty, while the 2024 dfrouter result tempers detector-based demand inference for complex urban grids.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to build a SUMO/Flow-style parameterized generator that explicitly samples network topology and demand hyperparameters, including intersection count, grid/arterial topology, lane count, lane length, turn restrictions, origin-destination matrices, departure-time distributions, vehicle types, and average traffic density, and to validate the generated environments with network statistics, route-plausibility checks, conservation diagnostics, and simulation throughput before using them for PPO training. This directly addresses the gaps in [(Kheterpal et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7bc1dffcf9f72f1c801199ed5676967baa07cbd) and [(Krajzewicz et al., 2012-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e62ded610aeb17cc65f9f7159477e48248a98a2), which provide pipeline and SUMO tools but not density-conditioned randomized TSC validation. The second most important action is to include dynamic, heterogeneous, and real-world-calibrated traffic demand rather than only uniform fixed flows, because [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) shows that regular synthetic traffic can make the performance gap to MaxPressure appear smaller, 8.08%, than on real dynamic traffic, 19.89%; the pipeline should therefore sample time-varying rush-hour profiles, mixed OD patterns, and imported real-world networks for generalization testing. The third most important action is to adopt the manager-based scenario composition and reproducibility practices from [(Li et al., 2021-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9dae8d598f9dd9d8c96f771699f5e37a27e71df3), including multiple random seeds, parallel rollout workers, reported FPS or CPU usage, and statistical significance tests, and to benchmark PPO against a more stable alternative such as SAC if training instability appears. The least important, but still necessary, action is to use detector-based route generation such as [(Salenek et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a2aaef2834fec90492be7967312125145fc17b9f) only for fully instrumented corridor segments, while avoiding conservation-only route inference in complex urban grids and instead using SUMO trip/route generation with explicit OD demand and plausibility validation.

## We will simultaneously train our model on multiple road networks of varying complexities to develop a unified model capable of generalizing across diverse environments and traffic demands. This will test our architecture's ability to handle variable input sizes and network topologies. | Synthesis: This multi-network training procedure tests generalization across different road topologies and traffic demands rather than fitting to a single network.

### Support

The strongest support is from the newest directly analogous work, [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), which co-trains one X-Light model on seven SUMO scenarios, uses a GPI module to map differently structured intersections into a unified four-leg representation with relative angles and masking, uses a TonT encoder with lower and upper transformers and actor-critic training, and evaluates leave-one-out zero-shot transfer; it achieved the lowest average trip time and delay in all five held-out scenarios, with Grid4x4 162.65 s trip and 50.90 s delay, Grid5x5 243.26 s and 193.84 s, Arterial4x4 361.38 s and 705.48 s, Ingolstadt21 280.20 s and 160.42 s, and Cologne8 88.49 s and 24.71 s, against baselines including FTC, MaxPressure, MPLight, IPPO, rMAPPO, CoLight, MetaLight, GESA, MetaGAT, and OursGNN. This directly supports the proposed simultaneous multi-network training and transformer-based generalization objective, although it normalizes intersection structure rather than explicitly varying network input size. The next strongest support is [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), which builds on earlier meta-learning by showing that a decentralized MetaVIM policy trained on Hangzhou transferred to Jinan, New York, and Shenzhen with only a 9 percent performance drop, while Individual RL and PressLight dropped 38 and 41 percent, and CoLight could not be evaluated because it required full state and a fixed number of intersections; this supports the plan's minimal-state and variable-intersection-count goals, but uses a recurrent latent task variable rather than transformer communication. [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) provides earlier support for structure-agnostic generalization, with MetaLight using FRAP++, shared parameters, 24 Hangzhou training scenarios, CityFlow 3600-s episodes, 10-s control, learning rate 0.001, learner 30 samples and one epoch per update, and meta-learner updates every 10 learner updates, reporting 5.52 percent average improvement in homogeneous tasks, 22.57 percent in heterogeneous tasks, and strong cross-city transfer to Jinan, Atlanta, and Los Angeles, though it is intersection-level rather than network-level. [(Li et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9ff9afb6c444ee7c0eae90a3d21490836a856c6) supports communication-based multi-agent control, with KS-DDPG using actor layers 512 and 256, critic layers 1024, 512, and 256, Adam learning rates 0.001 for critics and 0.0001 for policies, discount 0.95, replay buffer 1,000,000, batch size 1024, updates every 100 samples, and tau 0.01, reducing queue length by 28.9 percent, delay by 35.1 percent, stops by 21.0 percent, and increasing speed by 18.3 percent over MaxPressure on a 10x10 SUMO grid, and keeping queue below 10 veh/ln versus fixed-time above 25 veh/ln in Montgomery County high demand, but it trains separately on each network rather than jointly. Older work by [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573) and [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) provides weaker motivation: Kuyer tested max-plus coordination against TC-1 and TC-SBC under saturated traffic with 10 runs and 0.2 vehicle generation probability, but found no reported significant small-network difference when traffic was local, and Pol uses DQN source-to-target transfer with max-plus coordination but reports no quantitative outcomes. The weakest support comes from [(Bazzan, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b78b6c66c92fe452eae04a01d590a5dddc272488) and [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01), which are qualitative or fixed-topology: Bazzan reports effective learning in a 16-intersection hierarchical network without quantitative baselines, and Peng tests joint signal and route control on a fixed modified Sioux Falls network with 17 signal agents, 12 route agents, 3600-s episodes, 5-s steps, and 500,000 training steps, but does not vary network size or test multi-network co-training.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is from [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), because its best cross-network transfer result comes from a decentralized local-observation meta-RL policy that does not use neighbor communication, and it reports that CoLight, a centralized full-state method, was incompatible with networks of different sizes; this challenges the proposed core assumption that transformer-based inter-agent communication is necessary for generalization across variable topologies, and suggests that communication may introduce nonstationarity or fixed-size assumptions unless carefully masked. The second strongest contradiction is the limitation of [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83): although it supports co-training, it uses only seven scenarios, normalizes intersections to a four-leg representation, reports standard deviations with some zero-shot differences small relative to those standard deviations, and provides no statistical significance tests, so it does not fully validate arbitrary variable input sizes, topology generalization, or the specific PPO transformer design. [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) contradicts the network-level scope by showing transfer at the intersection level with DQN and meta-learning, without inter-agent communication, and without full road-network coordination. [(Li et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9ff9afb6c444ee7c0eae90a3d21490836a856c6) contradicts the simultaneous multi-network training claim by training KS-DDPG separately on the 10x10 grid and Montgomery County network, showing communication benefits but not a unified variable-size model. [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573) warns that larger state spaces can hurt learning, because TC-SBC performed worse than TC-1 and max-plus in the small saturated network, which is a caution for transformer models with variable and potentially large neighborhood inputs. [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) is weakly contradictory in that it relies on sequential source-to-target DQN transfer and max-plus coordination rather than simultaneous multi-network training, and it provides no quantitative evidence. The weakest contradictions are from [(Bazzan, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b78b6c66c92fe452eae04a01d590a5dddc272488) and [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01), which do not test variable network sizes: Bazzan uses a fixed 16-intersection hierarchy with qualitative results, and Peng uses a fixed modified Sioux Falls network with 17 signal agents and 12 route agents, so they do not support the specific variable-input multi-network training method.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to adopt and extend the leave-one-out zero-shot protocol from [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83) by simultaneously training one transformer PPO model on a diverse SUMO suite with variable agent counts and topologies, such as a 7-agent ring, a 17-agent Sioux Falls-like network, a 33-intersection Shenzhen-like network, a 73-agent grid, and real-world networks, then evaluating held-out networks without retraining; report average trip time, delay, queue length, stops, speed, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions over 3600-s episodes with multiple seeds, standard deviations, and significance tests, and use the automated dataset generation pipeline to create more than the seven scenarios used by Jiang. The second most important action is to include a no-communication or decentralized meta-RL ablation inspired by [(Zhu et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ee3f989a4c39836e92e801fa71266dc258049e00), using local observations, unified phase action masking, latent task or network embeddings, and intrinsic robustness rewards, to isolate whether transformer communication improves over strong local policies and to avoid CoLight-style fixed-size or full-state failure. The third most important action is to use structure-agnostic lane and phase encodings from [(Zang et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d766b67868b3a9a97ad4407cd496760698d2a427) and [(Jiang et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c94b6bd18cbd86d59947be9e8f168279f2348f83), such as unified four-leg or phase-setting representations with masking, and test heterogeneous phase settings including 4-phase, 6-phase, and 8-phase configurations, because MetaLight showed 22.57 percent heterogeneous improvement and X-Light's GPI module is directly relevant. The fourth most important action is to design traffic-demand scenarios that separate local from non-local traffic and saturated or recurrent congestion, following [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573) and [(Li et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e9ff9afb6c444ee7c0eae90a3d21490836a856c6), so coordination benefits are not masked by local trips and are tested under high demand such as Montgomery County flow rising from about 332 to 694 pcu/h/ln. The fifth most important action is to report implementation details needed for reproducibility, including PPO hyperparameters, transformer layers, attention masking, 2D positional encoding, batch size, rollout or replay settings, hardware, training steps, convergence curves, and comparisons to baselines such as MaxPressure, CoLight, MetaLight, GESA, MetaGAT, X-Light, and KS-DDPG. Lower-priority actions include adding hierarchical region-level attention or LSTM prediction as in [(Bazzan, 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b78b6c66c92fe452eae04a01d590a5dddc272488) for scalability, optionally testing route-guidance interactions as in [(Peng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/944324b39827f4274fa25e906dad6332043a0d01), and including a sequential transfer-planning baseline inspired by [(Pol, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b90978694d80bf595663904ae36d4c367273f669) to compare simultaneous co-training against source-to-target DQN transfer.

## We will evaluate our trained models on both synthetically generated networks and imported real-world road networks to assess transfer capabilities and practical applicability. | Synthesis: This generalization test measures whether models trained on synthetic or multiple networks can transfer to realistic road networks.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed generalization test is that [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) evaluates a graph-attention reinforcement learning traffic signal controller on both synthetic arterial and grid networks and imported real-world OpenStreetMap networks from New York, Hangzhou with 16 intersections, and Jinan with 12 intersections, which directly aligns with the research plan's goal of evaluating trained models on synthetic and real-world road networks to assess practical applicability. A second, strong point of support is that the related method uses local intersection observations such as lane vehicle counts and current signal phase, embeds them with an MLP, and then uses index-free graph attention layers to exchange information among neighboring intersections, which is conceptually consistent with the proposed transformer-based inter-agent communication and attention over spatially related agents. A third supporting detail is that geo-distance neighborhood definition performed similarly to node-distance on synthetic data but outperformed it on real-world data, which supports the proposed use of 2D positional encoding based on normalized longitude and latitude and distance-based attention masking. A fourth, weaker but still relevant support is that the related work uses average travel time as its primary evaluation metric, which overlaps with the proposed metrics including travel time, waiting vehicles, queue length, and delay, although the related work does not report fuel consumption or CO2 emissions. The weakest support is that the related work demonstrates practical applicability on real networks, but only at relatively small scale, with 16 and 12 intersections, so it provides limited direct evidence for the proposed 73-agent complex grid or multi-network training setting.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) does not explicitly test transfer from synthetic or multi-network training to unseen real networks; instead, it trains and evaluates per dataset without pre-trained parameters, which undermines the central assumption of the proposed generalization test that models trained on synthetic or multiple networks can transfer to realistic road networks. A second major contradiction is the scale mismatch: the related work uses synthetic 1x3 arterial, 3x3 grid, and 6x6 grid networks, and real-world networks with only 16 intersections in Hangzhou and 12 intersections in Jinan, whereas the proposed plan targets a 73-agent complex grid, multi-network training, and variable input sizes, so the related work provides no direct evidence that attention-based transfer will remain effective at that scale. A third contradiction is that the related work found performance was best when each intersection attended to about 2 to 5 nearby intersections and that adding more neighbors degraded performance, which contradicts an unconstrained transformer attention design unless the proposed model explicitly limits attention radius, uses k-nearest-neighbor masking, or applies strong distance decay. A fourth contradiction is that moderate multi-head attention helped, but benefits diminished or became negative when the number of heads exceeded 5, suggesting that the proposed transformer communication module may require careful head-count tuning and may not benefit from large attention capacity. A fifth, weaker contradiction is that the related work does not specify hardware, training epochs, or exact hyperparameters, and uses real-world traffic data derived from trip origins/destinations or roadside camera records, which limits reproducibility and makes it difficult to directly benchmark the proposed PPO-based transformer method against the reported graph-attention RL controller. The weakest contradiction is that the related method predicts Q-values for signal phase actions rather than using PPO, and it does not evaluate the three proposed state-information levels, so it does not directly support the proposed minimal-state hypothesis or the specific optimization algorithm.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the generalization test explicitly transfer-based rather than per-dataset: train the transformer model on synthetic networks and on multiple networks, then evaluate zero-shot and fine-tuned performance on held-out real-world networks, reporting both transfer gain and degradation relative to per-dataset training. The next most important action is to constrain and ablate the attention mechanism, testing k-nearest-neighbor attention with k around 2 to 5, distance-decay masking, and head counts up to about 5, because [(Wei et al., 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40968cf84b03ba9643278cb6c7c878e4accdb8cb) shows that broader attention and more heads can hurt performance. The third priority is to include the same practical baselines used in the related work, namely fixed-time control, MaxPressure, individual RL, neighbor RL, and GCN-based RL, so that the proposed transformer method can be compared against established traffic signal control and graph-based RL baselines. The fourth priority is to align the evaluation scale with the research plan by testing both small synthetic networks comparable to the 1x3, 3x3, and 6x6 settings and the proposed 73-agent grid, while also using real-world OSM networks of at least 12 to 16 intersections and, if possible, larger imported networks to test scalability. The fifth priority is to report the full proposed metric suite, including travel time, waiting vehicles, queue length, delay, fuel consumption, and CO2 emissions, because the related work only reports average travel time and therefore cannot validate the broader environmental and operational objectives of the plan. The final, less urgent action is to document hardware, training epochs, seeds, hyperparameters, and statistical significance for all synthetic and real-world evaluations, since the related work leaves these details insufficiently specified and the proposed method needs reproducible evidence that transfer is not an artifact of training budget or network-specific tuning.

## We will compare our models against static traffic control baselines using metrics including fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, number of waiting vehicles, travel time, queue length, and delay. Simulations will run for one-hour periods to capture meaningful traffic patterns. | Synthesis: This evaluation protocol quantifies the practical benefits of the proposed method in terms of congestion, emissions, and travel efficiency.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation protocol comes from [(Salaymeh et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b97eec52ca736f95d8e47cf11ee25f7c1a7df16f), which directly validates SUMO-based multi-agent traffic signal control with phase-level agents, MPI Allgather communication, adjacency-list information sharing, and congestion metrics including average delay, average queue length, and average car speed under peak and off-peak traffic. This is highly relevant to the proposed plan because it demonstrates that SUMO can be used to evaluate multi-agent TSC and that delay and queue metrics are informative, although it uses DQN/DDQN with HER or literature replay rather than the proposed transformer-PPO architecture. The paper reports that DDQN with HER achieved the lowest average delay and queue length and the highest average car speed, that DDQN outperformed DQN, and that HER outperformed literature replay with larger gains under peak traffic, but it does not provide numerical values, statistical significance, multiple seeds, episode duration, network size, or demand rates. The newer work by [(Ma et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab954896c78f4c1136e3178ef08a3316d996d316) provides weaker but still relevant support by evaluating a Net Actor-Critic policy-switching method in SUMO-RL with stochastic arrivals, observations of light conditions, vehicle densities, and queued vehicles, and an instant reward based on vehicle crossing efficiency with delay penalties for switching. It shows that an off-policy evaluator can quantify policy improvements with a net value increase of 6.2 to 7.0 on average, standard deviations of 0.4 to 0.7, 100% switch proportion from suboptimal policies, 0% from switch-optimal policies, and 100% responsible rate. The newer Ma work does not contradict Salaymeh's delay/queue findings, but it shifts the evidence from multi-agent delay/queue comparison to single-intersection policy-switching evaluation, so it is weaker support for the proposed multi-metric protocol.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed method is that neither related work compares against static traffic control baselines, which is central to the proposed evaluation. [(Salaymeh et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b97eec52ca736f95d8e47cf11ee25f7c1a7df16f) compares RL configurations only, and [(Ma et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab954896c78f4c1136e3178ef08a3316d996d316) compares policy switching against old policies, so there is no direct evidence that the proposed transformer-PPO model will outperform fixed-time or actuated static control. The next major gap is metric coverage: Salaymeh reports delay, queue length, and speed but not fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, travel time, or waiting vehicles, while Ma reports aggregate net value and switching/responsibility rates but not the proposed traffic efficiency and emissions metrics. The third tension is simulation duration and statistical rigor: the proposed one-hour runs are not evidenced; Salaymeh uses 300 episodes with five agents on separate PCs, agents synchronized after each episode, an Intel Xeon 8-core 2.6 GHz CPU, NVIDIA Quadro P4000 8 GB GPU, 30 GB system memory, and 75% straight traffic, but does not state episode duration, network size, demand rates, or seeds, and Ma does not state traffic experiment duration, seeds, hardware, or traffic-specific repetition count, even though its broader Gymnasium experiments used 10 Monte Carlo repetitions. The weakest but still important contradiction is scale and architecture: Salaymeh uses five agents and DQN/DDQN/HER, Ma uses a single intersection and actor-critic policy switching, whereas the proposed plan targets 7-agent and 73-agent networks with transformer communication and PPO, so the related work does not establish feasibility of the proposed architecture at that scale.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to implement the missing static traffic control baselines, such as fixed-time and actuated control, in the same SUMO networks and one-hour traffic scenarios, and compare the proposed transformer-PPO model against them on all proposed metrics: fuel consumption, CO2 emissions, waiting vehicles, travel time, queue length, and delay. The second priority is to make the evaluation statistically defensible by running multiple random seeds for each network, traffic demand, and state-information level, reporting means, standard deviations, confidence intervals, and significance tests, while explicitly specifying network size, demand rates, traffic composition, simulation duration, and hardware. The third action is to add SUMO emission modeling, for example HBEFA-based fuel and CO2 estimates, and to validate the emission pipeline against known baseline behavior before using it as a primary metric. The fourth action is to clarify implementation details that affect reproducibility and scalability, including PPO hyperparameters, transformer architecture, 2D positional encoding, distance-based attention masking, communication channel capacity, phase-level action spaces to avoid conflicting phases, and computational resources for 73 agents. The least urgent but still useful action is to include ablations over the three state-information levels and, if possible, compare against simpler RL baselines such as DQN/DDQN or PPO without communication, because the related work shows that replay and policy-evaluation choices can materially affect delay and queue performance.

## The experiments will systematically vary network complexity, traffic flow dynamics, and state information availability to isolate the impact of each factor on model performance and validate our hypothesis that minimal state information can achieve competitive results. | Synthesis: This systematic experimental analysis identifies which factors drive performance and supports the minimal-state hypothesis.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method's systematic variation of traffic-flow dynamics and state-information availability comes from [(Alegre et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37b8c642baebdefaca9282ef573aa290841d1a39), which directly ablates full queue+density state versus reduced queue-only state under scheduled flow-rate context changes at timesteps 20000, 40000, and 60000, using total vehicle waiting time as the primary metric; its finding that learning agents reached about 500 seconds by timestep 10000 versus about 2200 seconds for a fixed policy, and that continued learning with fixed exploration is necessary after context changes, supports the plan's dynamic-flow and state-ablation design, although it uses independent Q-learning rather than PPO or transformers. A secondary but relevant support is [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573), which isolates local versus non-local traffic through baseline uniform destinations, nonuniform destinations requiring at least two intersections, and long-route network modifications in a small saturated network with vehicle spawning probability 0.2, and evaluates average trip waiting time, stopped-vehicle ratio, and total queue length; this supports the plan's use of controlled scenario variation and waiting/queue metrics, and its observation that TC-SBC underperformed relative to TC-1 and max-plus, attributed to slower learning from a larger state space, provides weak support for the plan's concern that richer state can impede learning, but it reports only 10 runs without confidence intervals or significance tests and does not test transformer communication, PPO, or the plan's 7-agent/73-agent networks. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Salaymeh et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b97eec52ca736f95d8e47cf11ee25f7c1a7df16f), which validates SUMO-based multi-agent phase-level signal control with adjacent-intersection traffic-flow information, peak/off-peak traffic, and metrics including average delay, queue length, and car speed, supporting the plan's SUMO environment, phase actions, and evaluation metrics; however, its 5-agent, 300-episode setup on an Intel Xeon 8-core 2.6 GHz CPU and NVIDIA Quadro P4000 GPU does not evidence scalability to 73 agents, multi-network training, or minimal-state performance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the minimal-state hypothesis is from [(Alegre et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37b8c642baebdefaca9282ef573aa290841d1a39), where the reduced queue-only state learned faster initially but converged to higher total waiting time and suffered a significant performance drop at timestep 60000 despite having experienced the new context, while the full queue+density state achieved lower waiting times and recovered more rapidly after context changes; additionally, reducing queue discretization from 10 bins to 4 bins caused substantial degradation after context changes, with total waiting time increasing up to three times at timestep 40000, indicating that minimal or coarse state information may not remain competitive under nonstationary traffic unless the architecture compensates. A weaker contradiction to the plan's scalability and rigor assumptions is that [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573) does not provide quantitative evidence that minimal state is competitive; it only reports that TC-SBC performed worse than TC-1 and max-plus, attributed to slower learning from a larger state space, and it lacks numerical waiting-time values, confidence intervals, and significance tests, so it cannot support the plan's claim that minimal state can match richer state in transformer-based PPO. The weakest contradiction is that [(Salaymeh et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b97eec52ca736f95d8e47cf11ee25f7c1a7df16f) does not test the proposed transformer communication, distance-based attention masking, or the three state-information levels, and its DQN/DDQN+HER results, while showing lower cumulative delay for DDQN+HER, provide no numerical values or statistical tests and are limited to 5 agents and 300 episodes, leaving the plan's scalability and minimal-state claims unsupported.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make the state-information ablation directly test the minimal-state hypothesis under nonstationary traffic by using the plan's no-traffic, limited, and full observation levels in the 73-agent dynamic grid and, if possible, the 7-agent ring, with scheduled flow-rate changes analogous to [(Alegre et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37b8c642baebdefaca9282ef573aa290841d1a39) at timesteps 20000, 40000, and 60000, while controlling PPO exploration, learning rate, and training budget and reporting total waiting time, delay, queue length, and recovery time after each context change; this should also include observation-precision ablations because [(Alegre et al., 2020-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/37b8c642baebdefaca9282ef573aa290841d1a39) showed 4-bin queue discretization can triple waiting time relative to 10 bins. The next most important action is to add the statistical and scenario rigor suggested by [(Kuyer et al., 2008-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c5787226587d6612fe702d7f3a2a869a72cf6573): report numerical means, confidence intervals, and significance tests over multiple independent runs, and include local-versus-non-local traffic ablations such as uniform destinations with local trips, destinations requiring at least two intersections, and long-route network modifications, so that network-structure effects are not confounded with state-information effects. The least important but still useful action is to incorporate the implementation details and baselines from [(Salaymeh et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b97eec52ca736f95d8e47cf11ee25f7c1a7df16f), including phase-level actions to avoid conflicting phases, peak/off-peak demand, SUMO metrics such as average delay, queue length, and car speed, DQN/DDQN or HER-style baselines where appropriate, and explicit hardware/episode reporting, while scaling beyond its 5-agent, 300-episode, Quadro P4000 setup to the plan's 7-agent and 73-agent networks and multi-network training.
