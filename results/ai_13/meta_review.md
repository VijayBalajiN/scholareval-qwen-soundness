## Core system design: We propose UO-Explainer (Unified and Orbit-based Explainer), which decomposes GNN predictions using predefined orbits as interpretable units. This is the central system design that frames the entire plan around orbit-based, human-interpretable GNN explanations.

### Support

Read as an iterative line from role discovery to GNN explanation, the strongest support for UO-Explainer comes from [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), which directly uses predefined small graphlets as interpretable units for model-level GNN explanation by computing 29 graphlet frequencies, specifically 2 three-node, 6 four-node, and 21 five-node graphlets, training an MLP surrogate on graph classification probabilities, and ranking class-explanatory substructures on Reddit-Binary with 2000 thread graphs, MUTAG with 188 molecular graphs, BA-2Motif with 1000 synthetic graphs, and a Shape dataset; its fid- and fid+ edge-removal fidelity tests show that selected graphlets can be sufficient or necessary, supporting the plan's model-level class-orbit decomposition and the choice of 2-5 node graphlets, although it operates at graph level rather than node-level orbits. The next strongest support is [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), which validates local instance-level explanation on the same synthetic motif family used in the plan, including BA-House-Shapes, BA-Community, BA-Grids, Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grids, and BA-Bottle-Shaped, plus Bitcoin-Alpha and Bitcoin-OTC, using a linear SGC surrogate, convex optimization, fidelity, average precision at top-k, and runtime; its reported 3-hop top-3 precision of 95.8 on Bitcoin-Alpha and 94.8 on Bitcoin-OTC, and FastDnX being more than forty thousand times faster than PGMExplainer, support the plan's emphasis on local neighborhoods, fidelity, sparsity, and efficient PyTorch/PyTorch Geometric implementation, but it does not use orbits. [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f) further supports synthetic motif ground-truth evaluation and human-interpretable local explanations by learning Bayesian networks over perturbation and prediction-change variables on six synthetic motif datasets, Bitcoin-Alpha with 3783 accounts and Bitcoin-OTC with 5881 accounts, and MNIST SuperPixel-Graph with a 55K/5K/10K split, and by using a subjective 10-user, 48-graph, 0-10 rating test, which aligns with the plan's need for motif-based synthetic validation and qualitative human assessment, although it selects nodes rather than orbits. [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a) supports sparse, faithful local explanation through GraphLIME's HSIC LASSO on Cora and Pubmed with 10 added noisy features and 200 test samples from GraphSAGE or GAT models above 80% test accuracy, showing that local explanations can avoid useless features, which supports the plan's sparsity and denoising concerns, but it explains node features rather than orbit contributions. [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) supports domain-interpretable relational explanations on synthetic TREE-GRID and TREE-BA datasets with a 3-layer GCN and on MUTAG with 188 compounds, using AUC-ROC against known right-reason structures and infidelity, and qualitatively identifying hexagonal carbon rings and -NO2 groups, which supports the plan's goal of exposing human-meaningful structures, but it does not use predefined orbits or model-level class decomposition. [(Sallmen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/025e9b4b3c750c172d00c77749e3d37da92068cf) supports the orbit formalism itself by defining multilayer graphlet automorphism orbits, computing node-layer graphlet degrees for up to three-node graphlets in three-layer settings and up to four-node graphlets in two-layer settings, and evaluating orbit-based distances with AUPR, precision-recall curves, and MDS, which supports careful orbit counting and redundancy handling, but it is a network-comparison method rather than a GNN explainer. [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2) supports motif-based structural role learning by using 2- to 4-node motif counts, logarithmic binning over bin sizes 0.01, 0.1, 0.5, 0.9, and 0.99, role concatenation, 128-dimensional embeddings, 10 walks per node, walk length 80, and p/q values 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, and 4 on NetworkRepository graphs, with link prediction AUC and p-value below 0.01, which supports the feasibility of small subgraph features as interpretable structural units, but it does not decompose GNN predictions. Finally, [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1) provides the oldest conceptual support for mapping structural and subgraph-pattern features to roles through feature search, clustering, SVD, or NMF, which is analogous to orbit basis learning and class-orbit scoring, but it lacks quantitative GNN evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct failure of orbits but a performance challenge: [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) shows that local node-level explanations without orbit units can achieve very high fidelity and precision, with DnX 3-hop top-3 precision of 95.8 on Bitcoin-Alpha and 94.8 on Bitcoin-OTC, and FastDnX best on most synthetic datasets except Tree-Cycles and Tree-Grids, where PGMExplainer is best on Tree-Grids for GCN, ARMA, and GATED; this means UO-Explainer must demonstrate that orbit decomposition improves sub-recall, edge-recall, or human interpretability beyond what local node/edge methods already achieve, rather than assuming orbit units are necessary. The next strongest challenge is from [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), which shows that perturbation-based Bayesian explanations can identify motif nodes on six synthetic motif datasets and achieve significantly higher precision than compared methods on Bitcoin-Alpha and Bitcoin-OTC for 3, 4, and 5 account explanations, while also being infeasible on Pubmed with up to an hour per prediction; this contradicts the assumption that orbit-based explanations will automatically be both more accurate and more efficient, because a non-orbit method can be accurate on motif ground truth but computationally problematic, and UO-Explainer's greedy orbit decomposition and BFS subgraph extraction must be benchmarked for wall-clock time and memory. [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a) challenges the plan's orbit basis learning by showing that feature-level local explanations can be evaluated through denoising, where GraphLIME and GNNExplainer selected around zero noisy features among 10 added features on Cora and Pubmed, while LIME did not; if UO-Explainer's orbit basis vectors are not regularized or sparsified, irrelevant orbit indicators may be selected, and the plan currently lacks a noisy-orbit or noisy-graphlet evaluation. [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) also challenges the necessity of predefined orbits because RelEx Gumbel achieved best AUC-ROC and infidelity on TREE-GRID and TREE-BA with a 3-layer GCN, and RelEx Gumbel and GNN-Explainer obtained similar best infidelity on MUTAG, identifying domain structures such as hexagonal rings and -NO2 groups without graphlet orbits or model-level class-orbit scores. [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) partially contradicts the unified node-level ambition by showing that model-level graphlet explanations can be obtained from graph-level 29-dimensional graphlet frequency vectors and an MLP surrogate, but it does not provide node-level orbit basis learning, class weight decomposition, or instance-level subgraph extraction, so its success does not validate the plan's more specific orbit decomposition. [(Sallmen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/025e9b4b3c750c172d00c77749e3d37da92068cf) raises a sensitivity issue: alternative orbit definitions produce different orbits with similar but not identical performance, and its orbit-based measures are evaluated for network comparison using AUPR and MDS rather than GNN prediction fidelity, which warns that UO-Explainer's conclusions may depend on the chosen orbit definition and graphlet size. [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2) provides weaker contradictory evidence in the sense that motif counts and roles improve graph embedding and link prediction with p-value below 0.01, but the excerpt reports no AUC values, no GNN prediction decomposition, and no explanation fidelity, so it cannot support the central claim that orbit units explain GNN class weights or node predictions. [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1) is the weakest contradiction because it is a framework survey that notes evaluation frameworks for role discovery are lacking and that some learned feature sets required more memory than the sparse graph itself, but it provides no numerical outcomes, datasets, hardware, or GNN experiments, so it mainly highlights missing evaluation rather than directly refuting UO-Explainer.

### Suggested Action

Most important, add orbit-specific quantitative evaluation and stronger baselines: retain the planned D4Explainer, GLGExplainer, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, and MotifExplainer comparisons, but also compare against [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a), [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a), and [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), using the same six synthetic motif datasets, Bitcoin-Alpha with 3783 accounts, Bitcoin-OTC with 5881 accounts, Cora, Citeseer, Pubmed, MUTAG with 188 graphs, Reddit-Binary with 2000 graphs, and BA-2Motif with 1000 graphs where applicable; report average precision at top-k under 1-hop and 3-hop candidate neighborhoods, fidelity, sparsity, runtime, and memory, because DnX/FastDnX show large runtime advantages and PGMExplainer can be infeasible on Pubmed. Second, make the orbit basis learning and class-orbit decomposition faithful and efficient by training orbit existence classifiers with node representations, using a linear SGC or orbit-level surrogate to approximate the GNN, applying L1 or group sparsity to orbit coefficients, and replacing or stabilizing the greedy class-weight decomposition with multiple random restarts, cross-validation, and statistical significance tests; this addresses the plan's concern about randomness and the need for reproducible PyTorch/PyTorch Geometric implementation. Third, adopt model-level fidelity metrics from [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), specifically fid- and fid+ edge-removal scores and modification ratios, and extend them to node-level orbit explanations by removing or masking orbit occurrences in the target node neighborhood, so that class-orbit scores are tested for necessity and sufficiency rather than only ranked by coefficient magnitude. Fourth, add denoising and right-reason tests inspired by [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a) and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a): inject noisy orbit features or irrelevant graphlets, measure the frequency with which UO-Explainer selects them, and evaluate AUC-ROC and infidelity against known right-reason structures on TREE-GRID, TREE-BA, and MUTAG, including domain structures such as hexagonal rings and -NO2 groups. Fifth, handle orbit definition sensitivity and redundancy following [(Sallmen et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/025e9b4b3c750c172d00c77749e3d37da92068cf), [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2), and [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1): define orbits for 2- to 5-node graphlets, remove redundant orbits, test alternative orbit definitions, use feature search or scoring to prune weak orbit bases, and report orbit counts, logarithmic binning or binary indicator choices, and the effect of graphlet size on explanation stability. Sixth, include human and domain validation for real-world PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks using subjective rating protocols similar to [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), where 10 users scored 48 explanations on a 0-10 scale, and qualitative pathway or motif alignment similar to [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a), with inter-rater reliability and expert review. Finally, report full reproducibility details missing from much of the related work, including dataset sizes, graphlet/orbit counts, surrogate architecture, optimizer, learning rate, number of perturbation or walk samples, hardware, wall-clock time, and multiple random seeds with confidence intervals, because several related works report qualitative or partial quantitative results without sufficient implementation detail.

## Orbit Basis Learning: We first preprocess the input graph to determine orbit existence for each node, creating binary indicators for whether each node belongs to specific orbits within 2-5 node graphlets. We then train logistic binary classifiers to predict orbit existence, using node representations as input. This process learns orbit basis vectors that capture both the distribution of orbits in the input graph and the message-passing behavior of the embedding model. This foundational step creates the orbit indicators and basis vectors used by both model-level and instance-level explanations.

### Support

The strongest support for Orbit Basis Learning comes from the newest and most directly class-oriented orbit-adjacency work, [(Windels et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91e902c5c57510e07a3caa99edcd7d9fef467486), which computes 28 orbit-adjacency embeddings, trains one-vs-rest linear SVMs with 10-fold stratified 80/20 splits, and shows that on Amazon-Computers the best orbit adjacency outperforms or ties the best random-walk adjacency and DeepWalk for 9 of 10 classes by micro-averaged F1, with 5 classes using orbit information not seen by random walks; this supports the plan's premise that orbit-level topology is class-relevant, especially because the study also uses a filtered PPI network with Gene Ontology labels. The next strongest support is [(Gu, and Milenković, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3ce3c5986f6ec7a302fb838608851b9cca184e8), which shows graphlet orbit-count embeddings rank first in 90.63% of 32 alignment tests, are strictly superior to node2vec and struc2vec in 62.5% of cases, remain more robust to edge rewiring, and are faster than node2vec and struc2vec on synthetic 1,000-node/6,000-edge graphs and PPI networks APMS and Y2H, using Orca on a 64-core AMD Opteron 6376 machine; this extends older graphlet-feature evidence to node-level orbit representations and PPI-scale graphs. Further support comes from [(Rossi et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/311d7fed687056e529c5ebcf842348931e9a896d), whose graphlet/orbit relational features outperform node2vec, DeepWalk, and LINE by an average 32% AUC and 37% F1, use logistic regression with L2 penalty, 10-fold cross-validation on 10% labels, 10 random seeds with p<0.01, and produce sparse node/edge densities of 0.162-0.334 and 0.164-0.318, supporting interpretable graphlet-based features and a logistic evaluation protocol. Domain-specific support is provided by [(Wang et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdfaebd423d28e40ac97e3ce3c0420910b2065aa), where 28 graphlet-interaction isomer counts improve disease-gene prediction on 42 disease families and 741 genes, reaching maximum F-score 0.466 versus 0.415 random walk, 0.436 Endeavour, and 0.4227 neighborhood, and outperform random walk on HPRD, BioGRID, KEGG, and integrated networks, motivating orbit-like patterns for gene/PPI explanations. Weaker but still relevant support includes [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2), which converts 2-4-node motif counts into role-based random-walk embeddings with D=128, R=10, L=80, p/q grid search, and 10 seeds with p<0.01, and [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f), which provides Randgraf and SAND/SAND-3D orbit-degree estimators for 3- and 4-node connected induced subgraphs on large SNAP graphs such as Flickr, Pokec, LiveJournal, YouTube, Wiki-Talk, and Web-Google. The weakest support is conceptual: [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838) shows typed-edge ego-graphlet/orbit degree features are applicable to small chronic-pain egocentric networks with 13 relationship types, and [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1) frames feature-based role discovery as a scalable way to learn interpretable structural roles, although it reports no empirical results.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is that the most direct orbit-adjacency evidence is still partial and does not validate the proposed logistic orbit-basis mechanism: [(Windels et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91e902c5c57510e07a3caa99edcd7d9fef467486) reports only that the best orbit adjacency outperforms or ties baselines for 9 of 10 Amazon-Computers classes, that 4 of those classes use orbit types also seen by random walks, and that no exact F1 values, confidence intervals, or significance tests are provided; it also does not train logistic orbit-existence classifiers, learn orbit basis vectors, or evaluate fidelity, sparsity, edge-recall, or sub-recall. The next strongest caution is that orbit features are not uniformly superior to random-walk or structural embeddings: [(Gu, and Milenković, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3ce3c5986f6ec7a302fb838608851b9cca184e8) found ties in 21.88% of tests, node2vec strictly superior in 6.25% and struc2vec in 3.125%, and alignment quality decreased at high noise, while using orbit counts rather than binary orbit-existence indicators and no GNN explanation setup. A further contradiction to the proposed binary-indicator design is that the strongest quantitative works rely on counts, isomer frequencies, or log-binned relational features rather than mere existence: [(Rossi et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/311d7fed687056e529c5ebcf842348931e9a896d) uses logarithmic binning and relational functions, and [(Wang et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdfaebd423d28e40ac97e3ce3c0420910b2065aa) reports that disease-gene scores correlate with the number of graphlet-interaction isomers, suggesting binary orbit presence may discard magnitude information useful for prediction. Additional limitations are that [(Rossi et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/311d7fed687056e529c5ebcf842348931e9a896d) evaluates link prediction rather than node-classification explanations, [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2) reports no numeric AUC or baseline outcomes despite p<0.01 and uses 2-4-node motifs rather than 2-5-node orbits, [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f) provides no visible accuracy, runtime, error, or significance values and focuses on maximum-degree nodes, [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838) reports no quantitative outcomes and uses small egocentric typed graphs rather than large GNNs, and [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1) is a survey with no empirical validation of the feature-based role framework.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a direct empirical validation of Orbit Basis Learning itself: compute exact or sampled binary orbit-existence indicators for 2-5 node graphlets, train logistic binary classifiers on GNN node representations, and report AUC, F1, calibration, and per-orbit accuracy with 10 random seeds and p<0.01, comparing against direct orbit counts, orbit-adjacency embeddings from [(Windels et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91e902c5c57510e07a3caa99edcd7d9fef467486), orbit-count embeddings from [(Gu, and Milenković, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3ce3c5986f6ec7a302fb838608851b9cca184e8), and node2vec/struc2vec/DeepWalk baselines. Second, because the strongest related evidence uses counts and log-binned features, include an ablation comparing binary orbit existence with raw orbit counts, log-binned counts, and orbit-adjacency embeddings, as in [(Rossi et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/311d7fed687056e529c5ebcf842348931e9a896d) and [(Wang et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdfaebd423d28e40ac97e3ce3c0420910b2065aa), to justify whether binary indicators preserve enough signal for explanations. Third, adopt scalable orbit computation and report runtime/memory: use Orca-style orbit counting from [(Gu, and Milenković, 2018-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c3ce3c5986f6ec7a302fb838608851b9cca184e8) and Randgraf/SAND sampling from [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f) for 3- and 4-node orbits, extend to 5-node orbits, and benchmark on PPI/gene networks and large SNAP-scale graphs with hardware specifications. Fourth, align the evaluation with the research plan by using the planned synthetic datasets and real PPI/LastFM/Gene networks, adding PPI Gene Ontology labels as in [(Windels et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/91e902c5c57510e07a3caa99edcd7d9fef467486) and disease-gene precision-recall/F1 protocols as in [(Wang et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fdfaebd423d28e40ac97e3ce3c0420910b2065aa), while reporting fidelity, sparsity, edge-recall, and sub-recall against D4Explainer, GLGExplainer, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, and MotifExplainer. Fifth, improve the model-level decomposition by borrowing feature-selection and sparse-representation ideas from [(Rossi et al., 2020-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/311d7fed687056e529c5ebcf842348931e9a896d) and [(Rossi, and Ahmed, 2014-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ff1ea6f6f8e3df14868d8d9e9e12b1c10ac171b1), such as regularization, sparse orbit bases, stability checks, and controlled greedy selection to reduce randomness. Finally, if the target gene or PPI graphs contain typed interactions, consider typed-edge graphlet/orbit variants inspired by [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838), and include motif/role preprocessing details such as logarithmic binning and hyperparameter grids from [(Ahmed et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0dafd1ff6dcfe051f428c745b57a0407b7f339e2) to improve reproducibility.

## Model-Level Explanation Generation: We decompose class weights into linear combinations of orbit bases using a greedy optimization approach. Rather than directly optimizing over all orbit bases (which introduces significant randomness), we iteratively select orbits that minimize the difference between class weights and the linear combination of selected orbits. The coefficients in this decomposition represent class-orbit scores, indicating each orbit's contribution to class predictions. This produces global, class-level explanations by assigning interpretable orbit contributions to each predicted class.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed model-level orbit decomposition is [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), which distills a graph classifier into a transparent MLP using a 29-dimensional graphlet frequency vector composed of 2 three-node, 6 four-node, and 21 five-node graphlets, then ranks graphlets or substructures per class on Shape, Reddit-Binary with 2000 graphs, MUTAG with 188 graphs, and BA-2Motif with 1000 graphs. This directly supports the research plan's premise that small connected substructures can serve as global, class-level explanatory units and that fidelity can be assessed by edge-removal fid- and fid+ metrics, although it does not validate the proposed greedy decomposition of class weights into orbit bases. A second strong support is [(Yi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e446ce9e9d08f4ab14eb73c9d71dfb2e6ab5f0), whose GDM synthesizes class-level interpretive graphs by optimizing Bernoulli adjacency and node-feature parameters with edge-sparsity and feature-distribution regularization, generates 10 interpretive graphs per class on MUTAG, Graph-Twitter, Graph-SST5, Shape, BA-Motif, and BA-LRP, and reports that trajectory-based matching improves fidelity over first-snapshot ablation while beta above 0.5 degrades utility; this supports the need for global class-level explanations and sparsity/fidelity evaluation, but it produces synthetic graphs rather than explicit orbit coefficients. A third support is [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747), which extracts class-specific decision regions from linear decision boundaries and evaluates robustness with top-k=8 ROC AUC under node-feature and edge noise on BA-2motifs, Mutagenicity, NCI1, BA-SHAPES, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, and TREE-GRID, showing that class-level common decision logic can be measured and that efficient explanations are feasible, with 0.01s ± 0.02 average time on Mutagenicity versus 1.2s ± 0.2 for GNNExplainer. Weaker but relevant support comes from [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), which learns a global edge-mask generator with a concrete distribution and reports motif AUC of 0.963±0.011 on BA-Shapes, 0.945±0.019 on BA-Community, 0.987±0.007 on Tree-Cycles, and 0.907±0.014 on Tree-Grid, demonstrating that global GNN explanations can be evaluated against known motifs and can be fast, though it does not expose graphlet or orbit units. The weakest support is [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2), which attributes VGG-16 class scores on Places365 to top and bottom five last-convolutional neurons and names them, supporting the general idea of class-level unit attribution but providing only qualitative examples, an IoU threshold of 0.05, and no fidelity, sparsity, recall, or statistical significance. Instance-level works such as [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a), [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), and [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) provide weaker support for the unified framework by showing that motif-aware subgraph explanations can be evaluated on synthetic TREE-GRID/TREE-BA, Bitcoin-Alpha with 3783 accounts, Bitcoin-OTC with 5881 accounts, and six motif datasets, but they do not support the model-level orbit-basis decomposition itself.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is indirect but important: [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) evaluates GNNExplainer and PGExplainer with Fidelity+ and Fidelity- versus sparsity on Graph-SST2, Graph-SST5, Graph-Twitter, BBBP, BA-2Motifs, and BA-Shapes, finds PGExplainer less promising than originally reported, and shows that GNNExplainer and PGExplainer perform well on node-classification BA-Shapes but not as expected on graph-classification datasets; this contradicts the assumption that a global explanation model automatically yields robust class-level structural explanations and implies the proposed orbit method must demonstrate fidelity, sparsity, and stability rather than only coefficient interpretability. A second contradiction is that instance-level methods can outperform or remain competitive on motif-heavy synthetic tasks, as [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) reports PGMExplainer obtaining the highest explanation accuracy on Tree-Cycles and Tree-Grids while FastDnX is best elsewhere, and DnX achieving top-3 average precision of 95.8 on Bitcoin-Alpha and 94.8 on Bitcoin-OTC; this suggests that cycle and grid motifs, which are central to the proposed orbit basis, may require local, prediction-specific reasoning and that a purely model-level orbit decomposition may miss instance-specific contributions. A third contradiction is that the closest graphlet-based model-level work uses a simpler 29-dimensional graphlet-frequency MLP and does not provide numerical FID values or statistical significance, so it does not establish that the proposed greedy orbit decomposition is superior to a simpler graphlet-frequency surrogate: [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336). A fourth contradiction is that relational and Bayesian instance-level explainers can identify right-reason structures without orbit bases: [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) reports RelEx Gumbel achieving the best AUC-ROC and infidelity among compared explainers on synthetic TREE-GRID and TREE-BA with a 3-layer GCN, and RelEx Sigmoid outperforming RelEx Gumbel on a three-class TREE-GRID-BA PSL/HL-MRF task, while [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f) reports PGM-Explainer outperforming GNNExplainer and a SHAP extension on six synthetic motif datasets, with especially large gains on datasets 2 and 6 requiring multi-neighbor or motif information; these results contradict the necessity of a global orbit-basis class-weight decomposition for capturing motifs and suggest that local perturbation or Bayesian structure learning may be sufficient for some tasks. Weaker contradictions include the absence of quantitative validation in the closest class-level unit-attribution work: [(Selvaraju et al., 2016-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5582bebed97947a41e3ddd9bd1f284b73f1648c2) provides only qualitative class-level neuron explanations and a failure case where door-related neurons were excluded by a noise-suppression threshold, contradicting the expectation that class-level unit attribution is automatically reliable without quantitative fidelity and threshold analysis.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add quantitative model-level evaluation for the greedy orbit decomposition using the research plan's synthetic datasets, reporting numerical fidelity, sparsity, edge-recall, sub-recall, and AUC-ROC against ground-truth motif edges or orbit memberships for BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycle, Tree-Grid, and Random Graph, with multiple runs, seeds, and significance tests, because [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) show that motif AUC, Fidelity+/-, sparsity, and stability are the standard evidence for global and instance-level GNN explanations. Next, compare directly against the closest model-level and graphlet-based baselines, especially [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), whose 29-dimensional graphlet-frequency MLP is a natural simpler alternative, and [(Yi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/99e446ce9e9d08f4ab14eb73c9d71dfb2e6ab5f0), whose GDM produces class-level synthetic graphs with fidelity, utility, and sparsity trade-offs, as well as [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) for class-specific linear decision boundaries and top-k=8 robustness AUC; in addition to the planned D4Explainer and GLGExplainer comparisons, this will show whether orbit coefficients add value over graphlet frequencies, synthetic interpretive graphs, or edge-level decision regions. A third important action is to evaluate robustness and stability by perturbing node features and edges while preserving predictions, measuring top-k explanation AUC and stability as in [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) and [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3), and to report Fidelity+ and Fidelity- across sparsity levels to avoid the failure mode where global explanations look interpretable but do not change predictions. Fourth, integrate the instance-level component with strong baselines and metrics from the related work, including GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, PGM-Explainer, DnX/FastDnX, SubgraphX, and RelEx, and evaluate top-k precision, AUC-ROC, infidelity, fidelity, runtime, and human-subjective scores where appropriate, because [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) show that motif-aware local explanations require rigorous local evaluation on synthetic motifs and real networks such as Bitcoin-Alpha and Bitcoin-OTC. Fifth, refine the orbit basis learning and greedy optimization by explicitly using 3-5 node graphlet/orbit indicators, reporting the number of orbits, selection thresholds, coefficient sparsity, residual norm after greedy selection, and computational cost, and considering a transparent surrogate or distillation step similar to [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) or [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) to reduce randomness and improve reproducibility. Finally, include human or domain-expert evaluation on real-world PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks, using qualitative motif recognizability and, where feasible, user scoring similar to the 0 to 10 human evaluation in [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), to demonstrate that orbit explanations are more interpretable than edge masks or synthetic graphs.

## Instance-Level Explanation Generation: We extend the class weight decomposition to individual prediction values by decomposing the prediction for each target node into orbit contributions. This yields node-class-orbit scores that quantify how much each orbit contributes to the prediction for a specific node and class. We then use breadth-first search algorithms to identify subgraphs within the input graph that match the highest-contributing orbit patterns. This produces local, node-specific explanations by linking orbit scores to concrete subgraphs in the input graph.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed instance-level orbit explanation is that graphlet/orbit-like small substructures can serve as interpretable units and be connected to GNN behavior through fidelity. [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) uses a 29-dimensional graphlet-frequency vector, with 2 dimensions for 3-node graphlets, 6 for 4-node graphlets, and 21 for 5-node graphlets, trains an MLP surrogate, and evaluates fid- and fid+ edge-removal fidelity on Shape, Reddit-Binary, MUTAG, and BA-2Motif; it recovers Lollipop clique cores, Grid and Star structures, Wheel triangle-plus-chain, Reddit branching, and BA-2Motif House explanations, while avoiding 6-node graphlets because they would add 112 patterns and reduce interpretability. This directly motivates limiting UO-Explainer to 2-5 node orbits and using fidelity to validate orbit subgraphs, although it is model-level and qualitative. A slightly weaker but highly relevant line is that node-specific local explanations can be quantitatively validated on motif ground truth. [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) shows DnX and FastDnX using a distilled surrogate and convex node-weight extraction achieve best or near-best accuracy on six synthetic motif datasets, highest average precision on Bitcoin-Alpha and Bitcoin-OTC, with 3-hop GCN top-3/top-4/top-5 precision of 95.8/91.9/87.9 versus GNNExplainer 80.1/74.9/70.9 and PGExplainer 81.5/78.1/69.5 on Bitcoin-Alpha, and 94.8/91.4/86.3 versus GNNExplainer 82.4/79.6/70.6 and PGExplainer 78.5/74.5/67.4 on Bitcoin-OTC, while also being about four orders of magnitude faster than PGMExplainer and reporting surrogate distillation accuracy above 86 percent. [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) supports compact K-controlled subgraph explanations, with Gem improving explanation accuracy by 30 percent at K=5 on BA-shapes, maintaining favorable accuracy at K=6 on Tree-cycles when GNNExplainer failed below K=8, identifying Mutag aromatic ring and NO2 at K=15, and producing log-odds differences more concentrated around zero. [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) provides quantitative support for learned instance-level subgraph masks, with PGExplainer AUC of 0.963±0.011 on BA-Shapes, 0.945±0.019 on BA-Community, 0.987±0.007 on Tree-Cycles, 0.907±0.014 on Tree-Grid, 0.926±0.021 on BA-2motifs, and 0.873±0.013 on MUTAG, plus speedups of 59x, 29x, 108x, 106x, 12x, and 42x over GNNExplainer. [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f) supports target-node-specific perturbation-based explanations, with PGM-Explainer learning a Bayesian network over L-hop perturbation indicators, achieving higher explanation accuracy than GNNExplainer and SHAP on six synthetic motif datasets, higher precision on Bitcoin-Alpha and Bitcoin-OTC for 3, 4, and 5 account explanations, and higher human ratings than SHAP and GRAD on MNIST SuperPixel-Graph with an average of 3.08 nodes per explanation. Further support comes from subgraph-level and game-theoretic methods that connect local structure to prediction quality: [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) uses GStarX HN-value node scoring, top gamma|V| node-induced subgraphs, tau=0.01, exact threshold m=10, Monte Carlo J=n, and H-Fidelity across sparsity levels 0.5 to 0.85, outperforming baselines on 4 of 6 datasets and being more than two times faster than SubgraphX on BBBP; [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) shows SubgraphX outperforming other methods in fidelity-sparsity comparisons and motivates stability evaluation using 10 perturbations that attach two noise nodes to the L-hop neighborhood; [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) provides a reproducible node-level MCTS setting with 3-hop computational graphs for GCN, GAT, and GIN, MCTS iterations 20, lambda 5 or 10, Monte Carlo steps 100 for SubgraphX and 1000 for MCTS baseline, and one Nvidia V100 GPU; and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) supports right-reason structural explanations using AUC-ROC and infidelity on TREE-GRID, TREE-BA, and MUTAG, with qualitative recovery of hexagonal rings and NO2 structures. Pattern-matching and fidelity-based selection also support linking patterns to concrete subgraphs: [(Chen et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b1e64e590d3c4cb6e9c0ccd57acbe5be7b3b7fa) shows ApproxGVEX and StreamGVEX matching higher-level patterns to subgraphs, achieving higher Fidelity+ on all datasets except MUTAGENICITY and lower Fidelity on all datasets, with a maximum quality gap of 0.023 and evaluation on large graphs including a 2,449,029-node PRODUCTS subgraph sampling; [(Köhler, and Heindorf, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bf33abdb4ad61864f0e08d16b8ad423a4a85d9f) shows that fidelity-maximizing class expressions achieve fidelity 0.97 and explanation accuracy 1.00 on Hetero-BA-Shapes, close to ground-truth fidelity 0.93 to 0.96, while GNN-output-maximizing expressions achieve only 0.50 to 0.51 fidelity; and [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e07400198b777cd822602d2c6812635f986bda44) supports edge-induced subgraph explanations, with EiG-Search achieving higher overall fidelity than node-induced and edge-induced baselines and faster post-hoc explanation time than PGExplainer, GNNExplainer, and DEGREE. Older and weaker support includes [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), which introduced GNNExplainer mask learning on BA-SHAPES, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, TREE-GRID, MUTAG, and REDDIT-BINARY but reports only qualitative/quantitative claims in the excerpt; [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a), which shows GraphLIME and GNNExplainer suppress 10 added noisy features on Cora and Pubmed with 200 test nodes and GraphSAGE or GAT models above 80 percent test accuracy; [(Sánchez-Lengeling et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/861cf64943e90074cd25eada6e4c3912aef17eb0), which evaluates CAM on subgraph-logic tasks with 1,200 graphs per logic combination and 10 percent test but provides no quantitative outcomes in the excerpt; [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679), which supports model-level pattern generation with XGNN, including cyclic-class 3-node probability 0.7544 on Is_Acyclic and Mutagenic probabilities 1.0000 or 0.9999 on MUTAG with maximum node limit 5; [(Shin et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/67c0a7c64b62c2b6370c3788c685d00d610963d6), which supports model-level prototype explanations with PAGE using two Gaussian clusters, three nearest graphs per cluster, five search sessions, decay rate 10, up to 1000 iterations, and faithfulness via Kendall tau; and [(Chen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94e4648f161a9383f1118c8e2a7a9833bb201d2b), which supports generative subgraph explainers achieving faithfulness comparable to or better than non-generative methods on MUTAG, BBBP, MNIST75sp, BA-2Motifs, and BA-MultiShapes with inference times of order 10^0 or less after training.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that strong non-orbit node-weight and subgraph methods already achieve high motif recovery, fidelity, and efficiency, so orbit BFS is not yet shown necessary. [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) shows DnX and FastDnX outperform or match GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, and PGMExplainer on most synthetic motif datasets, achieve at least 14 percent absolute precision gap over the best baseline on Bitcoin-Alpha and 11 percent on Bitcoin-OTC for 3-hop candidates, are about four orders of magnitude faster than PGMExplainer, and report PGMExplainer taking up to an hour per prediction on Pubmed; this directly challenges the added value of orbit scores and BFS if they are slower or only qualitatively interpretable. A second strong contradiction is that prediction-contribution maximization can select spurious structures unless constrained by fidelity: [(Köhler, and Heindorf, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bf33abdb4ad61864f0e08d16b8ad423a4a85d9f) reports that class expressions selected by maximizing GNN output achieve fidelity 0.50, 0.50, and 0.51, explanation accuracy 0.45, 0.63, and 0.63, and GNN outputs 12.52, 12.39, and 11.89, while selecting by fidelity achieves fidelity 0.97, explanation accuracy 1.00, and GNN outputs 0.40, 0.40, and 2.67; if node-class-orbit scores are derived only from prediction values, UO-Explainer may select spurious orbits rather than true house-motif or domain patterns. Third, iterative comparisons show learned mask and subgraph methods are not uniformly robust, so the proposed method cannot assume a single decomposition will dominate: [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) reports that PGExplainer was less promising than its original reported results, SubgraphX outperformed other methods in fidelity-sparsity comparisons, GNN-LRP obtained highest accuracy on BA-shapes, GNNExplainer obtained highest accuracy on BA-Community, and DeepLift had best stability but significantly worse accuracy; this contradicts the earlier strong PGExplainer results in [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and motivates stability and multi-baseline evaluation. Newer methods further contradict the necessity of orbit-specific search: [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) shows Gem consistently achieving best explanation accuracy overall and outperforming GNNExplainer and PGExplainer on BA-shapes, Tree-cycles, and Mutag; [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) shows GStarX outperforming baselines on 4 of 6 datasets with H-Fidelity; and [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e07400198b777cd822602d2c6812635f986bda44) shows EiG-Search achieving higher overall fidelity than node-induced and edge-induced baselines and faster explanation time, with PGExplainer having 225.5 seconds training plus 0.08 seconds inference per sample on BA-2Motifs. Fourth, the closest graphlet-based evidence is model-level and qualitative, not instance-level: [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) uses graphlet counts and an MLP surrogate for graph classification, reports qualitative Shape, Reddit-Binary, MUTAG, and BA-2Motif explanations, and does not provide numeric fidelity, node-level orbit scores, or BFS subgraph extraction; therefore it supports the orbit idea but does not validate the proposed instance-level decomposition. Fifth, search and perturbation methods can be expensive, which is a risk for BFS over orbit patterns on PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks: [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) reports PGMExplainer infeasible on Pubmed with up to an hour per prediction, and [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) uses MCTS with 20 iterations and Monte Carlo sampling of 100 or 1000 steps on a V100 GPU, indicating that subgraph search requires careful efficiency controls. Sixth, older work provides weaker or superseded evidence: [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) reports GNNExplainer qualitatively and is later outperformed by PGExplainer, Gem, DnX, and GStarX in several settings; [(Sánchez-Lengeling et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/861cf64943e90074cd25eada6e4c3912aef17eb0) provides no quantitative outcomes for CAM, so it cannot support orbit decomposition over activation attribution; [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679) generates new graphs rather than extracting subgraphs from the input and reports no baseline comparisons; [(Shin et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/67c0a7c64b62c2b6370c3788c685d00d610963d6) is model-level prototype extraction rather than node-specific orbit explanation; and [(Chen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94e4648f161a9383f1118c8e2a7a9833bb201d2b) evaluates graph-level generative explainers rather than node-level orbit contributions. Finally, reproducibility gaps and dataset inconsistencies weaken direct transfer: MUTAG is reported as 4,337 molecule graphs in [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) and [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) but as 188 mutagenic compounds in [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) and [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), and many excerpts omit hardware, random seeds, hyperparameters, or statistical significance; the planned real-world PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene-network datasets also lack ground-truth orbit patterns, making edge-recall and sub-recall uncertain unless synthetic ground truth is injected or domain validation is used.

### Suggested Action

Most important, formalize the node-class-orbit decomposition before claiming interpretability, because GNN predictions are nonlinear and orbit contributions may not sum exactly to the prediction; report reconstruction error or residual, compare learned logistic orbit-basis classifiers against exact orbit-existence indicators, and if necessary use a local linear or SGC-style surrogate or Shapley-style orbit contributions, while limiting the basis to 2-5 node graphlets and selected interpretable orbits rather than all possible 6-node patterns, following the interpretability concern in [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336). Second, run a comprehensive evaluation on synthetic motif datasets with known ground truth, including BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, BA-2Motifs, Hetero-BA-Shapes, and subgraph-logic tasks, and on the planned real-world PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene-network datasets; report sparsity, fidelity+, fidelity-, H-Fidelity, edge-recall, sub-recall, orbit-recall, orbit-precision, AUC-ROC, infidelity, log-odds difference, explanation accuracy, runtime, and stability, with multiple runs, statistical significance, hardware, random seeds, and software versions, following protocols in [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1), [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5), and [(Yuan et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3). Third, compare against strong instance-level baselines under the same maximum explanation nodes, edges, L-hop neighborhood, and sparsity levels, including GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, SubgraphX, PGMExplainer, Gem, DnX, FastDnX, GStarX, EiG-Search, RelEx, CAM, GraphLIME, the planned TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, and MotifExplainer, generative explainers such as RCExplainer, GraphCFE, and GSAT, and model-level D4Explainer, GLGExplainer, PAGE, and XGNN for the unified framework, using the dataset and model settings reported in [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5), [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1), [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5), [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e07400198b777cd822602d2c6812635f986bda44), and [(Chen et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/94e4648f161a9383f1118c8e2a7a9833bb201d2b). Fourth, prevent spurious orbit selection by optimizing or filtering orbit explanations with fidelity rather than only highest node-class-orbit score; use fid- and fid+ edge-removal tests, log-odds difference, prediction preservation, noisy-feature and noisy-orbit controls, and ground-truth homomorphism or motif-criterion checks, motivated by [(Köhler, and Heindorf, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bf33abdb4ad61864f0e08d16b8ad423a4a85d9f), [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), [(Huang et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8819adc0f064d5293bd2b783a63e23f68109738a), and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a). Fifth, make the breadth-first search efficient and intuitive by precomputing orbit existence for each node, restricting search to the L-hop computation graph, returning connected edge-induced subgraphs rather than disconnected node sets, using top-k orbit patterns, beam search, caching, and parallelism, and reporting inference time and scalability on large PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks, with comparisons to DnX/FastDnX and PGMExplainer runtimes and consideration of streaming or incremental pattern matching inspired by [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), [(Lu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e07400198b777cd822602d2c6812635f986bda44), and [(Chen et al., 2024-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2b1e64e590d3c4cb6e9c0ccd57acbe5be7b3b7fa). Sixth, for real-world datasets without ground-truth orbits, evaluate using fidelity and sparsity, human or domain-expert studies, biological pathway validation, label-corruption faithfulness, consistency across hidden-layer configurations, and Kendall tau faithfulness, and create controlled ground truth by injecting known motifs into real graphs, following [(Shin et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/67c0a7c64b62c2b6370c3788c685d00d610963d6), [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), and [(Sánchez-Lengeling et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/861cf64943e90074cd25eada6e4c3912aef17eb0). Seventh, ensure the unified model-level and instance-level framework is internally consistent by showing that class-orbit scores aggregate to or predict node-class-orbit scores, using graphlet/orbit-frequency surrogates or greedy class-weight decomposition, and reporting prototype faithfulness, consistency, density, and reconstruction error, as motivated by [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), [(Shin et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/67c0a7c64b62c2b6370c3788c685d00d610963d6), and [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679). Finally, improve reproducibility by specifying exact dataset sizes and splits, resolving the MUTAG 188 versus 4,337 discrepancy, reporting PyTorch and PyTorch Geometric versions, hyperparameters for orbit basis learning and class-orbit score learning, BFS depth and top-k, random seeds, hardware, and statistical tests, and releasing code and explanation visualizations.

## Unified expert interface: The unified framework allows domain experts to select relevant graphlets as interpretable units and request explanations based on these units, while our method provides both global patterns (model-level) and local explanations (instance-level) simultaneously. This makes the method usable by domain experts who can choose scientifically meaningful graphlets and receive both model-level and instance-level explanations.

### Support

Strongest support for the proposed unified expert interface comes from [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), which directly treats small graphlets as interpretable units by converting graphs into a 29-dimensional graphlet frequency vector containing 2 three-node, 6 four-node, and 21 five-node graphlets, training a transparent MLP surrogate, and deriving model-level explanatory substructures on Reddit-Binary with 2000 graphs, MUTAG with 188 graphs, BA-2Motif with 1000 graphs, and a Shape dataset; its fid- and fid+ edge-removal fidelity analysis supports the plan's use of graphlet-based model-level explanation and fidelity-style evaluation, although it does not provide instance-level explanations or an expert selection interface. The next strongest support is [(Dai, and Wang, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0e0b032c29d71507b132caa0da77749c9b0ba01), which demonstrates that a single self-explainable GNN can provide both class-level prototype explanations and instance-level explanations using a two-layer GCN encoder, prototype generator, Euclidean similarity, K prototypes per class searched over 2 to 6, M=K, alpha values 100 to 0.01, beta values 10 to 0.1, five repeated runs, and evaluation on Cora, Pubmed, MUTAG, Graph-SST2, BA-Shapes, and Syn-SST; this supports the plan's unified model-level plus instance-level objective, but its learned prototypes are not expert-selectable graphlets or orbits. [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) also supports the need for simultaneous local and class-level explanations by combining instance masks with graph prototypes on BA-SHAPES with a 300-node Barabasi-Albert base and 80 house motifs, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, TREE-GRID, MUTAG, and REDDIT-BINARY, while comparing GRAD and ATT, although it uses masks rather than graphlets. [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679) supports model-level structural explanation by generating small class-specific graphs under maximum node limits of 3 to 7 on Is_Acyclic and maximum node limit 5 from single atoms on MUTAG, producing carbon-ring and NO2 patterns with predicted probabilities of 0.7544, 1.0000, or 0.9999, but it lacks instance-level explanation and graphlet decomposition. Instance-level support is strongest from [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), whose PGExplainer achieves AUC 0.963±0.011 on BA-Shapes, 0.945±0.019 on BA-Community, 0.987±0.007 on Tree-Cycles, 0.907±0.014 on Tree-Grid, 0.926±0.021 on BA-2motifs, and 0.873±0.013 on MUTAG, with inference times of 10.92, 24.07, 6.36, 6.72, 80.13, and 9.68 ms, showing that compact subgraph explanations can be accurate and fast. The newer benchmark in [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) supports the plan's fidelity, sparsity, accuracy, and stability evaluation design by comparing SubgraphX, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, GNN-LRP, GNN-GI, DeepLift, and Grad-CAM on Graph-SST2, Graph-SST5, Graph-Twitter, BBBP, BA-2Motifs, and BA-Shapes, while the SubgraphX method in [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) supports instance-level subgraph search with MCTS, 20 iterations, 100 Monte Carlo steps for SubgraphX, 1000 for the MCTS baseline, lambda 5 or 10, three-hop computation graphs, and an Nvidia V100 GPU. [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) supports fidelity-sparsity evaluation through H-Fidelity, sparsity levels 0.5 to 0.85, exact HN computation threshold m=10, Monte Carlo J=n, and graphs under 200 nodes. [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) supports the need for efficient surrogate-based explanation by distilling 3-layer GCN, GIN, GATED, and ARMA models into SGC surrogates with over 86% distillation accuracy and reporting FastDnX as about four orders of magnitude faster than PGMExplainer on Bitcoin. Weaker but still relevant support comes from [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a), which uses AUC-ROC and infidelity on TREE-GRID, TREE-BA, a 3-layer GCN, and MUTAG to show relational explanations can recover known motifs; [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f), which uses Bayesian-network Markov blankets on Bitcoin-Alpha with 3783 accounts, Bitcoin-OTC with 5881 accounts, and MNIST SuperPixel-Graph with 101365 parameters and a 10-user human test; [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1), which reports a 30% explanation-accuracy improvement over GNNExplainer on BA-shapes at K=5 and K-dependent motif recovery on Tree-cycles, Mutag, and NCI1; and [(Ying et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d18b48f77eb5c517a6d2c1fa434d2952a1b0a825), which qualitatively visualizes DIFFPOOL cluster assignments on COLLAB with 25% of nodes as clusters and reports a 6.27% average improvement over GraphSAGE, supporting structural interpretability but not graphlet-based explanation.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is that none of the related work directly validates the proposed expert-selectable graphlet/orbit interface that simultaneously returns model-level and instance-level explanations. [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) uses a fixed 29-graphlet frequency vector and an MLP surrogate for model-level explanation but does not let experts choose graphlets, does not learn orbit basis vectors, and does not produce instance-level orbit contributions; [(Dai, and Wang, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0e0b032c29d71507b132caa0da77749c9b0ba01) provides unified prototype explanations but uses learned prototypes rather than domain-selected graphlets; and [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) provides prototypes and masks but not graphlet/orbit units. This means the central human-interpretable interface claim is currently extrapolated from separate model-level and instance-level results rather than demonstrated. A second strong contradiction is that model-level structural explanations can be spurious if they merely maximize GNN output. [(Köhler, and Heindorf, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bf33abdb4ad61864f0e08d16b8ad423a4a85d9f) shows that on Hetero-BA-Shapes with a 10,000-node Barabasi-Albert graph plus 1,000 house motifs and a two-layer GNN, class expressions selected by maximizing GNN output achieved fidelity 0.50, 0.50, and 0.51 and explanation accuracy 0.45, 0.63, and 0.63, while fidelity-maximizing expressions achieved fidelity 0.97 and explanation accuracy 1.00; the GNN-output expressions contained spurious A-to-C and D-to-D triples. [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679) similarly generated chlorine-containing graphs predicted as mutagenic on MUTAG, exposing possible model inconsistencies. This contradicts a greedy class-weight decomposition that is not explicitly constrained by fidelity, domain validity, or anti-sparsity regularization. Third, existing instance-level methods are already strong, accurate, and sometimes much faster, so the proposed orbit-based BFS must show clear gains. [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) reports PGExplainer AUC improvements of 4.1%, 13.0%, 4.1%, 3.7%, 24.7%, and 11.5% over GNNExplainer and speedups of 59x, 29x, 108x, 106x, 12x, and 42x; the newer benchmark in [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) reports SubgraphX outperforming other methods on Fidelity+prob and Fidelity-prob across Graph-SST2, Graph-SST5, Graph-Twitter, BBBP, BA-2Motifs, and BA-Shapes; [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) reports GStarX achieving highest average H-Fidelity on four of six datasets and being more than two times faster than SubgraphX on 50 BBBP graphs; and the newer [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) extends earlier node-level explainers by reporting DnX precision gaps of at least 14% on Bitcoin-Alpha and 11% on Bitcoin-OTC, with FastDnX about four orders of magnitude faster than PGMExplainer. Fourth, the plan's orbit enumeration and BFS may face computational constraints that related work highlights. [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) excludes larger graphlets for interpretability and because most GNN architectures use four or fewer layers, while the plan uses 2-5 node graphlets; [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) requires an Nvidia V100 GPU, MCTS iterations 20, Monte Carlo steps 100 or 1000, and three-hop computation graphs; [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) uses exact HN computation only for m=10 and Monte Carlo J=n; and [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e) notes PGMExplainer can take up to an hour per prediction on Pubmed, making runtime a critical comparison. Fifth, the planned real-world domains are not directly supported by the related work. The related work evaluates MUTAG, BBBP, Reddit-Binary, Bitcoin-Alpha, Bitcoin-OTC, Cora, Pubmed, Citeseer, Graph-SST2, Graph-SST5, Twitter, NCI1, and synthetic motif datasets, but not Protein-Protein Interaction, LastFM-Asia, or Gene networks; therefore the claim that experts will find orbit explanations useful in those domains remains untested. Sixth, fidelity and sparsity metrics can reward arbitrary sparse subgraphs and may not capture orbit-specific interpretability. [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) shows DeepLift had the best stability but significantly worse accuracy, and SubgraphX was excluded from accuracy and stability because its subgraph explanations could not be converted to soft edge masks; [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5) uses H-Fidelity normalized by sparsity, which may favor compact but not necessarily graphlet-aligned explanations. Finally, much of the evidence is qualitative or lacks statistical significance, weakening the empirical foundation for the proposed method: [(Ying et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d18b48f77eb5c517a6d2c1fa434d2952a1b0a825) reports qualitative cluster visualizations without cluster-quality metrics; [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) does not provide numeric fidelity or significance in the excerpt; [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) does not provide numeric metric values or confidence intervals; [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6ae2967bb0a5e57cc545176120a4845576e068a3) does not report numerical outcomes in the excerpt; and [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) reports qualitative fidelity-based outcomes without numerical comparisons or significance.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the plan should implement and evaluate the expert interface itself rather than only the orbit decomposition. Define a graphlet/orbit library for 2-5 node graphlets, expose orbit existence indicators and class-orbit scores to domain experts, and run a user study with experts from protein, social, and gene-network domains to measure task completion, time, preference, and whether selected graphlets improve explanation accuracy; compare against fixed graphlet surrogates like [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), learned prototypes like [(Dai, and Wang, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a0e0b032c29d71507b132caa0da77749c9b0ba01), and mask/prototype methods like [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c). Second, constrain model-level greedy orbit decomposition with fidelity and anti-spurious objectives. Use fid- and fid+ edge-removal fidelity from [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), Fidelity+prob and Fidelity-prob from [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5), H-Fidelity from [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5), and the fidelity-maximizing lesson from [(Köhler, and Heindorf, 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5bf33abdb4ad61864f0e08d16b8ad423a4a85d9f) to avoid selecting orbits that merely increase GNN output, as in [(Yuan et al., 2020-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75c8466a0c1c3b9fe595efc83671984ef95bd679). Third, benchmark UO-Explainer against the strongest instance-level baselines under matched constraints: PGExplainer with its concrete-distribution subgraph model and reported AUC and runtime, SubgraphX with MCTS iterations 20, Monte Carlo steps 100 or 1000, lambda 5 or 10, and V100 hardware, GStarX with tau=0.01, exact threshold m=10, J=n, and sparsity 0.5 to 0.85, DnX/FastDnX with SGC distillation and convex extraction, Gem with K-controlled compact subgraphs and 3-layer GCN explainer dimensions 32, 32, and 16, PGM-Explainer with Bayesian-network Markov blankets, and GNNExplainer with mask and prototype outputs. Because D4Explainer and GLGExplainer are named in the plan but absent from the provided related work, they should still be included as model-level baselines with the same fidelity and orbit-recall metrics. Report AUC, edge-recall, sub-recall, fidelity, sparsity, stability, runtime, memory, and statistical significance over multiple seeds. Fourth, make the method computationally credible for real-world graphs. Precompute orbit counts, use sampling or approximate orbit detection for large PPI, LastFM-Asia, and Gene networks, limit BFS depth and candidate subgraphs, and report hardware, runtime per explanation, and scalability; compare against the speed evidence from [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), [(Zhang et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/bb608cf0cfef9103f14f7d326be36e999dc88af5), and [(Pereira et al., 2023-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f945b6788d4042c950e57e6032c0ad122566661e), including the warning that PGMExplainer can take up to an hour on Pubmed. Fifth, align datasets and ground truth with the strongest existing motif benchmarks. Use BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, and BA-2Motifs with known motif edge masks as in [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5), and add real-world MUTAG, BBBP, Reddit-Binary, Bitcoin-Alpha, Bitcoin-OTC, or Cora/Pubmed where domain motifs or ground truth are available, before or alongside the planned PPI, LastFM-Asia, and Gene networks. Sixth, clarify the orbit basis learning and greedy decomposition. Specify the number of orbits for 2-5 node graphlets, the logistic classifier architecture, regularization, training data, and how orbit basis vectors are learned from node representations; report coefficient stability, confidence intervals, and ablations comparing greedy selection to direct optimization, random orbit selection, and the 29-dimensional graphlet frequency surrogate from [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336). Seventh, add human interpretability metrics beyond automatic fidelity, such as the 10-user 0-to-10 scoring protocol used by [(Vu, and Thai, 2020-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a8ae2d8232db04d88cf622e5fabd11da3163aa8f) on 48 correctly predicted graphs, and expert validation of whether orbit explanations match known biological pathways or social motifs. Finally, report full reproducibility details that are often missing in the related work: PyTorch and TorchGeometric versions, GPU/CPU specifications, random seeds, hyperparameter grids, dataset splits, number of runs, and significance tests, because several related works omit hardware, hyperparameters, or statistical tests.

## Dataset selection: We plan to use five synthetic datasets (Random Graph, BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycle, Tree-Grid) with known ground-truth patterns, and three real-world datasets (Protein-Protein Interaction, LastFM-Asia, Gene networks) representing diverse application domains. The synthetic datasets will allow us to verify whether our method correctly identifies predetermined structural patterns, while real-world datasets will demonstrate practical applicability. This establishes the data foundation for controlled validation and real-world evaluation.

### Support

Strongest support comes from [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), which quantitatively evaluates PGExplainer on the same four motif-based synthetic node-classification datasets used in the plan, BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, and Tree-Grid, using motif edges as positives and non-motif edges as negatives, reporting edge-level AUC with standard deviations over 10 runs and showing gains over GNNExplainer of 4.1% on BA-Shapes, 13.0% on BA-Community, 4.1% on Tree-Cycles, 3.7% on Tree-Grid, 24.7% on BA-2motifs, and 11.5% on MUTAG, with inference times of 10.92, 24.07, 6.36, 6.72, 80.13, and 9.68 ms versus 650.60, 696.61, 690.13, 713.40, 934.72, and 409.98 ms; this directly validates the plan's choice of these synthetic datasets as controlled ground-truth tests for orbit/motif recovery and supports edge-recall-style evaluation. The next strongest support is [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), which established the same BA-SHAPES, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, and TREE-GRID construction protocol, including a 300-node Barabasi-Albert base with 80 five-node house motifs and 0.1N perturbation edges, two BA-SHAPES communities with Gaussian features, an 8-level tree with 80 six-node cycles, and a tree with 3-by-3 grids, and used GNNExplainer, GRAD, and ATT as baselines, thereby providing a prior experimental template for the plan's synthetic validation and baseline set. [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) supports the graphlet/orbit premise by using 29-dimensional 3-, 4-, and 5-node graphlet frequency vectors, a transparent MLP surrogate, and fid+/fid- fidelity on synthetic BA-2Motif and Shape plus real Reddit-Binary and MUTAG, showing that graphlet-based model-level explanations can capture class-defining structures such as House, Grid, Star, and branching patterns, which aligns with UO-Explainer's use of graphlet orbits as interpretable units. [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b) supports the synthetic-plus-real design by using motif-based node-classification graphs with known ground-truth nodes and real-world Cora/PubMed noise-injection tests, including an ablation finding that capturing indirect node effects improves performance by 19% on average, although its real datasets differ from PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks. [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) and [(Hua et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f72d557d3a564c03eb8c1624d9fe25b5587008) support the dataset-selection rationale by documenting that explainability-specific graph benchmarks are limited and by recommending task-specific datasets, multiple runs, standard deviations, and metrics such as accuracy, ROC-AUC, AP, MAE, fidelity, sparsity, and stability, with Hua et al. explicitly requiring at least five runs and reporting dataset statistics for node- and graph-level benchmarks. [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) supports pretraining GIN as a base model because sum-aggregation GINs outperform mean/max variants on structure-heavy Reddit datasets, with mean-MLP near random at 50.0% on REDDIT-BINARY and 20.0% on REDDIT-MULTI5K, indicating that GIN can encode structural patterns relevant to orbit-based decomposition. Finally, [(Zhou et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea5dd6a3d8f210d05e53a7b6fa5e16f1b115f693) supports the plan's emphasis on fair baselines, multiple splits, hyperparameter tuning, and statistical significance, although it does not evaluate explanation metrics.

### Contradictions

Strongest contradiction is [(Himmelhuber et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a63fa4b6d85c5bb1037666a5877befb340b0591d), which directly challenges the plan's assumption that the full house or cycle motif is the correct ground truth for BA-Shapes and Tree-Cycles: the house-motif ground truth does not consistently have lower entropy than trivial baselines such as the target node or receptive field, compact submotifs such as squares or triangles can be equally valid, and average precision-recall AUC can be up to 47 percentage points lower than ROC AUC because most edges are negative, so the plan's edge-recall and sub-recall metrics may be overly optimistic unless threshold-dependent, imbalance-robust, and entropy-controlled. The next strongest contradiction is [(Amara et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c559dc62c0d64295f9c0dfb5f322b3f30ddf44eb), which shows that rankings on type-1 synthetic motif datasets can be inconsistent with rankings on real datasets and that synthetic-only evaluation can understate gradient-based methods; for example, PageRank was most accurate on synthetic datasets while Saliency had low synthetic accuracy but high real-dataset characterization scores, only Saliency, Distance, and Occlusion reached necessary-fidelity above 0.6 in at least one setting, and Occlusion was about 10,000 times slower than Saliency, so the plan's reliance on synthetic orbit recovery alone may not predict real-world PPI, LastFM-Asia, or gene-network performance. A further contradiction is that none of the related works directly validates the proposed Random Graph dataset, PPI, LastFM-Asia, or gene-network datasets, nor orbit-specific ground truth; prior real-world explanation evaluations use MUTAG, Reddit-Binary, Cora, PubMed, or molecule graphs [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b), [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), leaving the plan's real-world applicability and domain-specific orbit relevance unproven. [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) also introduces a theoretical limitation: aggregation-based GNNs are bounded by the Weisfeiler-Lehman test and training accuracies do not exceed the WL subtree kernel, so GCN/GIN embeddings may not distinguish all orbit configurations, which could limit orbit basis learning and orbit contribution decomposition. [(Zhou et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea5dd6a3d8f210d05e53a7b6fa5e16f1b115f693) warns that model rankings can change dramatically across dataset splits and that architecture choices such as depth and aggregation materially affect performance, so if the plan does not control splits, seeds, and hyperparameters, conclusions about dataset selection and explanation quality may be unstable. Finally, [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336) is only partial support because it evaluates graph-level graphlet-frequency surrogate explanations rather than node-level orbit scores or BFS instance-level subgraph extraction, so it does not directly validate the proposed unified model-level and instance-level orbit framework.

### Suggested Action

Most important, revise the synthetic ground-truth protocol to address [(Himmelhuber et al., 2021-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a63fa4b6d85c5bb1037666a5877befb340b0591d) by reporting entropy of the declared motif, trivial baselines, alternative submotifs, and threshold-dependent recall/precision-recall AUC alongside edge-recall and sub-recall, and by defining ground truth at the orbit-membership level rather than assuming the entire house, cycle, or grid motif is necessary. Second, specify the Random Graph dataset in detail, including generator, number of graphs, node and edge counts, class labels, orbit distributions, negative controls, and class imbalance, so that it can serve as a non-motif baseline and not introduce uncontrolled confounds. Third, strengthen real-world evaluation for PPI, LastFM-Asia, and gene networks by reporting dataset sizes, splits, class balance, domain-specific ground truth where available, necessary and sufficient fidelity, sparsity, stability, runtime, and qualitative pathway validation, following the synthetic-plus-real and noise-injection logic of [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b) and the benchmark gap identified by [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) and [(Hua et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f72d557d3a564c03eb8c1624d9fe25b5587008). Fourth, adopt a more complete metric suite inspired by [(Amara et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c559dc62c0d64295f9c0dfb5f322b3f30ddf44eb) and [(Lu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/718617785bdac736287f02b46be1bbec83f83336), including fid+/fid- or equivalent necessary/sufficient fidelity, top-k sensitivity, hard/soft mask variants, sparsity, runtime, and multiple runs with standard deviations and significance tests as recommended by [(Zhou et al., 2018-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea5dd6a3d8f210d05e53a7b6fa5e16f1b115f693) and [(Hua et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87f72d557d3a564c03eb8c1624d9fe25b5587008). Fifth, control the base-model training for GCN and GIN by fixing architecture, hyperparameters, seeds, and splits, reporting WL-related limitations from [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9), and comparing GIN variants if orbit sensitivity is affected by aggregation. Sixth, include the proposed baselines D4Explainer and GLGExplainer for model-level explanations and GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, and MotifExplainer for instance-level explanations under the same top-k, mask, and run protocol, using the quantitative edge-AUC and motif-recovery setup of [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) as a template. Finally, add orbit-specific diagnostics, such as orbit-existence classifier accuracy, orbit basis recovery, graphlet/orbit count alignment, and BFS subgraph match statistics, to show that UO-Explainer recovers the intended 2- to 5-node orbit patterns rather than only generic motif edges.

## Synthetic validation: For synthetic datasets, we will pre-train GCN and GIN models on node classification tasks where each class corresponds to specific orbit memberships. We will then evaluate whether our explanations correctly identify the ground-truth orbit patterns. This tests whether UO-Explainer recovers known structural patterns in controlled settings.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed synthetic validation is quantitative evidence that controlled node-classification graphs with planted local structures can be used to test whether an explainer recovers ground-truth edges. [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) evaluates PGExplainer on BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, and Tree-Grid, where labels are tied to house, community, cycle, or grid motifs; it reports edge-level AUC with motif edges as positives over 10 runs, with PGExplainer achieving 0.963±0.011 on BA-Shapes, 0.945±0.019 on BA-Community, 0.987±0.007 on Tree-Cycles, and 0.907±0.014 on Tree-Grid, outperforming GNNExplainer, GRAD, and ATT. This directly supports the plan's assumption that synthetic orbit-membership node classification can provide a ground-truth test for UO-Explainer, although the paper uses motifs rather than graphlet orbits and does not report sub-recall, fidelity, sparsity, or GCN/GIN target models. The next strongest support is that GIN is a more expressive base model than mean-pooling GCN for structure-dependent tasks. [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) shows GIN-0 and GIN- nearly fit training sets and outperform mean-1-layer GCN and mean-MLP on nine graph-classification benchmarks, while mean-1-layer GCN and mean-MLP reach random-level accuracy on Reddit with uninformative node features, 50.0±0.0 on REDDIT-BINARY and 20.0±0.0 on REDDIT-MULTI5K; degree features improve mean-MLP to 71.2±4.6 and 41.3±2.1 but remain below sum-based GNNs. This supports pre-training both GCN and GIN, with GIN as the stronger orbit-learning model, and warns that GCN may be insufficient when node features do not encode orbit membership. The related work also forms an iterative validation sequence: [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) introduced BA-SHAPES with 300 base nodes and 80 house motifs, BA-COMMUNITY with 8 role classes, and TREE-CYCLES/TREE-GRID with 80 motifs on an 8-level tree, but only qualitatively assessed GNNExplainer; [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) later strengthened this with quantitative edge AUC; [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) and [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b) extended the motif-validation idea to K-controlled subgraph size and Shapley-style indirect effects, with Gem using a 3-layer GCN encoder of dimensions 32, 32, and 16, learning rate 0.01, and reporting a 30% explanation-accuracy improvement over GNNExplainer on BA-shapes at K=5, while GraphSVX reports a 19% average ablation gain from capturing indirect node effects. These support the plan's use of known local patterns, sparsity, and additive contributions, but they remain motif-based rather than orbit-based. Weaker but relevant support comes from orbit and graphlet feature work: [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f) provides orbit-aware CIS sampling and SAND orbit-degree estimation with O(1) or O(log d_v) complexity on SNAP graphs such as Flickr, Pokec, LiveJournal, YouTube, Wiki-Talk, and Web-Google, which could help compute or verify orbit indicators; [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) supports additive binary feature attributions and linear explanation models, analogous to orbit-basis decomposition; [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478) supplies a reproducible two-layer GCN node-classification protocol with 20 training labels per class, 500 validation examples, 1,000 test examples, Adam learning rate 0.01, and early stopping after 10 epochs; and [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838) supports graphlet/orbit features for node classification in typed ego networks with up to 10 alters and 13 edge types, though it does not test GNN explanations.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that standard mean-pooling GCN may fail to learn the structural patterns that UO-Explainer is supposed to explain. [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9) reports that mean-1-layer GCN and mean-MLP reach random-level test accuracy when node features are uninformative, 50.0±0.0 on REDDIT-BINARY and 20.0±0.0 on REDDIT-MULTI5K, while GIN-0 and GIN- fit training sets and achieve much higher test accuracy; even with degree features, mean-MLP only reaches 71.2±4.6 and 41.3±2.1, far below sum-based GNNs. The same paper also notes that aggregation-based GNNs are bounded by WL expressivity, so a two-layer or shallow GCN/GIN may not distinguish all 2-5 node orbit memberships, especially when node features are absent. This contradicts the plan's assumption that pre-training GCN on orbit-membership classes will reliably produce orbit-sensitive predictions. The second major contradiction is that the synthetic explanation literature validates motifs and roles, not graphlet orbits, and therefore does not directly support the orbit-specific claims of UO-Explainer. [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) uses motif edges as positive examples and reports edge-level AUC, but not orbit membership, sub-recall, fidelity, sparsity, or explicit GCN/GIN target models; [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) provides only qualitative evidence for GNNExplainer on BA-SHAPES, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, and TREE-GRID; [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) relies on explanation accuracy and visual inspection with an unspecified target GNN; and [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b) evaluates motif-node accuracy rather than orbit-pattern recovery. Thus, strong motif recovery does not prove that UO-Explainer will recover 2-5 node orbit bases or unified model-level and instance-level orbit scores. A further contradiction is that sparsity and explanation size can cause failure: [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) reports that GNNExplainer fails to produce effective explanations on Tree-cycles when K is less than 8, while Gem and PGExplainer remain favorable at K=6, implying that UO-Explainer's BFS subgraph matching must be calibrated to the true orbit size or it may miss ground-truth orbits. Weaker contradictions include the fact that [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478) provides a semi-supervised citation-network GCN protocol rather than an orbit-classification protocol, so its hyperparameters and label budgets may not transfer to synthetic orbit tasks; [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f) and [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838) provide orbit/graphlet features but no GNN explanation evaluation; and [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) supports additive attributions but not higher-order graphlet interactions, so a linear orbit decomposition may miss nonlinear co-occurrence effects.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make GIN the primary pre-trained base model and treat GCN as a weaker baseline, while explicitly reporting whether the base GNN learns orbit membership before explanation. Following [(Xu et al., 2018-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/62ed9bf1d83c8db1f9cbf92ea2f57ea90ef683d9), use uninformative or degree-only node features, report per-orbit-class training and test accuracy, confusion matrices, and WL/expressivity diagnostics, and avoid attributing explanation failures to UO-Explainer when the GCN base model underfits. The second most important action is to adopt the quantitative synthetic evaluation protocol from the motif-explanation literature and adapt it to orbits. Using the synthetic datasets introduced by [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) and quantified by [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1), define ground-truth orbit edges and orbit subgraphs, report edge-level AUC, edge-recall, sub-recall, fidelity, sparsity, and K, run at least 10 repetitions, and report mean, standard deviation, and statistical significance; sweep K around the orbit size, including K=5, 6, and 8, because Tree-cycles shows that too-small K can cause failure. Third, use orbit-aware sampling and estimation to generate and verify orbit labels. [(Wang et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc3601286a0c40f8b254ff5fc5db58502aea232f) provides Randgraf-3/4 CIS sampling and SAND orbit-degree estimation for 3- and 4-node orbits, which can be used to compute binary orbit indicators and validate the orbit basis; this should be extended to 5-node orbits and to typed or directed edges if the real-world gene or social networks require them. Fourth, align the orbit decomposition with additive explanation properties and compare against additive baselines. [(Lundberg, and Lee, 2017-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/442e10a3c6640ded9408622005e3c2a8906ce4c2) suggests reporting local accuracy, consistency, and efficiency for the linear orbit-basis model, and possibly comparing UO-Explainer to Kernel SHAP or SHAP-style feature attributions on the same synthetic orbit task. Fifth, use the GCN training protocol from [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478) as a reproducible baseline, but adapt it to orbit classes by specifying labels per orbit, validation/test splits, Adam learning rate 0.01, early stopping, row-normalized features, and multiple seeds. Sixth, include ablations and real-world extensions motivated by later work: test whether orbit basis learning captures indirect structural effects, as suggested by the 19% average ablation gain in [(Duval, and Malliaros, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6c26f638f0b16244f634e163562594993b06ec9b); evaluate K-controlled sparsity as in [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1); and consider typed-edge ego-graphlet degree vectors for real-world typed networks, as in [(Jia et al., 2022-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f020e454149a7f80c848fdea08e9964e9f90838).

## Real-world validation: For real-world datasets, we will focus on fidelity and sparsity metrics since ground-truth patterns are not available. We will conduct qualitative analysis through visualization of explanatory subgraphs, particularly examining whether identified gene networks align with known biological pathways. This evaluates practical explanation quality and domain plausibility on real networks.

### Support

The strongest support is that [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) directly evaluates GNNExplainer on real-world graph classification without ground-truth explanations, using fidelity and sparsity on MUTAG (4,337 graphs) and Reddit-Binary (2,000 graphs), reporting GNNExplainer fidelity about 0.8 and sparsity about 0.1 on MUTAG, fidelity about 0.9 and sparsity about 0.05 on Reddit-Binary, versus GRAD fidelity near 0.1-0.2 and sparsity near 1.0, and ATT fidelity around 0.3-0.4 and sparsity around 0.6-0.7, plus qualitative molecular and thread motifs; this validates the proposed real-world validation choice of fidelity, sparsity, and visualization, although it is graph classification rather than node-level gene networks and reports no significance tests. A strong protocol-level support is [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659), which recommends heuristic fidelity, fidelity+, sparsity, and expert visualization for real-world graphs without ground-truth rationales, including molecule graphs and sentiment graphs such as Graph-SST2 with 70,042 graphs, Graph-SST5 with 11,855 graphs, and Graph-Twitter with 6,940 graphs, directly matching the plan's fidelity/sparsity plus qualitative pathway plausibility, though it provides no empirical results. A strong node-level support is [(He et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26647ac28bfd1bed6cf2d81d1cdd01dc1e852b69), which uses prediction-retention Essentialness Percentage and sparsity on real-world and node-classification settings, with a 2-layer GCN with 64 hidden channels, top-5/top-10 nodes within 2-hop neighborhoods, and reports ILLUMINATI 7.1% higher EP than GNNExplainer on average across two node-classification datasets, supporting prediction-retention and sparsity for node explanations, although it does not visualize gene pathways. Moderate support comes from [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1), which uses real-world Mutag (4,337 graphs) and NCI1 (4,110 instances) without ground-truth motifs, a three-layer GCN explainer with dimensions 32, 32, and 16, learning rate 0.01, K=15, explanation accuracy, log-odds difference, and qualitative chemical inspection, supporting ground-truth-free prediction preservation and compactness, but it does not report explicit fidelity/sparsity or gene pathways. Moderate support also comes from [(Lin et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfd1ba68e0dee2bbf86dd202e79587599939539c), which evaluates OrphicX on MUTAG and NCI1 under edge-ratio sparsity constraints R and log-odds difference, supporting sparsity-controlled real-world explanation evaluation, but the excerpt gives no numerical accuracy, confidence intervals, or pathway analysis. Moderate support for qualitative motif inspection is provided by [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), which uses MUTAG motif ground truth, top-K=10 edge visualization, and reports PGExplainer AUC 0.873 ± 0.013 versus GNNExplainer 0.727, GRAD 0.783, ATT 0.765, and Gradient 0.773 over 10 repeats, with 9.68 ms versus 409.98 ms inference, showing that visual motif alignment can be meaningful, although it uses motif AUC rather than fidelity/sparsity. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef), which demonstrates PPI node classification with GraphSAGE on unseen graphs, with micro-averaged F1 of 0.502 for unsupervised pool and 0.612 for supervised LSTM, K=2 layers, S1=25 and S2=10, Adam, and TensorFlow, supporting PPI as a real biological node-classification domain, but it does not evaluate explanation fidelity, sparsity, or pathway interpretability.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Zhang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4936c5acfffcc6368e99cd8326fc7555c76a58f0) explicitly excludes fidelity metrics because they can be affected by distribution shift between original graphs and explanations, and instead evaluates MixupExplainer with AUC-ROC on edge importance plus cosine similarity and Euclidean distance between GNN graph embeddings; it reports average AUC-ROC improvements of 12.3% for Mixup-GNNExplainer on node classification and 22.6% on graph classification, and 5.41% and 19.4% for Mixup-PGExplainer, with a 3-layer GCN target, Adam weight decay 5e-4, GNNExplainer lr 0.01 for 100 epochs, PGExplainer lr 0.003 for 30 epochs, lambda=0.1, eta=1, and 10 random seeds, directly challenging the proposed reliance on standard fidelity for real-world validation. A strong related contradiction is [(Chen et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fd8cf39a8c70c386c938535a1fe02ce19e431e2), which argues that standard perturbation-based fidelity can suffer out-of-distribution issues and uses robust fidelity metrics Fid_alpha1,+ and Fid_alpha2,- with alpha1=0.1 and alpha2=0.9, reporting that ProxyExplainer outperforms GradCAM, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, ReFine, and MixupExplainer across 10 seeds on MUTAG, Benzene, Alkane-Carbonyl, Fluoride-Carbonyl, BA-2motifs, and BA-3motifs, although it relies on ground-truth edge labels and does not report sparsity or gene pathways. A moderate contradiction is that when domain motifs or pathways are known, fidelity/sparsity alone may be insufficient: [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) shows PGExplainer achieves motif-edge AUC 0.873 ± 0.013 versus GNNExplainer 0.727 on MUTAG, and qualitatively identifies both NO2 groups whereas GNNExplainer includes carbon rings and only part of an NO2 group, suggesting that pathway/orbit overlap or motif AUC should complement fidelity/sparsity if gene pathways are available. A weaker contradiction is that the most direct fidelity/sparsity evidence is not node-level gene-network evidence: [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c) uses graph classification on MUTAG and Reddit-Binary and reports no statistical significance or pathway alignment, and [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) provides only metric definitions without empirical gene-network results. Additional weaker contradictions are that several real-world explanation studies use alternative metrics rather than fidelity/sparsity, such as [(He et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26647ac28bfd1bed6cf2d81d1cdd01dc1e852b69) using Essentialness Percentage and sparsity, and [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) and [(Lin et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfd1ba68e0dee2bbf86dd202e79587599939539c) using explanation accuracy and log-odds difference, which implies the proposed fidelity/sparsity protocol is not universally established. The weakest contradiction is that [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef) evaluates PPI node classification but not explanations, and its inductive multi-graph setting with unseen test graphs and no explanation metrics means it cannot validate the proposed real-world explanation evaluation, though it does not refute it.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to replace or supplement standard fidelity with robust, distribution-aware fidelity measures: adopt Fid_alpha1,+ and Fid_alpha2,- with alpha1=0.1 and alpha2=0.9 as in [(Chen et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fd8cf39a8c70c386c938535a1fe02ce19e431e2), report log-odds difference as in [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) and [(Lin et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfd1ba68e0dee2bbf86dd202e79587599939539c), and monitor distribution shift with cosine similarity and Euclidean distance between original and explanation GNN embeddings as in [(Zhang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4936c5acfffcc6368e99cd8326fc7555c76a58f0). The second most important action is to make the gene-network qualitative analysis operational: if known biological pathways are available, convert them into semi-ground-truth edge/node/orbit labels and report pathway overlap, edge-recall, sub-recall, motif/orbit AUC, and expert-rated plausibility, using the motif-AUC logic of [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246) and the expert-visualization recommendation of [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659); if pathways are unavailable, use blinded expert scoring with inter-rater agreement rather than unstructured inspection. The third action is to align the real-world protocol with node-level GNN explanation: report prediction-retention metrics such as Essentialness Percentage and sparsity as in [(He et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/26647ac28bfd1bed6cf2d81d1cdd01dc1e852b69), define sparsity both as edge ratio R as in [(Lin et al., 2022-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfd1ba68e0dee2bbf86dd202e79587599939539c) and as fraction of computation-graph edges/nodes as in [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), and evaluate top-K node/edge/orbit subgraphs on PPI and gene networks, including unseen-graph inductive settings where relevant as in [(Hamilton et al., 2017-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6b7d6e6416343b2a122f8416e69059ce919026ef); for LastFM-Asia, use social-interaction motif plausibility and robust fidelity/sparsity because biological pathway ground truth is absent. The fourth action is to add statistical rigor and reproducibility: run at least 10 random seeds as in [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ec342df97e060b527a8bc18ae4e97401f246), [(Zhang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4936c5acfffcc6368e99cd8326fc7555c76a58f0), and [(Chen et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fd8cf39a8c70c386c938535a1fe02ce19e431e2), report means, standard deviations or confidence intervals, and paired significance tests, and document hardware, target GNN architecture, optimizer, weight decay, learning rates, epochs, K/R/lambda/eta hyperparameters, and baseline settings such as GNNExplainer lr 0.01 for 100 epochs and PGExplainer lr 0.003 for 30 epochs from [(Zhang et al., 2023-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4936c5acfffcc6368e99cd8326fc7555c76a58f0). The least important but useful action is to include model-level orbit scores alongside instance-level subgraphs in the real-world validation, so that the unified framework is tested by showing whether globally selected orbits also appear in locally visualized gene-network subgraphs, and to compare against the planned baselines GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, MixupExplainer, and other orbit/motif methods under the same robust fidelity, sparsity, and pathway-overlap protocol.

## Evaluation metrics: We will assess explanation quality using four key metrics: (1) Sparsity - measuring the ratio of edges in explanations compared to total computation graph edges, (2) Fidelity - quantifying the difference in prediction probabilities when explanations are excluded, (3) Edge-recall - determining how many explanation edges match ground-truth edges, and (4) Sub-recall - measuring the proportion of entire explanations that correctly match ground truths. This provides quantitative criteria for comparing explanation compactness, predictive relevance, and correctness.

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), which uses exactly the four proposed metrics, sparsity as explanation edges over computation-graph edges, fidelity as prediction-probability change when the explanation is removed, edge-recall as overlap with ground-truth edges, and sub-recall as full explanation subgraph match, on BA-SHAPES, BA-COMMUNITY, TREE-CYCLES, TREE-GRID, MUTAG, and REDDIT-BINARY, with GRAD and ATT baselines. This directly validates the plan's synthetic motif datasets and metric suite, although the excerpt lacks numeric values. The next strongest support is [(Zheng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc92ae2ce3caf2142a92bed5957a8d5519215eb7), which tests fidelity surrogates against a gold-standard edit distance on Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, BA-2motifs, and MUTAG with GCN and GIN, reporting that Fid alpha1,+ has Spearman correlation near -1.0 with edit distance and edge-classification AUC is perfectly aligned with edit distance, while the original Fid+ is mixed; this supports using fidelity and edge correctness but also requires careful fidelity direction. A similarly strong quantitative support is [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68), which reports PGExplainer AUC of 0.963±0.011, 0.945±0.019, 0.987±0.007, 0.907±0.014, 0.926±0.021, and 0.873±0.013 on BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, BA-2motifs, and MUTAG, with 10 runs and large speedups over GNNExplainer, supporting motif-edge correctness analogous to edge-recall. [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) supports combining compactness, factual/counterfactual prediction preservation, and ground-truth Precision/Recall/F1, and shows FNS correlates with ground-truth F1 and Accuracy on BA-Shapes, Tree-Cycles, and Mutag0, with Gem exceeding CF2 Accuracy by 0.62 percentage points on BA-Shapes, which is useful for real-world ground-truth-free evaluation. [(Lucic et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11b9f4729c8e355dec7122993076f6e2788c03c4) supports sparsity, fidelity, and ground-truth accuracy on the same tree-cycles, tree-grids, and ba-shapes families, reporting CF-GNNExplainer accuracy above 94 percent on tree-cycles and tree-grids, smaller explanations, and higher fidelity than 1-hop baselines, with 3-layer GCNs, hidden size 20, at least 87 percent test accuracy, and about 375 hours on one Nvidia TitanX Pascal GPU. [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) supports edge-level correctness and stability by using top-k edge overlap AUC with k=8 on BA-2motifs, Mutagenicity, and NCI1, finding RCExplainer highest robustness AUC, GNNExplainer worst due to run-to-run instability, and reporting efficiency values such as SubgraphX 77.8s ± 4.5 per explanation. [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) supports prediction-preservation and motif recall by reporting Gem improves explanation accuracy by 30 percent over GNNExplainer on BA-shapes at K=5, maintains favorable accuracy at K=6 on Tree-cycles, and classifies a non-mutagenic Mutag explanation with probability 0.82. [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) supports the same evaluation logic by defining edge-level F1 and ROC-AUC for known motifs, Fidelity+ and fidelity heuristics, sparsity, and stability on BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, Syn-cora, and real-world molecule/sentiment graphs, though its sparsity is weight-based rather than edge-ratio. [(Hsieh et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20e0abdfed000269d3eabe1313223701030c2ed9) supports cardinality-constrained fidelity-like evaluation through Robustness-Sr and Robustness-Sr complement on MNIST, ImageNet, and Yahoo! Answers, with p=2 perturbations and 100 random examples, although it is not GNN-specific. [(Hooker et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f614777d25efd14b7426e99cb2544f2d6be133e) supports removal-based fidelity by retraining ResNet-50 after removing t fractions of pixels, generating 540 modified datasets and averaging 5 models, with squared guided backprop improving over unsquared guided backprop by 8.43% ± 0.97 at t=0.9, but it is pixel-level and expensive. [(Rathee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bff78ef88c6f1bbbf2fb77de4439f7d2168d8e9) supports sparsity and fidelity evaluation through entropy-based sparsity and rate-distortion fidelity on GCN, GAT, APPNP, and GIN with 300 CORA/CITESEER nodes, 700 PUBMED nodes, and 50 MUTAG/PROTEINS graphs, although it lacks numeric results in the excerpt. [(Hase et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a44417703e9facebf5a630069b7484664d6087) supports sufficiency/comprehensiveness perturbation evaluation with 500 test points, bootstrap confidence intervals, and p<.05 significance, showing Parallel Local Search gains up to 12.9 points over LIME on Sufficiency and 20.8 points over LIME on Comprehensiveness, although it is NLP. [(Sánchez-Lengeling et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/861cf64943e90074cd25eada6e4c3912aef17eb0) supports ground-truth subgraph attribution in molecular graphs using CAM-like node/edge attributions and Boolean subgraph queries, with 1,200 graphs per logic combination and 10 percent test, although it does not report the proposed metrics. [(Baldassarre, and Azizpour, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb202cdcfec3b0e7ba0e3f88949d6d923b48b2d) supports perturbation-based fidelity but emphasizes semantically valid graph perturbations, using a hand-crafted progressive atom-elimination example in chemistry. [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) supports ground-truth AUC-ROC and infidelity evaluation on TREE-GRID, TREE-BA, HL-MRF, and MUTAG with a 3-layer GCN black-box, finding RelEx Gumbel best on synthetic GNN datasets and similar best infidelity to GNN-Explainer on MUTAG. [(Yuan et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/123139463809b5acf98b95d4c8e958be334a32b5) supports subgraph-level evaluation under a maximum explanation-node budget on MUTAG, BBBP, Graph-SST2, BA-2Motifs, and BA-Shape with GCN, GAT, and GIN, using one Nvidia V100, M=20, lambda 5 or 10, and T=100, although the excerpt lacks numeric outcomes. [(Kakkad et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea3ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68) supports the proposed sparsity and fidelity definitions for GNNExplainer on Mutag, but its fidelity is label/prediction mismatch rather than probability difference. [(Piaggesi et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0f1584c78bfc3fbc3fe2cef59b15695b70dc0990) provides weaker support for sparsity-aware structure by showing DINE improves community-aware interpretability of embeddings on CORA, CITESEER, PUBMED, BLOG, FLICKR, and WIKI, but it does not evaluate GNN prediction explanations. [(Yu et al., 2021-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/afe13acf0a5a5c126d0394e09a5a55616d581128) provides the weakest support because it describes VGIB subgraph discovery and post-hoc GNN explanation but the excerpt reports no quantitative outcomes, baselines, or metric values.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the proposed fidelity, defined only as the difference in prediction probabilities when explanations are excluded, is underspecified and can be directionally ambiguous. [(Zheng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc92ae2ce3caf2142a92bed5957a8d5519215eb7) shows that the original Fid+ has mixed Spearman correlations with gold-standard edit distance, while a revised Fid alpha1,+ has correlation near -1.0, and that edge AUC aligns with edit distance; this implies the plan must state whether fidelity rewards a drop in the target-class probability, a rise in the explanation-subgraph probability, or a generic probability change. [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) similarly separates factual reasoning, which preserves the prediction, from counterfactual reasoning, which changes it, and reports that GNNExplainer tends to have higher Recall while CF-GNNExplainer tends to have higher Precision, so a single unsigned probability difference may conflate sufficiency and necessity. [(Lucic et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11b9f4729c8e355dec7122993076f6e2788c03c4) uses counterfactual edge removal and reports fidelity improvements over 1-hop baselines, but its objective is to change the prediction, which is not identical to the plan's likely factual explanation goal. The second major contradiction is that edge-recall and sub-recall require ground-truth edges or subgraphs, whereas the plan's real-world datasets, Protein-Protein Interaction, LastFM-Asia, and Gene networks, are stated to lack ground-truth patterns. [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) explicitly switches to heuristic fidelity for real-world molecule and sentiment graphs without ground truth, and [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) introduces Probability of Sufficiency, Probability of Necessity, and FNS for ground-truth-free settings, indicating that the four proposed metrics cannot be applied uniformly. [(Rathee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bff78ef88c6f1bbbf2fb77de4439f7d2168d8e9) and [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) also rely on rate-distortion fidelity, robustness AUC, or top-k edge overlap rather than direct ground-truth recall. The third contradiction is that sparsity as a ratio of explanation edges to computation-graph edges may be too coarse for orbit-based explanations. [(Rathee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bff78ef88c6f1bbbf2fb77de4439f7d2168d8e9) uses entropy-based sparsity over normalized soft masks, [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) describes sparsity of explanation weights, and [(Hsieh et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20e0abdfed000269d3eabe1313223701030c2ed9) uses a cardinality K constraint, so an edge ratio alone may miss feature-mask sparsity, orbit-count sparsity, or soft-score concentration. The fourth contradiction is that sub-recall as a binary proportion of entire explanations matching ground truths may be too strict and may ignore partial correctness. [(Zheng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc92ae2ce3caf2142a92bed5957a8d5519215eb7) uses edit distance as a gold standard and edge AUC, [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) uses Precision, Recall, and F1, [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68) uses motif-edge AUC, and [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a) uses AUC-ROC against known right-reason structures, all of which provide graded correctness rather than a single all-or-nothing subgraph match. The fifth contradiction is that excluding explanation edges may create semantically invalid graphs, especially in molecular or domain-constrained settings. [(Baldassarre, and Azizpour, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb202cdcfec3b0e7ba0e3f88949d6d923b48b2d) warns that changing a single node or edge can drastically alter graph meaning and that chemistry perturbations must be semantically constrained, which challenges a naive edge-exclusion fidelity metric. The sixth contradiction is that the proposed metric set omits stability or robustness, which is central in the literature. [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) reports that GNNExplainer can have robustness AUC significantly below 1 even without noise because different runs produce different explanations, and [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659) explicitly defines stability under small graph perturbations. [(Hase et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a44417703e9facebf5a630069b7484664d6087) and [(Hsieh et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20e0abdfed000269d3eabe1313223701030c2ed9) also show that perturbation-based explanation quality depends on robustness to counterfactual or adversarial changes. The seventh contradiction is that the metrics are primarily instance-level, while the plan also evaluates model-level explanations against D4Explainer and GLGExplainer. None of the cited metric definitions directly measures class-orbit recall, class-level sparsity, or model-level fidelity for orbit bases, so the current four metrics do not fully cover the unified model-level component. The eighth contradiction is computational and fairness-related: removal-and-retraining or repeated explanation generation can be expensive, as shown by [(Hooker et al., 2018-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f614777d25efd14b7426e99cb2544f2d6be133e) generating 540 modified datasets and training 5 ResNet-50 models per variant, [(Lucic et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11b9f4729c8e355dec7122993076f6e2788c03c4) requiring about 375 hours on one Nvidia TitanX Pascal GPU, and [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) reporting SubgraphX at 77.8s ± 4.5 per explanation, so the plan's multiple runs and significance testing need explicit budgets. The weakest contradiction is that no related work directly validates orbit- or graphlet-based explanations with these exact metrics, so the plan's central orbit interpretation remains an extension rather than an established evaluation setting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to formalize fidelity as a signed, target-class-specific probability or log-odds change, reporting both factual and counterfactual variants. Following [(Zheng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc92ae2ce3caf2142a92bed5957a8d5519215eb7), the plan should validate the chosen fidelity against gold-standard edit distance or edge AUC on Tree-Cycles, Tree-Grid, BA-2motifs, and MUTAG, and following [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) it should report Probability of Sufficiency, Probability of Necessity, and FNS for real-world datasets. The second action is to add ground-truth-free metrics for Protein-Protein Interaction, LastFM-Asia, and Gene networks, including robustness AUC or top-k edge-overlap stability from [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747), rate-distortion fidelity or comprehensiveness/sufficiency from [(Rathee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bff78ef88c6f1bbbf2fb77de4439f7d2168d8e9), and sufficiency/comprehensiveness with bootstrap confidence intervals from [(Hase et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e8a44417703e9facebf5a630069b7484664d6087). The third action is to augment sparsity with absolute explanation edge count, orbit count, feature-mask entropy, and top-k concentration, as motivated by [(Rathee et al., 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0bff78ef88c6f1bbbf2fb77de4439f7d2168d8e9), [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659), and [(Hsieh et al., 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/20e0abdfed000269d3eabe1313223701030c2ed9), and to report sparsity curves against explanation size K rather than a single ratio. The fourth action is to replace or supplement binary sub-recall with graded correctness metrics such as edge F1, edge AUC, edit distance, subgraph F1, and orbit-level precision/recall, using the synthetic ground-truth motifs in BA-Shapes, BA-Community, Tree-Cycle, and Tree-Grid as in [(Ying et al., 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1510fe9ba6e9e576e46688b5c9f0f3950257175c), [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68), and [(Zheng et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cc92ae2ce3caf2142a92bed5957a8d5519215eb7). The fifth action is to define semantically valid perturbations for fidelity, using zero-padding, feature masking, or domain-constrained edge edits rather than arbitrary edge deletion, especially for molecular or gene-network graphs, as advised by [(Baldassarre, and Azizpour, 2019-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8fb202cdcfec3b0e7ba0e3f88949d6d923b48b2d). The sixth action is to add stability or robustness evaluation by perturbing node features, edges, or graph structure, regenerating explanations, and measuring edge-overlap AUC, Jaccard similarity, or prediction stability, following [(Bajaj et al., 2021-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f7231aee1e18428d6c0b314b5e1e65d6707e8747) and [(Dai et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cfb35f8c18fbc5baa453280ecd0aa8148bbba659). The seventh action is to create model-level metrics for the unified framework, such as class-orbit recall, class-orbit precision/F1, class-level sparsity, and class-level fidelity, so that comparisons with D4Explainer and GLGExplainer are not limited to instance-level subgraph metrics. The eighth action is to standardize fair baselines and reproducibility by fixing GNN architectures, for example 3-layer GCN or GIN with hidden sizes comparable to 16, 20, or 32 used in [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b) and [(Lucic et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/11b9f4729c8e355dec7122993076f6e2788c03c4), fixing maximum explanation size K to motif size on synthetic datasets and to a graph-size-based value on real-world datasets as in [(Lin et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2a5a8db41940990dc8fe8e7717ed85ba043204e1) and [(Tan et al., 2022-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4f0925684db82985f9c48566065d4ead5e00a16b), reporting mean and standard deviation over at least 10 runs as in [(Luo et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d9f5ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68), and including hardware, runtime, and significance tests. The least important but still useful action is to add qualitative domain validation, such as visualizing orbit subgraphs and checking alignment with known biological pathways or chemical groups, as supported by [(Sánchez-Lengeling et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/861cf64943e90074cd25eada6e4c3912aef17eb0), [(Zhang et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7fd293b1984cac5900434ea796bbe74e05e6623a), and [(Kakkad et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ea3ecb8b809e7d5ae1bbc267863c0c4e72401a68).

## Technical implementation: We will implement our approach using PyTorch and PyTorch Geometric frameworks, with careful hyperparameter tuning for both orbit basis learning and class-orbit score learning phases. We will ensure fair comparison by extensively exploring hyperparameter spaces for all baseline methods and conducting multiple experimental runs to report statistical significance. This supports reproducible implementation and fair empirical comparison.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed technical implementation comes from [(Fey, and Lenssen, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a513832f56addb67be81a2fa399b233f3030fc), which directly validates the plan's emphasis on fair empirical comparison, repeated runs, and reproducible hyperparameter choices in GNN node classification. That work evaluates APPNP, ARMA, SGC, GCN, and GAT under homogeneous settings, reports average accuracies over 100 runs, and compares a fixed train/validation/test split with randomly initialized splits having uniform class distribution in the training split; it also notes high reproducibility relative to source papers and that APPNP generally performs best while ARMA, SGC, GCN, and GAT follow closely. As a newer empirical benchmark, it extends the older GCN protocol of [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478) by showing that split construction and repeated runs materially affect reported performance, which is directly relevant to the proposed fair-comparison requirement, although it does not evaluate orbit-based explanations. A second, more indirect support is [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478), which provides a concrete GCN baseline protocol relevant to the plan's synthetic pre-training of GCN and GIN models: a two-layer GCN with sparse-dense matrix multiplications, O(|E| H F) evaluation complexity, Adam learning rate 0.01, maximum 200 epochs, early stopping after 10 epochs without validation-loss improvement, Glorot initialization, row-normalized input features, and hyperparameter tuning of dropout, first-layer L2 regularization, and hidden units on a 500-example validation set before transfer to Citeseer and Pubmed. It also uses 100 repeated random weight initializations for propagation-model comparisons and GPU/CPU runtime measurements, which aligns with the proposed need for repeated runs and runtime-aware implementation, even though the excerpt omits numerical accuracies, significance values, and wall-clock times. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1), because the proposed UO-Explainer will be built in PyTorch and PyTorch Geometric, and that paper documents PyTorch's eager execution, asynchronous CUDA-stream GPU execution, shared-memory multiprocessing, and built-in profiler for timing a single training step on two Intel Xeon E5-2698 v4 CPUs and one NVIDIA Quadro GP100 GPU; this supports the feasibility of implementing orbit-basis classifiers, class-orbit score optimization, and baseline comparisons in PyTorch, but it provides no GNN explainability, graphlet/orbit, or explanation-quality evidence.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct empirical refutation but a substantial evidentiary gap: none of the related works provides quantitative evidence that orbit/graphlet-based decomposition improves the plan's explanation metrics—sparsity, fidelity, edge-recall, or sub-recall—over D4Explainer, GLGExplainer, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, or MotifExplainer. [(Fey, and Lenssen, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a513832f56addb67be81a2fa399b233f3030fc) only benchmarks GNN operators and reports average accuracies over 100 runs without confidence intervals or formal significance tests, so it cannot support the proposed claim that multiple runs and fair hyperparameter exploration will establish statistically significant explanation-quality advantages. [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478) similarly describes a GCN baseline and 100 repeated random initializations, but the supplied excerpt contains no numerical outcomes, baseline comparisons, statistical significance values, or wall-clock times, and it uses TensorFlow rather than the proposed PyTorch/PyTorch Geometric stack; therefore it supports experimental hygiene but does not validate orbit basis learning, class-orbit score learning, or explanation metrics. A further tension is that [(Fey, and Lenssen, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a513832f56addb67be81a2fa399b233f3030fc) finds test performance worse for all models under random data splits than under the fixed split, which contradicts any assumption that repeated runs alone are sufficient for fair comparison unless the proposed protocol explicitly controls split construction, class balance, and seed variation. The weakest contradiction is that [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1) reports only qualitative framework performance and profiling details, with no graph datasets, GNN architectures, hyperparameters, or explanation metrics, so it cannot substantiate the proposed method's core scientific contribution or its statistical reporting requirements.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a direct, quantitative evaluation of the orbit-based explanation mechanism itself, because the related work supports only the surrounding GNN benchmarking and PyTorch implementation, not the proposed interpretability contribution. The plan should report sparsity, fidelity, edge-recall, and sub-recall for UO-Explainer against D4Explainer, GLGExplainer, GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, TAGE, MixupExplainer, SAME, EIG, and MotifExplainer on the five synthetic datasets and three real-world datasets, with ground-truth orbit patterns used for edge-recall and sub-recall on synthetic data and fidelity/sparsity on real-world data. The second most important action is to adopt the repeated-run and split-sensitivity protocol evidenced by [(Fey, and Lenssen, 2019-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/63a513832f56addb67be81a2fa399b233f3030fc): use at least 100 runs where feasible, report mean and standard deviation or confidence intervals, specify fixed versus random train/validation/test splits, control uniform class distribution in training splits, and perform formal statistical tests such as paired t-tests, Wilcoxon signed-rank tests, or bootstrap confidence intervals for fidelity, sparsity, edge-recall, and sub-recall. The third action is to make the hyperparameter tuning protocol as explicit as the GCN baseline in [(Kipf, and Welling, 2016-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/36eff562f65125511b5dfab68ce7f7a943c27478), including search spaces and selection criteria for orbit basis learning and class-orbit score learning, such as number of hidden units, dropout, L2 regularization, learning rate, maximum epochs, early-stopping patience, initialization, and feature normalization, with validation-set-based selection and transfer rules across datasets. The least important but still useful action is to use the PyTorch profiling and hardware-reporting practice suggested by [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1) to document runtime, GPU utilization, memory usage, and reproducibility of the PyTorch/PyTorch Geometric implementation, including hardware specifications, CUDA streams, random seeds, library versions, and whether orbit-basis preprocessing or BFS subgraph matching dominates wall-clock time.
