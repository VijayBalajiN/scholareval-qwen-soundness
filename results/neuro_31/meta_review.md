## Context: Overarching experimental framework for comparing temporal context across scales. Method: We will design a novel paradigm to investigate how temporal context shapes perception across micro, meso, and macro scales within a single experimental framework.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method is that [(Uscumlic, and Blankertz, 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/992d43aff1aa73adc10255dcbc63e3c994300088) provides a published simultaneous EEG and eye-tracking target-monitoring dataset in which participants silently counted targets among serially presented non-targets, with no motor responses during the critical viewing periods, directly matching the plan's stated need to re-analyze a previously published EEG dataset while avoiding motor confounds. It also demonstrates that linear discriminant analysis is viable for temporal decoding of fixation-related EEG in a serial visual monitoring task: shrinkage LDA and hierarchical discriminant component analysis classified target versus non-target single trials above chance in all three appearance protocols, using non-overlapping 100-ms windows from 100 to 800 ms after fixation onset, ten-fold cross-validation, AUC, and label-permutation chance estimates. This supports the plan's use of multivariate LDA to examine expected versus unexpected visual events and to compare decoding accuracy over time. A weaker but still relevant support is that the study's inter-protocol transfer from the most discriminant pop-up interval, 200 to 600 ms, to smooth and motion appearance conditions with 50-ms sliding shifts and maximum posterior probability shows that temporal decoding windows can be shifted and transferred across stimulus protocols, which is conceptually useful for the plan's micro, meso, and macro temporal-context comparisons, especially because the transferred classifier revealed delayed discriminant activity peaking at 100 to 150 ms in smooth and motion conditions. The study also provides concrete implementation details that can inform the plan's reproducibility: 16 participants, 63 active EEG electrodes at 1000 Hz, 250-Hz eye tracking, a 100-pixel fixation region of interest corresponding to about 2.5 degrees of visual angle, fixation detection at 40 deg/s and 50 ms minimum duration, target probability of 25%, and random inter-stimulus intervals of 1 to 1.5 s in the pop-up condition. Finally, the exclusion of very frontal channels without a significant change at the 1% level suggests that eye-movement-related EEG activity may be controlled without eliminating all frontal signal, which is useful for a plan that combines EEG with pupillometry and expects oculomotor or attentional activity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Uscumlic, and Blankertz, 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/992d43aff1aa73adc10255dcbc63e3c994300088) does not implement the proposed core manipulations: it does not test micro-scale repeat versus alternate effects, meso-scale five-event sequences, macro-scale probabilistic biases, task-related versus task-unrelated spatial planes, inverted encoding modeling, or behavioral precision/recall. Its target versus non-target classification is a binary detection task in active visual search, not a serial-dependence or expectation task with Gaussian blobs, speeded binary judgments, and left/right versus above/below reference planes, so it cannot by itself support the plan's central claim that a common mechanism operates across micro, meso, and macro temporal contexts. A second important contradiction is that the dataset's temporal structure is not aligned with the plan's proposed serial sequences: the pop-up condition used random inter-stimulus intervals of 1 to 1.5 s, while smooth and motion conditions used fading, enlarging, and motion effects, so any re-analysis would estimate target/non-target discriminability under appearance protocols rather than under controlled repeat/alternate or 75/25 probability manipulations, including the plan's Experiment 2 macro biases and Experiment 3 fixed 200-ms duration. This limits the value of using it as the primary EEG evidence for the proposed framework. A third contradiction is that the study's sliding-window intra-protocol control improved performance only slightly, whereas inter-protocol transfer from pop-up improved smooth and motion classification, indicating that apparent temporal decoding gains can come from transfer of a fixed discriminant window rather than from scale-specific temporal context; the plan must therefore include controls that separate temporal alignment, transfer, and genuine context effects. A fourth contradiction is that behavioral and ocular expected/unexpected effects were weak or heterogeneous: there was no target versus non-target difference in gaze response time in any condition, and target versus non-target differences in first fixation duration were significant for only 4 of 16 subjects in pop-up, 5 of 16 in smooth, and 9 of 16 in motion, while dwell-time differences were significant for 5, 6, and 15 of 16 subjects respectively. This suggests that the plan's pupillometry and behavioral precision measures may require larger samples and more sensitive metrics than the related work's sample of 16 participants. Finally, the related work reports only that AUC was above chance without providing specific AUC values, and it used Wilcoxon signed-rank or rank-sum tests with Bonferroni correction rather than the plan's cluster-corrected temporal statistics, so it provides limited quantitative benchmarking for the plan's planned statistical inference.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to treat [(Uscumlic, and Blankertz, 2016-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/992d43aff1aa73adc10255dcbc63e3c994300088) as a feasibility benchmark rather than as the primary dataset for the proposed micro, meso, and macro comparison, and to collect new EEG and pupillometry data with explicit repeat/alternate micro manipulations, five-event meso sequences, 75/25 macro probability biases, and task-related versus task-unrelated planes, because the related work lacks these manipulations. The second most important action is to adopt the related work's LDA/HDCA implementation as a baseline pipeline: use shrinkage LDA, 100-ms non-overlapping windows from 100 to 800 ms, ten-fold cross-validation, AUC with label-permutation chance, and sliding-window analyses with 50-ms shifts and maximum posterior probability, while adding intra-protocol sliding controls to ensure that temporal decoding gains are not merely transfer artifacts; because smooth and motion appearance produced delayed discriminant peaks, the plan should retain Experiment 3's fixed 200-ms condition and also test variable onset/offset to separate temporal-context effects from stimulus-appearance timing. The third action is to integrate inverted encoding modeling with the LDA decoding plan, but validate IEM precision separately from classification AUC, because the related work only demonstrates target/non-target classification and does not show that feature reconstruction can dissociate expected from unexpected events. The fourth action is to strengthen behavioral and oculomotor controls by pre-registering effect sizes, increasing sample size beyond 16 participants, and measuring fixation duration, dwell time, gaze response time, and pupil dynamics, since the related work found no target/non-target gaze response difference and heterogeneous fixation effects. The fifth action is to match or exceed the related work's recording and analysis constraints for reproducibility: use at least 63-channel EEG at 1000 Hz or justify lower density, record eye movements at 250 Hz or higher, define fixation regions and thresholds explicitly, report exact AUC and decoding precision values, and use cluster-corrected permutation statistics for temporal EEG effects rather than only Bonferroni-corrected window comparisons.

## Context: Attentional dissociation between task-relevant and task-irrelevant history. Method: To assess the influence of attention, we will sort trials according to two spatial reference planes: task-related (corresponding to participants' binary judgment, e.g., left/right of fixation) and task-unrelated (orthogonal to the task-related plane, e.g., above/below fixation).

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed task-related left/right plane comes from [(Ling, and Carrasco, 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab8954503b8eb0f7ea0f6368902aaa6bb76df1a5), which used a spatial cueing paradigm with Gabor patches at two peripheral locations around fixation and a binary left/right two-alternative forced-choice judgment about which patch appeared brighter or higher in contrast. Cued patches were judged brighter or higher contrast significantly above the 50 percent chance baseline, showing that a task-defined left/right spatial reference can index attention and that attention changes perceptual judgments in a measurable forced-choice metric. This directly supports the research plan's use of a task-related plane corresponding to participants' binary left/right judgments, because it demonstrates that left/right forced-choice accuracy can reveal attention-induced appearance changes rather than only response bias. A weaker but still relevant support is the methodological control in that study: observers fixated, a brief peripheral cue directed transient covert attention, stimuli were presented on a calibrated display under controlled luminance conditions, and cue validity and response bias were controlled. This supports the plan's need for controlled fixation, calibrated stimuli, and chance-level behavioral baselines when sorting trials by spatial reference planes. The weakest support is that the study used peripheral spatial locations and a left/right task axis, which is compatible with the plan's central fixation and randomly positioned Gaussian blobs, but it did not test an orthogonal above/below plane or temporal-history sorting, so its support for the full task-related/task-unrelated dissociation is indirect.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is not a direct failure but a gap: [(Ling, and Carrasco, 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab8954503b8eb0f7ea0f6368902aaa6bb76df1a5) only manipulated and measured the task-relevant left/right cued versus uncued dimension, and therefore provides no evidence that an orthogonal task-unrelated above/below plane can be independently sorted or that history effects will differ across the two planes. If the proposed method assumes that attention or temporal context will produce dissociable effects on both task-related and task-unrelated planes, this related work cannot validate that assumption and may even suggest that attentional modulation is strongest along the task-relevant axis. A second contradiction is that the study used a brief spatial cue to direct transient covert attention, not serial temporal history, repeat/alternate sequences, or probabilistic macro biases. Thus it does not support the plan's core temporal-context sorting across micro, meso, and macro scales, and it leaves open whether cue-induced attention and history-driven attention engage the same spatial-plane dissociation. A weaker contradiction is that the stimuli and measurements were limited to Gabor contrast/brightness judgments in a small group of human observers, with no EEG, pupillometry, neural decoding, or motor-confound analysis, so it cannot support the plan's neuroimaging and physiological components or its use of Gaussian blob position as the primary stimulus.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add a direct validation of the task-unrelated above/below plane, for example by including an orthogonal cueing or history condition that explicitly manipulates above/below location and measuring whether above/below judgments or neural decoding show effects independent of left/right task performance. This would address the main gap in [(Ling, and Carrasco, 2007-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ab8954503b8eb0f7ea0f6368902aaa6bb76df1a5) and make the proposed dissociation testable. The next most important action is to adopt the behavioral controls and statistics implied by that study: use calibrated displays, controlled luminance, fixation, cue validity or history validity checks, and test forced-choice accuracy against 50 percent chance with binomial or mixed-effects models, reporting effect sizes and confidence intervals for both task-related and task-unrelated planes. A further improvement is to align the proposed Gaussian blob paradigm with the validated attentional appearance judgment by including a contrast/brightness or position discrimination task that can reveal attention-induced changes, rather than relying only on speeded binary judgments. Finally, the plan should add a transient cueing benchmark or pilot condition to compare cue-induced attention with temporal-history effects, and it should pre-register sample size, spatial planes, and chance-level baselines to improve reproducibility.

## Context: Experiment 2 tests macro-scale expectation through probabilistic structure. Method: We will manipulate the probability of repeat versus alternate stimuli (75% vs 25%) along task-related and task-unrelated reference planes, counterbalancing conditions across participants to isolate expectation effects from stimulus-specific differences.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Experiment 2 comes from [(Utzerath et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96aa132ec736d2e1fd1c8180499918ed4744a085), which used an fMRI oddball paradigm with expected and repeated visual conditions and reported expectation effects in V1 and repetition effects in LOC. This is directly relevant to the proposed method because both approaches use probabilistic visual sequences to separate expectation from stimulus-specific repetition, and the prior work shows that oddball-style visual manipulation can produce dissociable expectation and repetition signatures in visual cortex. A weaker but still relevant support is the use of a category discrimination task with visual stimuli, which is conceptually compatible with the proposed speeded binary judgments on Gaussian blobs, although the prior task did not use the proposed task-related and task-unrelated reference planes. The weakest support is the inclusion of AQ as a moderator, which demonstrates that individual-difference variables can be tested in expectation and repetition paradigms, but the null AQ result does not directly support the proposed counterbalancing or the specific 75/25 macro-scale manipulation.

### Contradictions

The strongest apparent contradiction is that [(Utzerath et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96aa132ec736d2e1fd1c8180499918ed4744a085) did not implement the proposed 75/25 probabilistic repeat/alternate manipulation or the task-related/task-unrelated reference-plane counterbalancing, so it cannot validate the specific design choice to isolate expectation from stimulus-specific differences. A second, more indirect contradiction is that the prior work reported expectation and repetition effects in different regions, expectation in V1 and repetition in LOC, which may challenge the proposed assumption that a single dampening or sharpening mechanism will appear uniformly across task-related and task-unrelated history effects; however, this is not a direct contradiction because the proposed method uses EEG, pupillometry, and behavioral precision rather than fMRI ROIs. The weakest contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes, effect sizes, sample size, scanner parameters, trial probabilities, and statistical thresholds in the provided excerpt, which limits the strength of any support for the proposed method and makes it difficult to benchmark expected effect sizes for the 75/25 manipulation.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make Experiment 2 explicitly oddball-like and counterbalanced: pre-register the 75/25 repeat/alternate probability manipulation on both task-related and task-unrelated planes, randomize or counterbalance which plane receives the 75% repeat bias across participants, and include an equal-probability control condition to separate expectation from simple stimulus-specific repetition. The next priority is to add quantitative power and effect-size planning, because [(Utzerath et al., 2017-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/96aa132ec736d2e1fd1c8180499918ed4744a085) provides only qualitative region-level findings and no numerical outcomes; a pilot or simulation should estimate the expected behavioral RT/accuracy and neural decoding differences for expected versus unexpected trials. A further improvement is to align the neural analysis with the prior dissociation by testing both early visual and higher visual signals, for example by using EEG source estimates, early visual components, or decoding in V1/LOC-analogous regions, so that expectation and repetition can be dissociated rather than collapsed into a single temporal-context index. Finally, if individual differences are of interest, include a pre-registered AQ or attentional-control measure, but treat it as exploratory given the null AQ moderation reported in the related work.

## Context: Behavioral outcome measures across all experiments and reference planes. Method: For each experiment, we will measure response time, accuracy, and recall precision across the different reference planes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed measurement of response time and accuracy comes from [(Nunez et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a01d3f2c26fa33dd9aed7c1bb8380551040a5e), which used a speeded two-alternative forced-choice visual orientation task and recorded response time and accuracy across 540 trials per participant, with 90 trials per condition, 750 ms fixation, 750 ms noise-only interval, a 1000 to 2000 ms response interval, 250 ms post-response fixation, no-answer trials scored as incorrect, and three bar-rotation variance by three contrast-noise difficulty levels. This directly supports the feasibility of the proposed speeded binary judgment measures and provides a concrete template for trial timing, difficulty manipulation, and scoring. The next strongest support for the reference-plane component comes from [(Luo et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d335b3258beae522989cc6e964f779ad92d780d8), which analyzed behavioral serial dependence in 2-D continuous spatial perception under task-relevance conditions using multiple linear regression in an EEG experiment and linear mixed models in a MEG experiment. Its report that task relevance modulated past-present interactions supports sorting trials into task-related and task-unrelated reference planes and analyzing behavioral outcomes separately for each plane. Weaker but still relevant support is the use in [(Nunez et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a01d3f2c26fa33dd9aed7c1bb8380551040a5e) of 128-channel EEG, phase locking index, principal component analysis, and a hierarchical Bayesian diffusion model to link response time and accuracy to attentional neural measures, which supports the broader plan to combine behavioral and neural measures, although it does not address recall precision or reference-plane sorting.

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the feasibility of measuring response time, accuracy, and recall precision, but the strongest conceptual contradiction comes from [(Luo et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d335b3258beae522989cc6e964f779ad92d780d8), which reported that past choice, rather than past stimulus, mediated attractive serial bias. If the proposed method attributes behavioral serial dependence primarily to past sensory experiences while manipulating stimulus history, it may overlook a major non-sensory source of temporal context, especially in speeded binary judgments where choice history can be confounded with stimulus history. The next strongest contradiction is the same study's report of a two-stage repulsive-followed-by-attractive past-present interaction and task-relevance modulation, which contradicts a simple monotonic serial-dependence analysis based only on angular distance from previous stimuli; the proposed micro-scale repeat/alternate and continuous angular-distance analyses may miss non-linear, stage-specific effects unless modeled explicitly. Weaker limitations that reduce support rather than directly contradict include [(Nunez et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a01d3f2c26fa33dd9aed7c1bb8380551040a5e), which did not measure recall precision or sort trials by task-related versus task-unrelated reference planes, and reported only model convergence with R <= 1.01 without quantitative behavioral outcomes, participant count, or statistical significance values, so it cannot validate the proposed recall-precision metric or reference-plane decomposition.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to make choice history and reference-plane task relevance explicit factors in the behavioral analysis, following [(Luo et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d335b3258beae522989cc6e964f779ad92d780d8), by using linear mixed models for response time, accuracy, and recall precision with fixed effects for task-related versus task-unrelated plane, repeat versus alternate, probability bias, angular distance, and past choice, and random intercepts and slopes for participants and trials. This should supplement or replace the plan's non-parametric behavioral tests because mixed models can handle repeated observations and participant-level variability. The next most important action is to validate the recall-precision component, because neither related work measures it; the 10 percent reproduction trials should be piloted to ensure they do not inflate response time or reduce accuracy, and precision should be quantified with circular angular error or an equivalent metric and analyzed with the same mixed-model framework. A further improvement is to adopt the speeded binary task and scoring details from [(Nunez et al., 2015-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83a01d3f2c26fa33dd9aed7c1bb8380551040a5e), including no-answer trials scored as incorrect, controlled trial timing, and difficulty manipulation, and to consider a diffusion model of response time and accuracy per reference plane, but with boundary separation and bias estimated rather than fixed if the goal is to test reference-plane effects. Finally, the plan should pre-register quantitative reporting standards, including effect sizes, confidence intervals, statistical significance, and model convergence diagnostics, and should model two-stage repulsive-then-attractive serial dependence when analyzing micro- and meso-scale history effects.

## Context: Neural data source chosen to avoid motor confounds during critical periods. Method: For neural analysis, we plan to re-analyze a previously published EEG dataset where participants viewed visual stimuli while monitoring for targets, allowing us to examine neural correlates without motor confounds during the critical analysis periods.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed neural re-analysis method comes from the most recent related work, which directly combines EEG, visual target monitoring, and LDA-based decoding. [(Turner et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/589924ad8aba348e89ed30888c8b4c24393d83ad) used a visual target-monitoring task in which participants viewed moving visual stimuli while monitoring for targets, and applied LDA-based mapping together with SVR-based analyses to extract stimulus-position information. This is the closest methodological match to the proposed plan, which explicitly plans to re-analyze a published EEG target-monitoring dataset using multivariate linear discriminant analysis to classify stimulus locations and inverted encoding modeling to reconstruct stimulus features over time. The Turner et al. study therefore supports the feasibility of using LDA in a target-monitoring EEG context and suggests that position-related representations can be tracked across processing stages, which is relevant to the plan’s goal of examining temporal context effects on visual feature representations. A second, slightly weaker but still important source of support is the use of inverted encoding models and cluster-corrected temporal statistics in a visual EEG paradigm. [(Yashiro et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b421d75655192e7ecc68ba4812bd186411e8aad) used EEG to decode visual orientation representations from Gabor stimuli, applied ICA-based cleaning, linear classification, and inverted encoding models estimating six channel responses to 180 orientations from -89 degrees to 90 degrees at each time point. The study used 11 adults, 32 electrodes, 1000 Hz acquisition downsampled to 500 Hz, 1-80 Hz band-pass filtering, epochs from -1000 ms to +1500 ms, baseline correction from -300 ms to 0 ms, and artifact component removal when estimated artifact probability exceeded 50 percent. It also used cluster-based permutation tests with cluster-defining threshold p < 0.05 and cluster threshold p < 0.05, and found significant decoded orientation windows around 568-668 ms and 640-766 ms depending on stimulus-set composition. This supports the proposed plan’s use of IEM to reconstruct visual features over time and its use of cluster-corrected statistics to identify significant temporal windows, especially because the proposed plan also involves visual stimuli, temporal decoding, and precision-related measures. The weakest but still relevant support comes from earlier EEG multivariate pattern analysis work showing that stimulus-related neural representations can be decoded and that cross-temporal and univariate controls are useful. [(Wolff et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a682d80d4c31d67f44f97d5f18a8230dfb200cbb) applied EEG multivariate pattern analysis to memory-item and impulse representations, used cross-temporal decoding to test whether representations were stable or dynamic, and used univariate mean-amplitude analyses to test whether decoding was driven by simple amplitude differences rather than distributed patterns. Although this study did not use a target-monitoring dataset and did not report classifier type, regularization, numerical accuracies, or statistical thresholds, it supports the broader methodological logic of the proposed plan: EEG decoding can reveal dynamic stimulus-related representations, and multivariate decoding should be interpreted alongside univariate controls. Taken together, these works form an iterative methodological progression: Wolff et al. established the value of EEG MVA and cross-temporal/univariate controls, Yashiro et al. extended this to visual feature reconstruction with IEM and cluster-based inference, and Turner et al. brought LDA-based decoding into a target-monitoring EEG setting, which is the most direct precedent for the proposed re-analysis.

### Contradictions

The most important contradiction is that the proposed method’s central assumption that re-analyzing a published EEG target-monitoring dataset will allow examination of neural correlates without motor confounds during the critical analysis periods is not sufficiently supported by the related work. Target-monitoring paradigms typically involve button presses or other responses, and if the proposed analysis windows extend into response-locked or late post-stimulus periods, motor preparation, motor execution, and post-response processes may contaminate the neural signal. [(Turner et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/589924ad8aba348e89ed30888c8b4c24393d83ad) is directly relevant because it uses a visual target-monitoring task, but the available excerpt does not specify whether the analysis avoided motor-response epochs, what response types were used, or how motor-related activity was controlled. Therefore, it supports the use of LDA in target monitoring but does not validate the claim that motor confounds are absent during the critical periods. A second contradiction is that the closest methodological precedent for IEM and cluster-corrected temporal decoding in visual EEG, [(Yashiro et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b421d75655192e7ecc68ba4812bd186411e8aad), was not a re-analysis of a published target-monitoring dataset and explicitly included motor responses, including button judgments and continuous bar-adjustment reports. Its sample was also small, with 11 adults, and its retained trial counts were 922 plus or minus 136 in Experiment 1 and 444 plus or minus 150 in Experiment 2A. This means that while the study supports IEM and cluster permutation testing, it does not demonstrate that the same pipeline will work in a previously published target-monitoring dataset with the trial counts, condition labels, and motor-free windows required by the proposed plan. A third contradiction is the lack of quantitative validation for the proposed LDA/IEM re-analysis in the most directly relevant target-monitoring study. [(Turner et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/589924ad8aba348e89ed30888c8b4c24393d83ad) reports LDA-based mapping, SVR-based analyses, and an STDP-based hierarchical network, but the available excerpt does not provide decoding accuracies, latency values, effect sizes, statistical significance, participant count, electrode configuration, stimulus parameters, trial counts, or correction details. This limits the strength of the evidence that LDA-based location decoding and feature reconstruction will yield reliable temporal-context effects in the proposed re-analysis. A fourth, weaker contradiction is that the earlier MVA evidence from [(Wolff et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a682d80d4c31d67f44f97d5f18a8230dfb200cbb) is also under-specified: it does not provide classifier type, regularization scheme, numerical decoding accuracies, statistical test values, effect sizes, participant count, electrode montage, sampling rate, or cluster-correction parameters. Thus, while it supports the general use of EEG decoding and univariate controls, it does not provide strong quantitative evidence that the proposed LDA/IEM pipeline will be reproducible or sensitive enough for the planned comparisons between repeat and alternate stimuli, sequence types, and task-related versus task-unrelated reference planes.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to explicitly define the motor-confound-free analysis windows and verify that the published target-monitoring dataset contains sufficient trial counts for the planned temporal-context comparisons. The plan should specify the exact dataset, participant count, electrode montage, sampling rate, preprocessing steps, artifact rejection criteria, and available trial labels for target/non-target, repeat/alternate, sequence type, and spatial position. If the dataset includes button presses, the analysis should either restrict the critical EEG windows to pre-response periods, exclude response-locked epochs, or include a sensitivity analysis comparing pre-response and post-response windows to test whether motor-related activity changes the results. This is essential because the current method claims to avoid motor confounds, but the related work does not demonstrate that target-monitoring EEG analyses can be interpreted as motor-free without explicit temporal exclusion or response-locked controls. The second most important action is to make the LDA and IEM pipeline fully reproducible and statistically rigorous. The plan should specify whether LDA will be regularized, how cross-validation will be nested, what class imbalance handling will be used, what spatial or temporal smoothing will be applied, and what permutation or cluster-correction procedure will be used. Given that [(Yashiro et al., 2023-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b421d75655192e7ecc68ba4812bd186411e8aad) used cluster-based permutation tests with cluster-defining threshold p < 0.05 and cluster threshold p < 0.05, the proposed plan should report analogous thresholds, the number of permutations, the cluster definition, and whether spatial, temporal, or spatiotemporal clustering will be used. For IEM, the plan should specify how many position or orientation channels will be used, how channel responses will be estimated, and how decoding precision will be quantified, because the research plan explicitly wants to compare decoding accuracy and precision for expected versus unexpected events. The third important action is to add the controls that the related work suggests are necessary for interpreting multivariate decoding. Following [(Wolff et al., 2015-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a682d80d4c31d67f44f97d5f18a8230dfb200cbb), the plan should include univariate mean-amplitude or power analyses to test whether multivariate decoding effects are driven by simple amplitude differences. It should also include cross-temporal decoding to determine whether temporal-context-related representations are stable across time or dynamically changing, which is directly relevant to the plan’s goal of examining temporal context across micro, meso, and macro scales. Finally, the plan should consider complementary modeling approaches such as SVR or hierarchical network fitting, as used in [(Turner et al., 2025-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/589924ad8aba348e89ed30888c8b4c24393d83ad), if the goal is to characterize how position representations emerge across processing stages. At minimum, however, the method should report all implementation details, hyperparameters, computational resources, and statistical thresholds needed to reproduce the LDA/IEM re-analysis and to distinguish genuine temporal-context effects from motor, attentional, or amplitude-driven artifacts.

## Context: Multivariate neural analysis of stimulus representation over time. Method: EEG Analysis will involve re-analyzing existing data using multivariate linear discriminant analysis to classify stimulus locations and inverted encoding modeling to reconstruct stimulus features over time.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed EEG analysis is that [(Oosterhof et al., 2016-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/114e76b6a37dc9718e7651e72c1c62dfb811a0d6) demonstrates a time-locked M/EEG searchlight framework for multivariate neural effects in channel-by-time space, which is directly compatible with the plan's goal of examining stimulus-location representations over time in EEG. The method treats each trial as an independent chunk, reassigns samples into two halves for split-half correlation, and forms a neighborhood by crossing 10 sensor locations with 5 consecutive time bins, providing a concrete spatial-temporal inference template that could be adapted to LDA or IEM decoding windows. A weaker but still relevant support is the paper's emphasis on cluster-based multiple-comparison correction for sensor-time maps, which aligns with the plan's use of cluster-corrected statistical analyses to identify significant time periods. The weakest support is that the paper's split-half correlation measure is a reliability-oriented multivariate index rather than a discriminative classifier or generative reconstruction model, so it only indirectly supports the plan's LDA classification and IEM reconstruction of stimulus features.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not evaluate the proposed analytic core: it uses split-half correlation, not multivariate linear discriminant analysis for stimulus-location classification, and it does not use inverted encoding modeling to reconstruct stimulus features over time. Therefore it provides no evidence that LDA or IEM will reveal repeat versus alternate, task-related versus task-unrelated, or micro/meso/macro temporal-context effects in the planned EEG re-analysis. A second contradiction is that the example is MEG, not EEG, and uses Neuromag306 gradiometer data from 145 stimulation and 145 pre-stimulation epochs, so its spatial resolution, noise structure, and sensor neighborhood assumptions may not transfer directly to the planned EEG dataset. A third contradiction is the absence of quantitative outcomes: no classification accuracy, correlation magnitude, effect size, corrected threshold, baseline comparison, or statistical significance is reported, limiting the ability to benchmark the proposed LDA/IEM pipeline. Finally, the paper recommends threshold-free cluster enhancement over fixed-threshold cluster correction, which contradicts a generic plan to use cluster-corrected analyses if the implementation relies on a fixed initial threshold rather than TFCE or permutation-based cluster inference.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to operationalize the EEG analysis as a searchlight LDA/IEM pipeline with explicit spatial-temporal neighborhoods, using the Oosterhof et al. 10-sensor by 5-time-bin neighborhood as a starting point but recalibrating it to the EEG channel layout, sampling rate, and stimulus epoch length. The next priority is to replace or supplement generic cluster-corrected statistics with threshold-free cluster enhancement or permutation-based cluster correction, pre-registering the neighborhood, baseline period, and correction method to avoid inflated false positives. The method should also include split-half or cross-validation reliability checks, chance-level baselines, and quantitative reporting of decoding accuracy, IEM reconstruction error, effect sizes, and corrected p-values for repeat versus alternate and task-related versus task-unrelated conditions. Finally, because the related work does not address temporal context, the plan should add explicit contrasts for micro, meso, and macro scales and validate that LDA/IEM can recover known stimulus-location features before interpreting them as temporal-context effects.

## Context: Theoretical test of whether prediction sharpens or dampens expected representations. Method: Our theoretical framework will examine whether predictive mechanisms operate through "dampening" (reduced sensitivity to expected features) or "sharpening" (increased selectivity for expected features) by measuring both behavioral precision and neural decoding accuracy for expected versus unexpected events.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method is that Tang et al. used multivariate encoding analysis and a computational model to separate adaptation from prediction in mouse visual cortex, showing that prediction altered single-neuron activity and population coding of orientation and that predictions repelled perception away from the expected orientation [(Tang et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5037b93c9d576794126d0b80c013590d63b350aa). This directly supports the proposed plan to test dampening versus sharpening by combining behavioral precision with neural decoding accuracy, because it demonstrates that expected-feature effects can be dissociated from adaptation and can be observed in both perceptual bias and population-level neural coding. A secondary support is that the study used orientation stimuli and population coding, which is conceptually aligned with the proposed EEG analysis using inverted encoding modeling and linear discriminant analysis to reconstruct or classify stimulus features for expected versus unexpected events, although the proposed human temporal-context paradigm is broader than the mouse orientation-prediction task. A weaker support is that the computational-modeling approach suggests the proposed theoretical framework should not rely solely on raw decoding differences, but should model competing mechanisms such as adaptation and prediction, which is relevant to the plan’s goal of distinguishing attention-modulated rapid responses from automatic encoding of unexpected events.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that Tang et al. found predictions repelled perception away from the expected orientation, which is not straightforwardly compatible with a simple sharpening account in which expected features are represented with greater selectivity or higher decoding precision; instead, it suggests that prediction may produce a repulsive or dampening-like bias against the expected feature, so the proposed method must treat sharpening and dampening as competing signed biases rather than assuming expected events are uniformly enhanced [(Tang et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5037b93c9d576794126d0b80c013590d63b350aa). A moderate contradiction is that the evidence comes from mouse visual cortex with GCaMP6f calcium imaging and orientation stimuli, not human EEG, pupillometry, or serial temporal-context sequences, so it cannot directly validate the proposed LDA/IEM pipeline, cluster-corrected time-window statistics, or the micro-meso-macro design. A weaker contradiction is that the supplied excerpt lacks quantitative decoding accuracies, effect sizes, sample sizes, imaging hardware details, stimulus parameters, decoding hyperparameters, and statistical thresholds, limiting the ability to benchmark the proposed method’s expected effect sizes, reproducibility constraints, or statistical power.

### Suggested Action

The most important improvement is to add an explicit computational model, analogous to the adaptation-versus-prediction model in Tang et al., that predicts both signed perceptual bias and neural decoding precision for expected versus unexpected events, so that the proposed behavioral precision and EEG decoding metrics can be interpreted as sharpening, dampening, or repulsion rather than as a single accuracy difference [(Tang et al., 2022-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5037b93c9d576794126d0b80c013590d63b350aa). The next priority is to operationalize the sharpening-versus-dampening test with signed metrics: for behavior, measure bias away from or toward the expected position or angle and precision separately; for EEG, use IEM to reconstruct stimulus features and LDA to classify expected versus unexpected trials, then test whether expected trials show higher reconstruction precision, lower feature contrast, or a signed repulsive bias, with cluster-corrected permutation thresholds and cross-validated decoding accuracies reported with effect sizes and confidence intervals. A further action is to pre-register the analysis pipeline and report implementation details that Tang et al. omit, including sample size, EEG hardware, preprocessing, LDA/IEM hyperparameters, cross-validation scheme, cluster size and threshold, and non-parametric behavioral tests, to improve reproducibility and allow comparison with the mouse population-coding evidence. Finally, the plan should explicitly position Tang et al. as a micro-scale prior for orientation prediction and use it to motivate controls for adaptation, such as repeat versus alternate comparisons and probability-bias manipulations, while acknowledging that the human temporal-context paradigm requires additional evidence to generalize from mouse orientation coding to serial visual context.
