## Our experimental design will systematically vary several key parameters: environmental quality (mortality rates from m = 0.1 for benign to m = 0.3 for harsh environments), costs of defensive tasks on survival (xh = 3, 5, and 7), and the requirement for division of labor (comparing scenarios where breeder productivity is maximized when both tasks are performed equally versus scenarios with no task-specific constraints). This parameter sweep identifies the conditions under which division of labor emerges and which tasks are favored under different selective regimes.

### Support

Given the single related work, it can be treated as an earlier deterministic baseline relative to the proposed newer stochastic evolutionary method. The strongest support from [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781) is that computational parameter sweeps in cooperative-breeding vertebrate models are a viable way to test how demographic parameters alter group composition and stability, which parallels the proposed sweep over mortality m = 0.1 to 0.3, defensive-task costs xh = 3, 5, and 7, and division-of-labor constraints. Ghosh et al. used a deterministic delay-differential population model for blue-tailed bee-eaters in the Gangetic delta plain, solved numerically with DDE23 in MATLAB, and varied one parameter at a time, including helping rates hp and hs and immigration rates iB and iS, then recorded time to steady state and steady-state sizes of breeders, primary helpers, secondary helpers, and prey. This directly supports the proposed use of parameterized simulations to identify conditions under which helper roles and population stability change, although it does so at the level of demographic equilibrium rather than evolutionary task specialization. A second, weaker support is the use of equilibrium-based statistical tests: paired t-tests compared steady-state sizes across helping-rate and immigration-rate values, and the paper reported significant effects for breeder immigration, with prey decreasing at p < 0.05 while breeders, primary helpers, and secondary helpers increased. This supports the proposed plan to compare outcomes across parameter values and to use statistical tests, but the related work's acceptance rule of p > 0.05 for sustainability is weaker than the inferential standard needed for evolutionary claims. The weakest support is the paper's generalization logic, which recorded time to steady state and steady-state size under different maturation times tau, helping rates, and immigration rates; this is analogous to the proposed plan's requirement to run simulations to equilibrium or 200,000 generations and to assess consistency across 20 replicas, but Ghosh et al. did not report stochastic replicas, evolutionary trajectories, or reaction-norm parameters gamma_t and gamma_0.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781) is a deterministic, non-evolutionary delay-differential population model, whereas the proposed method requires a stochastic individual-based model that tracks evolution of helping strategies, task specialization, dispersal, dominance, and reaction norms over 200,000 generations. Ghosh et al. solved model 7 with DDE23 in MATLAB using fixed species-specific parameters for blue-tailed bee-eaters and varied parameters such as hp, hs, iB, and iS, but did not model heritable behavioral variation, selection, direct versus indirect fitness, or the emergence of division of labor. Therefore, its equilibrium results cannot be taken as evidence that the proposed evolutionary hypotheses about defensive versus work task costs will be supported. A second contradiction is that the related work does not include the key selective mechanisms in the proposed plan: it has no defensive-task survival cost xh, no work-task body-condition cost, no comparison of scenarios where breeder productivity is maximized by equal task performance versus no task-specific constraints, and no model variants separating group augmentation from kin selection. Its immigration sweeps are not equivalent to the proposed environmental-quality sweep from m = 0.1 to m = 0.3, because immigration changes group composition without modeling mortality-driven breeding-position turnover, inheritance, or direct fitness opportunities. A third contradiction is methodological: the paper used one-parameter-at-a-time sweeps and paired t-tests on steady states, with a sustainability hypothesis accepted when p > 0.05, which is a failure-to-reject criterion rather than positive evidence. This is less suitable for the proposed method, where evolutionary outcomes may be stochastic, path-dependent, and require multiple replicas, effect sizes, and convergence diagnostics. A fourth, weaker contradiction is that the reported effects were sometimes small or insignificant: a nine-fold increase in secondary helper immigration produced only an insignificant increase in secondary helpers, with breeders changing from about 3.578 to 3.584, primary helpers from about 7.530 to 7.534, secondary helpers from about 0.10 to 0.20, and prey from about 74.40 to 74.415, suggesting that not all demographic parameter changes will produce strong detectable shifts in group structure. Finally, the paper does not report hardware specifications, stochasticity, evolutionary time scales, or task-specific fitness costs, limiting its direct reproducibility as a template for the proposed individual-based evolutionary experiments.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to keep the proposed stochastic individual-based evolutionary model as the primary method and use [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781) only as a deterministic baseline or calibration reference, not as evidence that equilibrium parameter sweeps alone can test division-of-labor evolution. Specifically, the proposed model should explicitly implement the missing mechanisms: overlapping generations, monopolized territories, complete reproductive skew, inheritance of breeding positions, defensive tasks with survival costs xh = 3, 5, and 7, work tasks with body-condition costs, and model variants with both direct and indirect benefits, only group augmentation, and only kin selection. The second most important action is to strengthen the statistical and experimental design beyond the related work's paired t-tests and p > 0.05 acceptance rule: run the planned 20 replicas per parameter combination, pre-register convergence criteria for 200,000 generations or equilibrium, report effect sizes, confidence intervals, mixed-effects or permutation tests for repeated parameter comparisons, and use multiple-comparison correction when sweeping m, xh, and task-constraint scenarios. The third action is to convert the related work's one-at-a-time sweep logic into a factorial or sensitivity analysis that captures interactions among environmental quality, defensive-task cost, and division-of-labor requirement, because the proposed hypotheses depend on joint selective regimes rather than isolated demographic effects. The fourth action is to incorporate the related work's useful equilibrium metrics as secondary diagnostics: track time to steady state, steady-state group sizes, helper-to-breeder ratios, and prey or resource levels, and compare deterministic DDE23-style baselines with stochastic individual-based outcomes to identify where evolutionary dynamics diverge from demographic equilibrium. The fifth action is to improve reproducibility by documenting implementation details absent from the related work, including random seeds, hardware or system specifications, simulation software, hyperparameters, mutation or recombination rates if any, dominance-value reaction-norm parameters gamma_t and gamma_0, dispersal rules, relatedness calculations, and convergence diagnostics. The final action is to calibrate parameter ranges where possible to cooperative-breeding birds, using the blue-tailed bee-eater parameter values and maturation-time tau from [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781) as one empirical anchor, while explicitly testing whether results generalize beyond that species-specific deterministic model.

## We will measure multiple outcome variables including: levels of helping behavior, degree of task specialization, age-dependent task switching patterns (temporal polyethism), dispersal rates, group sizes, within-group relatedness, and survival rates. These outcomes quantify the evolutionary and demographic consequences of the modeled fitness regimes.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed method comes from [(Donepudi, and Ramaswamy, 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43a40cecf9003573d14de43e82cc27767dbd021a), which used a stochastic individual-based NetLogo simulation of vertebrate foragers and directly supports the proposed emphasis on individual-level helping behavior, group size, and survival/age outcomes. That model assigned each bat a foraging efficiency between 0.5 and 1, used storage capacity c from 3 to 12, and compared sharing versus non-sharing populations initialized with equal numbers, efficiencies, and resource input. It measured stable population size, total stored food, per-capita resource use, competitive replacement, average age, and age at death, which maps closely onto the proposed outcome variables of helping behavior, group sizes, and survival rates. Its quantitative result that sharing increased stable population size from 277 to 417 individuals, about a 50 percent increase, and increased total daily resource assimilation by about 11 percent, supports the plausibility that simple helping rules can produce measurable demographic consequences in vertebrate groups. The second, more indirect support comes from [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781), which extended cooperative-breeding demographic modeling by explicitly manipulating helping rates and dispersal or immigration in a deterministic delay-differential-equation model of blue-tailed bee-eaters using MATLAB DDE23. This supports the proposed inclusion of dispersal rates and group-size outcomes, especially because it varied breeder and secondary-helper immigration over approximately 0 to 0.06 and reported equilibrium sizes for breeders, primary helpers, secondary helpers, and prey. As an iterative development after the 2018 individual-based work, the 2021 model adds dispersal/immigration as a demographic driver, but it does so at the population level rather than the individual level, so its support for the proposed stochastic individual-based model is partial rather than direct.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to an overconfident interpretation of the proposed method is that [(Donepudi, and Ramaswamy, 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43a40cecf9003573d14de43e82cc27767dbd021a) found average age and average age at death to be statistically similar between sharing and non-sharing populations, and not affected by resource availability or initial population size, even though sharing increased sustainable population size by about 50 percent. This cautions that helping behavior and larger group size do not automatically produce detectable survival-rate differences; therefore, the proposed survival-rate outcome must be driven by explicit task-specific mortality costs, such as the defensive-task costs xh = 3, 5, and 7, rather than assumed from helping alone. The next contradiction comes from [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781), which found that a ninefold increase in secondary-helper immigration produced only insignificant changes in secondary-helper equilibrium size, breeder equilibrium size, and primary-helper size, all with p > 0.05, while breeder immigration had significant effects on prey and helper/breeder sizes. This suggests that dispersal-rate outcomes may be weak, class-specific, or difficult to detect without sufficiently large effect sizes, longer transients, or stronger statistical power. A weaker but important contradiction is that neither related work implements the central proposed mechanisms: task specialization, temporal polyethism, within-group relatedness, direct versus indirect fitness decomposition, or reaction-norm evolution of gamma_t and gamma_0. The 2018 model lacks dispersal, relatedness, and task-specific fitness costs, while the 2021 model lacks individual-level stochasticity, task allocation, and evolutionary reaction norms. Thus, the related work supports the proposed demographic metrics but does not validate the proposed evolutionary task-specialization framework.

### Suggested Action

Most important, the proposed method should add explicit task-specific cost modules and reaction-norm tracking that are absent from both related works, using the defensive-task survival costs xh = 3, 5, and 7 and the work-task body-condition costs to generate measurable differences in survival, dominance value, and inheritance probability. The model should also use the simple sharing rule from [(Donepudi, and Ramaswamy, 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/43a40cecf9003573d14de43e82cc27767dbd021a) as a null baseline for helping behavior and group-size effects, and the deterministic DDE formulation from [(Ghosh et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d3a8ef4242f197a7bf47b3286d45e79fdbe2781) as a population-level limit for validating group-size and dispersal dynamics. Second, dispersal should be modeled as class-specific and statistically testable: vary breeder, primary-helper, and secondary-helper immigration or dispersal rates over ranges comparable to the 0 to 0.06 and ninefold manipulations in the 2021 work, and report effect sizes, confidence intervals, and variance across the 20 replicas rather than relying only on significance thresholds. Third, the proposed direct-versus-indirect fitness variants should be implemented as explicit ablations, with group augmentation retained or removed and kin selection retained or removed through cross-fostering or relatedness manipulation, because neither related work tests this decomposition. Fourth, the statistical plan should be strengthened: 20 replicas over 200,000 generations may be insufficient for detecting subtle changes in gamma_t, gamma_0, temporal polyethism, or dispersal propensities, so the authors should use mixed-effects models or Bayesian hierarchical models, report convergence diagnostics, use multiple random seeds, and correct for multiple comparisons across environmental quality, task cost, and fitness-regime variants. Finally, reproducibility should be improved by reporting software, hardware, runtime, hyperparameters, random seeds, convergence criteria, and code availability, following the reproducibility advantage of the NetLogo code in the 2018 work and the parameterized MATLAB DDE23 setup in the 2021 work.
