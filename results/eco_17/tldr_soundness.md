## Strengths 
The strongest support is for the core optical-sensing premise: near-infrared field sensors can detect insect passages and extract wing-beat frequency from backscattered signals, as shown by a 148-day 980 nm field deployment with harmonic wingbeat confirmation [(Genoud et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d99be82ff97b0e19d5678704ae7d6d3a41a4387f). A controlled 1320 nm backscatter study directly supports separating a slowly varying body component from wing modulation and extracting wing-beat frequency, with high sex-classification accuracy using wing-beat frequency alone [(Genoud et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fcd98fb07a48b9ae4a670d6f5ae843ecfdbcd8b). The proposal's clustering assumption is supported by near-infrared insect recordings at 808/975 nm that were converted to spectral features and clustered into species-like groups with higher ARI and AMI than linear and kernel baselines [(Rydhmer, and Selvan, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18bfc290cbfd1e6837a5dbe4a72140b3ff96bae3). The conventional reference design is also well supported by standardized invertebrate-monitoring guidance emphasizing replication, taxonomic resolution, vouchers, and biodiversity metrics [(Montgomery et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a326b1afaab1f003e6aefdc9b98d40858995a71b), and learned insect-versus-noise detection is supported by compact CNN and SVM baselines on short insect signal intervals [(Kiskin et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2619b9ae34fcc27fa7c84c43cc60aec877fc540f). Sweep-net reference sampling is also supported by agricultural sweep-net collections that reached species-level resolution and calculated alpha-diversity metrics [(Belamkar, and Jadesh, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/346ce0531172515235b982ce65a67dce7cee97f9), and autonomous field operation is further supported by solar-powered, cellular, GPS-enabled agricultural monitoring systems [(Parsons et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0fee975fcf2bd48492d8d4b47f5761bc0201928d).

## Weaknesses 
The strongest contradictions concern the proposal's central claim that DBSCAN cluster count can serve as a species-richness proxy. The closest clustering evidence used K-means in a learned latent space and evaluated ARI and AMI against labelled species groups, not DBSCAN cluster-count correlation with Malaise or sweep-net richness [(Rydhmer, and Selvan, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18bfc290cbfd1e6837a5dbe4a72140b3ff96bae3). Wing-beat-frequency-only discrimination is also weak for multi-class tasks: a six-class species/gender classifier achieved only 62.3 percent overall accuracy with substantial distribution overlap [(Genoud et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fcd98fb07a48b9ae4a670d6f5ae843ecfdbcd8b), and wingbeat-only classification reached only 0.68 accuracy compared with 0.92 for camera-only features [(Tschaikner et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c8e31a876b92218ca85eac3ce1fd5e36578b949). The proposed 810/970 nm LED, 5-70 L monostatic field system is not directly validated by the strongest field evidence, which used a 980 nm bistatic laser and reported large relative mass errors for small insects [(Genoud et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d99be82ff97b0e19d5678704ae7d6d3a41a4387f). Finally, the opaque proprietary cloud filter and discard rule are under-supported because related work does not demonstrate reliable non-insect rejection or show that discarding weak wing-beat or body-to-wing observations will not bias richness estimates [(Kiskin et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2619b9ae34fcc27fa7c84c43cc60aec877fc540f), [(Tschaikner et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1c8e31a876b92218ca85eac3ce1fd5e36578b949). The conventional reference also has gaps because agricultural arthropod surveys often report family-level richness and use different trap types, and sweep-net protocol details are insufficient to guarantee comparable effort [(Armando et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1412fadfe026f74fe7b94bd07c23d78bbad09625), [(Belamkar, and Jadesh, unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/346ce0531172515235b982ce65a67dce7cee97f9).

## Top 3 Suggestions 
Add a pre-field validation stage for the 810/970 nm sensor and noise filter: collect labeled insects in a controlled arena or field cage, quantify wing-beat frequency and body-to-wing ratio extraction accuracy, measurement-volume calibration, multi-insect overlap, and signal-to-noise; replace the opaque proprietary cloud filter with a reproducible insect-versus-noise classifier evaluated with precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrices, and run a sensitivity analysis retaining low-confidence or missing-feature observations to test whether discarding biases cluster counts, using body/wing separation from [(Genoud et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fcd98fb07a48b9ae4a670d6f5ae843ecfdbcd8b), field event detection from [(Genoud et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d99be82ff97b0e19d5678704ae7d6d3a41a4387f), and classifier baselines from [(Kiskin et al., 2018-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2619b9ae34fcc27fa7c84c43cc60aec877fc540f).

Replace sole reliance on stochastic-gradient-descent tuning of DBSCAN with a transparent clustering validation workflow: use site-level nested cross-validation, multiple 30/70 splits, k-distance or reachability plots, and stability metrics to select epsilon and min_samples; compare DBSCAN with HDBSCAN, K-means, Gaussian mixture, and PCA or Kernel-PCA baselines; and validate cluster count against a labeled subset and conventional metrics with Spearman confidence intervals, ARI, and AMI where labels exist, as motivated by [(Rydhmer, and Selvan, 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/18bfc290cbfd1e6837a5dbe4a72140b3ff96bae3) and the wing-beat overlap evidence in [(Genoud et al., 2018-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5fcd98fb07a48b9ae4a670d6f5ae843ecfdbcd8b).

Strengthen the conventional reference and statistical alignment by fully standardizing sweep-net effort, including transect length, sweep rate, vegetation stratum, replicate locations, and sampling duration; add rarefaction or coverage estimates to account for unequal effort between Malaise traps and sweep nets; require voucher specimens, double-identification for dominant orders, and family/order sensitivity analyses; and fit mixed-effects models that align sensor, Malaise, and sweep-net windows while including crop, month, field, weather, sensor downtime, and detection volume as covariates, following [(Montgomery et al., 2021-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a326b1afaab1f003e6aefdc9b98d40858995a71b) and addressing trap and effort mismatches highlighted by [(Armando et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1412fadfe026f74fe7b94bd07c23d78bbad09625).
