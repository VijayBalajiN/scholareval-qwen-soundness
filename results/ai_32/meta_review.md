## To address the core research question, the plan proposes: 'We propose SPACE (Series Prediction Augmented by Causality Estimation), an end-to-end trainable model that integrates temporal dependencies and causal relationships.'

### Support

The strongest direct support for SPACE is from [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) because it constructs a transfer-entropy causality matrix with significance threshold c=0.005 and uses it as the adjacency for a GNN with convolutional temporal features on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq at horizons 5, 10, and 15, reporting outperformance over most baselines including VAR, CNN-AR, RNN-GRU, LSTNet, MLCNN, and multi-head attention; this supports the Cross-TE plus CGNN design, although the excerpt is qualitative and uses a separately computed TE matrix rather than SPACE's end-to-end fast-pTE. The next strongest support is quantitative causal-graph evidence from [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd), where CDF infers lagged and contemporaneous causal structure with VARLiNGAM and then uses a GNN plus LSTM; on 15 Google data-center datasets it reduces MSE versus non-causal GNN+LSTM, for example from 0.039 to 0.012 in L1 at H=10 and from 0.03 to 0.001 in L1 at H=20, supporting the hypothesis that explicit causal weighting can improve forecasting, but it is not TE-based and lacks significance tests. Strong support for TE-informed dynamic graph aggregation comes from [(Sriramulu et al., 2023-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/35c970fc96b86f719a303b1e02d63c52ec67b3ca), where ADLGNN combines transfer entropy among seven dependency matrices, top-20 sparsification, and dynamic sparse convolutional attention, reports the lowest RSE on Solar, Electricity, and Traffic, improves RSE by 4.9% over MTGNN, and on a 1494-node electricity dataset is 2.30877% faster than MTGNN while decreasing RSE by 13.383%. [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) also supports TE-derived directed graphs, using sliding-window transfer entropy, a weighted GNN with three graph and three temporal convolution modules, and reporting about 9% average F1 improvements across anomaly-detection datasets, with flexible graphs such as 727 edges out of 1444 possible in one SMD subset. Broader support for relationship-matrix aggregation comes from [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2), [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9), and [(Cao et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/645054d31fa26b29bbfb0cf73b75f8906c359415), which show that learned adjacency, heterogeneous causal-relevance graphs, or latent correlation matrices can improve multivariate forecasting, with StemGNN reporting average 8.1% MAE and 13.3% RMSE improvements over the best baseline for each dataset. Temporal-attention support relevant to the Sequence Enhancer and patchified temporal embedding comes from [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716) and [(Shih et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d1d53c671b20db116ba8c91c6446bb4757614da), where LSTNet's skip and attention variants improve RSE on periodic datasets, for example horizon-24 Solar-Energy RSE 0.4403 for LSTNet-Attn and 0.4643 for LSTNet-skip versus 0.4852 for RNN-GRU, and TPA-LSTM reports 24-step RSE values of 0.4397 to 0.4431 on Solar Energy, 0.4696 to 0.4812 on Traffic, and 0.0997 to 0.1021 on Electricity. Weaker, more indirect support comes from [(Wang, and Cai, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac57d82de65b46951b2d664c9fa18b1dd420d637) and [(Schulz et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e483dc8e7ffc4668851056e57efc553102d368a7), which show that temporal patterns and trial-to-trial dependencies can be modeled, but they do not test causal TE, multivariate causal graphs, or the planned long-horizon ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial-index forecasting setup.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the claim that causal TE will outperform correlation or attention is non-causal context-based forecasting in [(Sriramulu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/cd3a5091d16b3c568041c3d40f0a8e254a91f553), where ContextRNN uses correlation spanning tree or mutual information rather than transfer entropy, reports best or near-best RSE on Electricity and Traffic, a 3.4% RSE reduction at the 24-hour horizon, a 28.29% RSE reduction on RE-Europe relative to the best existing GNN, and statistically significant outperformance over GNN baselines while using 48.15% of the computation time of the state-of-the-art technique; this suggests that carefully selected non-causal context can rival or beat graph methods and that SPACE must demonstrate causal gains beyond context selection and runtime. A similarly strong contradiction is correlation-based graph learning in [(Cao et al., 2020-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/645054d31fa26b29bbfb0cf73b75f8906c359415), where StemGNN learns a latent correlation matrix without predefined topology and reports average 8.1% MAE and 13.3% RMSE improvements over the best baseline, outperforming DCRNN, ST-GCN, GraphWaveNet, and often DeepGLO, indicating that learned correlations alone may be sufficient on many datasets. Attention-based temporal models also challenge the necessity of explicit causality: [(Shih et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d1d53c671b20db116ba8c91c6446bb4757614da) reports that TPA-LSTM outperforms LSTNet and many baselines, with 24-step RSE values such as 0.4397 for softmax position on Solar Energy, 0.4696 on Traffic, and 0.0997 on Electricity, while [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716) shows that temporal attention and skip connections are not uniformly superior, with Exchange-Rate horizon-24 RSE AR 0.0445 beating LSTNet-skip 0.0449 and LSTNet-Attn 0.0590. Direct TE or causal-relevance baselines are not always best: [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) reports that MTHetGNN outperforms TEGNN in most cases, with exceptions only for MLCNN on Exchange-Rate at horizon 3 and MTGNN on Traffic at horizon 24, suggesting that static or simple TE relevance may be weaker than heterogeneous dynamic relations. Methodological contradictions also weaken the proposed end-to-end pTE claim: the cited TE works often use separate causal discovery or static graphs rather than end-to-end pseudo transfer entropy, e.g. [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) thresholds TE with c=0.005 and reports qualitative gains, [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd) uses VARLiNGAM and reports point estimates without significance, and [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) uses sliding-window TE for single-step anomaly detection with five runs but no significance tests; none validates the Gaussian pseudo-TE approximation or the O(d^2T) to O(dT) fast-pTE reduction on the planned datasets. Finally, [(Schulz et al., 2016-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e483dc8e7ffc4668851056e57efc553102d368a7) and [(Wang, and Cai, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac57d82de65b46951b2d664c9fa18b1dd420d637) provide only weak or non-causal evidence, with Schulz modeling four univariate participants with 500 trials and no numerical forecasting baselines, and MSL using shapelets on 50 US states with T=28, C=3, RAE 1.0081, RSE 1.0213, and CORR 0.4667, which does not validate causal TE on the planned ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial-index long-horizon setup.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add direct TE/causal and strong non-causal graph baselines under the identical SPACE protocol, including CauGNN or TEGNN, ADLGNN, MTGNN, StemGNN, ContextRNN, TPA-LSTM, and LSTNet, on ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and the financial indices with input length 96, prediction lengths F in {96,192,336,720} or {48,96,192,336}, MSE and MAE, multiple random seeds, and statistical significance tests, because the current baseline list of DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, Stationary, and TimesNet does not directly test the causal-graph hypothesis. Second, validate the Cross-TE module by comparing fast-pTE against exact transfer entropy, testing the Gaussian pseudo-TE assumption on non-Gaussian series, ablating TE thresholds, top-k sparsification, static versus dynamic adjacency, random or shuffled TE matrices, and replacing TE with conventional attention, while reporting runtime, memory, and the claimed O(d^2T) to O(dT) complexity on the NVIDIA RTX 4090. Third, strengthen the statistical evaluation by reporting per-horizon MSE and MAE with confidence intervals or p-values, adding RSE, RAE, and CORR where useful for comparability with prior work, and documenting normalization, train/validation/test splits, hyperparameter search, batch size, learning rate, dropout, and hardware. Fourth, align the experimental scope with prior evidence by including shorter horizons where LSTNet, TPA-LSTM, and CauGNN were evaluated, examining Exchange-rate where simple AR was competitive, and describing the proprietary financial-index datasets enough to assess reproducibility. Fifth, make the interpretability analysis more rigorous by visualizing learned adjacency matrices against attention weights, TE matrices, and domain expectations on Weather, but avoiding causal overclaims from observational transfer entropy and, where possible, adding intervention-style or domain-specific validation. Sixth, ablate the Sequence Enhancer and patchified 2D temporal embedding against PatchTST, iTransformer, and TimesNet to show that the drop-in attention replacement contributes beyond standard temporal attention.

## To estimate causal dependencies in multivariate time series forecasting, the plan uses: 'Cross-TE Module: We employ transfer entropy (TE) methods to compute causal weights, specifically using pseudo transfer entropy (pTE) which assumes normal distribution for time series data.'

### Support

The strongest support for the Cross-TE Module is [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), which is the closest prior forecasting analogue to SPACE: it computes a TE matrix from training/validation data, thresholds edges at c=0.005, and feeds the causality matrix to CauGNN with convolutional kernels 3, 5, and 7, 12 channels, GNN hidden dimensions searched from 10 to 100, and shared settings hidCNN=12, hidGNN1=30, hidGNN2=10, window=32. On Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq with horizons 5, 10, and 15, it reports outperforming most baselines including VAR, CNN-AR, RNN-GRU, LSTNet, MLCNN, and multi-head attention, although the excerpt lacks numeric MSE/MAE, confidence intervals, and significance tests. This directly supports using TE-derived causal weights as a CGNN adjacency matrix, especially for the plan's Exchange-rate dataset, but it does not validate pTE, normal-distribution pTE, or fast-pTE. As an iterative progression, the newer work [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) extends TE graph construction to anomaly detection with sliding-window TE, sampling to reduce cost, and an unconstrained directed adjacency matrix in a weighted GNN with three graph convolution and three temporal convolution modules, 15-step input, single-step output, lr 1e-3, batch 32, 10 epochs, on RTX 3050 Ti; it reports average point-wise, composite, and point-adjusted F1 of 0.4948, 0.7445, and 0.9635, about 9 percent better than baselines, and shows flexible out-degrees such as 727 of 1444 possible relations in SMD. This supports TE graph construction and variable out-degree, but it is anomaly detection and single-step forecasting, not the plan's long-horizon MSE/MAE setting. Weaker but methodologically relevant support comes from [(Lizier, 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70b45b61d38bb3d44794b57fdd1dbf9d1212b18b), which provides a Gaussian TE estimator using closed-form differential entropies from covariance matrices, parameter-free, in nats, with complexity proportional to samples times the square of joint dimensionality, and validates it by unit tests against an external script; this supports the pTE normal-distribution assumption and implementation, but not forecasting performance. Earlier TE work by [(Ito et al., 2011-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcfd0216cf342c87fbf9465a568093622e40e15d) supports TE as a causal-dependency estimator by comparing D1TE, delayed TE, HOTE, and NCC/NCCH baselines with ROC, 30-minute recordings, and bin-size effects, but it provides no forecasting metrics and shows sensitivity to binning and recording length. Finally, [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932) and [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e) provide weaker support that TE can guide neural learning or causal networks: the former improves XOR convergence from 349.9 to 62.2 average epochs and classification accuracy in some datasets, while the latter uses KSG TE with 4 nearest neighbors, delay 1, and bootstrap p<0.01 on 64-channel MEA data, but neither validates pTE-based MTS forecasting.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction or caution is [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4), because it evaluates TE under the same Gaussian linear stochastic assumption that motivates pTE, X_t = A X_{t-1} + xi_t, and finds that unconditioned TE has false-positive rates that increase substantially as the spectral radius rho(A) approaches one, while false negatives are high when rho(A) is near zero. It also shows conditional Granger becomes unreliable when network size approaches sample size, whereas optimal causation entropy keeps false positives near the permutation-test level of about 1e-3 even with sample size 50 and networks up to 500 nodes. This directly challenges the plan's use of pTE causal weights as direct causal adjacency if the weights are unconditioned or if the system has strong propagation. A closely related contemporaneous contradiction is [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95), which shows symbolic TE in a three-node chain Z -> Y -> X with no direct Z -> X can be positive for the indirect Z -> X path, while an iterative causation-entropy procedure avoids the spurious edge; this warns that raw TE or pTE may encode indirect dependencies unless conditioned on intermediate variables. A more domain-specific contradiction is [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e), where continuous plant electrical signals were non-Gaussian and required KSG kernel TE with 4 nearest neighbors, delay 1, and empirical bootstrap p<0.01, whereas GC required ADF stationarity tests and differencing; this suggests the pTE normal-distribution assumption may be misspecified for some real MTS and that significance testing is necessary. [(Ito et al., 2011-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcfd0216cf342c87fbf9465a568093622e40e15d) provides weaker but important caution that TE performance depends on bin size, recording length, and computational cost, which threatens reproducibility of pTE if delay, binning, or window length are not controlled. Newer work also limits the fast-pTE claim: [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) reports pairwise TE complexity O(N^2 w) per sample and O(G N^2 w) overall, not O(dT), and uses single-step forecasting for anomaly detection, so it does not support the proposed O(d^2T) to O(dT) reduction for long-horizon forecasting. Finally, [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932) is a weak contradiction in scope: its TE factor is a feedback term in MLP training for XOR and classification, not a pTE causal-weight estimator for MTS forecasting, so its convergence gains cannot be transferred to SPACE's MSE/MAE claims.

### Suggested Action

Most important, revise the Cross-TE Module to condition pTE on relevant intermediate variables or to use a parent-set/causation-entropy-style selection, because [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4) and [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95) show unconditioned TE can mistake indirect paths for direct edges, especially as spectral radius increases. The plan should add a synthetic Gaussian linear benchmark with X_t = A X_{t-1} + xi_t, controlled n, average degree, spectral radius, sample length T, and permutation significance near 1e-3, reporting false-positive/false-negative rates for unconditioned pTE, conditional pTE, conditional Granger, and optimal causation entropy. Second, add statistical significance and thresholding to the causality matrix: use shuffle/bootstrap tests, a threshold such as c=0.005 from [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) or p<0.01 from [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e), and report sparsity, out-degree distributions, and stability across windows, as in [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7). Third, validate the pTE normal-distribution assumption and implementation by comparing against the Gaussian TE estimator in [(Lizier, 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70b45b61d38bb3d44794b57fdd1dbf9d1212b18b) and, for non-Gaussian datasets, against KSG TE with 4 nearest neighbors, delay 1, and bootstrap significance from [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e); include stationarity tests or differencing where needed. Fourth, prove and benchmark fast-pTE: report the algebraic reduction from O(d^2T) to O(dT), runtime/memory on NVIDIA RTX 4090 for ETT d=7, Weather d=21, Exchange-rate d=8, input length 96, and horizons 96-720, and compare against O(N^2 w) TE sampling from [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) and binning/delay sensitivity from [(Ito et al., 2011-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcfd0216cf342c87fbf9465a568093622e40e15d). Fifth, align the CGNN and evaluation with the strongest forecasting evidence: use a TE threshold, GNN hidden dimensions, and ablations similar to [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) and [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7), and compare against the planned baselines plus VAR, LSTNet, MLCNN, and attention-only SPACE, reporting MSE/MAE with multiple seeds, confidence intervals, and significance tests. Least important but useful, use Weather adjacency visualizations for interpretability only after conditioning and significance testing, and avoid claiming direct causality unless the synthetic and real-data evidence supports it.

## To make causal weighting computationally feasible, the plan develops: 'To address computational bottlenecks, we develop a fast-pTE algorithm that reduces complexity from O(d²T) to O(dT) by flattening series in the last two dimensions.'

### Support

The strongest support for the fast-pTE component is direct evidence that pseudo transfer entropy is a viable Gaussian conditional-variance estimator for causal direction and can be implemented with lower computational cost than direct transfer entropy. [(Silini, and Masoller, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c06602553082b3eecab4e85220eef3454bfb54cd) shows that pTE recovers known causal direction in bivariate systems, produces larger scores for the true causal direction than for the reverse direction, and is statistically distinguishable from surrogate nulls, which supports SPACE's use of pTE in the Cross-TE module and its normal-distribution assumption. This support is limited because the paper is bivariate, does not test multivariate patchified tensors, and does not establish an O(d^2T) to O(dT) reduction. The next strongest support is that transfer-entropy-derived causal graphs can be used for multivariate forecasting. [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) uses TEGNN, which builds a transfer-entropy matrix over the whole series as a graph for Exchange-Rate with 8 country series from 1990 to 2016, Traffic with 862 highway occupancy sensors from 2015 to 2016, and Solar-Energy with 137 photovoltaic power plant outputs from 2007, using a 6:2:2 train-validation-test split, input window T=32, horizons {3,6,12,24}, and metrics RSE, RAE, and CORR; it reports TEGNN as a strong graph-based baseline, supporting the premise that causal adjacency matrices can be useful for forecasting. However, the same paper notes that MTHetGNN outperforms TEGNN, especially on datasets with many variables, because whole-series transfer entropy may smooth short-segment fluctuations, so SPACE should not assume a single global TE matrix is sufficient. Older work supports the Gaussian/linear TE formulation and the computational bottleneck. [(Lizier, 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70b45b61d38bb3d44794b57fdd1dbf9d1212b18b) provides a parameter-free Gaussian transfer entropy estimator with stated O(N d^2) complexity, implemented in Java and validated through JIDT/JUnit unit tests against a modified Granger-computation script, which is conceptually close to pTE but still quadratic in joint dimensionality and does not implement the proposed flattening. [(Ito et al., 2011-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcfd0216cf342c87fbf9465a568093622e40e15d) explicitly discusses computational performance of TE for multivariate series, including 30 min simulated data, longer recordings, bin-size effects, and ROC evaluation, supporting the motivation for fast-pTE, but the excerpt provides no runtime numbers, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance. Weaker support comes from real multivariate TE causal-network analysis and neural integration: [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e) computes pairwise TE on plant electrical signals using KSG with 4 nearest neighbors, lag 1, and empirical bootstrap p<0.01, on optical datasets with 130 regions at 5 fps over 492 images and 540 regions at 1 fps over 128 images, and on 64-electrode MEA recordings, showing TE can be applied to real multivariate series, but it does not use pTE or Gaussian assumptions; [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932) shows that a transfer-entropy factor can improve MLP training on XOR and ten classification datasets, with FF+FB reaching 100 percent training accuracy in an average of 62.2 epochs versus 349.9 for FF on XOR under a 300-epoch cap, 10 runs, 2-2-1 architecture, 200 training vectors per epoch, binning threshold 0.7, and learning rate 0.025, but the setting is classification, not multivariate time series forecasting, and it provides no evidence for fast-pTE complexity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the central fast-pTE claim, reducing complexity from O(d^2T) to O(dT) by flattening the last two dimensions, is not supported by the related work and may be mathematically overstated. [(Lizier, 2014-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/70b45b61d38bb3d44794b57fdd1dbf9d1212b18b) reports a Gaussian transfer entropy estimator with O(N d^2) complexity, indicating that joint-dimensional Gaussian TE generally carries quadratic dimensionality cost; [(Silini, and Masoller, 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c06602553082b3eecab4e85220eef3454bfb54cd) validates bivariate pTE and vectorization but does not show that multivariate pairwise pTE can avoid the d^2 pair count; [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) and [(Ito et al., 2011-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dcfd0216cf342c87fbf9465a568093622e40e15d) motivate TE cost but provide no runtime, memory, or complexity evidence for a flattened O(dT) implementation. If fast-pTE still computes all d(d-1) pairwise scores, flattening may reduce constants or enable GPU vectorization but not asymptotic O(dT); for example, on Weather with d=21 and input H=96, naive pairwise computation is on the order of 21*20*96 pair-time units, while O(dT) would be 21*96, a large gap that requires proof rather than implementation detail. The next strongest contradiction is that TE-based causal weights can be unreliable for network inference, which threatens the quality of the SPACE adjacency matrix. [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4) shows that transfer entropy with only K={i} conditioning produces increasing false positives as the spectral radius rho(A) approaches one in Gaussian linear networks X_t = A X_{t-1} + xi_t, while optimal causation entropy maintains false positive and false negative ratios close to zero, with false positive ratio saturating near 1-theta = 10^-3 even for small sample sizes such as T=50 on networks up to 500 nodes, and conditional Granger causality becoming extremely inaccurate when network size exceeded sample size; this directly cautions against using simple pairwise TE/pTE weights without stronger conditioning. [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95) demonstrates that in a Z->Y->X chain with coupling epsilon in [0.2,0.4], TE from Z to X is positive despite no direct coupling, whereas iterative causation entropy selects the immediate neighbor and avoids the spurious edge. Newer evidence extends this concern to real signals: [(Tanizawa, and Nakamura, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93ed71aa2a409526e0c4ecd611ebf17b2a7b54ca) finds standard TE unreliable in a three-component linear system with heterogeneous time scales and amplitudes, where x2 produces larger TE values than x1 despite x0 not depending on x2, and in 10-channel resting-state EEG recorded at 1024 Hz with 1025 one-second samples, maximum lag 25, 251-term dictionary, and TE up to lag 30 with k=l=1, it produces flat or spurious high-TE sources such as C3 and Oz for Fz, even though the reduced auto-regressive model for Fz includes Cz at lags 1 and 2 and F3 at lags 5, 13, and 20; this suggests fast-pTE must preserve correct lags and avoid amplifying indirect or physiologically implausible sources. A further contradiction is the normality assumption: [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e) uses KSG kernel TE with 4 nearest neighbors and bootstrap p<0.01 for non-Gaussian plant electrical signals, implying that Gaussian pTE may be less appropriate for real multivariate data unless preprocessing or robustness checks are added. Finally, [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) contradicts the sufficiency of a whole-series TE matrix by showing MTHetGNN outperforms TEGNN, especially on high-dimensional datasets, because global TE may smooth short-segment fluctuations; this is not a contradiction to fast-pTE per se, but it contradicts the idea that a single global causal matrix is automatically optimal for forecasting.

### Suggested Action

Most important, prove and benchmark the fast-pTE complexity claim before using it as a methodological contribution. Derive FLOPs and memory for naive pairwise pTE and flattened fast-pTE on SPACE datasets with d=7 for ETT, d=21 for Weather, d=8 for Exchange-rate, and the financial index datasets, using input H=96 and prediction horizons F in {96,192,336,720} or {48,96,192,336}, and report runtime, peak GPU memory, numerical equivalence, and GPU utilization on NVIDIA RTX 4090. If flattening only reduces constants, revise the claim to O(d^2T) with a lower constant or design a true O(dT) algorithm using shared covariance computation, low-rank approximation, local patch neighborhoods, or a restricted candidate graph. Second, validate causal-weight quality on controlled synthetic networks that include indirect paths, time-scale heterogeneity, amplitude differences, and limited sample sizes, measuring false-positive/false-negative ratios, AUC, lag recovery, and significance against surrogate or permutation nulls, following the concerns in [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4), [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95), and [(Tanizawa, and Nakamura, 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/93ed71aa2a409526e0c4ecd611ebf17b2a7b54ca). Third, add statistical significance and multivariate conditioning to the Cross-TE module, such as surrogate-based pTE significance, FDR correction, conditional pTE, or iterative causation-entropy selection, to reduce spurious indirect edges. Fourth, test robustness to non-Gaussianity by comparing Gaussian pTE with KSG TE, Granger causality, and attention on the same ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial datasets, and include stationarity, normalization, differencing, or transformation checks inspired by [(Chen et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4e2bd6be9c1e0486515a123336dc2fc857d48e7e). Fifth, preserve temporal resolution by computing pTE on patches or local windows rather than only the whole input, or by using multi-scale TE, to address the limitation noted in [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) that whole-series TE may smooth short-segment fluctuations. Sixth, include ablations that replace fast-pTE with conventional attention, global TEGNN-style TE, local patch TE, and no Sequence Enhancer, reporting MSE, MAE, runtime, and memory, and visualize adjacency matrices on Weather to verify climate-scientific relationships. Finally, report full reproducibility details: lag/embedding dimensions, normalization, significance threshold, random seeds, GPU, and hyperparameters, because the related work leaves many implementation details unspecified.

## To combine the estimated causal structure with temporal patch information, the plan uses: 'Causal Graph Neural Network (CGNN): This module integrates information using the causality matrix from Cross-TE as its adjacency matrix, featuring a 2DMixer to handle information within and across patches before aggregation.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed CGNN is that transfer-entropy-derived graphs can serve as adjacency matrices for graph-based MTS forecasting, and that whole-series TE may miss short-segment dynamics, which motivates patch-level Cross-TE. [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9), appearing after the initial TE-GNN and learned-graph work, evaluates TEGNN and MTHetGNN on Exchange-Rate, Traffic, and Solar-Energy with input window 32, horizons 3, 6, 12, and 24, and 6:2:2 splits; it explicitly notes that TEGNN transfer entropy measured over the whole series may smooth short-segment fluctuations, while MTHetGNN emphasizes dynamic correlations in shorter segments, directly supporting the plan's patchified 2D tensor and Cross-TE idea. Next, [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) extends this local-causality line by using sliding-window pairwise transfer entropy to build a directed adjacency matrix and feeding it to a weighted GNN with three graph convolution modules, three temporal convolution modules, skip connections, gating, and single-step forecasting; on SWAT, WADI, SMAP, MSL, SMD, and PSM it reports average F1 0.4948, composite F1 0.7445, point-adjusted F1 0.9635, and a 9 percent average improvement, using PyTorch 1.13.1, CUDA 11.6, RTX 3050 Ti, Adam lr 1e-3, 10 epochs, batch 32, and window 15, which supports the feasibility of local TE plus GNN aggregation, although the objective is anomaly detection rather than multi-step MSE/MAE. [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) provides direct forecasting evidence for a TE adjacency matrix: CauGNN estimates a transfer entropy matrix, thresholds it at 0.005, uses it for GNN aggregation, and combines parallel convolutional kernels of sizes 3, 5, and 7 with 12 channels; it reports outperforming most baselines on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq at horizons 5, 10, and 15, with tuned hidCNN 12, hidGNN1 30, hidGNN2 10, window 32, and 1000 epochs, supporting the core CGNN hypothesis. [(Faruque et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8072f6e96371a2006e81a6cc3b2f05e744a24241) supports the 2D patch-oriented aspect by using a causal convolution 2D layer over normalized inputs shaped as T, n, lmax+1 to learn instantaneous and lagged adjacency matrices with an acyclicity constraint, achieving best summary-graph SHD, F1, and FDR on synthetic datasets except DAG-GNN FDR 0.00 on dataset-1, which is indirect but relevant to 2DMixer. Finally, [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2) supports graph-plus-temporal modeling more generally: MTGNN learns a shared adjacency matrix and interleaves graph and temporal convolutions with residual and skip connections, reports state-of-the-art single-step and multi-step results, and reduces graph learning complexity from O(N^2) to (N/s)^2 with subgraph sampling, but it is less specific because the adjacency is learned rather than TE-derived.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that a transfer-entropy graph alone may be insufficient when dynamic or heterogeneous relations are modeled. [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9), appearing after the initial TE-GNN and learned-graph work, reports that MTHetGNN outperforms TEGNN, especially on high-dimensional Traffic with 862 variables and Solar-Energy with 137 variables, and that TEGNN whole-series TE may smooth short-segment fluctuations; if SPACE's CGNN relies on a static Cross-TE adjacency without dynamic, heterogeneous, or learnable refinement, it may inherit the same limitation. A second important contradiction is that learned adjacency matrices can be highly competitive, suggesting fixed TE adjacency may not dominate modern correlation-based or learned graph models. [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2) shows MTGNN learns an adjacency matrix end-to-end and achieves state-of-the-art results on four MTS benchmarks and two spatial-temporal GNN benchmarks, with subgraph sampling and curriculum learning, but the excerpt lacks numerical values and significance tests, so the contradiction is architectural rather than quantitative. Third, the older TE-GNN evidence is not directly aligned with the proposed patch/2DMixer design. [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) uses a non-patch CNN/GNN, a fixed TE threshold of 0.005, and older baselines such as VAR, CNN-AR, RNN-GRU, LSTNet, MLCNN, and MultiHead Attention, so its positive results do not prove that a 2DMixer or pTE will improve over PatchTST, iTransformer, Crossformer, Stationary, TimesNet, DLinear, TiDE, or RLinear under H=96 and F 96 to 720. Fourth, the strongest local-TE evidence is task-mismatched. [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) uses standard transfer entropy, single-step forecasting, and anomaly-detection F1 metrics rather than multi-step MSE/MAE, so its 9 percent average F1 improvement does not directly validate SPACE's forecasting objective. Fifth, the newest 2D causal-convolution evidence is causal discovery rather than forecasting. [(Faruque et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8072f6e96371a2006e81a6cc3b2f05e744a24241) learns an acyclic adjacency matrix on synthetic and small real datasets with SHD, F1, and FDR, and does not test transfer entropy, pTE, or 2DMixer forecasting, so it supports 2D mixing but also highlights that TE-derived graphs may need acyclicity or causal-validity checks not specified in the plan.

### Suggested Action

Most important, modify CGNN so the Cross-TE matrix is not the only adjacency: combine it with a learnable residual adjacency or dynamic/heterogeneous graph module, inspired by MTGNN's learned shared adjacency and MTHetGNN's heterogeneous dynamic aggregation, and ablate static Cross-TE, learned-only, and hybrid alpha-weighted adjacency on ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial indices. Second, directly validate the Cross-TE and fast-pTE assumptions by comparing whole-series TE, sliding-window TE, standard TE, and pTE on the same H=96 windows, reporting adjacency sparsity, threshold sensitivity around Duan's 0.005 and 0 to 0.1 search, normality diagnostics, and forecasting MSE/MAE; if pTE underperforms on non-Gaussian financial or ETT data, replace or regularize it with nonparametric TE or a robust pseudo-TE variant. Third, isolate the 2DMixer contribution by comparing it against 1D temporal convolutions, Duan's parallel kernels 3, 5, and 7, MTGNN-style temporal convolutions, Febrinanto's three TCN/GCN stack, and Faruque's 2D causal convolution, using identical patch sizes, hidden dimensions, and RTX 4090 timing to show whether within-patch and across-patch mixing improves over standard GNN aggregation. Fourth, align the evaluation with the plan's modern baselines and multi-step settings by reporting MSE and MAE for F 96, 192, 336, 720 and financial F 48, 96, 192, 336, with multiple runs, confidence intervals or significance tests, and parameter/FLOPs counts, because related work mostly reports older baselines, single-step or anomaly metrics, and limited numerical detail. Fifth, add scalability controls for high-dimensional financial indices: use sparse TE, top-k or thresholded adjacency, subgraph sampling or block-wise graph convolution as in MTGNN, and report complexity of fast-pTE O(dT) versus O(d^2T), memory, and training time. Finally, strengthen interpretability by visualizing Cross-TE adjacency, learned residual adjacency, and attention weights, comparing edge distributions to Febrinanto's non-uniform out-degree example, and checking Weather edges against climate-scientific relationships.

## To represent each time series as patch-based latent inputs, the plan adopts: 'Temporal Embedding: We adopt a cross-temporal approach where individual time series are patchified into 2D tensors and projected into latent space, rather than treating all data points at the same time as column vectors.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed cross-temporal patch embedding comes from [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8e57155bbcc1ce8a112482c85b3a3bb25f3fe52), which directly patchifies multivariate series with patch length 16 and maximum token length 17, uses a 2-layer lightweight transformer plus a 6-layer GPT-2 backbone, trains end-to-end with AdamW at 0.0001 for at most 10 epochs on an NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU, and obtains the best performance in 79 of 80 cross-dataset entries while remaining competitive with per-dataset models; its zero-shot protocol from ETTh1, ETTm1, and ETTm2 to ETTh2, Electricity, and Weather also supports the plan's long-horizon, cross-dataset evaluation. This is followed by [(Cheng et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac7b15e0b7fc9c51624bfd1a2f5926059a7e0ed8), which shows that converting raw series into local patch tokens before sequence modeling is beneficial, with deformable patching improving over pointwise and uniform patching on most of 10 UEA classification datasets and forecasting over 9 datasets, including gains over PatchTST of more than 8% on ETTh2 and more than 11% on Illness, using a single NVIDIA 4090 24GB GPU, three repeats, and MSE/MAE evaluation; this supports the plan's patch-first representation and its 2DMixer/convolutional aggregation idea. Next, [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e) supports patch-based temporal embedding for the plan's benchmark-style setup with past length 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720, where Patchformer achieved Multi-Energy 720-step MSE 0.121 and MAE 0.193, reported 15.70% and 11.40% lower than the second-best, and was best or second-best on Weather and ETT datasets. [(Cao et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83ac79bb8e8695fb3c3c024be74790d862adea74) provides weaker but still relevant support because PatchTST, a patch-level latent baseline, is evaluated under zero-shot many-to-one transfer on ECL, Traffic, Weather, and ETT datasets, and the paper's TEMPO method improves over PatchTST on all dataset averages, suggesting that patch representations can be useful but benefit from additional structure such as decomposition or prompts, analogous to the plan's causal module. [(Kim et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1114eae74b9beff6e3b0fade038f2f03648ff7c) supports the broader attention-based plan by showing that CATS outperforms PatchTST on M4 with a 19.94% MASE reduction and achieves the lowest MSE/MAE on Traffic for all horizons using 4 NVIDIA RTX 4090 24GB GPUs, indicating that cross-attention or sequence enhancement can complement patch tokens. The weakest support is [(Lee et al., 2025-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1b55449e740b6c806db0bf37f70043f3e6a3f13), which uses PatchTST as a patch-token baseline for weather, finance, and healthcare event prediction and shows it is competitive but not dominant, with average ranks of 5.14 for F1 and 5.00 for AUC; this is less directly supportive because the task uses F1/AUC event prediction rather than the plan's MSE/MAE forecasting.

### Contradictions

The most direct contradiction to the proposed fixed cross-temporal patch embedding is [(Cheng et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac7b15e0b7fc9c51624bfd1a2f5926059a7e0ed8), which compares pointwise, uniform, and deformable patch predictors and finds that deformable patching improves over pointwise and uniform patching on most datasets, with the largest reported gains of 20% on FM and 22% on DDG using a two-layer convolutional predictor, and that a fully convolutional block outperforms a six-layer transformer encoder in most forecasting block comparisons; this suggests that the plan's fixed patchification and transformer-centric design may be suboptimal unless patch size or position is made adaptive. A broader contradiction to patch-only sufficiency comes from [(Cao et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83ac79bb8e8695fb3c3c024be74790d862adea74), where PatchTST is worse than TEMPO on all zero-shot many-to-one dataset averages, for example ECL average MSE/MAE 0.49/0.545 versus 0.216/0.308, Traffic 1.039/0.652 versus 0.503/0.358, Weather 0.304/0.340 versus 0.282/0.316, ETTm1 0.741/0.566 versus 0.501/0.458, ETTm2 0.334/0.376 versus 0.280/0.328, ETTh1 0.592/0.535 versus 0.428/0.427, and ETTh2 0.394/0.425 versus 0.361/0.398, although no statistical significance tests are reported. This contradicts the idea that patch-level temporal embedding alone is sufficient for strong cross-dataset forecasting. For the plan's financial-index component, [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e) provides a clear warning: Patchformer was best on Exchange Rate across all prediction lengths but showed sharp long-horizon degradation, with MSE rising to 1.071 at 720 steps, suggesting that patch-based temporal embedding may struggle with volatile financial series unless augmented by causal or sequence-enhancement mechanisms. [(Lee et al., 2025-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1b55449e740b6c806db0bf37f70043f3e6a3f13) further contradicts the robustness of patch tokens in finance, where PatchTST achieved F1/AUC of 0.373/0.573 on S&P 500 and 0.391/0.640 on Nikkei 225 and average ranks of 5.14 for F1 and 5.00 for AUC, while TIMECAP achieved average ranks of 1.14 for F1 and 1.86 for AUC; however, this is weaker evidence because it evaluates event prediction rather than the plan's long-horizon MSE/MAE forecasting. Finally, [(Kim et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1114eae74b9beff6e3b0fade038f2f03648ff7c) contradicts reliance on self-attention patch tokens alone by showing that CATS reduces MASE by 19.94% relative to PatchTST on M4 and obtains the lowest MSE/MAE on Traffic for all horizons, implying that the plan's Sequence Enhancer and causal graph must demonstrably add value beyond patch self-attention.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to add a dedicated representation ablation that isolates the proposed 2D cross-temporal patch embedding from the causal modules, comparing it against pointwise tokens, column-vector multivariate tokens such as iTransformer, uniform PatchTST-style patches, and deformable or adaptive patch predictors inspired by [(Cheng et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac7b15e0b7fc9c51624bfd1a2f5926059a7e0ed8); this should use the plan's H=96 input, F in {96,192,336,720} or financial F in {48,96,192,336}, the nine datasets, NVIDIA RTX 4090, MSE/MAE, at least three seeds, and report confidence intervals or significance tests because none of the cited works provide them. Second, the patchification should be made more reproducible and possibly adaptive: specify patch length, stride, maximum token length, projection dimension, and whether patch boundaries are learned, while testing patch length 16 and maximum token length 17 as a strong prior from [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8e57155bbcc1ce8a112482c85b3a3bb25f3fe52) and including deformable patch-size/position variants from [(Cheng et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ac7b15e0b7fc9c51624bfd1a2f5926059a7e0ed8). Third, because the plan includes proprietary financial indices and pTE assumes normality, the Cross-TE module should include distribution diagnostics, non-Gaussian or robust transfer-entropy alternatives, and long-horizon stress tests on volatile series, motivated by the Exchange Rate 720-step MSE degradation in [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e) and the weak PatchTST finance event-prediction results in [(Lee et al., 2025-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1b55449e740b6c806db0bf37f70043f3e6a3f13). Fourth, the experiment design should include a zero-shot or cross-dataset transfer protocol with explicit source and target domains, domain instructions or indicators, and no target-domain training, following [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b8e57155bbcc1ce8a112482c85b3a3bb25f3fe52) and [(Cao et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83ac79bb8e8695fb3c3c024be74790d862adea74), to test whether the causal graph improves transfer rather than only in-distribution forecasting. Fifth, the Sequence Enhancer and CGNN should be ablated against strong cross-attention and attention baselines such as CATS, iTransformer, and PatchTST, as in [(Kim et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1114eae74b9beff6e3b0fade038f2f03648ff7c), to show that the causal adjacency matrix and 2DMixer are not redundant with standard attention. Finally, the paper should report full implementation details, hardware, hyperparameters, training epochs, early stopping, batch size, optimizer, learning rate, and statistical significance, since reproducibility is limited in the related work and the proposed fast-pTE O(dT) claim needs empirical validation. A lower-priority action is to strengthen the Weather interpretability analysis by visualizing learned adjacency matrices and comparing them with attention weights, but this should not substitute for the quantitative ablations above.

## To enhance sequences before causal processing, the plan uses: 'Sequence Enhancer: A multi-head attention module that shares information and patterns from other patches to enhance sequences before causal processing, utilizing the auto-regressive characteristics of time series.'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed Sequence Enhancer comes from [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d), which shows that patch-level attention can share cross-patch, cross-variate, and cross-time information before forecasting and achieves best MSE on 7 of 9 long-term datasets and best MAE on 8 of 9 among 11 baselines, with fixed lookback 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720; its dispatcher also reduces memory from 2.56 GB to 2.33 GB on ETTm1 and avoids out-of-memory failures above 40 GB on ECL and Traffic, while patch size 8 outperforms patch size 64 at lookback 64, directly supporting a patch-based pre-causal enhancer. This is the most direct analogue and the newest in the attention-enhancement line, and its patch-level results supersede earlier sparse-attention and saliency-attention evidence as the most relevant support for a pre-causal patch enhancer. Next, [(Yu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52a4c435945dd38a430de2904fe9e38e3c1429f6) provides ablation evidence that auto-regressive self-attention reduces MSE by 19.02% on average versus a linear layer and channel-wise self-attention by 21.09% across ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, Traffic, Weather, and Solar-Energy, supporting the plan's use of auto-regressive temporal attention and suggesting that cross-channel attention should be included. [(Duong-Trung et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4549a05610f799246c5c1cb2510e84b121c9763) further supports attention-based sequence enhancement by outperforming Autoformer, Informer, LogTrans, Reformer, LSTNet, and LSTM in all 23 MSE cases on ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, Exchange, and ILI, with 31% average MSE and MAE reduction and dataset-specific reductions of 65%, 29%, 65%, 18%, and 56%, although it does not use patches or causal TE. [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/afeeb8f5018eebb1a1d334b94dbbfc48d167efef) supports cross-variate attention because iTransformer's variate-attention design outperforms ablated variants and vanilla temporal attention, which is relevant if the Sequence Enhancer must exchange information across series as well as patches. [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e) supports lightweight attention before forecasting, reporting 14.33% average improvement over TSMixer with about 4x fewer parameters and showing learnable channel-wise attention beats identity attention, while SAM rho above 0.7 helps. [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa) supports patch-level causal attention and probabilistic decoding, achieving mean MAE 0.312 and MSE 0.265 on LSF and sMAPE 0.672 on Datadog, but it is a pretrained 103M-parameter decoder trained on about one trillion points rather than a drop-in enhancer. [(Li, and Moura, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c06b28da803886be916279d1eb70944f4eb2bd3) supports attention over filtered history and graph-based spatial enhancement, with Forecaster RMSE 5.1879 and MAPE 20.1362, 8.8210% lower RMSE and 9.6192% lower MAPE than the best-width Transformer on 996 NYC taxi locations, but its graph is correlation-based. [(Srinivas et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2c5528b2690dc5d50e5be760a9082cb887d878b) provides weaker support for multi-head cross-series attention, reporting RMSE/MAE/MAPE of 3.81/1.89/4.54 at horizon 3 on METR-LA and 1.55/0.78/1.55 on PEMS-BAY versus DGCRN, but the horizons are only 3, 6, and 12 and the module is not patchified or causal. [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) and [(Flores et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15e5966f9f9d1570083c7693374f12d0529ea052) provide weaker support for sparse attention and lag-based multi-head attention, respectively, but the supplied excerpts lack numerical outcomes, so they cannot strongly validate the proposed patch-level causal pipeline.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that strong non-causal patch and attention models already achieve state-of-the-art performance, making it unclear whether a pre-causal Sequence Enhancer will add value beyond correlation. [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d) achieves best MSE on 7 of 9 long-term datasets and best MAE on 8 of 9 without transfer entropy, causal graphs, or CGNN, and its dispatcher patch attention already captures cross-patch, cross-variate, and cross-time dependencies; this directly challenges the need for an additional causal module after a similar attention enhancer. As an iterative contrast, this newer patch-attention result also weakens the incremental case for adding causality after earlier attention designs such as [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), because the strongest reported gains come from patch-level attention rather than from causal TE. [(Duong-Trung et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a4549a05610f799246c5c1cb2510e84b121c9763) also contradicts the causal motivation by showing that correlative temporal self-attention with saliency can reduce MSE by 65% on ETTh1, 29% on ETTh2, 65% on ETTm1, 18% on Exchange, and 56% on ILI, with 31% average reduction, without any TE or causal graph, although baseline numbers were reused from Autoformer and Informer reports after spot re-runs. A more specific contradiction to the proposed auto-regressive patch emphasis is [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e), whose ablation found channel-wise attention outperformed temporal-wise attention in SAMformer, suggesting that a Sequence Enhancer focused only on temporal or patch auto-regression may be suboptimal unless it also models inter-series dependencies. [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/afeeb8f5018eebb1a1d334b94dbbfc48d167efef) similarly suggests that variate-dimension attention can be more effective than vanilla temporal attention, because iTransformer's variate-attention design was best among ablated component designs while vanilla Transformer was worst. [(Li, and Moura, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c06b28da803886be916279d1eb70944f4eb2bd3) provides weaker but relevant contradiction by showing that a correlation-derived spatial graph with threshold 0.1 and average 2.5 dependencies per location improves over a temporal Transformer baseline, implying that explicit correlation structure may capture useful spatial dependencies without causal TE, and its six-seed results lack formal significance tests. [(Srinivas et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2c5528b2690dc5d50e5be760a9082cb887d878b) and [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) are weaker contradictions or limitations: MultiTs Net's MMA improves METR-LA and PEMS-BAY forecasting without causal patch processing, and Informer's ProbSparse attention is efficient but the excerpt lacks numerical evidence that it would outperform the proposed causal design. [(Flores et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15e5966f9f9d1570083c7693374f12d0529ea052) is the weakest because it reports no numerical outcomes, so it cannot contradict the method except by showing that univariate lag-based attention is a much simpler setting than the proposed multivariate patch-causal pipeline.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the Sequence Enhancer a multi-head patch attention module that explicitly combines auto-regressive temporal attention with channel-wise or cross-variate attention, and to ablate it against no enhancer, a linear replacement, temporal-only attention, channel-only attention, dual attention, and a dispatcher-based patch attention implementation inspired by [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d), because that work shows patch size 8 is preferable to patch size 64 at lookback 64, fewer dispatchers can save memory but hurt accuracy, and dispatcher attention avoids out-of-memory failures above 40 GB on ECL and Traffic. Second, the plan should benchmark against the strongest non-causal attention baselines, including UniTST, PatchTST, iTransformer, TSD, SAMformer, the dual-attention model from [(Yu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/52a4c435945dd38a430de2904fe9e38e3c1429f6), and a correlation-graph baseline inspired by [(Li, and Moura, 2019-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9c06b28da803886be916279d1eb70944f4eb2bd3), using the same H=96 input and F in {96,192,336,720} protocol, reporting MSE and MAE with multiple seeds, confidence intervals or significance tests, and memory/runtime on the planned NVIDIA RTX 4090, because several related works report large gains without causal modules and some omit formal significance. Third, the Sequence Enhancer should include architectural controls that are known to stabilize attention forecasting: causal or auto-regressive masking over patches, instance normalization or RMSNorm, SwiGLU feed-forward layers, and optionally a probabilistic Student-T mixture head with median-of-samples point forecasts, as in [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa), while keeping the planned point-forecast MSE/MAE evaluation for direct comparison. Fourth, the fast-pTE and CGNN integration should be evaluated with the Sequence Enhancer frozen or removed to isolate whether causal weights improve over the attention-enhanced representation, and the ablation should report learned adjacency sparsity, attention weight distributions, and Weather interpretability checks, because [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d) shows attention can become sparse and cross-variate pairs can dominate high-attention pairs. Fifth, if sequence length or variate count grows, adopt sparse or dispatcher attention from [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) and [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d) to control quadratic cost, and use SAM or Adam with careful rho/weight-decay sensitivity as in [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e). Finally, weaker suggestions include adding lag-selection diagnostics from [(Flores et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/15e5966f9f9d1570083c7693374f12d0529ea052) only as a sanity check, and including traffic or PEMS-style short-horizon tests if the goal is to compare with [(Srinivas et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f2c5528b2690dc5d50e5be760a9082cb887d878b), but these are less central than the ablation, baseline, and patch-attention design actions.

## To enable practical adoption in existing forecasting architectures, the plan states: 'The methodology integrates these modules within a conventional attention framework, enabling our workflow to function as a drop-in replacement for attention modules.'

### Support

The strongest support for SPACE's drop-in attention-replacement claim comes from [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d4f7cc5b270073061a3cf219c62881f55c6351), which explicitly positions MTS-Mixers as an alternative to attention in Transformer-style multivariate forecasting, reports best or second-best MSE and MAE, averaged MSE reductions of 18.1% on ECL, 10.8% on Traffic, 17.0% on PeMS04, 7.2% on Weather, and 39.0% on ILI, and shows better training/inference efficiency than Transformer-based methods under input length 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720, matching the planned protocol. This newer mixer-based result builds on earlier attention-module work such as [(Shih et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d1d53c671b20db116ba8c91c6446bb4757614da) and [(Liu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c12ecd93ba58cc38530d8ddd495ad1f76dde39), showing that the field has moved from adding attention to RNN/CNN backbones toward replacing or augmenting attention in transformer-style forecasting. The next strongest support is [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), whose FEDformer-f and FEDformer-w replace self-attention and cross-attention with Fourier or wavelet projections, report O(L) time and memory complexity with fixed modes, and show distribution-similarity advantages on ETTm1 and ETTm2, supporting modular attention replacement in the same long-horizon ETT/Weather-style setting. Strong support also comes from [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e), which modifies the attention component of a multivariate transformer, reports a 14.33% improvement over TSMixer with about four times fewer parameters, and shows channel-wise attention outperforming temporal-wise attention, indicating that attention-module changes can materially affect MSE/MAE on ETTh1, Exchange, and Weather. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) supports the conventional attention framework and exogenous-variable integration by using cross-attention between an endogenous global token and exogenous variable tokens, with strong averaged results on ECL, Weather, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, and Traffic under input length 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720, although its attention maps are correlational rather than causal. [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa) supports SPACE's patchified embedding and conventional transformer setting by using patchified inputs, instance normalization, causal decoder-only attention, and strong LSF results on ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Electricity, and Weather, but it uses a 512-step context and probabilistic head, so it is less direct evidence for a drop-in causal module. Earlier evidence that specialized attention can improve forecasting is provided by [(Shih et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d1d53c671b20db116ba8c91c6446bb4757614da), whose attention mechanism with CNN filters outperforms conventional attention and LSTNet variants on Solar Energy, Traffic, Electricity, and Exchange Rate, with ablations such as Solar Energy RSE 0.4389 +/- 0.0084 with Sigmoid and Position Filter versus 0.4598 +/- 0.0011 without CNN, although the architecture is RNN/CNN rather than a transformer drop-in. [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f) supports attention replacement conceptually because Autoformer uses auto-correlation and decomposition as an attention-replacement module in a transformer-like architecture, but the excerpt provides only qualitative model analysis rather than numeric MSE/MAE. [(Wu, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b4ec3cddfc06c1f060d66a3123f8a9a0b157d23) supports drop-in attention replacement by inserting FSatten and SOatten into Temporal and Variate Transformers, reporting full-rank attention matrices with rank 21 and condition numbers 1,519 and 1,480 versus 78,596,560 for conventional attention, but it lacks full benchmark MSE/MAE. [(Dong et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d72d80e8e1f6374b6f9322ddad7c1ff1f3613e0) supports graph-based correlation weighting in multivariate forecasting, with J-NGT improving MAE by 39.0% over DA-RNN, 47.7% over DSTP, and 39.6% over hDS-RNN on air quality, but it uses correlation-based graph attention rather than transfer entropy. [(Feng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f77ce56d4fc5500d43eaf215d17e816253a7cc6) supports attention-based sequence modeling with multi-scale attention and dynamic relative position embedding, reporting MSE reductions of 24% on Solar, 11% on Electricity, 17% on Traffic, and 23% on Wikipedia, but it is probabilistic and not a point-forecasting drop-in. [(Yang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5d824c29c8f8cd5d6f514224165f7d91c36b280) supports modular ablation by showing that replacing or removing VCA or KTD increases MSE and MAE, with larger VCA degradation on high-variate Traffic and Electricity and larger KTD degradation on volatile Exchange, but exact values are absent. Weaker support comes from [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), whose ProbSparse self-attention is an efficient attention module for long sequences on ETT, ECL, and Weather but the excerpt lacks quantitative results; [(Liu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c12ecd93ba58cc38530d8ddd495ad1f76dde39), whose DSTP-RNN attention modules are reported to improve over encoder-decoder without attention but lacks numeric RMSE/MAE; and [(Liu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30dcc0e191a376fea0e7a46f94c53872c029efc9), whose Pyraformer PAM is a hierarchical attention module but the excerpt provides no datasets, metrics, or quantitative outcomes.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed causal drop-in method is that several newer non-causal attention or non-attention modules already achieve state-of-the-art forecasting, weakening the claim that causal transfer entropy is necessary for superior performance. [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d4f7cc5b270073061a3cf219c62881f55c6351) shows MTS-Mixers, a non-attention mixer, achieves best or second-best MSE/MAE and large MSE reductions, while observing that many vanilla Transformer attention heads capture little useful information, suggesting that replacing attention with a causal module must overcome a very strong non-causal baseline. [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e) shows non-causal channel-wise attention with SAM and reparameterization outperforms TSMixer by 14.33% with fewer parameters, and [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) shows correlational cross-attention over exogenous variables is highly competitive on ETT, Weather, ECL, and Traffic, so SPACE's causal weights must demonstrate gains beyond correlation. These newer results contradict the older premise from attention-augmented RNN work such as [(Shih et al., 2018-09)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5d1d53c671b20db116ba8c91c6446bb4757614da) and [(Liu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c12ecd93ba58cc38530d8ddd495ad1f76dde39) that adding a specialized attention component is sufficient for strong forecasting. A second major contradiction is that many successful attention replacements are not simple drop-in modules but require architectural co-design. [(Liu et al., unknown_d)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/30dcc0e191a376fea0e7a46f94c53872c029efc9) presents Pyraformer as a full hierarchical transformer with CSCM and PAM rather than a drop-in causal module; [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f) couples auto-correlation with progressive series decomposition; [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06) couples frequency-enhanced attention with seasonal-trend decomposition and fixed Fourier/wavelet modes; [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e) relies on SAM, spectral/nuclear-norm-preserving reparameterization, and channel-wise attention; and [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa) relies on patchification, instance normalization, pretraining, and a probabilistic Student-T head. This contradicts the assumption that SPACE's Cross-TE and CGNN can be inserted unchanged into arbitrary conventional attention frameworks. A third contradiction concerns the pTE normality assumption. The plan uses pseudo transfer entropy assuming normal distribution, but related work includes non-periodic, volatile, skewed, or high-dimensional series where Gaussian assumptions are risky: [(Feng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f77ce56d4fc5500d43eaf215d17e816253a7cc6) notes Exchange lacks obvious periodicity and yields mediocre performance, [(Yang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5d824c29c8f8cd5d6f514224165f7d91c36b280) highlights volatile Exchange where KTD removal causes larger drops, and [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa) evaluates Datadog observability series with 17.07% extreme right skew and 12.20% sparse characteristics, while the plan's financial-index datasets are likely non-Gaussian. A fourth contradiction is scalability. Transfer entropy is computationally expensive, and although fast-pTE reduces complexity from O(d^2T) to O(dT), high-dimensional benchmarks such as Traffic with 963 dimensions in [(Feng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f77ce56d4fc5500d43eaf215d17e816253a7cc6), ECL with 321 clients in [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), NASDAQ100 with 81 exogenous series in [(Liu et al., 2019-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/82c12ecd93ba58cc38530d8ddd495ad1f76dde39), and Taxi with 1214 dimensions in [(Feng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f77ce56d4fc5500d43eaf215d17e816253a7cc6) make pairwise causal estimation a potential bottleneck. A fifth contradiction is architectural mismatch with channel-independent or variable-token attention. [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e) finds channel-wise attention outperforms temporal-wise attention, [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) uses variable-level exogenous tokens and channel independence, and [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d4f7cc5b270073061a3cf219c62881f55c6351) uses interleaved subsequences, all of which may not align with SPACE's patchified 2D temporal embedding and CGNN adjacency. Finally, the related work provides no direct evidence that transfer entropy or causal graph neural networks improve forecasting over correlation-based attention; the closest causal-adjacent evidence is correlation-based graph attention in [(Dong et al., 2022-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2d72d80e8e1f6374b6f9322ddad7c1ff1f3613e0) and attention-map interpretability in [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1), which do not establish causal superiority.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the drop-in claim testable by defining explicit tensor contracts for SPACE as a replacement for self-attention, cross-attention, and channel-wise attention, then validating it in at least three conventional backbones such as a Temporal Transformer, an iTransformer-style variate/channel-independent transformer, and a patch-based transformer, while keeping all other components fixed and ablating only the attention module. This should follow the modular evaluation style of [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d4f7cc5b270073061a3cf219c62881f55c6351), [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), and [(Wu, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b4ec3cddfc06c1f060d66a3123f8a9a0b157d23), reporting MSE and MAE with standard deviations over multiple seeds, parameter counts, training/inference time, and memory on the planned ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial-index datasets. The second most important action is to strengthen baselines beyond the planned eight models by including MTS-Mixers, SAMformer, TimeXer, FEDformer, Pyraformer, Informer, and, where feasible, the decoder-only transformer from [(Cohen et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/325e7ce8ff734f133cfa379bd8ac1021604c68aa), because these are strong non-causal or attention-replacement references on the same benchmarks. The third action is to address pTE normality and computational cost: compare pTE with conventional TE, kernel or robust TE, and correlation-based attention; standardize or transform series; profile fast-pTE on high-dimensional ECL, Traffic, and financial indices; and consider sparse top-k causal edges, low-rank adjacency, or learned causal weights to reduce O(dT) overhead. The fourth action is to align the experimental protocol with the strongest related work: use input length 96, horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720 where applicable, 6:2:2 or 7:1:2 splits, MSE/MAE, early stopping, Adam or AdamW, and report hardware, seeds, hyperparameters, and statistical significance, as done or implied by [(Li et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4d4f7cc5b270073061a3cf219c62881f55c6351), [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1), and [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e). The fifth action is to test channel-dependent and channel-independent variants, including variable-level exogenous tokens as in [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) and channel-wise attention as in [(Ilbert et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c8f5f6d981d96ebc84ba0a2665f00be3d1cfc27e), because a single 2D patchified CGNN may not be a universal drop-in. The sixth action is to add robustness and interpretability analyses: noise injection and longer-horizon tests inspired by [(Feng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8f77ce56d4fc5500d43eaf215d17e816253a7cc6), dataset-dependent module ablations inspired by [(Yang et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a5d824c29c8f8cd5d6f514224165f7d91c36b280), and Weather adjacency visualization against climate-scientific expectations, while also reporting attention rank/condition or sparsity metrics as in [(Wu, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0b4ec3cddfc06c1f060d66a3123f8a9a0b157d23) to avoid overclaiming causality from pTE alone.

## To validate the approach across real-world forecasting domains, the plan states: 'We will conduct comprehensive experiments on 9 real-world datasets to validate our approach:' using ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, and financial index datasets.

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed 9-dataset validation is the standardized TSLib protocol [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/425182f5c96c1d239da5cbe3a24371b2bab6319b), which fixes a look-back length of 96 and prediction horizons of 96, 192, 336, and 720, evaluates MSE and MAE, and covers ETT, Weather, Exchange, Electricity, Traffic, and Illness; this directly matches the SPACE plan’s H=96, F∈{96,192,336,720}, MSE/MAE evaluation, and public dataset overlap, and its factory-pattern fair-comparison design supports using identical training strategies and test sets. A second strong support is TimeFilter [(Hu et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a441f7bb94e09559b90a7999a8ee9ba299f3f78), which evaluates 9 long-term datasets with input length 96 and the same horizons, uses graph-based dependency modeling, and reports statistically significant gains, including 4.48% MSE and 2.23% MAE reductions versus Leddam with Wilcoxon p=4.66e-10, plus ablation evidence such as Weather MSE rising from 0.239 to 0.244 without filtering and Electricity MSE rising from 0.158 to 0.167; this supports the planned CGNN-style graph aggregation, ablation studies, and statistical reporting. UniTST [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d) supports the same 9-dataset, H=96, 96/192/336/720 protocol and the importance of cross-variate modeling, reporting best results among 11 baselines on 7 of 9 datasets for MSE and 8 of 9 for MAE, although it uses attention rather than transfer entropy. MSGNet [(Cai et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2133f29fe00e8c6b618789afd80412a6dfafe48a) supports graph-based multivariate forecasting on ETT and Weather with horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720, and provides useful hyperparameter sensitivity context for graph scale k, Mixhop order, and node embedding dimension, though it learns multi-scale correlations rather than causal TE weights. CauGNN [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) is the most direct conceptual support for transfer-entropy-guided graph forecasting because it computes a transfer entropy causality matrix, applies a significance threshold c=0.005, and uses that matrix to guide GNN aggregation on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq, but its support is weaker because it uses horizons 5, 10, and 15, older baselines, and reports only qualitative outperformance without numeric MSE/MAE or significance. Autoformer [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f) and FEDformer [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06) support the same H=96 and 96/192/336/720 benchmark protocol and provide strong non-causal decomposition and frequency-domain baselines that SPACE should compare against. Informer [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) supports the use of ETT and Weather long-term attention baselines, with ETT split 12/4/4 months and Weather split 28/10/10 months. DLinear [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e) supports including a simple linear baseline on the same dataset family, including ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Traffic, Electricity, Weather, ILI, and Exchange-Rate. Non-stationary Transformer [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092) supports adding stationarity diagnostics, using ADF tests and reporting relative stationarity in the range 97% to 103%, which is relevant to the plan’s need to separate causal gains from stationarity effects. TimesNet [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d), MOIRAI-MOE [(Liu et al., 2024-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a944dc164895d923498347894c7ed780d944066), FPN-fusion [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1732a14e54b1f4893c6538bb7cdd3dc2f740e23b), AdaWaveNet [(Yu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b67b39bf62e1ae544da2ca6427613c575b08121), and Das et al. [(Das et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f45f85fa1beaa795c24c4ff86f1f2deece72252f) provide weaker but still relevant support for the planned baseline ecosystem, patch-based or foundation-model comparisons, efficiency reporting, and real-world benchmark validation, although none directly validates transfer-entropy causality.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that newer non-causal cross-variate models achieve strong results under the same protocol, undermining the claim that causal TE is necessary. UniTST [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d) achieves the best result among 11 baselines on 7 of 9 datasets for MSE and 8 of 9 for MAE using attention dispatchers rather than transfer entropy, and its attention-weight analysis shows that 87.50% of all patch-token pairs are cross-variate and cross-time versus 89.91% for the top 0.5% highest-attention pairs, suggesting that learned correlation/attention may already capture much of the useful cross-variate structure. TimeFilter [(Hu et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a441f7bb94e09559b90a7999a8ee9ba299f3f78) also contradicts the necessity of causal TE by outperforming baselines on 9 long-term datasets with H=96 and the same horizons using kNN graph dependency filtration, with Wilcoxon p=4.66e-10, showing that non-causal graph dependencies can be sufficient. MOIRAI-MOE [(Liu et al., 2024-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a944dc164895d923498347894c7ed780d944066) shows that a patch-based mixture-of-experts transformer can be best among compared baselines in zero-shot out-of-distribution forecasting, including full-shot TiDE, PatchTST, and iTransformer, and can improve CRPS by 3%-14% and MASE by 8%-16% with only 11M activated parameters, suggesting that generic patch/foundation-model mechanisms may outperform specialized causal modules without TE. FPN-fusion [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1732a14e54b1f4893c6538bb7cdd3dc2f740e23b) reports that a linear-complexity FPN model achieves 13 best MSE and 11 best MAE results across 16 univariate ETT tests, with an average 11.0% MSE reduction versus DLinear and 7 better, 6 tied, and 3 worse MSE entries versus PatchTST, challenging the need for complex causal modules. AdaWaveNet [(Yu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b67b39bf62e1ae544da2ca6427613c575b08121) reports state-of-the-art aggregated forecasting performance using wavelet and non-stationarity mechanisms rather than causality, and its efficiency analysis on Nvidia A10 GPUs with input length 96, ETTh1 with 7 variates, Traffic with 862 variates, and batch size 16 shows that non-causal wavelet models can remain efficient. Das et al. [(Das et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f45f85fa1beaa795c24c4ff86f1f2deece72252f) reports that a single pretrained zero-shot model can come close to or surpass task-specific tuned baselines on held-out public benchmarks, suggesting that generic pretrained forecasting may reduce the need for dataset-specific causal modules, although the excerpt is qualitative and does not test ETT, Weather, Exchange-rate, or financial indices. DLinear [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e) provides a simpler linear baseline on the same dataset family, and although the excerpt lacks numeric outcomes, its existence challenges the assumption that causal TE is required for competitive performance. Non-stationary Transformer [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092) indicates that stationarity handling, not causality, can explain performance gaps, with ADF-based diagnostics showing that de-stationary attention restores predictions close to ground-truth stationarity at 97% to 103% relative stationarity. FEDformer [(Zhou et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06) and Autoformer [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f) provide non-causal frequency and decomposition mechanisms under the same H=96/96-720 protocol, meaning that gains attributed to causality may alternatively arise from trend-seasonal decomposition or spectral filtering. Informer [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) and TimesNet [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d) represent strong attention and TCN baselines that SPACE must beat, and the closest causal TE evidence, CauGNN [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), is weak because it reports only qualitative outperformance on three datasets, uses horizons 5, 10, and 15, compares against older baselines, and provides no numeric MSE/MAE, significance tests, or GPU/training-time details, so it cannot substantiate a robust causal advantage in the planned 9-dataset, H=96, long-horizon setting.

### Suggested Action

Most important, adopt the TSLib standardized protocol [(Wang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/425182f5c96c1d239da5cbe3a24371b2bab6319b) for all 9 datasets: fixed H=96, F∈{96,192,336,720} for most datasets and F∈{48,96,192,336} for financial indices, identical train/validation/test splits, normalization, MSE/MAE reporting, and multiple random seeds with confidence intervals or paired Wilcoxon tests, mirroring the statistical rigor of TimeFilter [(Hu et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a441f7bb94e09559b90a7999a8ee9ba299f3f78). Second, add a causal-specific ablation that replaces fast-pTE/CGNN with conventional attention, random adjacency, shuffled TE, and correlation-only attention, and compare against UniTST, TimeFilter, and MSGNet to isolate causality from general graph or attention effects [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d), [(Hu et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a441f7bb94e09559b90a7999a8ee9ba299f3f78), [(Cai et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2133f29fe00e8c6b618789afd80412a6dfafe48a). Third, retain the planned baselines DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, Stationary, and TimesNet, and also add Autoformer, Informer, FEDformer, Non-stationary Transformer, and FPN-fusion where feasible, because these non-causal models are strong under the same protocol [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e), [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f), [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092), [(Li et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/1732a14e54b1f4893c6538bb7cdd3dc2f740e23b), [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d). Fourth, validate the fast-pTE claim by reporting O(dT) runtime and memory on the NVIDIA RTX 4090, comparing it against O(d²T) TE, testing the normality assumption of pseudo TE on financial indices, and performing sensitivity analysis over TE thresholds, graph sparsity, patch size, hidden dimensions, learning rate, and encoder blocks, using CauGNN’s threshold c=0.005 and MSGNet’s k/Mixhop sensitivity as design references [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), [(Cai et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2133f29fe00e8c6b618789afd80412a6dfafe48a). Fifth, add stationarity and distribution diagnostics using ADF and KS tests to ensure that gains are not due to stationarity or distribution shift, following Non-stationary Transformer and FEDformer [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092), [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06). Sixth, strengthen interpretability by visualizing learned adjacency matrices, comparing them with attention weights, and checking Weather relationships against climate-scientific expectations, using UniTST’s attention-weight analysis and TimeFilter’s graph filtration ablations as templates [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9a1f121e4fc3b2ade9237ded9a4d91822e09714d), [(Hu et al., 2025-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4a441f7bb94e09559b90a7999a8ee9ba299f3f78). Finally, document the proprietary financial index datasets’ splits, leakage controls, normalization, horizon 48/96/192/336, and computational resources, and consider adding AdaWaveNet, MOIRAI-style patch/foundation-model, or zero-shot comparisons if the plan claims efficiency or generalization beyond the 8 named baselines [(Yu et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b67b39bf62e1ae544da2ca6427613c575b08121), [(Liu et al., 2024-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/7a944dc164895d923498347894c7ed780d944066), [(Das et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f45f85fa1beaa795c24c4ff86f1f2deece72252f).

## To benchmark against current practice, the plan states: 'We will compare against 8 state-of-the-art models including:' DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, Stationary, and TimesNet.

### Support

The strongest support for benchmarking against DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, Stationary, and TimesNet is that recent long-horizon MTS studies already use this exact or nearly exact baseline set under the same protocol as SPACE. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) evaluates TimeXer with look-back 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, 720 on ECL, Weather, ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, and Traffic, using MSE and MAE, and compares DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, Crossformer, and TimesNet; its reported averages (e.g., TimeXer 0.171/0.270 on ECL, 0.241/0.271 on Weather, 0.437/0.437 on ETTh1) show that the proposed baselines are current and directly comparable, and the paper states that implementation details, code, and statistical significance information are in appendices. [(Liu et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/afeeb8f5018eebb1a1d334b94dbbfc48d167efef) similarly positions iTransformer against ten baselines including Autoformer, FEDformer, Stationary, Crossformer, PatchTST, DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, SCINet, and TimesNet, and its ablation isolates variate-dimension attention versus temporal feed-forward, which is the same design axis SPACE plans to contrast with transfer-entropy weighting. [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002) further supports the benchmark and the CGNN idea by using H=96, horizons 96/192/336/720, MSE/MAE, four RTX 4090 24GB GPUs, batch size 32 reduced to 16 when memory was insufficient, and graph convolution with a learnable adjacency matrix; it reports best MSE/MAE 16 times and average reductions of 4.21 percent on ETT, 7.25 percent on Electricity, 4.47 percent on Weather, and 14.65 percent on PEMS versus iTransformer, although it does not use transfer entropy. Direct support for individual baselines comes from [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e) for DLinear, [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092) for Stationary, and [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d) for TimesNet; DLinear is a variate-wise linear trend/seasonal baseline on nine datasets, Stationary uses stationarization and De-stationary attention with ADF-based stationarity diagnostics and reports relative stationarity near 97 to 103 percent, and TimesNet uses 2D vision backbones across five tasks. Additional support for the benchmarking style comes from [(Zhou et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b7f5488c380cf5085a5dd93e993ad293b225eee), which follows TimesNet settings and compares TimesNet, PatchTST, DLinear, Stationary, Autoformer, FEDformer, N-HiTS, N-BEATS, ETSformer, and LightTS; [(Cheng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef564011ceac0666afdc0001cb58aae8437000f0), which compares DLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, and Crossformer on Weather, Traffic, Electricity, Illness, ETTh1, ETTm1, and Solar-Energy with look-back 336; [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16f01c1b3ddd0b2abd5ddfe4fdb3f74767607277), which uses ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity, Traffic, and ILI with MSE/MAE and compares PatchTST, DLinear, and TimesNet, although with input length 512; and [(Alharthi, and Mahmood, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/286a643cebfa81499f02ead76b0be31caa7d8735f), which supports the dataset families and long-horizon protocol, although with look-back 512 and without the proposed baselines. Weaker but still relevant support is provided by older attention benchmarks [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), [(Wu et al., 2021-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/fc46ccb83dc121c33de7ab6bdedab7d970780b2f), and [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), which establish ETT/ECL/Weather long-horizon evaluation, ProbSparse attention, progressive decomposition, and frequency-domain modeling, and by [(Olivares et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1c704cf788dabd2731a2944a48577b85d9c4a3f), which demonstrates rigorous forecasting benchmarking with Bayesian optimization using 42 iterations, ensembling that improved NBEATSx by about 3 percent, and Giacomini-White significance tests, even though its NBEATSx electricity-price setting does not use the proposed eight baselines.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the proposed causal-attention advantage is that simple linear and reversible-normalization baselines can already match or beat the attention and TCN models that SPACE plans to outperform. [(Li et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d652d1a8b71c6dbdca086ff93a7a5f5ebb0606da) reports that a simple linear baseline with reversible normalization can outperform PatchTST on ETTh1 across four prediction lengths, that PatchTST and TimesNet do not show significant improvement over simple linear baselines on real-world benchmarks, and that high-channel Weather/Electricity cases require channel-independent or nonlinear handling; this directly challenges the assumption that adding pTE and CGNN will produce a clear gain over DLinear/RLinear/PatchTST/TimesNet. A second strong contradiction is that patching plus lightweight attention can outperform much larger LLM-based forecasters, so SPACE must show that its Sequence Enhancer and causal graph add value beyond simple patch-attention encoders. [(Tan et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df0d604b8e8e3b2947d9865d735f204c08635012) finds ablations outperform Time-LLM in 26/26 cases, CALF in 22/26, and OneFitsAll in 19/26 across 13 datasets and two metrics, with 95% bootstrapped confidence intervals overlapping in some LLM-favorable cases, and reports extreme cost differences such as Time-LLM 6,652M parameters and 181 minutes versus w/o LLM 0.198M parameters and 0.99 minutes on ETTh1 horizon 96. A third contradiction is that the proposed eight-baseline set may omit stronger recent models. [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) shows TimeXer generally outperforms DLinear, TiDE, RLinear, PatchTST, Crossformer, and TimesNet on ETT/Weather, while also showing that the proposed baselines remain competitive in specific cells, e.g., iTransformer 0.428/0.282 on Traffic versus TimeXer 0.466/0.287, TiDE 0.244 MAE on ECL, and RLinear 0.434 MAE on ETTh1. [(Cheng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef564011ceac0666afdc0001cb58aae8437000f0) similarly shows GridTST is superior in 26 of 28 dataset-horizon tasks versus DLinear, iTransformer, PatchTST, and Crossformer, with Weather horizon 96 MSE/MAE 0.145/0.195 versus PatchTST 0.151/0.200 and iTransformer 0.163/0.212, although DLinear still beats GridTST on ETTh1 horizon 192 with 0.405/0.416 versus 0.409/0.418. A more targeted contradiction to the causal-graph component is that learned static graph structures can be more effective than dynamic inter-series correlations. [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002) reports that static graph structures were more effective than dynamic inter-series correlations for high-dimensional long-term forecasting, which suggests that SPACE's pTE-derived adjacency matrix must be carefully ablated against static learned or correlation-based graphs. Weaker contradictions concern protocol and model scope: [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16f01c1b3ddd0b2abd5ddfe4fdb3f74767607277) and [(Alharthi, and Mahmood, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/286a643cebfa81499f02ead76b0be31caa7d8735f) use longer look-backs of 512 and report strong performance on overlapping ETT, Weather, Electricity, Traffic, and ILI datasets, with PeMS also appearing in the latter, implying that SPACE's fixed H=96 may understate the difficulty or miss gains from longer context; [(Olivares et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1c704cf788dabd2731a2944a48577b85d9c4a3f) and [(Tan et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df0d604b8e8e3b2947d9865d735f204c08635012) also show that credible forecasting claims require statistical significance, ensembling or confidence intervals, and computational reporting, whereas the proposed plan currently specifies only MSE/MAE on an RTX 4090 without seeds, error bars, or significance tests. Finally, none of the supplied related work directly validates pseudo transfer entropy, normality assumptions, or causal CGNN as a forecasting advantage, so the causal hypothesis remains unsupported by the strongest quantitative evidence.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the benchmark statistically credible and reproducible before claiming causal superiority: run multiple seeds, report mean and standard deviation or 95% confidence intervals for MSE/MAE, and use paired significance tests such as Giacomini-White, Diebold-Mariano, or bootstrap tests, following [(Olivares et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1c704cf788dabd2731a2944a48577b85d9c4a3f) and [(Tan et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df0d604b8e8e3b2947d9865d735f204c08635012). The second most important action is to strengthen the baseline set by adding at least TimeXer, GridTST, and ForecastGrapher, or by explicitly justifying their omission, because [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1), [(Cheng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef564011ceac0666afdc0001cb58aae8437000f0), and [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002) show that these models are competitive with or stronger than several of the eight proposed baselines under H=96 or comparable protocols. The third action is to add causal-specific ablations that isolate the contribution of pTE and CGNN: compare pTE adjacency against learned attention, correlation-based adjacency, random/identity graph, static learned graph, and dynamic graph, and separately remove the Sequence Enhancer, patching, or the 2DMixer, because [(Li et al., 2023-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/d652d1a8b71c6dbdca086ff93a7a5f5ebb0606da) and [(Tan et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df0d604b8e8e3b2947d9865d735f204c08635012) show that simple linear, reversible-normalization, and patch-attention models are strong. The fourth action is to validate the causal interpretation and pTE assumptions: test normality and robustness of pseudo transfer entropy, compare learned adjacency to attention weights, partial correlations, Granger causality, or domain-known Weather relationships, and report stability across seeds and horizons, since the plan's interpretability claim currently lacks a direct causal ground truth. The fifth action is to align and stress-test the experimental protocol: use official implementations or TimesNet-style settings as in [(Zhou et al., 2023-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b7f5488c380cf5085a5dd93e993ad293b225eee), keep H=96 as the main setting supported by [(Wang et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/db5cb5354d8c1ff9cccf2a1c9ef5c218c9323cc1) and [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002), keep the financial-index horizons 48/96/192/336, add sensitivity analyses for H=336 or H=512 motivated by [(Cheng et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ef564011ceac0666afdc0001cb58aae8437000f0), [(Jin et al., 2023-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/16f01c1b3ddd0b2abd5ddfe4fdb3f74767607277), and [(Alharthi, and Mahmood, 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/286a643cebfa81499f02ead76b0be31caa7d8735f), and include stationarity/distribution diagnostics such as ADF from [(Liu et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8064d3873c646dc9ff949d72c54c634a906fc092) and KS tests from [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06) for nonstationary financial indices. The least urgent but still useful action is to report computational efficiency of fast-pTE, including the claimed O(dT) cost, training time, inference time, memory, and parameter count on the NVIDIA RTX 4090, because [(Tan et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/df0d604b8e8e3b2947d9865d735f204c08635012) and [(Olivares et al., 2021-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/e1c704cf788dabd2731a2944a48577b85d9c4a3f) show that forecasting comparisons are more persuasive when accuracy is paired with cost and reproducibility details.

## To standardize forecasting evaluation, the plan states: 'All experiments will use the same input length (H=96) and prediction lengths (F∈{96,192,336,720} for most datasets, F∈{48,96,192,336} for financial indices).'

### Support

The strongest support for the proposed evaluation standard comes from [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/180860f60e6655bebc9757b695b4fbc18a0c39ee), which uses the same fixed lookback H=96 and horizons F={96,192,336,720} on eight long-term MTS benchmarks, follows the iTransformer convention, and reports MSE and MAE; FreDF achieved the best result in 70 of 80 benchmarks, an average 13 percent MSE and MAE improvement over FEDformer, and up to 33 percent improvement on Exchange-rate, showing that the proposed horizon set is a productive and comparable protocol for ETT, Weather, and Exchange-rate. This is reinforced by [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e), which also fixes input length 96 and tests F={96,192,336,720} on ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Exchange Rate, and Multi-Energy, using MSE and MAE; Patchformer was best on Weather, competitive on ETT, and achieved 720-step MSE 0.121 and MAE 0.193 on Multi-Energy, directly validating the proposed non-financial horizon set. The canonical benchmark in [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) established the same H=96 and F={96,192,336,720} protocol on ETT and Weather, with Informer reporting ETTm1 MSE 0.396, 0.411, 0.434, and 0.458 and Weather MSE 0.230, 0.254, 0.276, and 0.303 across the four horizons, providing a historical baseline that SPACE can compare against. [(Patro et al., 2024-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/809bc79e023e69af78b2739fc7e4c60bfbdd1b7b) further supports H=96 as a common lookup window across Electricity, Weather, Traffic, and ETT, although it provides no quantitative values. [(Zuo et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/545aa2619d5704971b5475178645af299af023a3) supports the broader evaluation choices of direct one-time prediction, joint multivariate training, channel-averaged MSE and MAE, and multi-horizon testing, and its sweeps include F=48, which is relevant to the proposed financial-index horizon. [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73f58b90697f957832f5090946894480849dea3a) supports held-out ETT and Weather forecasting and the idea that a single model can be evaluated across forecast lengths, while [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f72a33524134abc68e0987cc51cc70704b846576) supports standardized horizon-based MSE comparison at scale, although it does not use H=96 or causal modules.

### Contradictions

The strongest quantitative caution is that [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e) reports sharp long-horizon degradation on Exchange Rate, with MSE rising to 1.071 at F=720, so applying F=720 to Exchange-rate or financial-like series may be less meaningful than the proposed financial-index set F={48,96,192,336}; the plan must clarify whether Exchange-rate uses the 720-step horizon. A second important tension is that [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373) evaluates DLinear on ETTh1, ETTh2, and Weather with the same F={96,192,336,720} but uses a 336-step lookback rather than H=96, and reports 10 repeated runs with standard deviations; this suggests that H=96 may not be universally optimal for these datasets and that the proposed fixed-H protocol should be validated against longer contexts and repeated-run uncertainty. [(Zuo et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/545aa2619d5704971b5475178645af299af023a3) provides evidence that shorter observation windows can be effective in data-scarce settings, including a fixed T=48 protocol with up to 34 percent MSE improvement over DLinear and JAAD horizons with T in {10,15,30} and up to 55 percent predictive-accuracy improvement, which challenges the assumption that H=96 is appropriate for short-horizon financial indices with F=48. [(Liu et al., 2024-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/73f58b90697f957832f5090946894480849dea3a) reports that longer lookback and flexible sequence lengths can improve forecasting, implying that a single fixed H=96 may be suboptimal even if it is convenient. Finally, many of the strongest protocol-matching works, including [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8), [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e), [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/180860f60e6655bebc9757b695b4fbc18a0c39ee), and [(Liu et al., 2024-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/f72a33524134abc68e0987cc51cc70704b846576), do not report confidence intervals or significance tests, and none directly validates the proposed transfer-entropy or CGNN causal mechanism; thus the related work supports the benchmark protocol but does not by itself support the causal hypothesis.

### Suggested Action

Most important: explicitly assign Exchange-rate and all financial indices to F={48,96,192,336}, or if Exchange-rate is kept at F=720, justify it and report degradation separately, motivated by [(Hong et al., 2024-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ca175146dfa9293e72c5d6fbb902be8565960d9e) and [(Zhang et al., 2024-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/180860f60e6655bebc9757b695b4fbc18a0c39ee). ## To measure forecasting accuracy, the plan states: 'We will assess performance using Mean Squared Error (MSE) and Mean Absolute Error (MAE) across different prediction horizons.'

### Support

First and strongest, [(Zhang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/017bc4b451726ea90d132a20452b99e708d9c510) directly validates the proposed evaluation by measuring MSE and MAE for MTST on seven multivariate datasets, including ETTh1, ETTh2, ETTm1, ETTm2, Weather, Electricity, and Traffic, using horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720, which matches the SPACE plan for most datasets; it also compares against DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, FEDformer, and Autoformer, and uses Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap at 5 percent significance, reporting best MSE/MAE in all cases and significance in 27 of 28 MSE cases versus PatchTST, so it supports both the metric choice and the need for statistical testing. Second, [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373) provides the strongest operational support for the metric definition by defining multivariate MSE and MAE as per-channel errors averaged over data points and then averaged across channels, treating each horizon as a separate task, using ETTh1, ETTh2, Weather, and ILI with 6:2:2 splits, DLinear backbone, 10 repeated runs, and standard deviations, which is directly transferable to SPACE. Third, [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e) supports the use of MSE and MAE and the inclusion of DLinear as a baseline on nine multivariate datasets, including ETT, Weather, Electricity, ILI, and Exchange-Rate, although the excerpt lacks numerical results. Fourth, [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06) supports the plan's input length of 96 and horizons 96, 192, 336, and 720 in a long-horizon transformer benchmark, and its Kolmogorov-Smirnov analysis on ETTm1 and ETTm2 shows that horizon-wise error metrics can be complemented by distributional similarity. Fifth, [(Koohfar, and Dietz, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a69105d4b35988027703d6c6dc8da521f61ae91c) supports horizon-wise MSE and MAE evaluation with detailed reproducibility details, including an NVIDIA A40 GPU, 50 epochs, batch size 256, three random seeds, Optuna tuning, and Adam hyperparameters, but it is weaker because it uses univariate datasets and shorter horizons of 24 to 96. Sixth, [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) supports the dataset selection for ETT and Weather multivariate forecasting, but the excerpt does not state MSE/MAE or horizon protocol, making it weaker. Finally, [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc68822fa8c86ae2774db8b635ee57496d36e045) supports the relevance of ETT and Weather forecasting tasks and adapter-based baselines, but it does not explicitly report MSE/MAE or horizons in the excerpt, so it is the weakest support for the proposed metric statement. Read iteratively, Informer 2020 established the ETT/Weather benchmark, FEDformer 2022 refined the input and horizon protocol and added distributional testing, LTSF-Linear 2022 anchored linear baselines, and MTST 2023 plus Arabi 2024 consolidated MSE/MAE multi-horizon evaluation with significance and repeats; however, none of these works directly validates the pseudo transfer entropy or CGNN component of SPACE.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to treating MSE and MAE alone as sufficient is [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), which shows that on ETTm1 and ETTm2 with 96-step inputs, most transformer baselines produce Kolmogorov-Smirnov p-values below 0.01, indicating predicted sequences may come from a different distribution than the input, while FEDformer and Autoformer have larger p-values; this contradicts the implicit assumption that point-error metrics fully capture long-horizon forecasting quality. A second contradiction is that [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83520860f93fab6bb6255ec82642f64ca45c886b) uses RMSE and MAE under a prequential, block-wise rolling-origin protocol with retraining after each 50-point block on 2000 simulated univariate series of length 2000, and reports Friedman p-values below 10^-30 and Hochberg post-hoc tests, suggesting that for drift-affected series, such as the planned financial-index datasets, a static horizon-wise MSE/MAE evaluation may be insufficient. Third, [(Zhang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/017bc4b451726ea90d132a20452b99e708d9c510) and [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373) contradict the current under-specified plan by showing that credible multivariate multi-horizon comparisons require statistical significance, repeated runs, and explicit per-channel averaging; MTST uses Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap at 5 percent, while Arabi et al. report standard deviations over 10 runs and note instability and GPU-memory failure for STAug on Weather. Fourth, the same newer works use a default lookback of 336 for ETTh1, ETTh2, and Weather, whereas SPACE fixes H=96, so the plan's fixed short lookback may not be directly comparable to the strongest recent transformer baselines. Fifth, [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc68822fa8c86ae2774db8b635ee57496d36e045) and [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) provide weaker contradictions or gaps because they do not explicitly report MSE/MAE, horizons, or statistical tests in the supplied excerpts, so they do not directly refute the metric choice but they leave the evaluation protocol incompletely anchored. Finally, the related work does not directly contradict the causal hypothesis, but it also does not provide quantitative evidence that pseudo transfer entropy or CGNN improves MSE/MAE over attention-based correlation, so the causal claim remains under-supported by this literature.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to specify the exact multivariate MSE and MAE computation, following [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373), by computing per-channel squared or absolute errors, averaging over time, then averaging across channels, and reporting separate results for F=96, 192, 336, 720 and for financial indices F=48, 96, 192, 336. Second, add repeated runs and statistical testing, following [(Zhang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/017bc4b451726ea90d132a20452b99e708d9c510) and [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373), by using at least three and preferably ten random seeds, reporting mean and standard deviation, and applying Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with bootstrap or Friedman/Hochberg tests against DLinear, PatchTST, TimesNet, iTransformer, Crossformer, Stationary, TiDE, and RLinear. Third, make the planned ablation that replaces transfer entropy with conventional attention a first-class statistical test, not only a qualitative comparison, by reporting MSE/MAE differences, confidence intervals or p-values, and adjacency-matrix validation on Weather; this directly tests whether the causal component justifies the added complexity. Fourth, add a distributional or uncertainty metric, such as a Kolmogorov-Smirnov test or energy distance, inspired by [(Zhou et al., 2022-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/563bac1c5cdd5096e9dbf8d4f3d5b3c4f7284e06), to check whether long-horizon predictions preserve the input distribution, especially on ETTm1 and ETTm2. Fifth, align the benchmark protocol with recent strong baselines by documenting splits, normalization, lookback sensitivity, and tuning; because [(Zhang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/017bc4b451726ea90d132a20452b99e708d9c510) and [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373) use lookback 336 for ETTh1, ETTh2, and Weather, SPACE should either justify H=96 or include a lookback ablation over 48, 96, 192, 336, and 720 where feasible. Sixth, for the proprietary financial-index datasets, consider a rolling-origin or prequential evaluation and RMSE alongside MSE, following [(Liu et al., 2023-04)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83520860f93fab6bb6255ec82642f64ca45c886b), if drift is present, or explicitly justify a fixed split. Seventh, report implementation details for reproducibility, including NVIDIA RTX 4090 memory, batch size, epochs, early stopping, optimizer, learning-rate schedule, seeds, and tuning method, as in [(Koohfar, and Dietz, 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a69105d4b35988027703d6c6dc8da521f61ae91c) and [(Arabi et al., 2024-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/8bf19ea01b4590278851956ab4349c08709fb373). Finally, retain DLinear as a mandatory baseline and include the planned transformer and TCN baselines, consistent with [(Zeng et al., 2022-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5f404dbba07619cc7f28d75d03f124a52290046e), [(Zhang et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/017bc4b451726ea90d132a20452b99e708d9c510), and [(Niu et al., 2023-11)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/dc68822fa8c86ae2774db8b635ee57496d36e045), while using [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8) only as dataset context.

## To standardize implementation, the plan states: 'We will use PyTorch implementation on NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.'

### Support

The strongest support is that PyTorch provides an eager-execution, Pythonic deep learning framework with CUDA-stream asynchronous GPU execution and shared-memory multiprocessing, which directly supports implementing the SPACE temporal embedding, sequence enhancer, cross-TE module, and CGNN as standard PyTorch modules on an NVIDIA GPU [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1). A second, more application-specific support is that a deep state-space forecasting model was implemented in PyTorch on a single NVIDIA RTX 2080Ti GPU, using shingled windows, batched random start points, deterministic time-feature exogenous variables, hidden-layer dimension 100, latent state dimension 10, one-week windows, K=5 SGVB, Adam lr 0.001, and 1000 Monte Carlo trials, showing that PyTorch-on-NVIDIA-GPU is a workable infrastructure for hourly multivariate forecasting [(Li et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad44fd783b9c9e7ca3581665e8d94a4eb7723b02). Weaker support comes from the later Informer work, which evaluates an attention-based encoder-decoder on ETT, ECL, and Weather with documented splits such as 12/4/4 months for ETTh1/ETTh2, 15/3/4 months for ECL, and 28/10/10 months for Weather, showing that the proposed ETT and Weather benchmarks are standard for attention-based MTS forecasting, although the excerpt does not confirm PyTorch or RTX 4090 [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8). The weakest support is the still later TimesNet work, which is relevant because the plan lists TimesNet as a TCN-based baseline and reports consistent state-of-the-art performance across forecasting, imputation, classification, and anomaly detection, but the excerpt does not provide PyTorch, RTX 4090, numerical MSE/MAE, or statistical significance [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d).

### Contradictions

No related work directly contradicts the choice of PyTorch, but the strongest tension is that none of the cited works validates the specific RTX 4090 hardware assumption. Paszke et al. benchmark PyTorch on a workstation with two Intel Xeon E5-2698 v4 CPUs and one NVIDIA Quadro GP100 GPU, not a consumer RTX 4090, so its framework-level performance evidence cannot be extrapolated to the proposed GPU [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1). A second limitation is that Li et al. use an older RTX 2080Ti and report only implementation details, not quantitative forecasting outcomes, baseline comparisons, or statistical significance, so it supports PyTorch time-series implementation but not the performance or reproducibility of the proposed SPACE model on a newer GPU [(Li et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad44fd783b9c9e7ca3581665e8d94a4eb7723b02). Weaker contradictions concern benchmark comparability: Informer's excerpt documents ETT, ECL, and Weather conditions but omits hardware, framework, hyperparameters, and metrics, so it cannot confirm that the proposed H=96 input, F in {96,192,336,720}, MSE/MAE evaluation, or RTX 4090 setup is directly comparable to prior attention baselines [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8). The weakest tension is that TimesNet is a strong TCN-based baseline but the excerpt defers implementation details to Table 1 and efficiency to Table 11 without confirming PyTorch, RTX 4090, dataset sizes, hyperparameters, or statistical tests, making it difficult to reproduce the baseline under the proposed standardized hardware [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d).

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the RTX 4090 standardization explicit and reproducible: report GPU VRAM, CUDA/cuDNN/PyTorch versions, driver, batch size, mixed precision, number of seeds, and a short benchmark of training throughput and peak memory for SPACE and each baseline on the largest dataset, because none of the cited works provides RTX 4090 evidence [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1). Next, use PyTorch's profiler and CUDA-stream asynchronous execution to identify bottlenecks in the cross-TE O(dT) fast-pTE and CGNN modules, and use shared-memory data loading if multi-process data loading is needed [(Paszke et al., 2019-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/3c8a456509e6c0805354bd40a35e3f2dbf8069b1). A third action is to align the experimental protocol with the most relevant time-series implementation precedent by documenting windowing, holdout, exogenous variables, optimizer, learning rate, hidden dimensions, and sampling settings, while adapting the PyTorch windowed-batch approach of Li et al. to the plan's H=96 input and F horizons [(Li et al., 2019-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ad44fd783b9c9e7ca3581665e8d94a4eb7723b02). Fourth, standardize baseline reimplementations in PyTorch on the same RTX 4090, especially TimesNet and attention baselines related to Informer, and report MSE/MAE with confidence intervals or significance tests, because the related excerpts lack quantitative outcomes and hardware confirmation [(Wu et al., 2022-10)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/47696145b3f88c4cc3f3c22035286b5d7ebce09d) [(Zhou et al., 2020-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/5b9d8bcc46b766b47389c912a8e026f81b91b0d8). Finally, if the 9 datasets, 8 baselines, ablations, and hyperparameter sensitivity exceed single-GPU capacity, specify a fallback to multi-GPU or larger-memory hardware while keeping PyTorch as the common framework.

## To isolate the contribution of causal weighting, the plan conducts the ablation: 'Impact of replacing transfer entropy with conventional attention.'

### Support

The strongest support for the ablation comes from [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd), which directly compares a forecasting model with learned causal-graph weights against a dense uniform relational GNN+LSTM baseline and a plain LSTM on 15 Google data centers over 533 days, using the top 200 services and overall network traffic as the target. For H=10, GNN+LSTM already reduced MSE relative to LSTM by 55.5% in L1, 9.14% in L3, 35.7% in L4, 23.3% in L8, and 37.6% in L9, while CDF with VARLiNGAM-derived causal weights further reduced MSE from 0.039 to 0.012 in L1, 8.69 to 7.65 in L3, 1.18 to 0.98 in L4, and 1.14 to 0.94 in L5; H=20 results were similar, and MAE and MAPE were consistent with the MSE trend. This supports the SPACE ablation because it shows that a generic dense relational or attention-like weighting scheme is a meaningful baseline and that causal weighting can change forecasting error, even though it uses VARLiNGAM rather than pTE and GNN/LSTM rather than the proposed attention/CGNN architecture. A second, more diagnostic support is [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4), which evaluates transfer entropy as a pairwise causal-link estimator on Gaussian linear stochastic networks up to about 500 nodes and sample sizes as small as 50, using permutation-test significance around 10^-3. It shows that transfer entropy without appropriate conditioning has low false negatives in noise-dominated regimes but false positives increase as spectral radius rho(A) approaches one, while optimal causation entropy keeps false-positive and false-negative ratios near zero. This supports the proposed ablation by demonstrating that pTE-derived causal weights may behave very differently from conventional attention in strongly causal or high-spectral-radius regimes, and it motivates reporting link-level false-positive and false-negative diagnostics alongside MSE and MAE. A third support is [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95), which shows in a three-node chain Z -> Y -> X that transfer entropy can produce a positive T_Z->X despite no direct coupling, while an iterative causation-entropy procedure selects the true dominant neighbor and avoids the spurious edge; this directly motivates testing whether replacing raw pTE with conventional attention changes model behavior, although the study uses symbolic sequences and coupling strengths epsilon 0.2 to 0.4 rather than ETT, Weather, Exchange, or financial-index forecasting. The weakest support is [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932), which finds that a TE-feedback MLP, FF+FB, reaches target accuracy faster than a standard MLP on XOR and several classification datasets, with average XOR epochs of 62.2 versus 349.9 and a 7 to 10 times reduction, but it uses a 2-2-1 MLP, binning threshold g=0.7, learning rate 0.025, 200 training vectors per epoch, and a 300-epoch cap, and it does not evaluate attention, MTS forecasting, or statistical significance.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction to the assumption that pTE causal weighting will reliably improve SPACE is [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4), which shows that transfer entropy without appropriate conditioning can over-infer causal links when indirect paths are hard to separate from direct ones: false-positive rates increase substantially as rho(A) approaches one, conditional Granger causality degrades as network size grows and especially when nodes exceed sample size, and only optimal causation entropy with an inferred parent set maintains near-zero false-positive and false-negative ratios. Because SPACE uses pseudo transfer entropy with a normal-distribution assumption and a fast O(dT) approximation, rather than an explicitly optimized parent-set or causation-entropy estimator, this evidence suggests that the causal adjacency may contain spurious links in strongly autocorrelated or high-signal datasets such as ETT or Weather, potentially making conventional attention a competitive or superior replacement. A second contradiction is [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95), which demonstrates that transfer entropy can infer a spurious direct Z -> X influence from the indirect path Z -> Y -> X, whereas an iterative causation-entropy procedure avoids that error; this directly challenges the use of unconditioned or pairwise pTE as a clean causal weighting mechanism, although the evidence is limited to small symbolic dynamical systems and does not quantify forecasting MSE or MAE. A third contradiction is [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd), because the dense uniform GNN+LSTM baseline already captures large forecasting gains over LSTM, with MSE reductions of 55.5% in L1, 35.7% in L4, 23.3% in L8, and 37.6% in L9 for H=10, while the additional benefit of learned causal weights is sometimes modest, such as 8.69 to 7.65 in L3 and 1.18 to 0.98 in L4, and no statistical significance tests are reported. This does not refute causal weighting, but it contradicts the expectation that causal weights will automatically dominate a conventional dense attention or relational baseline. The weakest contradiction is [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932), which shows that TE-based feedback is not uniformly stable: FF+FB failed to reach 100% training accuracy in one XOR run and hit the 300-epoch cap, while FF failed in other runs, and the ten-dataset classification results lack dataset-specific numerical values and significance tests. This suggests that TE weighting can be sensitive to task, hyperparameters, and optimization, but the evidence is from small MLP classification rather than the proposed attention-based MTS forecasting setting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to expand the ablation beyond a binary pTE-versus-conventional-attention comparison by adding at least three controlled variants: (i) conventional attention with the same architecture and parameter count, (ii) a dense uniform attention or relational baseline analogous to the fixed-weight GNN+LSTM in [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd), and (iii) a conditioned or iterative causal-weighting variant, such as causation entropy or transfer entropy with an inferred parent set, motivated by [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4) and [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95). This will separate whether any benefit comes from causal structure, from a non-uniform learned adjacency, or from the specific pTE estimator. The second priority is to validate the pTE adjacency itself before interpreting forecasting gains: report link-level precision, recall, F1, false-positive and false-negative rates against a domain-defined edge set for Weather and, where possible, against known or synthetic causal structures, using permutation or shuffle significance thresholds comparable to the 10^-3 level used in [(Sun et al., 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c71578f0b21109a12d4d0aba5c351218b21fcac4). Because the plan uses input length H=96 and datasets with 7, 21, 8, or proprietary series, the study should test sensitivity to sample length, spectral radius, stationarity, and the normality assumption of pTE, including preprocessing such as rolling median with window size 7, first-order differencing, and z-score normalization where appropriate, as in [(Fatemi et al., 2023-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21b1d3c9fa6f7dbae207a542a82ec27dad122fdd). The third priority is to add statistical rigor and reproducibility details: multiple random seeds, mean and standard deviation or confidence intervals for MSE and MAE across horizons F in {96,192,336,720} and financial F in {48,96,192,336}, paired bootstrap or permutation tests for each ablation, and explicit reporting of the RTX 4090 runtime and memory for fast-pTE versus exact pTE or attention. The fourth priority is to include TE-specific ablation controls inspired by [(Moldovan et al., 2020-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/83d7e0eef525f07867fe19279f9b64f1b5205932), such as fixing all pTE weights, scaling pTE to [0,1], randomizing pTE, or replacing pTE with a shuffled adjacency, to determine whether the model benefits from the actual causal signal rather than from an additional non-linear module. The least urgent but still useful action is to strengthen interpretability by visualizing learned adjacency matrices and attention maps, quantifying agreement with climate-scientific relationships in Weather, and comparing those visualizations to the spurious indirect-edge behavior documented in [(Sun, and Bollt, 2014-01)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/6fcaba22c15e0df6b14acb557f8f7c6117a63a95).

## To test the role of sequence enhancement, the plan conducts the ablation: 'Effect of removing the Sequence Enhancer module.'

### Support

The strongest support for the planned ablation, 'Effect of removing the Sequence Enhancer module,' comes from [(Li et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ba0186ed40656329c421f55ada7313293e13f17), which directly compares a fixed-length input autoregressive DCNN against DCRNN-SEQ, an encoder-decoder sequence-to-sequence variant, and reports that DCRNN-SEQ outperforms DCNN by a large margin on MAE across forecasting horizons. This is relevant to SPACE because the Sequence Enhancer is also a sequence-level component intended to improve forecasting by sharing patch-level temporal information before causal processing; the prior ablation therefore supports the scientific value of testing whether removing such a component degrades performance. A secondary, but still useful, point is that the related work finds the advantage of sequence-level modeling and scheduled sampling becomes more pronounced as the forecasting horizon increases, which aligns with SPACE's evaluation over F in {96,192,336,720} and suggests the Sequence Enhancer ablation should be interpreted separately for short and long horizons. Weaker support comes from the related work's implementation discipline: variants are described as having roughly the same number of parameters, hyperparameters are selected by Tree-structured Parzen Estimator on a 10% validation split, and evaluation is done across multiple horizons on METR-LA with 207 sensors, 4 months of 5-minute traffic speed data, Z-score normalization, and a 70/10/20 train/validation/test split. These details support the proposed ablation design in principle, but the evidence is qualitative rather than exact, uses MAE rather than SPACE's MSE/MAE pair, and is implemented in TensorFlow rather than SPACE's PyTorch/RTX 4090 setup.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is that the related work does not test the specific mechanism proposed in SPACE: a multi-head attention Sequence Enhancer that operates on patchified 2D tensors before pseudo transfer entropy and CGNN causal aggregation. Instead, [(Li et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ba0186ed40656329c421f55ada7313293e13f17) tests sequence-to-sequence modeling and scheduled sampling within a diffusion convolutional recurrent framework on a directed traffic sensor graph, so its positive result for sequence-level modeling cannot be taken as direct evidence that SPACE's attention-based Sequence Enhancer is necessary or beneficial. A second contradiction is that the related work's best variant, DCRNN, improves over DCRNN-SEQ through scheduled sampling, implying that part of the sequence-level gain may come from training dynamics and error propagation control rather than from an architectural enhancement module. If SPACE's ablation simply removes the Sequence Enhancer while leaving other autoregressive or attention components unchanged, the result may conflate the effect of the Sequence Enhancer with the effect of training strategy, decoder structure, or residual temporal modeling in the CGNN. Weaker contradictions concern scope and measurement: the related work reports only qualitative MAE outcomes without exact numerical values, statistical significance, or multiple seeds; it uses a single traffic dataset rather than SPACE's nine electricity, weather, exchange-rate, and financial datasets; and it does not involve patchification, fast-pTE, causal graph neural networks, or the H=96 input length used in the proposed plan.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to make the 'Effect of removing the Sequence Enhancer module' ablation a controlled comparison that isolates the attention-based Sequence Enhancer: run full SPACE and a no-Sequence-Enhancer variant with matched parameter count, identical H=96 inputs, identical F horizons, identical PyTorch/RTX 4090 training recipe, and an equivalent replacement path such as an identity map, linear projection, or attention-free patch mixer, then report MSE and MAE for each horizon. The second most important action is to add a training-strategy control, inspired by [(Li et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ba0186ed40656329c421f55ada7313293e13f17), to ensure that any performance difference is not driven by scheduled sampling, teacher forcing, or autoregressive feedback rather than the Sequence Enhancer itself; if SPACE uses autoregressive decoding or similar mechanisms, include a variant that changes only that mechanism. Third, report per-horizon ablation results and statistical significance, ideally with multiple random seeds and confidence intervals, because the related work suggests sequence-level effects may be horizon-dependent and its qualitative MAE evidence is insufficient to quantify the expected gap. Finally, use validation-based hyperparameter tuning for each ablation variant, as in the related work's Tree-structured Parzen Estimator setup, and document parameter counts, learning-rate schedules, and compute settings so that the ablation is reproducible and not confounded by unequal optimization.

## To assess the contribution of the encoder, the plan conducts the ablation: 'Contribution of the entire encoder structure.'

### Support

The strongest support for ablating the entire SPACE encoder comes from [(Le, and Shlizerman, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7139c23d54bb5b39cc5e30d5a6ab3c3332dce03), which quantitatively shows that adding a spatial multihead attention block to a temporal transformer encoder improves held-out predictive metrics: on MC_Maze, the best STNDT ensemble improved over NDT from 0.3676 to 0.3862 co-bps and from 0.2589 to 0.2686 fp-bps, with PSTH R2 from 0.6683 to 0.6693, and on DMFC_RSG the ensemble improved from 0.1886 to 0.1940 co-bps, 0.1828 to 0.1910 fp-bps, and 0.6064 to 0.6452 PSTH R2. Although this is Poisson spike-count modeling rather than MTS forecasting, it is the most direct quantitative evidence that an encoder-side relational component can contribute to prediction, supporting the SPACE plan's need to test whether the full encoder, including Temporal Embedding, Sequence Enhancer, Cross-TE pTE, and CGNN/2DMixer, is responsible for gains over attention-only baselines. The next strongest support is [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), which is methodologically closest because it ablates causality-guided GNN subcomponents, CauGIN, CauGNN-nCau, and CauGNN-nCNN, on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq using transfer entropy threshold 0.005, CNN kernels 3, 5, and 7, hidden dimensions 12, 30, and 10, input window 32, and 1000 epochs; this validates the idea of isolating causality-related encoder parts in forecasting, but the excerpt lacks quantitative variant deltas, so it supports the ablation design more than the magnitude of encoder contribution. [(Zaheer et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/044e13d7dd4e0655eb76f0bd00b2c1bdb44e2be3) then supports encoder-side structural ablation by showing that a sparse long-context encoder with a full decoder improves long-document summarization and does not harm shorter summarization datasets, with an encoder-only DNA result of 1.12 bits per character for BIGBIRD length 4096 versus 1.23 for BERT length 512 and 1.57 for SRILM; this suggests SPACE should test encoder contribution across prediction horizons and input lengths rather than only one setting. The weakest but still relevant support is [(Li et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ba0186ed40656329c421f55ada7313293e13f17), an older DCRNN ablation on METR-LA with 207 sensors, 5-minute windows, and 15, 30, and 60-minute horizons, showing that sequence-to-sequence temporal structure and scheduled sampling improve multi-step forecasting over DCNN and always-fed-output variants, but only qualitatively in Figure 5 without exact MAE values or significance, so it supports the general practice of structural ablation in time series forecasting but not the specific causal encoder claim. Taken iteratively, the progression from Li's qualitative traffic ablation to Duan's causality-GNN variants, Zaheer's encoder-context evidence, and Le's quantitative spatiotemporal transformer component comparison shows that newer work increasingly quantifies encoder contributions, but none directly ablates an entire causality-based MTS encoder.

### Contradictions

The strongest tension for the proposed ablation is that [(Le, and Shlizerman, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7139c23d54bb5b39cc5e30d5a6ab3c3332dce03) reports that ablating the contrastive loss, rather than the encoder, produced further co-bps and fp-bps gains across all four neural datasets, with the largest effect on MC_Maze, although exact ablation table values are not provided; this contradicts the assumption that encoder structure alone explains predictive gains, because a training objective or auxiliary loss can also improve performance, and for SPACE an end-to-end encoder ablation may change optimization dynamics, not only the causal information pathway. The next strongest contradiction is that the closest causality-GNN work, [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), does not provide quantitative results for CauGIN, CauGNN-nCau, or CauGNN-nCNN, even though the full CauGNN model outperforms most baselines on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq; this undermines the proposed method's implicit expectation that an encoder ablation will cleanly attribute full-model gains to causality, because the full-model superiority may instead come from the CNN feature extractor, GNN layers, transfer entropy threshold, or training details, and no statistical significance is reported. [(Zaheer et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/044e13d7dd4e0655eb76f0bd00b2c1bdb44e2be3) provides a weaker but important caution: sparse attention in the encoder improves long-context summarization and DNA likelihood, but the paper also notes that sparse attention does not hamper performance on shorter summarization datasets, suggesting that encoder-side contributions can be context-dependent and task-dependent; if SPACE's encoder only helps at long horizons or on particular datasets, a single ablation could overstate its general contribution. The weakest contradiction is [(Li et al., 2017-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/9ba0186ed40656329c421f55ada7313293e13f17), whose DCRNN ablation is qualitative, lacks exact MAE values, confidence intervals, and significance tests, and shows that the always-fed-output model performs worse mainly in support of scheduled sampling rather than the entire encoder; therefore it does not provide strong evidence that removing an entire encoder structure will yield a clean, quantifiable contribution estimate in the SPACE setting.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to define the entire SPACE encoder precisely and implement a parameter-matched null encoder, for example replacing Temporal Embedding, Sequence Enhancer, Cross-TE pTE, and CGNN/2DMixer with a minimal patch-to-decoder attention or linear temporal encoder while keeping the decoder, loss, optimizer, seeds, input length H=96, prediction lengths F=96, 192, 336, and 720, datasets, and NVIDIA RTX 4090 setup fixed; report MSE and MAE deltas with 95 percent confidence intervals or paired significance across seeds, plus parameter count, FLOPs, memory, and training time, because [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) shows the need for quantitative variant comparison and [(Le, and Shlizerman, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7139c23d54bb5b39cc5e30d5a6ab3c3332dce03) shows that non-encoder components can confound attribution. The next most important action is to add subcomponent ablations that mirror both the SPACE plan and the CauGNN variants: replace pTE with conventional attention, use identity or random causality matrices, remove CGNN/2DMixer, remove Sequence Enhancer, and remove patchified Temporal Embedding, then compare on Exchange-rate, ETT, Weather, and financial indices across all horizons to separate causality, temporal attention, and spatial mixing effects. Third, following [(Zaheer et al., 2020-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/044e13d7dd4e0655eb76f0bd00b2c1bdb44e2be3) and [(Le, and Shlizerman, 2022-06)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/a7139c23d54bb5b39cc5e30d5a6ab3c3332dce03), evaluate the encoder ablation across input lengths and prediction horizons, include multiple metrics such as MSE, MAE, and optionally MAPE or directional accuracy for financial indices, and report single-run and ensemble results if Bayesian tuning or ensembling is used, while documenting hyperparameters such as learning rate, hidden dimensions, encoder blocks, pTE threshold, dropout, batch size, epochs, early stopping, and the fast-pTE O(dT) complexity. The least important but still useful action is to add interpretability controls, such as visualizing learned adjacency matrices against attention weights, using the Weather dataset to check climate-scientific relationships, and testing shuffled or random causality matrices to ensure that any encoder gain is not due to spurious correlation rather than causal structure.

## To check robustness, the plan conducts: 'Hyperparameter sensitivity analysis for learning rate, hidden dimensions, and encoder blocks.'

### Support

The strongest support comes from [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), because it is the closest iterative successor to [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716) and extends LSTNet-style validation grid search to a causal graph forecasting model. Duan et al. search input window sizes as powers of two from 2^0 to 2^9, recurrent and convolutional hidden dimensions from 10 to 100, LSTNet skip length from 0 to 48, MLCNN hidden dimensions of 10, 25, 50, and 100, dropout rates of 0.1 and 0.2, and transfer-entropy causality threshold c in [0, 0.1], selecting c = 0.005; for CauGNN variants they fix convolutional kernel sizes to 3, 5, and 7 with 12 channels, search k-GNN hidden dimensions from 10 to 100, use Adam for up to 1000 epochs, and select by validation RAE or MAE. This directly supports SPACE's plan to test hidden dimensions and component-level robustness in a causal graph model, especially the CGNN and Cross-TE modules, on datasets such as Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq. The next strongest support is [(Zhao et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ebf1b9749a6f7a26eacaf69f4503479fd93c4e0), which shows that a newer causal graph forecasting model, MTSF-DG, includes a parameter sensitivity analysis for tau, the maximum number of previous feature vectors supplied to the reasoning network, on METR-LA with 207 sensors, PEMS-BAY with 325 sensors, PEMS04 with 307 sensors, PEMS08, Solar-Energy with 137 plants, and Electricity with 321 clients; the reported qualitative finding that performance improves up to tau = 4 and is relatively stable at tau = 5 and tau = 12 supports testing structural or temporal hyperparameters in encoder-like modules, although it does not test learning rate, hidden dimensions, or encoder blocks. The older [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716) provides foundational support for grid-searching hidden dimensions, window sizes, skip length, autoregressive regularization, and dropout in LSTNet variants, with tuned models achieving best column results in 17 cases for LST-Skip and 7 cases for LST-Attn across Traffic, Solar-Energy, Electricity, and Exchange-Rate, and horizon-24 RSE improvements of 9.2%, 11.7%, and 22.2% over RNN-GRU on Solar-Energy, Traffic, and Electricity; however, it predates transformer-style encoder blocks and does not analyze learning rate. The weakest support is [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2), which only mentions a parameter study in Appendix A.4 for MTGNN after main single-step and multi-step results, indicating that robustness checks are expected in graph-based MTS forecasting but providing no hyperparameter ranges, datasets, metrics, computational resources, or quantitative outcomes. The overall progression from Lai's LSTNet grid search to Duan's CauGNN component search, and then to Zhao's MTSF-DG structural tau study, shows an iterative move from generic time-series tuning toward causal graph robustness, but it still stops short of learning-rate and encoder-block sensitivity.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is to the implicit assumption that component-level grid search or a robustness claim is sufficient: [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) claims CauGNN can share the same hyperparameters across Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq with robust performance, yet the excerpt reports no sensitivity curves, exact metric values, p-values, statistical significance, number of runs, learning rate, or hardware, and only says CauGNN outperforms most baselines in most cases at horizons 5, 10, and 15; this contradicts the proposed method's goal of a rigorous robustness check and warns SPACE must not rely on a single validation-selected configuration or qualitative superiority. The next contradiction is [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2), because its parameter study is cited as supplementary robustness for MTGNN but lacks varied hyperparameters, datasets, computational resources, implementation details, evaluation setup, and quantitative outcomes, contradicting the assumption that prior parameter studies provide a reproducible template for SPACE's learning-rate, hidden-dimension, and encoder-block analysis. The next contradiction is [(Zhao et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ebf1b9749a6f7a26eacaf69f4503479fd93c4e0), because its tau sensitivity is qualitative, reporting only that performance improves up to tau = 4 and is relatively stable at tau = 5 and tau = 12, without numerical accuracy values, confidence intervals, significance tests, exact tau grid, learning rate, hidden dimension, number of encoder blocks, or metric definitions; this contradicts the idea that structural sensitivity can be adequately demonstrated without quantitative curves. The weakest contradiction is [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716), because although it performed validation grid search for hidden dimensions, window, skip length, regularization, and dropout, it did not report per-hyperparameter sensitivity curves or statistical significance and used Adam without a learning-rate grid or sensitivity analysis; it also lacked transformer-style encoder blocks, so it contradicts any reliance on older LSTNet-style tuning as evidence that learning-rate or encoder-block robustness has already been established.

### Suggested Action

The most important action is to convert the proposed sensitivity analysis into a quantitative, reproducible protocol for learning rate, hidden dimensions, and encoder blocks: specify exact grids or random-search ranges, for example learning rates from 1e-4 to 1e-2 with Adam, hidden dimensions such as 32, 64, 128, and 256 for the temporal embedding, Sequence Enhancer, and CGNN/2DMixer, and encoder-block counts such as 1, 2, 3, and 4 attention or CGNN blocks, then report MSE and MAE across all prediction horizons F = 96, 192, 336, and 720, or F = 48, 96, 192, and 336 for financial indices, using multiple random seeds, validation-based early stopping, and the planned NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. The next most important action is to add causal-specific and component-level sensitivity checks inspired by [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) and [(Lai et al., 2017-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/b4ebfebb47e7b61d674747e378fd8eb4516e7716), including transfer-entropy or pTE threshold, graph sparsity, patch length or input window, dropout, and ablations of the Sequence Enhancer, Cross-TE, CGNN, and 2DMixer, with reported curves rather than only the best configuration. The next action is to address the gap left by [(Zhao et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/2ebf1b9749a6f7a26eacaf69f4503479fd93c4e0) by reporting numerical sensitivity values, confidence intervals, paired significance tests or effect sizes, parameter counts, and runtime for each setting, so that structural choices such as encoder depth, patch context, or reasoning-window length are not assessed qualitatively. The next action is to avoid the opacity of [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2) by placing the full sensitivity grid, dataset splits, software versions, hardware, number of runs, and selection criterion in the main text or a reproducible appendix. The least important but still useful action is to test whether a single set of hyperparameters can be shared across the nine planned datasets, as claimed for CauGNN in [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117), and to report the performance degradation relative to per-dataset tuning, which would strengthen the robustness claim for SPACE.

## To provide causal interpretability, the plan states: 'We will visualize learned adjacency matrices to demonstrate that causality-based relationships provide more meaningful insights compared to attention-based correlations, particularly using the Weather dataset to verify climate-scientific relationships.'

### Support

The strongest support is method-specific and iterative. The closest and strongest evidence is [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7), which extends earlier transfer-entropy and GNN forecasting work into a full TE adjacency visualization pipeline: it computes pairwise transfer entropy over sliding windows, converts the estimates into a directed adjacency matrix, uses that matrix in a weighted GNN, and visualizes adjacency, out-degree distributions, and top transfer-entropy events; on SMD machine-1-1 it reports 727 directed relations out of 1444 possible, and the broader CGAD model reports average F1 0.4948, composite F1 0.7445, point-adjusted F1 0.9635, with an average 9 percent improvement over baselines and a SWAT composite F1 roughly double the second-best 0.5516, using window size 15, three graph and three temporal convolution modules, 16 input/output channels, 32 hidden/skip channels, Adam lr 1e-3, batch 32, 10 epochs, and O(G N^2 w) graph-generation complexity. This directly supports SPACE's plan to visualize a TE-derived adjacency matrix, although it does not compare against attention weights or use Weather. The next strongest support is [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002), which is newer and directly evaluates Weather with h=96 and prediction lengths 96, 192, 336, and 720, reports that ForecastGrapher achieved the best MSE and MAE 16 times, and decreased error by 4.47 percent relative to iTransformer on Weather, while arguing that static GNN graph structures were more effective than dynamic attention-based correlations for long-term forecasting; this supports the Weather-focused graph-versus-attention framing, but the graph is not transfer-entropy causal and no adjacency visualization is reported. [(Tank et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce18e93b5654065c7dbc56aaa93e39fc355ed7c4) then supports learned causal-graph visualization by showing penalized neural Granger models cMLP and cLSTM, hierarchical group lasso, and directed graph visualizations, with hierarchical penalty outperforming group and mixed penalties for K=5, 10, and 20 on Lorenz-96, and MoCap graphs at threshold 0.01 showing intuitive directed interactions such as right-knee-to-left-knee and hand-to-toe connections; this is relevant to SPACE's CGNN because it demonstrates that learned causal edge weights can be thresholded and interpreted, but it does not compare with attention or Weather. [(Saetia et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1af8f7ec8bf4b59cd2e18e915e723b42d6140c7) provides weaker but conceptually relevant support for causal adjacency visualization, using HCP motor task-fMRI from 50 subjects, FSL/FEAT ROI extraction, Tigramite lag estimation and conditional independence tests, and visualizing causal-effect matrices and mediation graphs, with a reported strongest effect I_CE 12->5(15)=0.82, although it is a single-subject demonstration and not a forecasting or attention comparison. [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) supports TE-guided GNN forecasting by using a TE matrix with threshold c=0.005 to guide CauGNN on Exchange-Rate, Energy, and Nasdaq, with horizons 5, 10, and 15, window size 32, hidCNN=12, hidGNN1=30, hidGNN2=10, and qualitative outperformance over VAR, CNN-AR, RNN-GRU, LSTNet, MLCNN, and attention baselines, but it does not visualize adjacency or test Weather. [(Deng, and Hooi, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21d2742e38f7167354dafcf7f565d3894b31d008) is weaker support for visualizing learned graph and attention weights, reporting SWaT precision 99.35 percent, recall 68.12 percent, F1 0.81 and WADI precision 97.50 percent, recall 40.19 percent, F1 0.57, with PyTorch 1.5.1, four RTX 2080Ti GPUs, Adam lr 1e-3, up to 50 epochs, and early stopping patience 10, but its graph is not causal and its task is anomaly detection. Finally, the older foundational work [(Runge, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e6d57e635ebf616ae8f039aa446dfe297f6f1a5) supports the broader idea of estimating directed causal adjacency from multivariate time series using a time-ordered PC algorithm with lags 1 through tau_max, conditional independence tests, and significance alpha, but it provides no quantitative Weather or attention-comparison evidence and highlights assumptions such as causal sufficiency, faithfulness, stationarity, and measurement error.

### Contradictions

The strongest contradiction is [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9), which directly compares a causal transfer-entropy graph baseline, TEGNN, with a heterogeneous graph model, MTHetGNN, and reports that the heterogeneous model is best in most settings, especially on high-dimensional Traffic with 862 sensors and Solar-Energy with 137 plants, using window 32, horizons 3, 6, 12, and 24, and 6:2:2 splits; this undermines the claim that a static TE-derived causal adjacency is automatically more meaningful or superior to dynamic, relation-rich correlations. The next strongest contradiction is [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2), whose MTGNN learns a latent adjacency matrix without transfer entropy, without causal claims, and without adjacency visualization, yet reports state-of-the-art single-step and multi-step forecasting on four MTS and two spatial-temporal GNN datasets using MAE, RMSE, MAPE, RRSE, and CORR, with O(N^2) graph learning reduced by sampling; this suggests that effective forecasting may not require an explicitly causal TE adjacency, weakening the necessity of SPACE's causal interpretability claim. The third strongest contradiction is [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002), which partially contradicts the causal-specific claim because its Weather improvement over iTransformer, a 4.47 percent error decrease with h=96 and F in 96, 192, 336, and 720, is attributed to a static GNN graph structure rather than transfer entropy or causal visualization, so the Weather result cannot by itself validate causality-based adjacency. [(Runge, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e6d57e635ebf616ae8f039aa446dfe297f6f1a5) also contradicts the simplicity of SPACE's pTE approach by showing that constraint-based causal discovery requires careful conditional independence testing, increasing conditioning-set size p, significance alpha, maximum lag tau_max, and multiple-testing control under causal sufficiency, causal Markov, faithfulness, stationarity, and measurement-error assumptions; SPACE's pseudo transfer entropy assumes normality and reduces complexity from O(d^2T) to O(dT), which may increase false positives or miss conditional dependencies. [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117) is a weaker contradiction because although it uses a TE matrix with threshold 0.005 and CauGNN, it provides no numerical MSE, MAE, or RAE values, no statistical significance, no adjacency visualization, and no Weather or climate validation, leaving the interpretability claim unsupported. [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) is also a limitation rather than a direct contradiction: its TE graph visualization is qualitative, reports no statistical significance for the causal edges, compares only to top-k, fully connected, and Gumbel-softmax graphs rather than attention-based correlations, and is applied to server-telemetry anomaly detection rather than Weather forecasting. [(Tank et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce18e93b5654065c7dbc56aaa93e39fc355ed7c4) similarly does not compare causal graphs to attention weights and its MoCap visualization uses threshold 0.01 and p=54, T=2024, H=8, length-20 windows, so it cannot establish that causal adjacency is more interpretable than attention in SPACE's setting. [(Saetia et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1af8f7ec8bf4b59cd2e18e915e723b42d6140c7) is the weakest contradiction because its causal-effect visualization is based on a single randomly selected HCP subject rather than aggregate evidence across the 50 subjects, and it reports no baseline, statistical test, or forecasting comparison. [(Deng, and Hooi, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21d2742e38f7167354dafcf7f565d3894b31d008) is weakly contradictory to the causal-specific interpretation because it visualizes learned graph and attention weights for anomaly detection, not causal TE, and its quantitative results are precision/recall/F1 on SWaT and WADI rather than interpretability metrics.

### Suggested Action

Most important, SPACE should replace the purely visual claim with a quantitative Weather interpretability benchmark that directly compares pTE causal adjacency to attention-based adjacency from the Sequence Enhancer or iTransformer-style baselines, and it should clarify whether the adjacency is fixed pTE or end-to-end learned because learned-adjacency models such as MTGNN can behave differently from fixed TE graphs. For the 21 Weather indicators, define a climate-scientific ground-truth or expert-validated edge set, for example among pressure, temperature, humidity, wind, and precipitation variables, then compute edge precision, recall, F1, AUROC, Matthews correlation, directional accuracy, and permutation-test p-values for pTE edges versus attention edges, with bootstrap confidence intervals and FDR correction across the 21x21 directed pairs and prediction horizons F=96, 192, 336, and 720. This should be paired with the planned ablation replacing transfer entropy with conventional attention, but the ablation must report MSE and MAE changes, edge-overlap metrics, and expert ratings rather than only visual inspection. Second, adopt a rigorous TE/causal graph construction protocol informed by [(Runge, 2018-07)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/0e6d57e635ebf616ae8f039aa446dfe297f6f1a5) and [(Tank et al., 2021-03)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/ce18e93b5654065c7dbc56aaa93e39fc355ed7c4): estimate lag selection, test stationarity and normality assumptions of pTE, compare pTE with PC-style conditional independence, Granger causality, and penalized neural Granger baselines, and use validation-based thresholding or hierarchical/group lasso penalties instead of an arbitrary cutoff such as the c=0.005 threshold used by [(Duan et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/4fb598f7669a30ae783344de6bde02f14ba91117). Third, follow [(Febrinanto et al., 2023-12)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/374376e2184f669322f842166a729f3bf54a22c7) by visualizing not only adjacency matrices but out-degree distributions, top-k causal events, and comparisons to top-k, fully connected, and Gumbel-softmax graphs, while adding statistical tests for whether high-pTE edges correspond to known meteorological mechanisms. Fourth, address the dynamic-correlation contradiction from [(Wang et al., 2020-08)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/87e77ac0c054abddfef9cd8e579f10c8559217c9) and [(Wu et al., 2020-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/75e924bd79d27a23f3f93d9b1ab62a779505c8d2) by adding baselines that use learned or heterogeneous adjacency matrices, including MTGNN-style learned adjacency and MTHetGNN-style dynamic relation embeddings, and by testing whether a time-varying pTE or attention-modulated causal graph outperforms a static pTE adjacency on Weather. Fifth, use [(Cai et al., 2024-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/60560da7e2483d4f788c228ebd5e226ae1f40002) as a Weather-specific forecasting benchmark: match its h=96 setup, prediction lengths, MSE/MAE reporting, and iTransformer comparison, and explicitly report whether SPACE's causal adjacency improves over ForecastGrapher's static graph and over iTransformer's dynamic attention. Sixth, improve reproducibility by reporting the fast-pTE complexity reduction from O(d^2T) to O(dT), runtime, memory, GPU specifications beyond NVIDIA RTX 4090, random seeds, hyperparameters for hidden dimensions, encoder blocks, learning rate, threshold, and number of epochs, and by providing code for adjacency extraction and visualization. Finally, draw on [(Deng, and Hooi, 2021-05)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/21d2742e38f7167354dafcf7f565d3894b31d008) and [(Saetia et al., 2021-02)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/c1af8f7ec8bf4b59cd2e18e915e723b42d6140c7) to make the interpretability analysis aggregate rather than anecdotal: compare learned causal edges to attention weights across multiple runs, report expert or domain validation for Weather variables, and avoid relying on a single case study.
